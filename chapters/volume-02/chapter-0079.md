# Chapter 0079 — Two Books And Two Hands

Two things were written down on the Tuesday morning of the thirtieth day of this month, in two rooms about two miles apart, in two hands, and in neither of them was anything about a route, and in both of them was the name of the same man, and neither of those two facts is a coincidence and about half of this month's work is in the gap between them.

---

The first was in the front room of the shop at Brant, in the second book, in the hand of Perdie Ammid, and it took her about nine minutes and she read it out twice before she wrote it and she would not let anybody else write it and she initialled the page.

> *Requested at Brant, in this room, on the thirtieth day of this month, by T. Rusk, of no office, of no district, eleven pence, two live charges, and in no standing in this Reach:*
>
> *That the four field books of this village, together with the Reach's own copy of the schedule of the works of the Reach and the Guild's volume of the Sallow Reach maintenance, be entered as **the record of the Sallow Reach**, the first fifty pages of the second book excepted and the reason for the exception entered with them; and that there be no copy of them made in this village; and that the record so entered may be read in any office and may not be amended by any office without the assembly of the villages being present.*
>
> *That the Sallow Reach be entered as supplying itself and no other district, and not supply district 14, North Sallow, and not any district, and that no return be received in respect of the works at Sallow Head from any district whatsoever until an assembly of the villages has said otherwise in writing on a day it has named.*
>
> *Entered this thirtieth day of this month. Witnessed by nine. — P. Ammid.*

"Read the last line of the first paragraph again," said Tarin Rusk.

"*And that there be no copy of them made in this village*," said Perdie Ammid. "I have put that in and you did not ask me to and you are not going to be able to argue me out of it in November. It is a record and a record that is copied is a thing with a copy of it in it and in about eleven years the copy is the one anybody checks. You wanted a record and I have given you one and I have taken away the excuse of having a second one, and you may be angry with me about that on a day when you have not got something else to be angry about."

"And it goes to Vellum," said Tarin Rusk.

"It goes to Vellum," said Perdie Ammid. "That is the sentence. That is the one I said on the twenty-fourth in a room and said I would say before you spent a shilling, and I am saying it now, and I am not going to say it again, and I would like it noticed that I have said it four times in six days and each time I have said it I have got about nine minutes further into the room, and that is not a system and it is not going to last.

"A record goes where records go. It goes to Vellum in a cart, on a day, and it will be on a shelf, and the shelf will be in a building with a reading card on it, and the reading card will be held by a man, and the man will have a shelf, and I have had a chest for twenty-six years and I have got four books out of it today for the first time, and I am fifty-four and I have wanted this for a very long time and I have said no to it for a very long time, and I would like the room to understand that I am not saying yes this morning because a man of thirty-three made a road out of my sentence.

"I am saying yes because a woman of seventy-one went west out of a field yesterday and said that it cannot go back, and a fitter of forty-six went home on a cart on Friday and is four miles down a lane with the only reading of a hand in this basin in her head, and a boy of nineteen said *then you did not find them on Thursday, you found them in this room on the twelfth* in front of fourteen people and has not been argued out of it and is right, and I am fifty-four and I have watched this Reach make three documents this month and every one of them has been made out of a thing somebody was going to lose.

"So the books go. And I am not a woman who has changed her mind, and I am going to write one line under that entry in my own hand tonight so that in eleven years nobody can say I came round."

*Written under, in the same hand: I did not come round. I was out of reasons and I had a week. — P.A.*

---

The second thing was made out in the shed at the foot of the embankment at about two in the afternoon, on a form that came out of the same forty that Margit Saye had carried up in a bundle on the eighteenth day of this month, and it is a single side of a sheet about the size of a wage note, and it is headed:

> **OPEN CHANNEL GUILD. REQUISITION FOR A PLATE OR OTHER INSTRUMENT.**
> *To be made out by the person having the care of a work, or by a person of the district supplied, and not by an officer. Four days' notice is not required and none is given.*
> *Particulars to be given by the person making the requisition, and are to be true, and a particular that is not known is not to be given.*
> *An instrument is not fitted until a keeper is named. A keeper is named by the assembly of the district supplied. A district supplied that has no keeper has no supply, and an instrument in a slot with no keeper in it is a piece of iron in a hole.*

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"There it is," said Jory Vant. "It says it on the form. We have a form that says no to itself and we are about to write it out and pay a post for it."

"Yes," said Sef Ordry, "and we are going to write it out and pay a post for it, and I want the reason said out loud in a shed and I have thought about it since about eight o'clock this morning and this is the whole of my reason.

"A request that is not sent is not a request. It is a thing somebody thought of. And in about four years this Reach is going to be in a room with a man who has a form in his hand, and he is going to say *when did anybody ask for a plate at that throat*, and everybody in that room is going to be able to say that nobody ever did, and the asking would have been on a piece of paper in a shed and it would have been no use to anybody.

"And in about four years the day the plate is fitted is going to be the day a keeper is named, and that is not a gate, that is a rule, and the rule is on the form, and the rule has been on the form since before the assize, and it is the only rule in this basin that nobody has ever managed to get round and I have watched four men in nine years try and I have watched one of them nearly do it and he did not want it and it came out of his own hands in the end, which is always the way.

"So it goes up on Tuesday, and the answer is already printed on it, and the answer has no date on it, and a day with no date on it is not a day, and I am going to write that on the counterfoil in my own hand so that in a year somebody can see that I knew."

---

The particulars were written out in the hand of Tarin Rusk, because Sef Ordry would not write them, and the reason he gave was that a particulars column filled in by the man who wants the iron is a particulars column with a man in it.

So a man of thirty-three wrote out, in a shed, on a form, in his own hand, the true particulars of a stone throat four hundred yards from where he was standing.

> *Work: the stone throat on the embankment line at Sallow Head, four feet above the Long Drain, being a cut slot in the face of a stone kerb, two inches wide and an inch and a half deep, being three square inches, and being that from which the figure of eight hundred and fifty gallons an hour on the card is taken.*
> *Trials: twenty feet in thirteen seconds on the twenty-ninth day of the month before, and twenty feet in thirteen and a half seconds on the first day of this month, in the same slot, unaltered. Eight hundred and sixty-five gallons an hour and eight hundred and thirty-three, and the figure agreed is eight hundred and fifty, and the card says in print that it is not a figure anybody should lean on.*
> *Bore asked for: three-eighths, being the bore of every other plate in this company, and not the half-inch that is in the ring at the intake.*
> *Particular known and to be entered: that no plate has ever been put in this slot by anybody, and that a plate is either in water or it is not, and a bigger bore reads a bigger number, and that the figure from a lath and a stop-watch in this slot is not the same quantity as the reading of a plate, and that nobody in this basin has ever put the two side by side, and that the fitter of twenty-four years standing in this company went home on the twenty-sixth day of this month and is four miles down a lane and cannot be asked.*

"It is a good form," said Sef Ordry, reading it back. "It is the most honest instrument requisition I have ever read and it is honest about the fact that nobody in this company will be here to put the plate in when it comes, and it is honest that the lath and the plate are not the same quantity, and it is honest that a third-eighths plate is the right bore and not the half-inch in the ring.

"There is one thing in it I do not like and I am going to say so and I am going to sign it anyway, because a man who will not sign a good form over one line of it is a man who will sign a bad one."

"Which line."

"*Being that the fitter of twenty-four years standing in this company went home on the twenty-sixth day of this month and is four miles down a lane and cannot be asked.*" Sef Ordry put his thumb on it. "It is true and it is not a particular of a work. It is a particular about a person, and it goes in a form that will be in a counting-house, and in about four years a clerk is going to read that and go and find a woman of forty-six who is at the end of it and is not the person he is looking for, and that is her door being opened for nothing.

"Take it out."

"No," said Tarin Rusk. "It stays in."

"Then it stays in and it is a thing I have permitted in my own shed and I have permitted it knowingly and you have asked me for nothing and I am not going to be thanked for permitting it."

---

The requisition went up the mountain service road on the Tuesday evening on the cart, with Jory Vant, and the counterfoil stayed on a nail in the shed beside the card, and the counterfoil is the top sheet of a pad and it is a copy and by the company's own rule a counterfoil is nothing, and it is the only copy there is.

And the particulars were in the hand of a man of thirty-three, and the signature at the foot of the counterfoil was S. Ordry, sluicewright, because the person having the care of that work is him, and has been for nine years, and it is the only thing in this basin he has ever been able to put on a piece of paper without anybody objecting.

And the particulars were in a shed, and not in a book, and the request was in a book in a village and not in a shed, and neither of them was a record of a route.

Nobody wrote that down on the day. It was put down on the Wednesday morning of the thirty-first day of this month by a woman of fifty-four, in a book, on a page of its own, in a single sentence, and it is the only sentence in the whole of this month that was not read out in a room to anybody at all.

> *On the thirtieth day of this month the name of T. Rusk was entered in the second book of this village in my hand, on a page of its own, being a request, and a request is not a record of a route. And on the same day his hand was on a particulars column of a Guild requisition for a plate, in a shed, and a particulars column is not a record of a route. There are two books and there are two hands and there are two places, a village and a shed, and he is in both of them, and neither of them is a record of a route, and both of them are his.
> *— P. Ammid, the thirty-first day of this month.*
