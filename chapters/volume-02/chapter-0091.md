# Chapter 0091 — Eleven Days

The slip of paper was four lines long and it was in the second book on a page of its own, and it was dated the eleventh, and Perdie Ammid read it out standing up in the front room of the shop at about ten past nine on the Wednesday morning of the fourteenth, with about nine people in the room and Bess Ilk behind the counter, and she read it out because a thing in a book that is not read out is a thing in a book.

> *A window is to be named on a day it is named.*
> *The window is four hours and six minutes, at eight hundred and fifty gallons an hour, being three thousand four hundred and eighty-five gallons.*
> *The window is to be held on the West Flume at Sallow Head on the twenty-second day of this month, from eleven o'clock at night.*
> *Entered at the close of the Sunday evening of the eleventh, at about seven, by P. Ammid, bookkeeper, of Brant. Witnessed by nine. No village is asked and no village is a party to this line.*

Nobody in the shop said anything about it for about nine seconds, and one of the nine put a question.

"Then it is in eight days," said the man of thirty-eight with one hand.

"No," said Perdie Ammid.

"It is the fourteenth this morning."

"It is the fourteenth this morning," she said, "and that sheet is dated the eleventh, and the eleventh to the twenty-second is eleven days, and you have counted from this morning because you are standing in this morning, and in about four years there is going to be a man in an office with a bundle and a dispute and he is going to count the way you have just counted, and he is going to be wrong, and there will be two dates in his bundle with a week between them and he will not be able to tell which one moved."

She put her hand flat on the page.

"That is why it is read out. A date in a book that nobody has heard said is a date that somebody can move. Read it again, Mr Venn, and say the number out loud with me, and then Mr Rusk is going to say it a second time, and then I am going to write it in the margin, and then it is in the book twice and nobody in this basin can have it moved without leaving a mark."

They did it. It took about eleven seconds and it was done by three people and it is in the margin of the eleventh in Perdie Ammid's hand, with the date of the fourteenth under it.

---

Then Tarin Rusk put the two figures on the table, which is what he was fetched for.

"I am going to say a number and then I am going to say another number and I am not going to do anything clever with them, and the reason is that a board is going up in Cresset on Monday with a date on it and a figure on it and a hundred people are going to read it, and if a hundred people are going to read it they are going to be told both.

"**The window is three thousand four hundred and eighty-five gallons.** That is eight hundred and fifty an hour, and eight hundred and fifty is the figure this Reach agreed in the second week of last month and agreed to print, and it is a lath and a stop-watch in a slot two inches wide and an inch and a half deep.

"And the launder's own figure, from a section of six inches by about three-quarters of an inch, is **eight hundred and forty-four**, and it was derived twice in this room on the eighth of this month and it is in a book. Four hours and six minutes at that figure is **three thousand four hundred and sixty**. That is the whole of the difference and it is twenty-five gallons.

"Twenty-five gallons. And I am going to say the reason and then I am going to leave it alone. A lath in a slot and a section of launder are not the same quantity, and nobody in this basin had put the two side by side until the eighth of this month. **A Reach that agrees a figure and then lets a fitter substitute her own has two figures for one water, and that is where this whole business started.** So the number on the board is three thousand four hundred and eighty-five, and the other number goes on the board underneath it, and the reason they differ goes on the board underneath that, in the same three lines, because a board that carries one figure and hides the other is a notice with a man in it."

"And what does three thousand four hundred and eighty-five do," said Nesta Sarg, from the wall, "against the figures we have got in this room."

"Against this Reach's own season it is about one part in fifteen hundred. Against one household's day at a fifth it is about three quarters. Against one household's whole share for the whole season, which is eighteen thousand nine hundred, it is about a fifth. Against one day of what leaves this Reach by the Sallow Dyke it is about a fifth. And across nine hundred and forty properties in a city sixty-one miles east it is **three and three-quarter gallons a property**, which is about a sixteenth of one cart, apiece, once."

She had all of them and she had them before he was half way, and she had been in this room for a fortnight.

"Write them on the board," she said. "All five. Not one. A board with four figures on it is a man who has picked the four that suit him, and this city has had a man who picked the four that suited him and it has been in four buildings since the middle of the month before this one."

Nobody thanked her and she said so before anybody asked, and said it had been said twelve times in a month now, which was one more than it had been on Saturday.

---

Then Sef Ordry put the bar down on the table, which he does, and said the thing that had to be said before anything else in a room could be a room.

"Two things this week is not about, and I am saying them now, in a room, once, and I do not want to hear either of them again before the twenty-second.

"**One. The gate at Sallow Head is shut and chained and it is staying shut.** It has read dry for six years with the right iron in it, and the head on the slip of 1902 is a figure no person in this basin has stood in front of, and the master has written to the man at Ostre four times and the answer is silence, and there is no version of this week in which that gate is opened by anybody in this valley. Not by us, not by him, not on the twenty-second. If a man in a room in this week asks me what I think is going to happen at Sallow Head, the answer is nothing, and it is nothing because the only thing that could make it anything is a plate, and the plate is held, and the plate is held under a printed line on the front of a form that has said it since before the assize.

"**Two. There is no instrument in that throat and there is not going to be one.** I do not want it. I said so in a shed in front of five men on the seventh and I have not changed my mind in a fortnight and the reason has not changed, and it is that the day a plate is fitted the lath stops, and the two trials on that card stop being trials and become a history, and in four years a city is going to be reading a plate and not a man, and neither of them will ever again have to stand in a lane with a boy of fifteen and time something they know is wrong.

"So. Neither of those. What is left is a six-inch stone launder that has had nothing in it for six years, and a chain, and a padlock, and a padlock's reason, and a paper I am going to have to ask nine men to sign."

He stopped there and let it sit, and then he said the rest of it.

"And I want the asking in writing, with a date on it, and I want it asked today, and I want the meeting to be on a day it names and not a day we pick for it, and I want it said in the paper that we are not asking for a window. **We are asking what a thing is.** Item one of the schedule of 1902 says the long drain is the sole discharge of this Reach and that no other water whatsoever shall be admitted to it, and it stopped me on the fifteenth of last month with a chain and my own reason and no angry woman anywhere, and it was right.

"And the flume's outfall is on that drain, two miles below the plank, and a thousand four hundred and seventeen gallons went down it on the fifteenth into a drain with nineteen households at the bottom of it, and three thousand four hundred and eighty-five is the fifteenth again two and a half times over. So the flume cannot be opened in this Reach without a thing in writing from the assembly of the villages, and that thing has to say one of two things. It can amend item one. Or it can say what else the flume is for.

"**And I want it to say what else the flume is for.** I have thought about it for six days and amending item one is the wrong instrument and I will tell the room why. Item one is in a book printed in this village in 1902 and it is the strongest document any of us owns and in four years it is going to be the thing somebody quotes. If we amend it, then in four years it reads as it was amended, and a man in an office will read the amendment and not the clause, and the amendment will be a date in a drought and a figure of three thousand four hundred and eighty-five, and that is a hole cut in the strongest thing we have got, and I do not want a hole in it in a drought.

"**A declaration of what a thing is does not touch the clause.** A man who wrote a flume in 1902 and a drain in 1902 and a clause about other water knew exactly which he was writing and why, and if we ask nine men in a room to say in their own words what the West Flume is, and it is in their own words, and it is signed, and it has a date on it, then item one stands as it is printed and there is a second document beside it that says what a flume is, and a clerk in four years has two things to read and not one thing with a hole in it."

Nobody argued with him, and about four people in that room understood why, and two of those four were the only two who would have argued with anybody.

---

And then Perdie Ammid said the thing about the book, and she said it standing up, and she said it before anybody asked her, because she has been getting in front of things all month and it is the only method she has.

"Before we do anything else. **The four field books, the third book and the Guild's volume go to Vellum on Friday morning.** Not on the cart that took the card. On their own. The cart is booked for nine in the morning at the kerb outside this shop and there is nothing else in it and there is a board across it and it is going four days.

"That is the record of the Sallow Reach. That is twenty-six years of a chest in a room over a shop. And the cart is on Friday morning, and from the moment it goes there is no book in this shop and there is not going to be one, and I have been in this book every evening since the tenth of this month and I am going to be in it on Wednesday and on Thursday and then I am not going to be in it at all, and I have not said that out loud in this room before and I would like it written at the top of the page.

"And I am not going to be thanked for it, and I want the room to hear me say that first, because I know exactly what this week is going to sound like in about eleven months. There is going to be a man in a room somewhere who says the bookkeeper sent her books to a city. **I did not send my books to a city. I entered in a book in this village, on the day it named, in front of nine people, that the record is the Reach's and is not any district's, and I was right, and being right cost this valley the only thing it had, and the cost is mine and I entered it in the column headed *what it cost* and I have not taken it back.**"

"You could have had a copy," said Tarin Rusk. "I asked for it on the thirtieth of the month before this one in this room and I was not refused, and the answer you gave me was correct and I did not argue with it, and I am not arguing with it now. A copy of a record is a thing that gets checked instead of the record."

"A copy of a record is a thing that gets checked instead of the record," said Perdie Ammid, "and that is the best sentence anybody has said in this shop in a month, and I wrote it, and I am not going to pretend I have not been saying it since about eleven o'clock this morning."

---

The sheet went out at about two in the afternoon. It was one side, in her hand, and it asked the assembly of the villages to meet at the room over this shop on a day it was to name, and to consider one question, and the question was printed in full and it was the second question and not the first, and the first question was on the sheet too and it was this.

> *That the assembly of the villages do me the courtesy of naming a day, and that the day be not earlier than the sixth day from the day this sheet is written, and that no man be asked to come twice.*

Under it, printed, in her hand, in nine spaces and nothing else in any of them:

> *What is the West Flume?*

There was a line under that, and the line was the only place in the whole of it where she had written anything that was hers and not the Reach's.

> *Item one of the schedule of the works of the Reach, printed at this village in 1902, forbids any other water whatsoever to the long drain, and is not proposed to be amended, and is not asked to be amended.*

She signed it at the foot as bookkeeper of a village that is a party to a paper, put the date on it, and did not sign any name of any man who had not put his own name to something in her hand before, and a boy of fifteen carried it down the lane at about half past two with the boy of nineteen, and the sheet went to nine villages and the lane is four miles and the toll bar is on it.

And on the way out the boy of fifteen asked him a question that has been asked of him four times this month and had never once been about water.

"Are you going to be standing at the gate?"

"I am going to be standing at one end of it," said Tarin Rusk, "and there is going to be a man at the other end who is not me and whose name I am not going to say in this shop, because if I say his name in this shop then in four years there will be a story about how a man from a city built a road out of a name, and I have been told what that costs and I have paid it once."

The boy thought about that for about half a mile.

"Then who is standing at the other end," he said.

"The other end of a route," said Tarin Rusk, "is a person. That is the whole of it. I have known that since a man of sixty-one said it in a hut four miles up the lane and I have been carrying it about and I have not used it until now, and I am not going to use it in a shop with nine people in it. You will both of you know on Saturday."

It was on the fourteenth and it was a Wednesday and the window was on the twenty-second, and it was eight days, and it was eleven, and Perdie Ammid had written both of those numbers in the margin of the eleventh with the date of the fourteenth under them, and the second book went to Vellum on the Friday in a cart with nothing else in it, and the flume sluice was shut and chained, and the third rod on a card at a culvert was empty, and a printer of sixty-eight at Culm was at that moment cutting a forme for a card with a hole in it, and the gate at Sallow Head was shut and was staying shut, and nine men in nine villages had a sheet in their hands and had not read it yet.
