# Chapter 0088 — Somebody At Every End

On the Saturday morning of the tenth day of next month the room over the shop at Brant had about twenty-six people in it, and Sef Ordry had asked for it, which he had never done, and said when he asked that a shed is where he says things to one man and a yard is where he says things to everybody, and a room of twenty-six is the only size there is for a thing that is about a trade and not about anybody's feelings.

He said the bill first, and it took him about four minutes, and about eleven people in that room had heard a version of it in a shed or a lane or a room in the last three weeks and had not heard all of it in one place in front of other people.

"On the fifteenth day of the month before I stood in that shed at a quarter to eight in the morning with a disc of red wax in my open hand and I did not touch anything for about two hours and a half and a quarter, so that in about four years there would be one man in this basin who could stand in front of a bench and say he did not break a Guild seal. I bought that. It cost me two hours of holding my hand out and the skin off both forearms.

"And on the Saturday of the twenty-seventh day of this month, at a quarter past eleven in the morning, a man of thirty-three put *Sef Ordry, sluicewright, of Bexhill, of no district* into a printed line on a form, without asking me, as a man who has the water, so that a thirsty man can come to my door.

"Both of those are in a row now, and there is nothing in this basin that will ever separate them, and in about four years a clerk in a counting-house is going to lay out four documents about a man in an afternoon and put them in a row, and he will be wrong, and it will not matter, because there is no form in this basin in which a man may object to a sentence in a margin.

"That is the bill. It is payable in about four years and not today. I have not asked anybody to pay anything, and I have come here because a professional cost that is said in a shed is a private thing, and I have had my say about it twice in private and been told twice in private to stop, and this morning I am saying it once in a room with twenty-six people in it, and then it is on the page and it will not need saying again by either of us."

---

Then he said the other half, and it is the only reason the room was called.

"Three days ago I refused a thing in a shed, on the Wednesday, and I said a reason for it, and Mr Marrow wrote it down in his own hand and it is in a book at this shop now. And the day before yesterday a man of thirty-three said out loud in a room that he had been building roads out of sentences, and he said it in front of twenty-six people and he did not know that I was in the room, and he has not been told yet and I am not going to be the man who tells him.

"So I am going to say the reason again, once, in a room, and I said on the afternoon of the twenty-fifth of this month, in a yard, to one man, that I would say it twice and never a third time, and I have said it twice, and I am going to say it now for the third time, and I want the room to understand that this is a deliberate breach of a thing I said out loud, and that I am breaching it because of what I am going to say after it.

"**A man who is going to do a thing on a Saturday tells the men who have to be there on the Saturday.** Not asks. Tells. And he tells them before he does it, and he tells them while they can still say no, and a man who has told a man and been told no has to do a different thing, and a man who has done a thing and then told a man has done it alone and dressed it afterwards.

"And I told that on the Friday to one man, in a yard, on purpose, knowing it was a habit and not a rule, and I said I would not say it twice because the second time it is a rule. And a man asked me to say it on the Saturday morning and I said no, and I was right to say no, and **the reason I am right is that the habit was in the room at eight o'clock and it was not in the room at a quarter past eleven, and a man who has got a sheet of paper and eight days and a habit in his head can carry all three, and he carried three, and one of the three was mine.**

"That is a bill and it is mine and not his, and I am not going to let anybody in this room put it in his account, and I said so at the time and I am saying it now and there is no third time."

He put the bar down on the table, which he does.

"Now the third time, and the reason for it, and it is about a field.

"**If I hold a gate for four hours and six minutes on a named Saturday, then that Saturday is mine and it is not a field's, and this Reach's field-work is on the lower field, and the lower field is nine hundred acres of beans and apple that are being got in by hand by about four people and a boy of fifteen, and a man of fifty-eight has eleven acres of it and there is nobody to lend him anybody.**

"So the reason costs somebody a field. Not a wage. Not a day out of a column in a book. A field. And I have got it by saying it out loud in this room, and if I had gone on not saying it, then on the eleventh a man of thirty-three would have named a Saturday and four men would have found out about it the way they found out about the form, and one of those four is a man of thirty-eight who has lost his winter work and a journeyman's paper over a hand, and one of them is nineteen, and one of them is me.

"And I am going to hold the gate. And I am not going to be in that field. And I have said so in a room, and Perdie will write it in a column with the date on it, because it is a day and it is somebody's and I am not going to have it be a thing that is said once in a room and then remembered wrong by whoever is looking for a day in about four years."

---

Then the man of thirty-three was asked three questions and he answered all three and he did not say one word in his own favour, and about nine people in that room noticed that he did not, and one of them has said since that she counted it.

The first question was from Sef Ordry and it was whether he had known on the Wednesday morning of the twenty-seventh that the fourth line could not be completed.

"And the copies," said the boy of nineteen from the wall. "Has anybody got a copy of the books in a village that is not this village."

"Nobody," said Tarin Rusk. "I asked for one on the thirtieth of the month before in this room and I was not refused, and I have not asked again, and the reason is that a copy of a record is a thing that gets checked instead of the record."

The third was from a man of thirty-eight with one hand, and it was the shortest question anybody asked that day, and it was: "What was it worth."

"Ten hours of a Saturday," said Tarin Rusk. "A day and an hour. A count that was not taken on the last Saturday in a month because four men were in a room in a village because of a sheet of paper in my coat. And a boy of fifteen in a barrow for five and a half hours of it who was not told what he was carrying. And that is the whole of what it was worth and it is in a column in the second book on a page of its own and it is to be added to and not taken back, and Wick Venn put it there and it is his column and I have not written in it."

Nothing was argued about at all, and the room did what rooms do.

---

And then the woman of fifty-four asked him the fourth question, and said that she had asked it at the end of a month in the month before with about four people in a room and had got a bad answer, and that she was not going to get a better one today and was going to ask it anyway.

"What are you going to do differently, and say it in one sentence, and I am going to write it in the book as it comes out of your mouth and I am not going to let you improve it on Friday."

Tarin Rusk looked at about twenty-six people for a while.

"I am going to stop making parties," he said. "I am not going to make a party out of a sentence again. Wyle signed a paper on the second of next month about an act on a day it named and that is a village's own act and it is in a book in that village and I did not send for it and I am not going to build on it. The Guild's requisition stands and it is signed by a man who is the party having the care of that work and that is right. And there is no return, and there is no fifth line, and there is no name at any door, and the four books go to Vellum on Friday as the record of a reach that supplies itself and nothing else and no district, and the district in the third book is struck and stays struck, and I am not going to write a sentence that a counting-house can carry for a hundred and thirty years on behalf of anybody who has not put their own name to it in their own hand."

He stopped.

"That is two sentences and I was asked for one and I am not sorry."

---

What it cost was done in about nine minutes and it was not argued about and Perdie Ammid entered all of it in the second book in one block with the date on it, and it is the ninth entry of a kind in that book in twenty-six years and she said so when she read it out.

"That is a refusal, and it is the second refusal anybody in this Reach has entered in this book in a fortnight, and the first was mine on Monday, and I want the room to hear what this one means in money, because the room has been pricing things all week.

"It means the count card goes to a printer on Friday with a third rod empty, and a fourth rod in a boy's hand that says a drain and not a supply, and a strip of paper that names a window that has not been had, and it comes back printed on the twenty-first with the hole in it, and nobody will be able to do anything about the third one. It means no district in this city is going to get a return from this work, because there is no return, and a demand of nine hundred and forty properties for a water that is not offered cannot be refused and cannot be withdrawn, and it will sit in a return in four buildings until somebody in four buildings takes it out.

"And it means **Wyle is a party to a paper about an act of the fifteenth, alone, with eight refusals tied in a bundle, and a village that signed on the day it named is the only party in this basin that cannot be moved, and it is a party to an act and not to a route.** That is what stopping has bought. It has not bought a drop of water."

And then Nesta Sarg said the thing that has been in a lane for a month and had never been said in a room, and she said it to Tarin Rusk and not to the room, and it is in the book because he wrote it down himself and gave it to her and she initialled it.

"**A route is a road with somebody at every end of it who has to be answerable for it, and the looking and the asking are the two things that are not a mechanic.** A man of sixty-one said that in a hut at a toll bar to about four people in forty years, and a man of thirty-three heard it on the sixteenth of this month and wrote it down and has been carrying it about, and I have read the entry, and I am telling him that the sentence is a tradesman's and he is a mechanic, and the tradesman is right, and the mechanic is not going to get anywhere with it, and the whole of his trouble for six weeks is that he has been a mechanic about a thing that has to be a road.

"Put somebody at both ends or do not put a road down. That is all of it. And you have nobody at this end and nobody at the other, and I have watched you build four of them out of sentences in a month, and every one of them was true, and a true sentence with nobody at the end of it goes on a card and nobody comes to the door.

"So stop. And I am not thanked for it, and I have said that nine times in a month now and I say it before anybody asks, and a man of thirty-three is the only one of you who has never once said it to me, and that is not a compliment, it is a fault, and I do not know yet which."

---

And then, at about ten past one in the afternoon, Sef Ordry did the thing he said on the Wednesday he would do, and it was not what anybody expected and it was on the card already and has been on the card since the first of next month, and nobody had connected it to a window.

"Right," he said. "Before anybody goes. There is one thing about a window that nobody in this room has said and that I have been not saying since the fifteenth of the month before, and I am saying it now because a man has just said he is going to stop making parties, and that means somebody is going to have to do a thing on paper that is not a party.

"**Item one of the schedule of the works of this Reach, printed in this village in 1902, forbids any other water whatsoever to the long drain, whether from a flume, a waste pipe, a culvert, a spill, a torn joint, or a wet stone.** That is what stopped the window on the fifteenth, and that is what a woman of fifty-four stood in a drain at about ten to ten in the morning with a book in her hand for, and she was right, and it has been right since before the assize, and the flume's outfall is on the long drain two miles below the plank, and a thousand four hundred and seventeen gallons went down it on the fifteenth into a drain with a village at the bottom of it.

"So if anybody opens that flume sluice for four hours and six minutes they have not moved the problem. **They have done the fifteenth again, two and a half times over, and there are nineteen households at the bottom of that drain, and item one will stop it, and it stopped me at an hour and forty minutes with a chain and a sluicewright's own reason and no angry woman anywhere.**

"And a window is not lawful in this Reach without a thing in writing from the assembly of the villages, on a day it has named, either amending item one or saying what else the flume is for, and that assembly has not met since the spring before, and there are nine men in it and about four of them would be offended, and I said that on a Sunday in a lane to a boy of fifteen and I am saying it again in a room because the boy is in the room and should hear it twice.

"That is not a thing anybody can do in a week. It has to be asked for, in writing, with a date on it, by somebody, and it is going to sit in that chest and wait, and everybody in this basin is going to spend the next fortnight not talking about it, because the alternative is talking about it. And I have said that sentence in three rooms in a month and I am not going to say it in a fourth."

Nobody in the room said anything about the flume, and about eleven people in that room had known about item one since the fifteenth of the month before and had not put the two facts together, and it was a man of thirty-one with a bar who put them together out loud in about nine words and then sat down again, and it is in the book.

On the Monday morning the eleventh day of next month a line went into the column headed *days lost, and by whom, and to what*, in Perdie Ammid's hand, and it is the fourth line and it is the only one that has ever been a man giving away a day on purpose.

> *The tenth day of the month following. One day, given by S. Ordry, sluicewright, of Bexhill, of no district, from the getting in of eleven acres of beans in the lower field, on account of a window of four hours and six minutes on a day not yet named, which was told to twenty-six people in a room on the Saturday before it was done and not after.*
