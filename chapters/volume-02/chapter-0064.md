# Chapter 0064 — A Hand Nobody In The Reach Can Read

The chaise left Brant at six in the morning on the eighth day of this month with a boot on the near side, and the woman of twenty-six rode in it and would have to go back to Culm for the other four pounds, and Yara Sen did not offer to make it right and the woman of twenty-six did not ask.

She said one thing at the door, to Perdie Ammid, and it was about the notice in the shop.

"That is a nail with a test on it and it is going to be read four hundred times and it is going to be argued with four hundred times and one person in a hundred is going to do it properly and boil something in a jar. That is the right number. Do not let anybody improve on it."

And then she said one thing to Tarin Rusk, at the gate, in the cold, and it was about six hundred and sixty gallons an hour, which was the difference between eight hundred and fifty and the figure at the plank, and which was on a card at a culvert.

"Take it out of the tank card when the tank card goes to Vellum in October," she said. "Put a line under the level that says that the figure below the level has not been traced and is not known to be leaving the works by the long drain, and that the difference is about six hundred and sixty gallons an hour, and put a number on that line. Do not put the sentence. Put the number. A number in a card at a counting-house for thirty years is worth more than a sentence in anybody's head, and the sentence will be gone the day after somebody dies."

And then she got in the chaise and it went down the lane and out of the Reach, and the last thing anybody in that yard saw of it was the off wheel.

---

Perdie Ammid was asked about it three times on the Monday, in three different rooms, by three different people, and she said "nothing" three times, and the reason she said it three times is that it was true and not because she was hiding it.

She had found it on the Monday morning at about seven, before the chaise came, alone, in nine minutes, and had put the ribbon back and the lid down and had gone out to the yard and had done the ordinary Monday, and had not mentioned it to anybody, and the reason was not fear.

"Write down the day you found it," said Tarin Rusk, "because when we come to put it in front of anybody, and we will have to, the first question anybody asks is when did she know, and the answer has to be the ninth and not the eighth, and the eighth is the day she found it and the ninth is the day she said it out loud."

"I know that," said Perdie Ammid, "and that is not why."

"Then say the why, because you have not slept and it is Monday afternoon and I would rather be told now than work it out by Thursday."

Perdie Ammid put her hand flat on the second book, which was the oldest of the four.

"Two reasons, and the second one is the true one and the first one is the one I would have said if you had not asked.

"First. I cannot read the hand. It is not a hand that any person in this Reach could read, and that is not a figure of speech, I have looked at it with Bess Ilk and with Nesta Sarg and with the printer of Culm, and there is nobody in this basin within forty miles of that village who could tell you whose hand it is, and I am a woman of fifty-four who has been taught to write in a fair hand by a woman of fifty-four and I can tell you the nib cut on a thing I have seen before and I cannot tell you the nib cut on a thing I have not.

"And that matters more than it sounds. If a person cannot read a hand then that person cannot be examined on the hand. Somebody in a room can stand up and say *the woman who keeps the books says she cannot read it*, and that is not an accusation, it is a fact, and the fact will be in the room and the amendment will be in the room, and the amendment will lose. Not because anybody proved anything against it. Because the one person who can be questioned about it has said out loud that she cannot read it.

"Second. And then say it. That is my own doing and I have had since Monday morning to find a better way of saying it and there is not one.

"I have spent twenty-six years refusing to let these four books be a record. I said out loud in that room on the twenty-seventh that a book of customs with a refusal in it is a record, and a record is a thing somebody else keeps, and I did it anyway because a man said no in front of forty-one people and there was nothing else I could do with it.

"And on Monday morning I found a thing in a chest in this village that has no force whatsoever except as a record, and if I have kept these books out of a counting-house for twenty-six years then there is an office in Vellum that has never been told any of it. So I have found, by doing the correct thing on the twenty-seventh, an amendment that only hurts if somebody is holding the book it is in, and the only person holding that book is me, in a chest, in a village, in my own name.

"And I have known since Monday morning that I would not have had to choose this week if the twenty-seventh had gone the other way, and I said out loud in that room that I had decided it eleven days in advance, and I had, and I have not got a better reason and I am not going to look for one."

---

She said the whole of it on the Tuesday morning in the room over the shop, with the doors open and about thirty people in it, which she had not asked for and had not prevented, because Bess Ilk opens the doors and Bess Ilk does that.

The chest was on the table.

It is a chest of oak about three feet long with a hasp and a lock that has not been locked since about 1811, and the lock has been kept oiled, and the chest is in the front room of a house in Brant and it is twenty feet from where a man of thirty-three has slept for sixteen nights.

Perdie Ammid took out the four books and put them in a row, and then she took out a fifth thing and put it on the end of the row, and the fifth thing was a bound book about the size of a quarto Bible with a ribbon and a printed spine that had come apart.

"Then I am going to name them," she said, "because there are four documents in this basin about the water of this Reach and not one of them knows that the other three exist, and I have known that for about twenty-six years and I have never said it out loud in a room with thirty people in it, and I am fifty-four and I have run out of year."

She put her hand on each one as she said it.

"The first is the Guild's. The Sallow Reach maintenance volume, the counting-house at Vellum, shelf C, volume forty-four, a hundred and eighty-one years old. It is in a drawer, the reading card has been held by three men in six years, and the man who holds it now has told a stranger he cannot put a number on this reach at all, which is the most honest sentence anybody in this basin has said this month, and it is the only one of these four that anybody outside this Reach has ever had in their hands.

"The second is mine. Four field books of the water of the Sallow Reach, and the second of them is the oldest and the first fifty pages of it are not in my hand, and the fourth of them has a refusal in it in a man's handwriting on the page before the notice, dated the twenty-seventh day of the month before, and I have been the keeper of the second book for thirty-one years and of all four for as long as I can remember.

"The third is this."

She put her hand on the bound book.

"The Reach's own copy of the schedule of the works of the Reach. The same schedule the printed slip off the pin in this room was cut out of, and it was made at the bookshop in this village in 1902, and this village has held a copy of it for about a hundred and thirty years, and a schedule is a thing a person keeps in a house and not in a shed, and I have had it in that chest for twenty-six years because I did not understand what I was keeping.

"And the fourth is a card the size of a man's hand on a nail in a shed door at the foot of an embankment, with six entries and no seventh, and a second line under the seventh written ten days ago by a sluicewright because I asked him to. I have never seen it and have not asked to, and I know what it says, because every village in this Reach has had one of them in a shed door since before anybody living.

"Four documents. Three of them in this village. One of them in a town forty miles off. And the fourth of them in a shed."

---

She opened the third book on the table and turned it round and put her finger on a column and nobody in that room could see what was under her finger except the four people at the table.

The column was headed *keeper of the reach*, and it ran down the page in a column of names in a hundred and thirty years of hands, and the last name in it before the amendment was in a hand that four people in that room could read, because it had been written in this village by a man who was paid to write it.

It was struck out.

A single line, in ink, right through the name, from the left margin to past the last letter, and the ink was not the ink of the name and the strike was six years old and had gone brown at the edges in the way that ink goes in a book that is shut in a chest.

And above the struck name, in the space where the heading was, in a small upright hand with no loops in it at all:

> *Supply district 14, North Sallow.*

"Read it out," said Tarin Rusk.

"Supply district fourteen, North Sallow," said Perdie Ammid.

"Again, and slowly, and say it as a thing that is written and not as a thing that is meant."

"Supply district fourteen, North Sallow," she said, "in a hand that is not the hand of the man whose name is under it, and it is six years old, and neither of the two of us can read it."

Margit Saye had not been in the room at ten past nine. She came in at about half past, on her way down from the works, and she was at the table by about a minute to ten, and she did not ask what was on the table because there were thirty people looking at it and the answer was going to come to her.

She looked at the struck name for about four seconds and then at the writing above it for about two seconds and she did not put her hand on it, which Tarin Rusk noticed and understood.

"That is a counting-house index hand," she said. "I will not say whose. I could give you two offices that write in it and I am not going to, and I am not going to say a man's name, and I want that put in the book in that order, because the moment a fitter says a man's name in a room then every other thing that woman says in rooms for the next four years is a thing that woman is thought to have an interest in.

"I have read about nine thousand hands in this basin. That is a Guild counting-house hand of about six years ago, and the Guild does not keep a sample of its clerks' hands because a clerk's hand is not evidence of anything and that is correct, and it is also the only reason there is nothing whatever in that line to be examined against. It is a lawful hand in a lawful form and it was written by somebody entitled to write it, and I will not guess, and guessing is the one thing in this trade that I will not do, and I have watched two men guess in my time and one of them guessed right and the man he guessed about never worked again."

"Then it is lawful," said Domen Prouse, from the middle of the room.

"It is lawful," said Margit Saye. "And I would like it noticed that it is lawful, and that lawful is not the word I would have chosen, and that there is not a better word in this basin than lawful for it, and that is what has happened here."

---

It was Tarin Rusk who had to say the thing that had to be said, and he said it badly, and then Margit Saye said it properly and it went in the book in her words.

He said, "The flume sluice was shut six years ago and that name was struck out six years ago and those are two things in one year and I would like somebody in this room to notice that I have not put them in the same sentence and that I am not going to."

Margit Saye said:

"The sluice was shut in the sixth year of the flume. The keeper was struck out in the sixth year of the schedule. Those are two different years' entries written down by two different people in two different places for two different reasons, and they are in the same calendar year, and a calendar year is not a cause.

"I have worked for a master for eleven years who has said *by the book* about nine hundred times and I know exactly what a man does with two facts in one year, and what he does is join them, and then the joined thing is a story, and the story is about a man, and there is no man in it, and in four years there will be a story in this Reach about a keeper who took a name off a schedule and shut a sluice, and it will be said in rooms, and it will be believed, because it is the best story anybody has got, and there will be nothing whatever in this basin that can be put against it.

"So I am going to say the two things separately and I am going to say them in this order. This reach has no keeper. It has not had a keeper since the sixth year and it has a supply district in a city of nine hundred and forty properties, and a district is not a person and cannot be asked anything at all.

"And nobody in this room knows who shut the flume sluice, and nobody in this room is going to find out from that book, and if anybody stands up in a room in the next four years and tells you they found out from a schedule, they are telling you about themselves and not about 1902."

---

What Perdie Ammid did with it on the Tuesday was nothing, and she said in advance that she was going to do nothing with it, and about six people told her that was a mistake and one of them was Tarin Rusk and he was wrong and she told him so.

"I am not taking this to a village meeting this week," she said. "I am going to say why in front of thirty people and I have thought about it since Monday night and here it is.

"There are nine villages in this matter and I know what eight of them will say and I have not asked one of them, because I do not need to. A village asked to be a party to a lawful act against an installation of the Open Channel Guild will say no, and it will say no for the best reason anybody in this room has ever heard, which is that the Guild is the only body in this basin that can put a man into a chamber and clear a silted pipe and set a plate.

"And if I take this amendment into a village room on any Wednesday I will lose it, and I will lose it to a motion, and the motion will be correct, and the amendment will then be a thing that came up once and went down, and there will not be one person left in this Reach who remembers it except me and a fitter from Bexhill who will be gone inside a week.

"So it does not go in a room this week. It goes on the card at the culvert on Sunday, in one line, with the reason underneath, because a card on a post is read by people who did not go to a meeting. And the book stays in the chest. And on the fifteenth, if anybody has anything at all to say to that sluice, they can say it in a place where the Reach's own schedule is open at the right page."

---

The chest was shut at about four o'clock on the Tuesday afternoon and the ribbon was tied and the lock was not put to, because it has not been put to since 1811.

And before the lid came down Perdie Ammid took the stub of a pencil out of her apron and wrote on the flyleaf of the third book, in pencil, in her own hand, in five words and a date:

> *Found the amendment. Said it on the ninth.*

And she said, to the room, with the lid still up:

"That is a flyleaf in a chest in a village, and I have spent a week learning that a thing written on a flyleaf is in exactly the position of an empty seventh line in a shed door. And I have learned one other thing this week and I am going to say it and then I am going to shut the lid.

"A man who says a thing out loud has cost less than a man who writes it in a book. That is not an argument for talking. It is an argument for knowing what you have got and what you have not got, and I have got a book that nobody outside this village can ask for, and I have got a stone on a bank at the third mile that nobody in this Reach has ever been shown, and neither one of those two things is going to help anybody before Monday, and I have not decided whether I am sorry about that or not, and I have not got time to."
