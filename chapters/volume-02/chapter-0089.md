# Chapter 0089 — An Answer In An Index Hand

The counterfoil was still on the nail in the shed door on the Sunday evening of the eleventh day of next month, under the card, four hundred yards below the shop, and by the company's own rule a counterfoil is nothing, and for four days it had been the only copy there was of a request for a piece of iron.

The answer came back on the Sunday evening of the eleventh on the afternoon cart, and it was a single side of a sheet and it was in a counting-house index hand — small upright, no loops, the cross of the t a little to the left and never the same way twice — and it is the same hand that has been in the Reach's own printed schedule of 1902 from the second page to the end.

Perdie Ammid would not have it read out in a room. She read it out herself, standing up, in the shop, at about seven in the evening on the Sunday, to about nine people, and she read it twice, and the second time she read it slower.

**The index desk dated it on the seventh, four days before the cart brought it back down the road, and that is a figure and not a feeling and it is in a book in a counting-house and it is the reason the date on this sheet is worth more than the date the cart stopped.**

> *OPEN CHANNEL GUILD. Kerrow Water. Sallow Head.*
> *Requisition for a plate or other instrument, received at this counting-house on the third day of the month following, from the shed at the foot of the embankment, with particulars in the hand of a person not being the person having the care of the work, and signed by S. Ordry, sluicewright, who is the person having the care of the work, and has been for nine years.*
>
> *1. That the requisition be **held**, and not refused, and be held under the fourth printed line on the face of the form, which is that an instrument is not fitted until a keeper is named.*
> *2. That a keeper is named by the assembly of a district supplied, and that the district supplied upon the flyleaf of the Reach's own copy of the schedule of the works of the Reach is **supply district 14, North Sallow**, and that the said district is not within this company and has no office of this company in it, and that no assembly of the said district has come to this counting-house in the six years of the return.*
> *3. That the works at Sallow Head is not on the schedule of this company, and that the slot named in the particulars is a waste-pipe slot and is not on the schedule, and that an instrument cannot be entered in a work that is not on the schedule, and that the requisition is therefore held under the same line and for the same reason and not for want of the particulars.*
> *4. That the particulars are received and are true so far as they go, and that the bore asked for, being three-eighths, is the bore of this company, and that this company does not dispute any figure on the card at the culvert and is not in a position to give any figure of its own upon the work named, there being no instrument in it, and this company having no person at the work named in six years.*
>
> *Entered at the index desk. — the seventh day of this month.*

---

Nobody in that shop said anything for about eleven seconds.

"Read the fourth one again," said Sef Ordry.

Perdie Ammid read the fourth one again.

"There is no figure in it," she said, when she had finished. "There is a name in it and a date and a *held*. And a company of three hundred men has looked at a card from a valley with a hole in it and has declined to give a figure, **and it has declined in writing, and it has dated it, and it has put it in a book that goes to Vellum**, and I want everybody in this shop to understand that that is the single most useful thing anybody outside this valley has done since the day a boy of fifteen cut a card in a room and nailed it to a post at a culvert, and I am not going to say one word about it until somebody has explained to me why we have spent a month assuming that a company does the other thing."

"They are not refusing us," said Tarin Rusk. "They have told us that the only thing that would stop them is an assembly of a district, in a city, on a day it names, and the district is the one in the third book, the one with a keeper's name struck out of it six years ago, and the one that has a card with a hole in it on a shelf in this city with a padlock printed under the hole."

"Yes," said Perdie Ammid. "And the day they wrote that sentence out, this village entered in the second book, in my hand, on a page of its own, that the Reach supplies itself and not any district, and not district 14, and not any district, and that no return be received in respect of the works from any district whatsoever until an assembly of the villages has said otherwise in writing on a day it has named.

"**My entry and their answer are the same lock, and they have two keys, and neither key is in this valley, and the second key is a meeting in a city that has no acting commissioner and a form with an empty line at the foot of it that has stood empty for six years.** And I have spent thirty-one years keeping a chest in a room over a shop and I have got the sentence in that lock right, and I am going to say the rest of it, because I have been waiting three weeks to say it and there is nobody else in this basin who can.

"So a plate cannot come to that throat. And a keeper cannot be named. And the third rod on that card stays empty, and the reason under it stays a padlock, and in four years there will be a card on a post with a hole in it and a padlock printed under the hole and a fourth rod in a boy's hand, and nobody will be able to do anything about any of it. **And that is not an injustice. That is the best lock in this basin, and I built my half of it myself, on the thirtieth day of the month before, in this room, and I would like it noticed that I did not know I was building it.**"

---

The thing that was in the middle of the sheet was not in the index hand, and it took the shop about four minutes to work out that it was a different hand at all.

It was a direction to the index desk, three lines long, in a hand that Perdie Ammid, Jory Vant and a boy of fifteen all recognised, and it was initialed at the foot with one letter and was not signed.

> *Direction to the index desk. The particulars are received and I will not have the word disputed used about them in this valley, and the company is not going to say in writing what a lath and a stop-watch in a waste-pipe slot is, and the company is not going to say in writing what a three-eighths plate would read at the head given in the slip of 1902, and if anybody in this basin wants either of those figures they are to come up the road and be measured. — I.M.*

Jory Vant read it out twice and then said, in the voice he uses for figures he has had to sit with:

"That is a master of the Guild's hand, and it is not signed, and a man of thirty-four in this room knows that a one-letter initial on a direction to an index desk is not a signature and does not commit a company to anything, and that the whole of that sentence is a man talking and not a company deciding, and **that the index hand is not able to sign for him and has not been asked to.**"

"So he wrote it and could not put it in the answer," said Tarin Rusk. "He got it into the same sheet, underneath, in a different hand, with one letter at the bottom, and the index desk did the rest and dated it the twelfth, and the twelfth is the day after the cart."

"The twelfth is the day it was done," said Perdie Ammid. "It was received on the Sunday and it is dated the Monday, and the company did a morning's work on it, and a morning's work in a counting-house is worth a great deal and is worth nothing whatever, because a date on a paper out of that office is the day a man looked at it and not the day a man thought about it.

"And there is one more thing on that sheet and I am going to say it and then I am going to bed, because it is the only part of that answer that will be worth anything in four years and it is not worth a shilling this morning.

"**The company has been in this business for a hundred and eighty-one years and it has never in that time been asked to say in writing what a plate would read at a head it has not measured.** And on the seventh of this month, at a counting-house desk, it wrote that down, and it wrote it in a book, and it put a date on it, and it is not a secret and it is not a scandal and it is not anybody's villainy. It is a company that has kept a reading card over four hundred works for a hundred and thirty years and has never once had to say, in ink, that a number it printed was not a number anybody could check. And it has said it. And a village four miles from a toll bar has got a copy of it in a book on a page of its own, and in about four years, when somebody in this basin asks how much water comes out of a stone throat in an ungauged slot, the answer will be that a company of three hundred men said so in writing on the seventh day of this month and would not say a figure."

She entered the whole of it in the second book on the Monday morning of the twelfth, in her own hand, with the date on it, and initialled the page, and then she did the thing she has done about four times in a month and would like to be stopped doing.

She wrote under the copy, in the same hand, and it is in the book:

> *The answer is in a hand and not in a man's, and I have read that hand out in this shop twice tonight and I have said that it is a desk, and I have said that a desk is a good thing and a good hand, and I am not going to take any of it back. And I am going to say the one thing I have not said: **the desk did not write that fourth line and could not have written it**, and a company wrote the first three and a man wrote the fourth, and they went up the same road in the same cart in the same envelope, and it is the only time in my life that I have seen a company and a man in one document and one of them was saying something and one of them was doing something, and I have not been able to put the two in a book in a way that anybody could check and I have tried for two days, and the honest entry is that a woman of fifty-four cannot do it, and the reason is that they are two hands and not one, and that I have been able to see the difference between them since the Tuesday of this month in front of a hundred and forty people, and that there is nothing in this basin which can say which of the two the company is.*

The counterfoil went off the nail in the shed door on the Monday afternoon, four hundred yards, in Sef Ordry's good coat, because a counterfoil is not to be carried in a barrow, and it went into the second book at Brant on a page of its own with the answer, and by the company's own rule a counterfoil is nothing, and it was the only copy, and there are now two.

And nothing was opened, and nothing was named, and the flume sluice was shut and chained, and the weep on the west shoulder of the road at the fourth mile was under its rind, and the plate at the throat was a piece of iron in a hole that was not in it, and the chain was not renewed, and there was still no keeper.
