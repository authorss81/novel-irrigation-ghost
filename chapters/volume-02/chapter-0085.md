# Chapter 0085 — She Came For One Room

There were a hundred and forty people in the front room of the shop at Brant on the Tuesday afternoon of the sixth day of next month, with the doors open and the light coming in off the road, and that is about four times the most that has ever been in it, and there is no door on this earth that holds a hundred and forty people and a man reading.

They came because the count was taken on the Saturday and the fourth rod was on a card at the culvert and the ground went like a bench on the twenty-fourth of this month and a hundred and forty people had nothing to do on a Tuesday afternoon in the second week of a month they had lost.

The thing they wanted was a name. Perdie Ammid had said in a room on the eighth of this month that the amendment could be traced to a desk and not to a man, and had written it down, and a fitter of forty-six had said the same thing two days later and put three specimen slips on a table and turned them face down. And a hundred and forty people had gone on thinking about it for twenty-nine days.

---

She came in a carrier's cart from a village four miles down the lane at about half past two in the afternoon, with the specification book in its oilcloth case under her arm, and she was let in at the back and came through the shop past the counter, and Bess Ilk says that the whole room turned round and that a hundred and forty people turned round in a room with a low ceiling is a thing you do not forget.

Margit Saye is forty-six. She put the case down in the corner of the room, against the wall, on the side away from the light, and she did not open it, and she did not sit down, and she said the first thing.

"Before anybody says anything at all I am going to say four things and they are all procedural.

"One. I said on the twenty-sixth of this month that a man of thirty-three was to ask me in a month whether he would still be sorry, and it is eleven days, and I want that said in this room first, because a fitter who is asked in eleven days for something she said would come in a month can say no for as long as she likes, and I am going to say no for a day and a half and then come here anyway, and the reason I am coming anyway is number four.

"Two. I have not come about the hand. There are a hundred and forty people in here who have come about the hand and I noticed and I am not going to be grateful to anybody for it, because I did not come for it and I would be insulted on behalf of the trade.

"Three. I am not going to be thanked at any point today and I will leave the room if I am. I have been thanked in a room about a plate three times in twenty-four years and twice it was by a man who wanted a price and once by a woman who wanted me to go, and a thank-you in a room gets in the way of the next question.

"Four. **I have come because a figure of seven hundred and thirty-five gallons an hour has been put on a card by a boy of nineteen and that card goes into a press in Culm on Friday and will be read in this village for as long as the assize is, and there is one person alive in this country who can say whether a card may carry that figure, and it is me, and it is not my card and it is not my figure, and the answer is yes, and it is not my trade to give it for nothing.**"

Nobody thanked her. About a hundred and forty people had been in that room for two hours and not one of them thanked her, and she had told them not to, and about nine of them had not heard her.

---

Perdie Ammid moved the man of thirty-three to the back of the room at about a quarter past three, and it took her nine seconds, and it was the whole of his part in the afternoon.

"You have been at the front of this room since two o'clock and you have not asked her a question and you have not been asked one, and there are a hundred and forty people in here who think you are waiting for your turn, and you are not, and if you stand at that table for another hour you will be the reason this room does one thing and not the other. Go to the back. Take your rule with you. You will be wanted at the tank and not in here."

"I am not being put at the back of a room by a bookkeeper—"

"You are being put at the back of a room by a bookkeeper," said Perdie Ammid, "and I am the bookkeeper, and it is my room, and you have been in the front of it for a month and I have let you, and today I am not letting you, and if you want to argue with me about it do it on Saturday where there are nine of us."

He went to the back of the room with his rule, and stood against the wall beside the boy of fifteen, and said nothing at all for two hours, and the boy of fifteen has said since that he counted it.

---

She did not read the words. That is the thing about the two hours, and everybody who was in that room has said so in four villages since yesterday.

She had the third book on the table, open at the flyleaf, and she did not read the column. She put her left hand flat on the page above the line so that her hand covered the words, and she read the hand.

"Right," she said. "Here is what I am looking at and it is not a name and I am not going to give you one and I am going to do this once and I am going to do it properly and then I am going to go out into the yard and anybody who has a hole in a thing may ask me about it.

"It is the index desk at the counting-house at Vellum. Small upright, no loops, the cross of the t crossing a little to the left and never the same way twice. That hand has been on that desk since about 1880 and there have been three clerks at it in fifty years and every one of them learned it off the same copybook, and the company issues every fitter in its first year with three slips of it. I have had mine in my case since I was twenty-two.

"That is the hand. It is a good hand. It is a hand that writes four hundred documents a day and signs none of them. **And it is not the hand that is in the rest of that book.**"

She lifted her hand off the page and turned back four leaves and put her finger on a column of figures in the same ink and the same pen.

"**The Reach's own schedule of its own works, printed in this village in 1902, is written in that hand from the second page to the end.** And it was not written by whoever printed it. A compositor does not write a schedule out in a counting-house hand and get it home and put it in a book, and there is a way of knowing that a book has been out of the room it was printed in, and this one has been out of the room, because the third book and the Guild volume in the chest at this shop are the same printing and the same figures and the same four errors and they were both in a counting-house and neither has been back.

"So the hand was in this book before the amendment. The hand was in this book in 1902. And the amendment is six years old and it is in that hand, and the amendment was written by somebody who had the book in front of him and who wrote the other four pages in the same week with the same pen.

"And I am going to say the two things that follow from that and I am going to say both of them, because a fitter who gives you half of it has come for something.

"**First: the hand did not begin six years ago. It began about 1902, and it is the company's own hand, and there is a company rule that a fitter's plates and a company's books are all kept in it, and that rule is right and I have argued for it. It means that a hand in a schedule in a village is not evidence of a man, it is evidence of a company.**

"**Second: the hand in the 1903 entries in the Guild volume, which are the two men, is not that hand. The two men wrote it themselves and it is a bad hand and it is the same bad hand in both halves, because it is the same man, and the volume does not say there were two and nobody in 1903 was asked to check the other half.**"

She shut the book.

"**So a hundred and thirty pages of a hundred-and-thirty-year-old book have been in a counting-house at Vellum at some time, and the company keeps no withdrawn specimen of an index clerk's hand, and a man is never to be taken to a desk on a specimen, and that rule has been right for fifty years and it is the reason this question cannot be answered, and nobody in that company is going to be told that by me in a room, because the rule is doing the work and the rule does not know it.**"

---

The noise started at about half past three and it went on for about half an hour and it is the politics of the whole of this month and it is on the page because Perdie Ammid wrote down the shape of it and not the words of it.

A hundred and forty people want a name. They have wanted one since the eighth of this month. They were given a desk on the eighth, and a desk does not answer to the door, and about a third of that room understood what a woman of fifty-four said on the twenty-fourth and about two-thirds of them wanted a man to be angry at.

What they did was the ordinary thing. They made noise. Somebody at the back said *you could read it again and say it slower*. Somebody said *you have only told us what it looks like*. And somebody said the thing the rest of the room was thinking, kindly, and it was the worst thing said in that room all afternoon.

"You have read that hand in nine counties," the voice said, "and you have never once been wrong about it, and there are three men, and one of them is alive and working, and you could not say which, and I do not think you want to."

"No," said Margit Saye. "I do not want to. And that is the second half of the reason I came and I have not said it yet, so I am going to say it now, and I am going to say it because there are a hundred and forty people in this room and that is the number at which it is cheap, and if I say it in a room of nine next month I will have thought of a better way of not saying it.

"**A name in a book in a village is the only thing in this basin that cannot be got out again.** I have said that three times this month in three rooms and every time I have said it a room has gone quiet, and I would like this room to understand that it is not a warning to anybody in it. It is a fact about a person, and the person is me, and I have been the only person in this country who could put a face to that sentence and I have been right about it every time for twenty-four years and I have got nothing out of it.

"If I name a man in this room this afternoon, then for the rest of my life I am a fitter who named a man in a room. There are three men in a counting-house who will be told about it inside a month, and two of them are not the man, and both of those two are going to spend the rest of their working lives being the man who was named. And in four years there will be a story in this valley about a woman of forty-six who had a hand and used it in public, **and the story will be better than the desk, and the story will be the thing that gets checked, and in about eleven years there will be a room in this basin with a document in it and a fitter's name at the top of it and a man's name in the middle of it, and the fitter will be the one who can be got at.**"

Nobody said anything for a while and then a man of about sixty at the front said, "Then what is the use of you."

"I am going to do the trade's job and not the trade's politics," said Margit Saye, "and I have answered that in the only way I know how, which is by doing it, and it will be duller than what you wanted.

"**Anybody in this room who has a hole in a thing and has never once had anybody come and look at it may ask me about it. I will answer, in the yard, and I will not charge for it, and there are about nine of you, and I have known since Monday which nine, because there are nine people in this valley whose names are on a works and I have read the works and I have not read their names.**"

---

They got about forty questions into that room in a quarter of an hour and nine of them were bores, and a hundred and ten went out of the door in a bad temper, and nobody thanked her, and about eleven people have said since that they wished they had, and she would rather they had not.

A boy of fifteen asked her about the slot in a stone kerb, two inches wide and an inch and a half deep, and she said three square inches, and she said a plate does not go in that and a plate has not ever gone in that and anybody who writes *plate* in a requisition for that slot is writing the wrong word and has been since about 1900, and she said it three times, and the third time she said *and you have written the right word and I do not know why and I have not got round to it.*

A man of thirty-one asked her the plate question — a bigger bore, a smaller number, and what a half-inch plate in a ring does at seven point three one metres — and she said that the answer was in a book four days away in Ostre that belongs to a man of eighty-one, and that she had written to him herself on the Friday the month before last, and that she was not going to answer it in a room and that if he asked her in a yard she would.

---

At about ten past five she was in the yard with the case under her arm, and Sef Ordry came out with a bar over his shoulder and asked her one question, which was the only question anybody asked her that day that had a date in it.

"Are you going to be at the sluice on Saturday."

"Ask me again in April," said Margit Saye.

"April," said Sef Ordry.

"I have said a date and I have said it out loud in a yard with about eleven people in it so that it cannot later sound like a thing I decided not to do," she said. "And I am going to the carrier's cart in a minute and I am taking the specification book home in the case it came in, because it is the company's and it has been in a shed for three days and a stranger stood next to it and nothing came out of it and that is a thing I am going to have to write about to a man at Bexhill, and I am not going to be in a room of a hundred and forty again this year, and if a hundred and thirty people in that room are still standing in that shop tomorrow morning then they have misunderstood me in a way that I am not going to come back and mend."

She got on the cart. The room over the shop at Brant was a hundred and forty people at half past five on the sixth day of next month, and about nine of them had asked about a hole, and a hundred and thirty-one had not, and there is no name in any book in that valley, and the third book is on a table in a shop with the flyleaf open and the column headed *keeper of the reach* and a name struck through in it, and above the struck name, in a hand that was in that book before 1902, six words that name a district in a city sixty-one miles east, and a woman of seventy-one who refused a box in a room of forty-one people on the twenty-seventh day of the month before was not asked for it and did not offer and was not in the room and is seventy-one and has not been thanked once.
