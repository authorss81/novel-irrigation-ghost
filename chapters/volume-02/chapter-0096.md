# Chapter 0096 — The Board

The board went up on the terrace road in Cresset on the Monday morning of the nineteenth at about ten o'clock, and it was a board, and not a notice, and the difference is that a notice is a thing a person puts up about a thing and a board is a thing an office maintains and reads out of a book on a stated day.

It was read out at about half past ten on the Monday morning by the man who had read the amendment out in a room of a hundred and forty people in a village sixty-one miles west, and by a woman of the Municipal Memory Office who had come down from Vellum on the Sunday, and there were about a hundred people on the pavement.

What was on it was the whole of a service window and nothing else. The date, which was the Thursday of this month, the twenty-second. The hour, which was eleven o'clock at night. The duration, four hours and six minutes. The figure, eight hundred and fifty gallons an hour, and three thousand four hundred and eighty-five gallons. And under that, in the same hand and the same size, three thousand four hundred and sixty, and the reason they differ, in one line: *the first is a lath and a stop-watch in an ungauged slot; the second is a section of launder; they are not the same quantity and the Reach works to the figure it agreed and agreed to print.*

And under that, five comparisons, all checkable by anybody who can divide: about one part in fifteen hundred of the Reach's own season of five million four hundred and eighty-one thousand gallons; about three quarters of one household's day at four thousand seven hundred and twenty-five; about a fifth of one household's whole one-fifth share of eighteen thousand nine hundred; about a fifth of one day of the water that leaves that Reach every day by the Sallow Dyke, seventeen thousand six hundred and forty gallons, which cannot be used and cannot be stopped; and three and three-quarter gallons a property, across nine hundred and forty properties, which is about a sixteenth of a good day's cart, apiece, once.

And under all of it, on the same board, four sentences that were not figures.

> *The flume sluice at Sallow Head is shut and chained. It will be opened on the night named above and chained again before daylight.*
> *The North Sallow branch draws off the same tank, and for about a day and a half after that night the cisterns on that branch will fill more slowly. This office cannot say by how much. Any person in this district may come and read the figure on the morning of the twenty-third.*
> *The third rod on the count card at the culvert is empty and the reason printed under it is a padlock. Nothing will be entered in that space.*
> *The gate at Sallow Head is shut and chained and is not being touched. The flume is not the gate and the flume does not feed the gate.*
> *The demand entered upon this works in the return of supply district 14 on the ninth of this month was entered by multiplication and not by measurement, and is not withdrawn by anything on this board.*

The last of those four was put there at the request of a boy of twenty-two who could not read and had had a figure read out to him three times, and nobody in the city argued about it, and it is on the board.

---

Mara Quill read the figures out and the sluicewright read the four sentences, and then there was the part of it that nobody had put on the board and that went on it anyway, in the middle, in a hand that was not either of theirs.

Tarin Rusk had written it on the Friday morning at the shop, on the back of a shutter, and the boy of fifteen had carried it to the toll bar and the carter had carried it sixty-one miles, and it is in four buildings and it is on a board, and it is the shortest thing on it.

> *I cannot tell you what this costs the nine hundred and forty properties in this district. I have a figure of three and three-quarter gallons a property and I have no instrument in this basin that turns that into a household, and there is not one now and there has not been one since the assize, which was done by men counting chimneys. I have tried it for six weeks. I am not going to do it in a street.*
>
> *On the morning of the twenty-third there will be a second figure on this board, and it will be smaller than the one that is on it now, and I will not be in this city when it goes up, and I will not be able to say by how much, and any person in this district may come and read it.*

A man of about sixty at the back of the crowd read that out twice, because he could not read it and had it read to him, and then said: "He is not going to measure it."

"He has told you he is not going to measure it," said Mara Quill, "in a document, in his own hand, with his name on it, on a public board, in a city of a hundred thousand people, six days before it happens. **A man who tells a city what he cannot do is the only kind of man a board is any use for, and it is the first time anybody has put one of these up in this city in a drought.**"

"Then who put it up."

"The Reach did, on the eleventh. Nine villages. And a man from the valley who is four days away on a road that is four days long, and who was asked by three people on the Friday whether he was coming, and who said he could not, and who gave the reason, which is that he is the only man in this basin who can stand on the coping of that tank on Thursday night with a level staff and a watch, and **a route with only a man in it cannot be held.**"

That got into the fourth building's report the same morning, in about eleven words, and the eleven words are on a pavement in a city and about a hundred people heard them and about nine of them wrote them down.

---

The rest of it was a form.

It was a single side, and it was in Mara Quill's hand, and it was the first time a person had put anything on that board in a form, and the reason she had been asked to do it — by a minute clerk of nineteen years who had come out of habit and was not on the review at all — was that this building held a copy of the count card, and a board that quotes a copy is a thing that somebody has to stand behind.

"Three things about my own paper before I read any of it," she said. "I am an officer of a city office and I am not a neutral person and I have not been one since I was twenty-one. I am not here about water and never have been. And I am not neutral about this card, and I have had a hand in a valley for a month and I am not going to pretend to be a person being asked about a stranger's paper."

"I am here about a copy. **A copy of a card that is nailed to a post at a culvert sixty-one miles west where anybody may walk up to it.** That is a strange sentence and it is the only kind of sentence anybody can check. There is a thing in this valley and there is a copy of the thing in this building and they are of one printing, and if they are not of one printing then every figure on this board is a rumour with a coat on."

She read the certificate and it was four lines and it established, in about a hundred words, that the copy in that building and the copy on the post at the culvert were of one printing cut on the third of this month, that the card bore four rods, that the third was empty, that the reason printed under the third was a padlock, and that the fourth was headed in one hand and figured and signed in another.

And then she read the part that made about nine people on the pavement go quiet.

"And I am not certifying the figure," she said. "**Three thousand four hundred and eighty-five is a discharge and not a supply, and it is entered in this city's return as a supply, and I have been asked three times this month to put my name to a form that would make it a supply, and I am not going to.**

"Item one of the schedule of the Reach's own works says the long drain is the sole discharge of that Reach. The number on this board is the volume of one night's discharge into a channel. The return of supply district 14 calls that volume a *demand upon the works named*, and a demand is what a person asks a company for and gets, and there are nine hundred and forty properties in it, and a district is a party and a party is a party and not a person, and there is nobody in that district with standing to refuse it.

"So there is a number in four buildings that says a city is owed three thousand four hundred and eighty-five gallons, and the number came off a card with a hole in it, and I have read the return and I have read the card and **the return is not a copy of the card and the card is not a copy of the return, and one of them is a discharge and the other is a demand, and nobody has ever put the two side by side, and I am the first person in this basin who is going to say so out loud.**

"And I am not going to say it quietly. **Certify that as a supply and the Reach has offered a city three and three-quarter gallons a property, once, and there is a paragraph in four buildings that says it did, dated and signed, with the man who is doing it named in it.** It is the easiest thing I have been asked to do in eleven years of service and it took me four minutes to write. I am not going to do it and I am not going to be thanked, and I would like somebody to notice that the woman who will not falsify a village's account is not brave: she is the person in the room who is paid to be difficult, and the village did not pay her and I have never been its officer."

---

Then there was the row number, and it was the last thing on the Monday, and it cost her.

"Two forms in this city cannot be completed without a district reference, and one of them is a certificate of copy and one of them is a return," she said, "and the certificate in your hand, Mr Fenn, has a line headed *place* and a line headed *district*, and the place is a valley sixty-one miles west, and the district is the one in the flyleaf of a schedule that is in a cart four days up a road, and that district has a number and it is not the number on the card.

"**The blue steps is inside that district and it has no row number.** And I have filed two certificates for that district's own examiner and neither of them could be accepted, and the reason both times was a line headed *row number*, and both times the line is a box and a box with nothing in it is not a mistake, it is a refusal somebody is making in the only place a person can make it.

"So I have entered one. This afternoon. In my own hand, and I was not asked to enter it and I did not ask anybody whether I might."

"How much standing have you to do that," said Fenn.

"None whatever," said Mara Quill. "That is why I did it and not somebody else. **Anybody in this building with standing to enter a row number would have had to put the district's name in a column headed *name supplied by*, and the name of that district is on a chalk board that is not a document, and there is a woman and a child in it and no name at all, and I was not going to be the person who wrote that down in a hand with an office behind it.** So I have entered a number and not a name, and my own name is on the form as the person who entered it, and the paragraph that explains it is going into four buildings this afternoon with the date on it, and it says that the row number is entered for the purpose of a count and not for the purpose of a supply, and that nothing in this entry creates a right to water in any district, and that the person entering it has no office in that district and has never had one.

"And I would like it read out in a street in about a month by somebody who is not me, and read out as a piece of administration and not as a rescue, because **a row number is a thing that makes a place countable, and countable is not the same as served, and I have watched a city in a drought confuse those two words for six weeks.**"

Nobody in that street thanked her, and about four people who had come expecting to said so afterwards to each other and not to her.

---

And at about two o'clock on the same Monday, in a room off the terrace road, a minute clerk of nineteen years read back into the record a requisition he had entered at half past eleven, and it was the ninth thing he had entered that month and the first one anybody had entered against a company in nineteen years, and it went on a cart on the Tuesday and it was four days up a road, and a copy of it came down the lane on the Wednesday afternoon in a boy's coat.

> *To the Open Channel Guild, at Bexhill. From the office of the Assembly papers, in this city. Requisition to permit an inspection of the counting-house at Vellum, and of shelf C, volume 44, by an officer of this office and one other, upon a stated day and between stated hours, upon the ground that a reach has been read for six years out of one shelf, and that no other office in this basin has been inside that building.*
>
> *The ground is not a complaint. The ground is a sentence written in a master's own hand in a letter to a village on the tenth of this month, and copied out in full into the record of the Sallow Reach, which that village sent to Vellum in a cart with nothing else in it on the sixteenth of this month, and a man in a post room at Vellum read it, and he is not the man entering this, and he is a clerk and he is not thanked and will not be.*
>
> *Entered at the eleventh day of the morning of this day and read back to me in the same words and I have signed it. — O. Fenn, minute, nineteen years.*

Perdie Ammid wrote the whole of it out on the Monday evening on a sheet of paper — sheet two, and she numbered it out loud before she numbered it in ink — from a copy that the boy of nineteen had carried down the lane in a cart that was going up the mountain road anyway, and she read it twice to about nine people in the shop at eight o'clock, and then she wrote one line under it, and read that out twice as well.

"**They have taken a sentence out of a book in a village and put it in a requisition, and the sentence was not written for a requisition, and nobody asked the man who wrote it, and he is four days away and cannot be sent for, and there is nothing in this he can stop and nothing in it he is thanked for.** Write that on this sheet, under my number, and put the date on it, and do not put his name under it, because he is not the one who paid it and I have been wrong about that twice this month and I am not going to be wrong about it a third time."

And on the Monday night of the nineteenth of this month there were about nine people in a room over a shop in a village, and a man from Cresset was on his fifty-eighth day in a valley, and a card was in a press at Culm with a hole in it, and a chain was on a gate, and a man of sixty-one at a toll bar was packing a bag for Thursday, and about four hundred yards below the shop, on a post at a culvert, there was a hole in a card with a padlock printed under it and a date under that, and the date was the third, and the card would be back on the post on the Friday.
