# Chapter 0083 — Four Rods

The count is read on the last Saturday in the month and Perdie Ammid had ruled it off on the Friday morning in the second book without asking anybody, and the last Saturday we shall read it on was the third day of next month, and it was taken.

They were on the tank at about eight in the morning. The roster said eight. There was a barrow and two pails and a lath and a watch and a rule and a boy of fifteen, and a man of thirty-eight with one hand, and a man of thirty-one with a bar, and a boy of nineteen, and a man of thirty-three with a bad shoulder who had carried a rule and nothing else.

The tank is twenty feet by fifteen feet and two feet eight inches to the coping, and a mark is cut in the inside of the wall with a chisel and undated, and the water was two feet six and a quarter inches against it, and the rule went in twice and the two readings were the same, and Jory Vant wrote both of them down and said the figure out loud and the boy of fifteen wrote it on the card.

"An eighth of an inch," said Jory Vant, "is the whole of six years, and you have now said that figure out loud twice in a month in front of people who write things down, and the day somebody says six years is a quarter of an inch the card will be the only thing that says it was not."

Then they went down to the plank, which is three hundred and forty yards below the throat, and the plank is Nesta Sarg's, and she was not there because she does not come out on a count morning and has never, and Wick Venn said so and did not explain why and nobody asked.

Six gallons in one minute and fifty-two seconds, and six gallons in one minute and fifty-three seconds, and the figure is one hundred and ninety gallons an hour, and both trials went on the card under it, and the boy of fifteen said the same thing he said last time, which is that a count which comes out the same is worth more than one that comes out different, and nobody argued with him because he is right about it and has been right about it since the second of the month.

Then the flume sluice, four hundred yards from the tank, which is shut and chained and has been for six years, and there is nothing whatever in eleven feet of stone launder, and there is a padlock on the inspection cover of it, and the chain has not been renewed and there is no keeper and there has not been one for six years.

Nobody measured anything. The card says nothing measured and zero is refused in print, and that is the correct entry and it is the entry that has been on that card since a boy of fifteen cut it in a village and it is not going to be improved on by anybody this morning.

And then the fourth rod.

---

It took about an hour and a half, all told, of the four and a half hours, and it is the only part of that morning that was in a field.

The Sallow Dyke is a cut in a blue clay at the second bridge in the lowest field, a foot wide at the bottom and a foot deep, with a coping of two courses laid by somebody who was not a mole man, and the water was in it and it was clear and it was moving.

There was nobody there but the five of them. It is a Saturday and about nine hundred acres of apple in this Reach are not sown and will not be sown this year because the ground went like a bench on the twenty-fourth of this month, and the lower field is a field that grows beans, and there are eleven acres of beans in it belonging to a man of fifty-eight who has not spoken to his sister in four years and who was out in it at seven in the morning and said good morning and nothing else.

The lath went in. Twenty feet, twice, and the boy of nineteen read the watch out loud both times and wrote both times down, and the two pairs were the same pair as the pair taken on the twenty-ninth of the month before, which is twelve seconds and twelve and a half seconds, which is seven hundred and fifty gallons an hour and seven hundred and twenty gallons an hour, and the figure on the card is seven hundred and thirty-five.

"That is what it was," said Jory Vant, twice, both times to Tarin Rusk and not to the card, because a count is not a measurement. A measurement is a thing you do to a channel to find out about the channel. A count is a thing you do on a Saturday to find out what a channel is doing today, and the two of them have been confused in this Reach since the fourteenth and the difference between them is about an hour and a half of a Saturday and it is the reason the two sets of hours are in a column in a book.

They did not work it out again on the bank. Nobody did the sum. The sum was done on the twenty-ninth in a field in front of eleven people and it is in the second book in four lines, and a man of thirty-three said out loud in that field that a fitter would have taken seven hundred and fifty and that he had taken the middle one because there is nobody in this Reach left to check him, and he said it once and said he was not going to say it again.

The whole of the hour and a half was walking to the second bridge, standing in a field in the sun with a lath, walking back, and putting a lath in a barrow.

---

The card was cut at about half past one in the afternoon in the front room of the shop at Brant, and the boy of fifteen cut it, and he has cut every one of them since the middle of the month before, and the frame it goes back into is ninety years old and was cut out of a much larger card for something else.

The third rod was empty and the reason under it was a padlock, and the reason under the reason was a date, and the date is the third of next month, and nothing else about the third rod changed, and Perdie Ammid would not let anybody put anything under the padlock.

"You are all going to want to write the reason the padlock is a padlock under the padlock," she said, "and the reason the padlock is a padlock is a room in this village on the twenty-seventh day of this month, and that is entered in the second book on a page of its own in a boy's hand with a signature under it saying he copied four lines of five and was not asked to copy the fifth, and it is in a stove. A card is a card. A card goes up a road in a cart and comes back in a fortnight off a press in Culm and it goes on a post at a culvert and any person may read it, and the only thing that goes on a card is a figure and a reason and a date. The reason is a padlock. Write it and let it alone."

And the fourth rod went on, at the bottom, under a line, in the space where there had been nothing, and it took the boy of fifteen about four minutes to rule it and about nine to head it, and the heading is in Perdie Ammid's hand because he cannot write a heading, and the heading is:

> *Fourth rod. The Sallow Dyke, second bridge, main cut. Not a supply. A drain. Figure read and not measured.*

And Jory Vant, nineteen years old, put the figure in under the heading, in his own hand, and then read the whole of the four rods out twice the way a man reads a proof to a printer, which he has never done before in his life and which he did because he has copied four lines of a form once already this month and got a signature for it.

Then he signed it, at the foot, under the date, which is not a thing a boy's name goes under on a card, and about eleven people in that room watched him do it.

"The fourth line above is entered in the hand of J. Vant, apprentice, of Bexhill, of no district, and it is a figure taken on the third day of this month by four persons and a boy of fifteen and it is not measured, and there is no instrument in this basin that would measure it, and the two trials on the twenty-ninth of the month before are the trials for it and they are in a book in this village and not on this card."

"That is a long line for a small card," said Bess Ilk.

"It is four rods long," said Jory Vant, "and the third of them is a padlock."

---

Nobody thanked him, and nobody thanked the boy of fifteen, and nobody thanked the man of thirty-one who wrote the seventh line of the shed card on the Wednesday of the month before, and the man of thirty-three who put two readings in the tank and then put the rule down and picked up nothing else for four and a half hours would have been embarrassed to be thanked for, and has said so in a yard in this month, twice.

What Wick Venn did was the thing he has done three times now, and it is his and it is not a moral and he will not be one about it.

"Four and a half hours," he said, at about two o'clock, in the yard, to nobody in particular. "That is what a count costs and it has been the same since the second of the month and it is going to be the same on the third of next month after this one, and there are two more rods on that card this month than there were and it did not take a minute longer, and I have been trying all week to work out why and I have worked out that it is because I have been reading two of them with a lath on a Saturday instead of walking a barrow to a padlock, and the barrow took longer and I did not know it was the barrow until the third of this month.

"Ten hours went in a column in this book on the Wednesday of the month before, and the column is to be added to and not taken back, and this morning is a line in it, and the line is four and a half hours, and a lath instead of a barrow, and there is no column on this card or in any book for what a boy of nineteen's hand on a card is worth, and I have looked for one, and there is not one, and I am not going to start one this month."

Perdie Ammid entered the third line of the column in the second book on the Monday evening, in her own hand, with the date, and the third line is the third of next month, four and a half hours, four rods, six people and a barrow.

The card went back on the post at the culvert on the Saturday afternoon, and it is the first one in four weeks that anybody in that village has been able to walk up to and look at, and there was a fourth rod on it that had not been there the last time anybody looked, and the third rod was where it had always been, and anybody may look at it.

**The card goes up on the Friday the ninth and comes back on the twenty-first, and between those two dates there is a hole on a post at a culvert that anybody may look at, and on the twenty-first there will be a new card on that post with a third rod empty and a fourth rod that did not exist a month ago, and nobody will be able to do anything whatever about the third one.**

**The second book is not going in that cart.** It has been the record of the Sallow Reach since the thirtieth day of the month before, entered in Perdie Ammid's hand and initialled and witnessed by nine, with the first fifty pages of the second excepted, and a record is a book and a book is four hundred leaves and a cart that has gone up to Culm for a printer has a board across it. **It goes on the Friday after this one, which is the fourteenth, and it goes alone, in a cart with nothing else in it, and there are four days left in which this book is in a room over a shop and is still being written in.**
