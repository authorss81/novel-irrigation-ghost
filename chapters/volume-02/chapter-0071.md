# Chapter 0071 — The Party Having The Care Of The Work

On the Thursday morning of the eighteenth day of this month a man of thirty-three sat down in the front room of the shop at Brant with a sheet of paper in front of him and a bad shoulder and twenty-six days behind him, and read out, in order, the whole of a column headed *keeper of the reach*.

He read it twice. He read it the second time slowly.

> *Keeper of the reach:*
> *[a name in the Reach's own hand, struck through with a single line]*
> *Supply district 14, North Sallow.*

"That is the whole of it," he said. "Six words, a date nobody has ever established, and a name of a village that is not in this valley and is not a person and has never been a person and cannot be asked anything by anybody in this room. And it is lawful. I want that said first and I want it said by me and not by somebody else later, because it is the only reason there is any of this in front of us: an office entitled to make that amendment made it, in a lawful form, six years ago, and nobody in this locality was asked, and there is no way anybody in this locality could have been."

Nobody in the room argued with that, because Perdie Ammid had found it on the Monday morning of the eighth day at about seven in nine minutes and had said nothing for a day, and had then put it on a card on a post, which is the loudest thing a woman of fifty-four has ever done with a piece of paper.

---

"What I want to know," said Tarin Rusk, "is what a keeper column is for."

Perdie Ammid put her hand flat on the table.

"It is for saying who answers," she said. "That is the whole of it and it has been the whole of it for a hundred and twenty-four years. It is not a column for saying where water goes. We have a column for that. It is on the second page and it is headed *discharge of*, and it has said the same thing since the day the book was printed, and it says the long drain."

"Which is now struck through."

"Which is now struck through," said Perdie Ammid, "on the flyleaf, in ink, six years ago, and the row above it is the one that was struck, which is the reason somebody wrote the row above it, and I have not worked that out in six days and I am not going to work it out in a room with nine people in it."

Margit Saye, who had been at the back with her case across her knees, said the thing she had said twice already in two rooms and which she said differently this time.

"I can read the hand. I have told a room of thirty people that and I am not going to stop. It is a counting-house index hand of about six years ago. A clerk who writes a great many documents in a day and signs none of them. The Guild trains that hand. There are three men alive in that counting-house who learned it off the same copybook and one of them is the man who is index clerk this month, and I will not say a name in a room, and not because of a stranger, and not because of a man, but because a hand in a room is a man in a room and I have been the only person in this basin who could put a face to that sentence and I would like it recorded that I could and did not."

"Then the hand tells us nothing," said Jory Vant, nineteen, from the wall.

"The hand tells us a room," said Margit Saye. "It does not tell us a man. There is a difference and it is the whole of what I have to say and I have said it."

---

Sef Ordry put down the bar he had not needed to be holding.

"Before anybody starts," he said, "the thing I said in a shed and in a yard and I am saying it a third time and I would like it minuted that it is the third time. A calendar year is not a cause. A keeper's name was struck out in the sixth year and a sluice was shut in the sixth year, and in four years there will be a story in this basin about a man who took a name off a schedule and shut a gate, and there is no man in it, and nothing anybody in this Reach can say will ever be against it, because the two things were done by two people in two places in the same year and a year is not a person."

"That is the last time," said Perdie Ammid. "Write it down as the last time."

"I will say it as many times as it needs," said Sef Ordry.

---

At about half past ten Jory Vant put a bundle of unused forms on the table, tied with a bit of tape, and untied it, and it was the fitter's bundle, because a plate fitter of twenty-four years carries printed forms the way other people carry string, and Margit Saye had put it in his satchel on the Thursday morning at the works and told him it was for the shed and not for the room and he had brought it to the room.

"There is about forty of them," he said. "They come up from Bexhill in a bundle twice a year and there is a shelf of them in a counting-house and about nine in ten are for things this basin does not have."

The top one was a single side of a sheet about the size of a wage note, headed:

> **OPEN CHANNEL GUILD. Kerrow Water. Sallow Head.**
> **RETURN OF WATER SUPPLIED FROM A WORK.**
> *To be made out by the party having the care of the work, and not by the district supplied, and not by any officer. Four days' notice is not required and none is given.*
> *The quantity is to be stated with the two trials and the times, or it is not to be stated.*

Bess Ilk put her tea down behind the counter and read it over the top of it, because she reads everything that goes across that counter, and she read the small type twice and then said the only thing in the room worth saying about it.

"There," she said, and pointed with one finger at a line about a third of the way down, in smaller type than the rest, with a space above it and a rule under it.

And it said:

> *Takers, named, so that a man who is thirsty may come to the door of the man who has the water.*

Nobody said anything.

Margit Saye said nothing for rather more than ten seconds, which is a long time in a shop, and then she said:

"That is the Sere."

"That is what," said Jory Vant.

"That is the third line of the oath." Her hand had gone flat on the table beside Perdie Ammid's. "Word for word. *I will be named among the takers, so that a man who is thirsty can come to the door of the man who has the water.* And it is in print. It is in types. It is in a counting-house at Vellum in a drawer in a bundle that comes up in a cart twice a year, and it has been in this basin for a hundred and thirty years, and nobody in the Reach has ever seen it, and about four people in this room have stood on a bank at the third mile below the intake and read four lines off two kerb stones, and one of those four is a woman of forty-six who has just read the same sentence in a form and is not going to say any more about it this month."

Perdie Ammid said, "Then it is not evidence and it is not a right, and I said that on a bank eleven days ago, and I said that a stone with four lines on it is a piece of evidence and the moment this Reach has a piece of evidence about the Sere somebody will start a case with it, and I have not changed my mind and I am not going to be thanked for saying it now in a room where it is in print."

"It is not a stone," said Tarin Rusk. "That is the difference and it is the whole of why it is worth a sheet of paper, and I have been in this Reach twenty-six days and I have thought about nothing else for four of them, so you are going to have to let me get it out in the order I got it out in."

He read the small type again.

"One. To be made out by the party having the care of the work. Two. And not by the district supplied. Three. And not by any officer.

"On the face of the Reach's own schedule of its own works, the party having the care of this work is supply district 14, North Sallow, since the sixth year, in a hand a fitter can read and a village cannot. A district cannot subscribe a return. A district is a column and a rate and a set of properties and there is nobody at Vellum with lawful authority in the matter of a flume sluice on a mountain who could be got to a door. There is no acting commissioner and the registry cannot answer a letter. I have been told that in a room in a city sixty-one miles east and I have had four letters sent to that city since the second of this month and one answer and it answered nothing.

"So the party having the care of this work cannot make this return, and that is not a hardship anybody in this room is going to feel, and I have sat up two nights finding that out, and the reason it matters is that it means the Reach is standing in a doorway with a wet coat on and the form is the only thing anybody in this basin will ever be handed to say what it is holding."

---

He wrote the four lines out on the sheet, in his own hand, slowly, and he read each of them as he finished it, and then he read the whole of it out, twice, the way a man reads a proof to a printer.

> *Works: the West Flume sluice at Sallow Head, an installation of the Open Channel Guild, together with the tank, the intake and the stone throat on the embankment line.*
> *Source: the tank at Sallow Head, of six thousand gallons of room, the water standing at two feet six and a quarter inches against a mark cut in the wall with a chisel and undated, and standing at that mark on every Sunday of six years in two men's hands, the whole of the variation being an eighth of an inch.*
> *Quantity supplied: eight hundred and fifty gallons an hour, from trials of eight hundred and thirty-three and eight hundred and sixty taken in the same slot in the same month, the figure agreed by the Reach, and marked on the card as not a figure anybody should lean on.*
> *District supplied: none. The water of this work is not delivered to any district and is not stated to be delivered to any district. The channel named in the schedule as the Sallow Dyke is two miles four chains with the flume sluice. The long drain from the wet common at Nether Sallow is four miles two chains with the sluice at its head, and no water is stated to be delivered into it by this work, and item 1 of the schedule of 1902 forbids it, and it is obeyed.*

And then he stopped, and put the pen down, and said:

"Now I am going to say the rest of it and I have said it to myself four times in two nights and I would rather say it in this room than anywhere in this basin.

"Every one of those figures is true. Six thousand gallons of room is a room and not a figure off it. The mark is a mark and not a reading. Eight hundred and fifty is the figure this Reach agreed and it has the two trials printed under it and the words *not a figure anybody should lean on* which are on the card in three places and were put there by me. Two miles four chains is on a printed slip in this room on a pin and it is two miles four chains. Four miles two chains is the same slip, and the slip has the year on the face of it and has been on that pin since before Bess Ilk was born, and it is four miles two chains, and the two of them are the same two figures with the numbers put in the wrong order, and a clerk in a print shop in this village in 1902 did that in a fortnight and thought nothing of it, and the print shop is the bookshop and the bookshop is in the family still.

"And not one of those numbers is about the same reach."

He said the last four words twice.

"So it is a good form. It is filled in honestly. Every figure on it can be checked by anybody who walks up that road with a rule, and there are three of those figures and one of them has a line under it saying a man with a stop-watch ought to be careful of it, which is more than any return in this basin has ever had. And it is false, and it is false in one place, and the place is the fourth line, and the fourth line is the only line on that sheet that is a sentence instead of a number.

"The Reach has water. That is true, and it is true by more than that form says, and I do not know by how much, and I am not going to invent a number at eleven o'clock on a Thursday to make a return look better than a form. Supply district 14, North Sallow, has no supply. That is also true. And a return of that, subscribed by a person with lawful authority in the matter, is a return. It goes up the road. It goes to a shelf in a counting-house where one volume is kept in a drawer and nobody has read it in six years. And in four days, or in a month, or on the morning that somebody east of here is short of water, a clerk in that building has a piece of paper that says this reach does not supply district 14, and district 14 has had a return for six years that says it takes its supply off the works at Sallow Head, and those two papers together are about four lines long and there is not one word in either of them that is a lie.

"And that is the whole of the trick and it is a hundred and thirty years old and it is printed in the small type, and it is *the party having the care of the work*. You do not need to be a thief to be dangerous. You need to be the right kind of honest in the right place on the right day, and every one of those three words is a word this basin has been getting wrong since before the assize."

---

Then he came back to the top of the sheet and put his finger on it.

"And it is nothing. As it stands. As it is on this table this morning."

He read the sentence in the small type again and made a man of thirty-one read it after him.

"A return subscribed by a person with lawful authority in the matter is a return. A return subscribed by a person without lawful authority is nothing, and is worse than nothing, because it may be read. That is in the small type on a form that has been in this basin since before the assize and it is the best sentence anybody has ever printed about what a piece of paper can do.

"I have two live charges against me and a name in four buildings and a district of *none held* and a wage that comes out of the villages and not out of any water account, and I could not vote in this room if I tried, and in a room on the twelfth day of this month I said that out loud in front of thirty-four people and listed who the takers would be, in order, with a reason for each, and I wrote it down. I am not a person in this matter. There is no office in this basin in which I have any authority over a flume, a tank, a throat or a drain, and if I put my name at the foot of this it will be read by a clerk in Vellum and by anybody else who ever gets hold of it, and the only thing it will have done is put a man who is a stranger in a place of a name.

"So the form wants a person. And it does not say that the person has to be *me*. It says the party having the care of the work. And a party who cannot be named cannot be given anything — that is in a letter that came to this village on Wednesday addressed to somebody else, and it was read out in this shop on Wednesday afternoon and I was in the shop, and I believed it when I heard it and I have stopped believing it, and I am not going to say who stopped me.

"So the form has to be subscribed. It is not subscribed. And a form that is not subscribed is nothing, and is worse than nothing, because it may be read — and I have got it in my hand, and I am going to take it out of this room in my coat, and there is one line on it that is not filled in, and it is the fifth line, and it is the only line on that sheet that is not about the water at all."

He put his finger on the rule under the fifth line.

"And a man who is thirsty comes to a door. Not to a village. A village has forty-one doors and a parish and a rate and a name that has been in the fourth book for two hundred years, and you cannot leave a district at a door because a district is not a place you knock at. It wants a person at a door, and a person at a door is somebody somebody can be sent for, and I wrote that down in a room on the twelfth day of this month as a list of debtors, and I have been wrong about it for four days, and I am not going to pretend I have not been.

"And there is one line. And it is on the form. And I have not written anything on it, and I am not going to write anything on it this morning, and I would like somebody to tell me why I think I am going to have to."

---

Bess Ilk put the cup down. Jory Vant said, "Seven hundred and thirty-five."

That got the room, because nobody knew where it had come from.

"There is no seven hundred and thirty-five on it," said Tarin Rusk, "and there is no such measurement, and I have been on the edge of asking you for four days and I have not asked you, and the reason I have not is that you are nineteen and I have watched you do one thing all this month that I have not been able to do, and that is stop when a thing is not right.

"Every figure I put on that sheet came off a rod, a pail, a watch, a staff, a lath or a wall, and I have said where it came from and you may all go and look at it. And there is a hole in the middle of it about the size of the Reach, and the hole is the six hundred and sixty gallons an hour that goes into the ground under a lane between the throat and the plank, and I have a figure for how much and no figure for where, and I have said that out loud in a yard in front of four hundred people and I will not leave it off a form in a room with nine people in it. If you have a number, I will take it and put it in and swear to it, and I will not take a number anybody has not written down twice."

Jory Vant wrote on the back of his own hand, in pencil, and did not read it out, which is what he has always done.

---

At about twenty to one Perdie Ammid stopped him at the door of the back room with her hand on the frame.

"Whose name is going in the fifth line."

"Mine," said Tarin Rusk.

"You cannot put yours in it," said Perdie Ammid. "You told a room of thirty-four people that on the twelfth day of this month, in front of a boy of nineteen, and you gave four reasons, and the second one was that a man of thirty-three with eleven pence and a name in four buildings could not be found at a door by anybody who needed him, and you were right, and I wrote it down because I write things down.

"So it is going to be somebody else's, and you know whose, and you have worked it out between this morning and this afternoon, and you have not said one word about it to one human being since eight o'clock this morning, and I have been sitting in this room for six hours watching you not say it.

"Good night, Tarin Rusk. I am sixty-one in February and I am going to bed."
