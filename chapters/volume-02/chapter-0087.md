# Chapter 0087 — The Price Of A Morning

On the Thursday morning of the eighth day of next month the room over the shop at Brant had about twenty-six people in it and a length of lath on the table with a rule against it, and nobody had asked for the meeting and it had been called by a woman of forty-one who does not ordinarily call meetings and had said so when she called it.

The flume is a stone launder six inches wide. When it is running it runs about three-quarters of an inch deep. That is a thing a man can put a rule to and it is not a thing anybody in that room had ever put a rule to, because the flume has been dry for six years and there has been no reason to.

"Rule in," said Nesta Sarg.

It went in twice and it came out the same both times and Jory Vant wrote both of them down and the boy of fifteen wrote them on the back of a shutter that is not in a book, because that is what they do with a figure nobody has a card for.

"Six inches by about three-quarters of an inch," said Jory Vant. "That is four and a half inches square. A square foot is a hundred and forty-four inches square, and a hundred and forty-four divided by four and a half is thirty-two exactly, which is a good thing, because I do not want to do a sum in a room. So it is **a thirty-second of a square foot**, and at about a foot a second that is a thirty-second of a cubic foot a second, and a cubic foot is seven and a half gallons in this basin and has never once been wrong, and so that is seven and a half divided by thirty-two of a gallon a second, which is a shade under a quarter of a gallon a second. **Eight hundred and forty-four gallons an hour.**"

"Printed twice," said Tarin Rusk. "The rule in twice and the division done twice, and both of you write the number down before either of us is told it."

They did it again and it was the same.

"That is the launder's own figure," said Tarin Rusk, "and it is the first figure in six weeks that has come out of a rule and not out of a lath. I am going to say the other one and the difference, then four things that can be checked, and then the one that cannot."

"Four hours and six minutes," said Perdie Ammid. "It is on the card, in a strip of paper an inch and a half wide, in my hand, and it says a window is to be named on a day it is named, and that the window is four hours and six minutes, and that the figure is eight hundred and fifty gallons an hour — and all of that is on the card because the window has not been had, and a window that has not been had is a figure in a strip of paper."

---

"Four hours and six minutes is four point one hours," said Tarin Rusk. "**At eight hundred and fifty that is three thousand four hundred and eighty-five gallons. At eight hundred and forty-four it is three thousand four hundred and sixty.**"

He wrote both figures on the back of the shutter and did not read either of them out twice because a multiplication is not a measurement and he said so before he did it.

"Twenty-five gallons. That is the whole of the difference and I am going to say the reason for it and then I am going to leave it alone for the rest of my life, because it is the same reason as everywhere else in this month and it is the reason a Reach cannot be given two figures for one water.

"Eight hundred and fifty is a lath and a stop-watch in a slot two inches wide and an inch and a half deep, on the fourth of a second, at a velocity whatever the water was doing that day. Eight hundred and forty-four is a section of launder at about a foot a second. **They are not the same quantity and nobody in this basin has ever put the two side by side until this morning**, and a woman of forty-six said in a shed this month that a Reach which has agreed a figure and then lets somebody substitute their own has two figures for one water, and that is where this whole business started, and she was right, and I have wanted that sentence in a book ever since.

"So the window is **three thousand four hundred and eighty-five gallons**, because eight hundred and fifty is the figure this Reach agreed in the second week of this month and agreed to print, and I am not going to let a fitter's arithmetic in a room in a drought overrule a village's own figure, and if anybody wants to work it at eight hundred and forty-four they may, and the number will be three thousand four hundred and sixty, and both of those numbers go on the card."

Two people said afterwards that they had wanted to argue about it and had worked out that he was doing the thing Margit Saye had described, which is a harder thing to argue with.

---

"Four things that can be checked, and I am going to say them once and they are going in a book and nobody is to derive them again this month.

"**One.** Against this Reach's own season. **The second book had the figure of that season on Monday and it is five million four hundred and eighty-one thousand gallons**, and I am not going to do the assize out loud a second time in four days, and three thousand four hundred and eighty-five is **about one part in fifteen hundred of what this Reach's own assize says it wants for a season.** I have divided it twice.

"**Two.** Against one household. Four thousand seven hundred and twenty-five gallons a household a day at a fifth is what the assize says, and three thousand four hundred and eighty-five is **about three quarters of one household's day**, on one day of four, for one household out of two hundred and ninety. And against a whole household's one-fifth share for the whole season, which is eighteen thousand nine hundred, it is **about a fifth.**

"**Three.** Against the Sallow Dyke. Seventeen thousand six hundred and forty gallons a day, and that is the water going west out of the lowest field, and three thousand four hundred and eighty-five is **about a fifth of one day of it.** Which means the window is worth about four hours of what leaves this Reach every day and does not stop.

"And **four**, and this is the one I have been putting off since the Monday. Three thousand four hundred and eighty-five gallons down a six-inch launder is going to run in a trench in a field and stand in it, and the dyke is the low field's own drainage, and the low field is nine hundred acres that is not a marsh because that cut has been taking it away since before the assize was written down. **So the last of the four is: a window of three thousand four hundred and eighty-five gallons, put down where the window of the fifteenth put one thousand four hundred and seventeen gallons, in a field, in a season, is a thing that has to be said out loud in a room by the man who is proposing it, and I am the man proposing it.**"

---

"And the one that cannot be checked," said Nesta Sarg, standing up again, "and I am going to say it in about nine seconds and then I am going to sit down and I am not going to be thanked.

"**You cannot put this window against Cresset.** The Reach counts in gallons a household a day and Cresset counts in gallons a head a day and there is no instrument in this basin that turns one into the other and there has not been one since the assize, which was done by men counting chimneys. A man of thirty-three said that in a room on the last day of the month before last and it was put to one side, and it cannot be put to one side any more, and that is the whole of the price, and it is not a number and it will not be a number.

"So the four figures on that shutter are checkable by anybody in this room. And the fifth one, which is what this is actually for, is a comparison with nine hundred and forty properties sixty-one miles east, and I cannot make it, and a man in that city cannot make it either, and between the two of us we have a price for a window and no way of knowing who is paying it."

Nobody in that room could tell her to make it and nobody tried. What happened in the last half hour of the Thursday was not an argument either: twenty-six people looked at three thousand four hundred and eighty-five gallons on the back of a shutter and about nine of them said the word *small* and nobody said it twice, and a man of thirty-eight with one hand said that a count is a day and a window is a morning and that this is the first morning anybody in this Reach has been able to price.

And then a man of thirty-three said the thing the room had been called for, and he said it standing up, and there were twenty-six people in the room and it is in the second book because Perdie Ammid wrote it down in her own hand on the Thursday night and read it out in the shop on the Friday morning before she would let anybody take the shutter away.

"I have been building roads out of sentences for a month," he said, "and I have got four of them in books in four places, and not one of them has a person at the end of it who can be sent for, and I have been the second one since I was thirty-one and I have never once been the first one, and Doctor Sen in the lower fold says the second one has cost more people than the first one ever would have, and this Reach has lost a Saturday and a padlock and a count and nine hundred acres.

"**And I am not going to hold this one back, and nobody in this room can stop me, and that is the thing I want written down in the worst possible way.**

"A road is a road with somebody at every end of it who has to be answerable for it, and this one has an end in this room and the other end is sixty-one miles east in a city on a cut of a fifth, and there is no way on God's earth for me to make anybody there refuse a figure, and I have thought about that for two days and there is no version of this week in which I do not send it, and I would rather be the man who sent it than the man who sat in this room with a number on a shutter and did not.

"So it goes up on Friday with the card. And I am going to tell them what it costs, because a price that is not told is not a price, it is a surprise, and I have been the cause of one of those this month and I am not going to be the cause of another. And I am not going to pretend that telling them makes it their business. **Telling somebody a price is not the same as asking them to pay it, and the distance between those two things is the whole of what I have learned in six weeks.**"

---

The card went up the mountain service road on the Friday morning of the ninth, in a cart, with a boy of nineteen, and the card had three rods and two figures and a hole and a fourth rod, and there was nothing written anywhere in this basin about what the hole was going to cost anybody.

Sixty-one miles east, on the same Friday morning, the arithmetic came back down the mountain and out along a clerk's road, and it did not come as a document and that is the whole of what happened there.

It came because a plate had been requisitioned for a works, and the requisition went into the counting-house at Vellum on the third day of next month with the particulars on it, and the particulars name the works, and the works is named in the returns of a district in another city, and a clerk of thirty-four in the post four years went looking on the Thursday for a figure to put against that works in a column of the return headed *demand upon the works named*, which is a real column and has been empty on every one of that district's returns for six years.

He could not measure anything. He multiplied.

> *Demand upon the works named: 3,485 gallons, being 850 gallons an hour for four hours six minutes, the figure of eight hundred and fifty being that entered on a card at a culvert on the mountain service road, a copy of which is held in this building, and the duration being that named on the same card. Entered by multiplication and not by measurement. The works has no keeper and no plate is fitted and no instrument in it has been read in the six years of the return. — T. Cray, clerk, the ninth day of this month.*

And there were nine hundred and forty properties in that district, and three thousand four hundred and eighty-five over nine hundred and forty is **three and three-quarter gallons a property**, and a good day's cart up the mountain is sixty gallons, and three and three-quarter gallons is about a sixteenth of one cart, apiece, once, for four hours and six minutes, and the whole of what the Sallow Reach is offering nine hundred and forty properties in a city on a cut of a fifth is a sixteenth of a cart each, and it cannot be refused by anybody, because there is nobody in that district with standing to refuse it and the form that would take a refusal has a line at the foot headed *takers* and it has stood empty on twenty-three returns in six years.

Sluice Warden Pell read it out.

He read it out in the front room of the warden's station on the terrace road at about eleven in the morning on the ninth, standing up, in daylight, to five people, one of them a boy of twenty-two who could not read and had been sent for because the district's count was the only count of that district, and because a paragraph had gone into a return on the first of the month in four buildings and the man who wrote it had wanted it read.

"There is no box on this form for a demand," Pell said, after he had read it, "and I have wanted a box for one thing since the fifteenth day of the month before, and I am not going to get it, and I have said that in public once already and I am not going to keep saying it.

"What I can enter is what I have seen. And what I have seen is this. That form asks me for a source and I have put in that the source is not accounted for, and I did that on the sixteenth day of the month before and I have entered it in a book with four copies of it in this city and it has gone up. And it has come back in a form with a figure in it, and the figure is a multiplication, and the multiplication is mine because I sent the form up, and a man in a counting-house sixty-one miles west has multiplied a figure that a village agreed to on a Saturday by a duration that a village has not had, and has made it into a demand on a district, and has written **entered by multiplication and not by measurement** at the foot of it, in his own hand, and dated it, which is more than anybody in this basin has done with a figure in a month.

"I am a warden and I count channels and not stories, and I am not going to argue with a clerk about a column. I am going to enter the figure as it is, with his name on it and the date, and I am going to enter under it, in my own hand, the time at which I read it out, because a demand on a district that has never been made before ought to have a time against it in four buildings or it is not a demand, it is a sum."

He wrote the time on the form. It was twenty past eleven in the morning.

Then he read the figure to the boy of twenty-two three times, because the boy asked him to and because a warden reads things out, and the third time the boy had it.

"And that is not all of it," said Niko Venn, "and I want one more thing read and I want it read now and not on Thursday, because on Thursday it will be a week old and the week will be the part everybody will remember."

"Read it," said Pell.


"There is no door in this district," said Niko Venn. "You read that line out on the first of the month and I said it in a room in the city and a man at the back of the review wrote it into a return, and it is in four buildings, and I have thought about it for eight days and I am going to say the rest of it.

"**A demand on a district is not a thing a person can refuse. A person is a different thing, and there is no form in this basin that lets anybody have the one without the other, and a clerk of sixty wrote that on the twenty-fourth day of the month before in a whole side of a sheet and it is four miles from a village in a book in that village.** And I have got no row number and I am not a party to anything and I am twenty-two and I have four pebbles in a tin and I have been doing a count since the first of the month before for nothing.

"So there is nobody here to say no to three and three-quarter gallons. And the only thing I can do with a number I cannot refuse is write it down somewhere that is not the form, so that in four years there is a second place it is in, and somebody in this city has told me that is what a counter-record is, and I have not got the word right and I do not want it."

He took the fourth copy of the card out of his coat. It had a hole in it and a reason printed under the hole and a fourth rod in a boy's hand with a signature under that, and it had been in his coat for twenty-one days.


And on the back of it, in chalk, on the Wednesday, a girl of about fifteen from the top end of the lane read back to him three and three-quarter gallons a property, and he wrote it, and he wrote the date under it, and he wrote *entered by multiplication and not by measurement* underneath that because he could not read it and had it read to him and it was the best sentence anybody had said to him in a month, and he gave it to the woman with the slate, and there were three copies of the price in that district before noon and not one of them had been signed by anybody, and a district of nine hundred and forty properties could not refuse any of the three.
