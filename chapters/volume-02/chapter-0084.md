# Chapter 0084 — At The Price Of The Thing

Nesta Sarg did the plank on the Sunday morning of the fourth day of next month by herself, because it is her plank and it has been her plank for thirty-two years and she has never once asked anybody to help her with it and there is not going to be a Saturday in this month when she starts.

**One hundred and ninety gallons an hour, the same pair as Saturday, and she timed it herself and wrote the figure on the back of her own kitchen shutter with a stub of chalk, and she did not read the watch out loud to anybody because there was nobody there, and she did not write the two trials down because they are on a card at a culvert and she has nothing against that card.**

The boy of fifteen found her at about eleven.

"Are you going to the shop tomorrow," he said.

"No," said Nesta Sarg. "The shop is shut on a Sunday and I have not been in it on a Sunday in nine years and I am not starting in a drought."

"There is a room over it," said the boy of fifteen.

"There is," said Nesta Sarg. "And what would I want with a room over it on a Sunday."

Then he asked her the question, and she has repeated it since as the only sensible thing anybody asked in that week. The fourth book says four gallons a household a day and the assize says four thousand seven hundred and twenty-five at a fifth, and the woman at the shop read all three of them out and everybody said *that is the water of the fields* and went on drinking out of a cart. Which one of the two is it, then. And Nesta Sarg said it is both, and one of them is about a field and one of them is about a house, and they are not the same water and they are not the same number, and that is not an error, it is the whole of a thing about a place.

And he asked her what a fifth was for.

And she stopped with the pail in her hand for longer than he expected, and then said: a fifth is what a place decides when it has not got as much as it wants. It is not a discount. It is a share, and a share has to be agreed by the people who are doing without, and there is a line in a book in a chest at Brant that says the four several times are to be appointed in writing by the assembly of the villages and not by any officer.

And the boy of fifteen said, "Then who decides."

And she said, "That is the whole of it, and you are fifteen, and I am going to tell you the answer and neither of us is going to be thanked for it, because nobody ever is.

"**The assembly of the villages decides. And it has not met since the spring before, and it will not meet this year, and if it met tomorrow it would be nine men in a room over a shop and about four of them would be offended.**"

---

The room over the shop was full on the Monday afternoon of the fifth, which is the best day that a room in that village gets in a year, and there were about twenty-four people in it, which is the most that has been in it since the sowing shut.

The fourth rod was on the card at the culvert since the Saturday, in a boy's hand, with a signature under it, and it had been carried into that room by about nine people in the way that a thing gets carried into a room in a village, without anybody deciding that it should be.

The man of fifty-eight with the eleven acres of beans was at the back and had been at the back since about two, and his sister was at the front, and they had not spoken to each other in four years over a paper with a different name on it, and they signed the same words on the same paper on the twenty-seventh of the month before this one, when three names went into a printed line without anybody asking them, and that paper is in four buildings and in the second book on facing pages, and nobody in that room referred to it and two people in that room wanted to very badly.

Nesta Sarg stood up to do it, which she does not ordinarily do, and Tarin Rusk sat down, which he had not intended to.

"Right," she said. "Nobody is a physician in this room and I am not one either, and I have been asked in the last three weeks by three separate people what I take out of a drain and I have never once been asked what a household's day is, and I am forty-one and I have been pricing drains for thirty-two years and I have never priced a house, and I am going to do it now, and I am not going to be thanked, and if anybody thanks me I am going to go out of the room and I have not done that in front of people since I was nineteen.

"So here is the Reach's own figure and it is two hundred and fifty years old and it is in a locked press at Culm in a hand that is a copy of a hand two centuries older.

"A house in the lower field, in the season, ninety-four thousand five hundred gallons, delivered in four days at four several times, and the four several times appointed in writing by the assembly of the villages and not by any officer. A house in the upper field takes the whole of that and no more. And a house that does not take its water at the four several times forfeits the price of it, and the price of it is the water and nothing else.

"A fifth of ninety-four thousand five hundred is eighteen thousand nine hundred. Over four days, that is **four thousand seven hundred and twenty-five gallons a household a day**, for four days, at four times. Two hundred and ninety households at a fifth for the season is **five million four hundred and eighty-one thousand gallons.** Those are the numbers and they are the Reach's and they have been the Reach's for two hundred and fifty years.

"And the fourth book says **four gallons a household a day.** Four against four thousand seven hundred and twenty-five is about a thousand two hundred times. And a good day's cart up the lane is sixty gallons, and four thousand seven hundred and twenty-five is about seventy-nine times that, and a grandmother's generation called sixty gallons a wet week. Both of those sums are about eleven years apart and both of them are right and I have said them out loud and I am not going to say either of them again this month.

"So there are two figures for a house's water in this valley and they are a thousand two hundred times apart and they are not the same water. One is what a man drinks. The other is what a field has to have put into it before the field will give a crop, and there is no instrument in this basin that will turn one of those into the other and there never has been, and anybody who tells you the assize is wrong is talking about a house, and anybody who tells you the fourth book is wrong is talking about a soil, and the man who read all of them out on the Monday in the month before last was right and he has been right since and he has not been thanked for it either."

---

"Which brings us to the money," said Tarin Rusk, "and I have got here in one sentence because I have been in this Reach forty-four days and I have got very good at getting to things in one sentence, and I would like somebody to stop me doing that, and nobody ever does."

"Go on," said Nesta Sarg. "Say the money. I have been waiting for a man to say the money since the twelfth day of the month before."

"There is no money in this room," said Tarin Rusk. "Not one shilling of it. I have five shillings a day, the villages have not paid me a rate, and the last money anybody in this Reach saw was a printer of sixty-eight opening a locked drawer for nothing. And there is a bill in a city sixty-one miles east and I have not seen it and I am not going to pretend I know what is on it, and I am telling you it exists because a card went up on a Friday and came back in a fortnight, and a card that comes back is a thing somebody has read.

"So here is the money and it is one multiplication and anybody can do it in a head. That throat passes eight hundred and fifty gallons an hour and it has for six years and that is **twenty thousand six hundred and forty gallons a day**, and a day of it at a penny a gallon is **one thousand seven hundred and twenty shillings**, and at threepence it is **five thousand one hundred and sixty**, and eighty-six pounds a day is thirty-one thousand three hundred and ninety pounds in a year and two hundred and fifty-eight pounds a day is ninety-four thousand one hundred and seventy pounds a year. In one sum. That is the whole of what a rate is and I have just done it in a room in front of twenty-four people and it took me four seconds.

"And every one of those four figures depends on a second figure, and the second figure is what a gallon is worth, and that number is not ours, and it is not the villages', and it is being arrived at in a city sixty-one miles east by men who have not been up this road and who are not going to come up it, and if they come up it, we cannot refuse them, because we would be refusing the only thing anybody has ever offered us."

Nobody said anything for a while.

Then Nesta Sarg said the sentence, and it took her about twenty-two words, and she said it to the room and not to him.

"**You can leave a district at a door. You cannot leave a bill.** A household that cannot refuse a bill is not a household, it is a rate, and a rate is not a village and it is not a Reach and it is not an assembly, and the second you are on a rate you have handed a man in an office the four several times without ever asking us to appoint them, and he will appoint them.

"And I have read the oath of the Sere off a bank on the last day of the month before last, because a man of thirty-three read it out in the ordinary voice to five people and one of them was me, and I have not slept properly since and I have not thanked him and I am not going to, and the second line of it is: **I will pay for what I take, at the price of the thing, and not at the price of the asking.** Two hundred and fifty years. And there is a difference between the price of a thing and the price of an asking and I have spent thirty-two years learning it out of drains and the difference is the whole of my trade.

"So the thing has a price and the asking does not, and any man who comes up this road with a number on a piece of paper and tells me what my water is worth to him is selling me the asking, and I have been sold the asking four times in my life by four men with four documents and I paid every time and I would like somebody in this room to notice that what I got was a drain cleaned and not a price for the drain."

---

Tarin Rusk put both hands flat on the table, which he does, and said, "Then we do not sell it at a rate. We sell it at four several times, appointed in writing, by the assembly of the villages, and the assembly appoints them, and until it does there is no water and there is no bill and there is no price, and I am going to write that down today in the second book and I am not going to pretend it is a solution, because it is a way of not being sold, and not being sold is not the same as being paid."

"And who signs the bill," said a man at the back whom nobody had looked at.

"You do not sign it," said Tarin Rusk. "Nobody signs anything this month. I have been the second one since I was thirty-one and I have never once been the first one and Doctor Sen in the lower fold says the second one has cost more people than the first one ever would have, and I have been the second one all month and I have cost this Reach a Saturday, a padlock, four rods and a count, and I am not going to cost it a rate as well.

"**I am not making a party. I am not making a price. I am going to write one number in a book in this village and the number is the Reach's own, and if that is a refusal of eighty-six pounds a day then it is a refusal, and I will put that in as well, in my own hand, and it will be the first sentence I have written this month that I have not taken a day to take the end off.**"

It went in on the Monday evening, in Perdie Ammid's hand, with the date on it, and it is four lines long and it took her about eleven minutes to word and she read it out twice before she wrote it, and this is the whole of it.

> *Requested at Brant, in this room, on the fifth day of this month, by T. Rusk, of no office, of no district, and in no standing in this Reach:*
>
> *That the water of the Reach be stated in the Reach's own figure and no other, being four thousand seven hundred and twenty-five gallons a household a day for four days at four several times, which is one fifth of the assize of a house in the lower field, and that the four several times be appointed in writing by the assembly of the villages and not by any officer, as the assize has said for two hundred and fifty years; and that until that assembly has appointed them in writing on a day it has named, no water be sold, promised, apportioned, priced, or given a second time, and no price be entered in any book, and no bill be rendered to any household in this Reach by anybody for water out of the works at Sallow Head or out of any channel whatever.*
>
> *Witnessed by nine. — P. Ammid.*

And under it, in the same hand, on the same evening, one line more, which is hers and not his and which she said out loud to the room before she wrote it, and which is the reason the entry exists.

> *The Reach has no rate, no roll of ratepayers, no ratepayer and no collector, and a thing that has no roll cannot be rated, and this is not a refusal of money. It is a statement that there is nobody here to refuse on behalf of anybody, and that is a different sentence and I am fifty-four and I know the difference and I have had it out twice this month with a fitter and once with a man of thirty-one.*

Nobody thanked her. A man of fifty-eight with eleven acres of beans in the lower field said one sentence to his sister in four years at about four o'clock that afternoon, and it was that the beans would be in by the sixth if anybody was going to cut them and nobody was, and she said, "I know," and that is all of it and it is in the second book on facing pages and it is not the end of anything, because the assize is a hundred and thirty years old and is a plan for ever, and on Wednesday the ground went like a bench, and the next sowing is not a thing anybody in this basin has a date for.
