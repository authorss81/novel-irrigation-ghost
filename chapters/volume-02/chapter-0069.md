# Chapter 0069 — A Woman In A Drain With A Book

The purpose was written on a sheet of paper at a quarter past seven in the morning on the fifteenth day of this month, in Sef Ordry's hand, in front of two men and a woman and a man of thirty-three who was standing back and had been told he was going to stand back and had agreed to it in about four seconds.

> *West Flume sluice at Sallow Head, an installation of the Open Channel Guild. Cover sealed, sixth year, the wax being whole at the time of this writing. Purpose: to open the gate and put a measured service window on the flume, on the authority of the notice served on the villages and of the one signature on it, and of nothing else. Two men to stand at the slot: S. Ordry, sluicewright, and M. Saye, fitter. The sheet behind the slot to be read out in full and copied in two hands before the wax is broken.*

The wax came out of the slot at eight o'clock and Jory Vant read the sheet out loud, all of it, standing up, at about a foot a second, twice — once through and once back — and he read the words *the chain is to be renewed and a keeper named* in the same voice he read everything else in and that was the correct thing to do and Sef Ordry had told him to do it in that voice on the Sunday night.

Margit Saye copied it. Jory Vant copied it. Neither of them wrote a single line until the wax was off, and the wax was off before either of them put a pen down, and both of them signed while the wax lay in the palm of Sef Ordry's hand, and Tarin Rusk was six feet away and watched the whole of it and did not once go closer, and it is in this account because it happened and because he was standing in it.

The cover came off in three pieces because the plate is iron and the frame is iron and the screws are old, and it went into a barrow, and the chain came off with a bar and a chisel, and the padlock came off with a cold chisel and about four minutes and a bent drift.

"Year on the stamp," said Margit Saye, holding it up.

"Forty-nine," said Jory Vant.

"Right," said Margit Saye, and put it in the barrow, and said to about six people who were not there, "Now, in about eleven years, somebody is going to stand in that shed door and see the date on that lock and put two things together, and I am not going to be one of the men who stands there and nods. A date is a date. It is not a name. I have never in twenty-four years seen a lock that told me anything at all about the man who used it, and a fitter who claims otherwise is a fitter who is about to be very surprised in front of a room."

The chain and the padlock went in a bucket and the bucket went under the shed bench, because they were going back on at about eleven o'clock, and everybody who was there understood that they were going back on, and nobody said so.

---

The gate came up at four minutes past eight and the water came into the launder with a sound that four of the six men present had not heard before in this basin, which was not a sound of water but a sound of a thing being filled, and Jory Vant said, "That is a channel emptying for the first time in six years," which is a sentence a nineteen-year-old is entitled to say once.

The first quarter of an hour was brown.

That is the eleven feet of missing invert at one and a quarter miles, where the stones are on their sides in the silt, and the flume ran dirty and there was a smell of a wet road coming up the road behind them for about a mile, and then it ran clear, and then Margit Saye put a lath in and Jory Vant had the watch.

"Twenty feet in twenty seconds."

"That is exactly a foot a second."

"A stone launder that has not been relined in a hundred and eighty-one years does not go at exactly a foot a second," said Margit Saye, "and I have said that out loud, and I am going to use it anyway, and the reason is that if I write the figure I would prefer to a person with a stop-watch in his coat and a better launder than this one, then in four years the man with the stop-watch is holding the card and I am holding the argument, and I have been in this trade twenty-four years and I have watched that happen.

"Four and a half square inches in the cut and about a foot a second, and a cubic foot is seven and a half gallons in this basin, and that is eight hundred and forty-four gallons an hour.

"And the figure on the card at the culvert is eight hundred and fifty, and it is the figure on the card because the Reach agreed to a middle one on the first of this month and put the words *not a figure anybody should lean on* under it, and I am going to use eight hundred and fifty for the sum on Monday, and not my own, because a Reach that has agreed a figure and then lets a fitter substitute her own is a Reach that has two figures, and a Reach that has had two figures for one water inside one month is standing exactly where this whole business started, and I have read the fourth book as well as the specification book and I know what a name that has been written down twice costs in about thirty years."

Wick Venn went down the flume at ten past eight with a lamp, on the road side, because a barrow and a lamp and one good hand is the most a man can do on a road beside a launder, and the boy of fifteen went with him as far as the piers and then no further, because Wick Venn told him to go on to the outfall and wait there, and because a boy of fifteen with a slate and a bucket is worth more at the end of a flume than in the middle of one.

And Perdie Ammid was at the outfall, and the reason she was at the outfall is that she is the only person in the Sallow Reach who is a party to anything this month, and a party is a person who has to be standing where the water ends up.

---

At about twenty past nine on the Monday morning the water came off the end of the last stone of the flume into the long drain, in about a four-foot drop, and the boy of fifteen saw it and wrote it on the slate and then looked up the flume because he had been told to look up the flume about once a minute for an hour and a bit, and the flume was a wet stone with a hand's depth of water in it going away up the hill.

And Perdie Ammid was in the drain.

She was standing in about two feet of water in gravel about nine feet below the flume's end, in a dress with a shawl over it against the cold of the morning, and she had the third book in her hand and she was not looking at the water.

She said, "Go and fetch the fitter."

And the boy of fifteen said, "I'm not going to leave you in a drain."

"You are going to leave me in a drain," said Perdie Ammid, "and you are going to take this book in both hands and you are going to take the lamp and you are going to go up that flume as fast as you have ever gone anywhere in your life, and you are going to come to that gate, and you are going to hand it to one of the men there and say *she says the schedule*, and you are going to say those words and no others, and if anybody asks you what is in the book you are going to say you do not know because you cannot read, and that is not a lie, and it is the best thing you will do in your life.

"And you will not be asked to do it twice. You will do it once. Wick Venn says you can do that road in twenty minutes and you will do it in eighteen, and if anybody asks you in eleven years how long it took, you will say you do not remember, and you will remember."

The boy of fifteen took the book and the lamp and went, and Perdie Ammid stood in about two feet of water in the long drain, two miles below the plank, and read the clause of the Reach's own schedule of 1902 aloud, twice, to nobody, with the water going over the end of the flume about nine feet above her head. The flume's outfall is two miles below the plank and the long drain is four miles two chains from its head, and the nineteen houses are at the far end of it, and the water that came over the flume's last stone never came near any of them.

And nobody at the plank at Nether Sallow knew, and the pail was full and the pail was empty, and that is two miles away, and a clause in a schedule does not care how far away it is.

> *Item 1. The Long Drain, from the wet common at Nether Sallow, with the sluice at the head of it, is the sole discharge of this Reach. No other water whatsoever shall be admitted to the long drain, whether from a flume, a waste pipe, a culvert, a spill, a torn joint, or a wet stone; and any such admission is a trespass upon the rights of the villages named in this schedule, and may be stopped by any person having the care of the drain, without prejudice to any other remedy.*

She had found it on the Monday morning of the eighth day of this month in a chest in a village twenty feet from where a man of thirty-three slept, and she had said nothing for a day, and she had said it out loud in a room of thirty people on the Tuesday, and she had not put it on a card, and she had told a room of thirty-four people on the Friday that a stone on a bank was not a witness and a book in a chest was not a notice, and she had put the amendment in a flyleaf in pencil, and she had signed a notice on the Friday morning in front of eleven witnesses and had said that after Monday she would be a document and not a person.

The window opened lawfully on the fifteenth day of this month at four minutes past eight in the morning, and it was stopped at about an hour and forty minutes by a person, and nothing whatever supernatural happened, and the book was the Reach's own and the clause was printed in it in 1902 and the water went into a drain that a lane of nineteen households has been catching with a plank for thirty-two years, two miles below where the plank is, and a woman of fifty-four stood in two feet of it in a shawl and stopped a route with a page, and the clause she stopped it with is a clause about the Reach and not about the water in the pipe.

---

The boy of fifteen came up the flume in eighteen minutes, which is two miles and four chains at a shade under eight minutes a mile, and Wick Venn had said on the Sunday night in a shed that he could do that road in twenty and that he would not stop, and had said, "And I have watched him not stop, and I have watched a man of forty-four not stop on a wet stair, so do not build anything in the shed tonight on that."

He came round the shed at the Sallow Head at about twenty to ten in the morning, on his knees, with a lamp in one hand and a bound book about the size of a quarto Bible in the other, and he gave it to Sef Ordry and said the words and did not say anything else, ever.

Sef Ordry put the book on the bench with a lamp beside it and found the page, because Perdie Ammid had told him on the Tuesday that the drain was the first page and not the fourth, and he had remembered the first page.

He read the clause out loud, once, at the foot of a gate with three men and a boy standing in front of it and a flume running, and about a hundred and twenty gallons a minute going past them down a stone.

Then he said, out loud, to a man of thirty-three who was standing back:

"It is the Reach's own schedule. It is the right of it. I have had a page in front of me since Friday afternoon and I did not ask for it, and that is on me and not on her, and I am going to say it once in daylight with six people at it and then never again.

"Chain on."

The chain went on at about ten past ten, at about the fortieth minute of the second hour, and the launder drained in about nine minutes to about a wet stone with half an inch of water in it, and the boy of fifteen and Jory Vant carried the padlock and the chain and the bar up the bank together, and the barrow went to the culvert with the cover in it in three pieces, and nobody in the Sallow Head works could be got to say a single word about it that was not about a launder.

---

Tarin Rusk did the sum on the flags outside the shed at about half past eleven, in front of about twenty people who had walked up, and he did it once, and he did it out loud, and he did not do it again that day.

"Eight hundred and fifty an hour, which is the figure on the card and not the figure the fitter would have used and she has said so in a shed. One hour and forty minutes. That is one thousand four hundred and seventeen gallons.

"Two hundred and ninety households in this Reach at four gallons a household a day, in the figure in the fourth book, is one thousand one hundred and sixty gallons a day. So this morning was a day and about a fifth of what the households of this Reach drink, by the only figure any of us has ever agreed.

"And it went down a flume and into a long drain, past a plank that four of us stood at on Sunday morning, and into gravel, and there is a village at the bottom of that drain of nineteen households and it has been catching the water out of it with a pail and a plank since before anybody now living, and this morning the water in it went up by about four and a half times, for about a hundred minutes, and there was nobody in that lane who knew, and there would not have been one anybody if a woman in a chest had not been found on Monday morning and read out on Monday morning.

"And I am not going to say the word *loss* about that, because nothing was lost, and I am not going to say the word *refused* either, because nobody refused us. A woman found a clause and stood in a drain."

Nobody thanked her. She was four miles away and did not know it was over, and came back down the flume at about two o'clock with her boots wet to the knee, and when Bess Ilk said *well* she said, "Do not," and Bess Ilk said, "I was not going to," and it turned out that she had not been.

And then, in the front room of the shop at Brant, with about thirty people in it and a card nailed up in the doorway with a third rod empty on it, Perdie Ammid said the thing that the whole of this month had been about and that nobody had said.

"You did not come here for a witness," she said, to Tarin Rusk, and she was not looking at him, and she was looking at the card on the nail. "You have had a witness box on a notice for four weeks and you filled in two boxes out of five and a man in a room of forty-one people refused you a name and you signed his refusal into a book I keep in a chest, and then you spent ten days looking for a witness in the right places in the right order, which you did, and you did not find one, and you have not said so out loud once and you are not going to, and that is correct and I am not going to make a thing of it.

"You came here for a rod. You came for the rod at the embankment and the rod at the plank and the rod at the sluice, and you have got two of the three, and the third one is on that card with nothing in it, and the reason it has nothing in it is a padlock, and everything that has happened in this Reach in eleven days has happened because you could not get a number.

"A witness is a person. A person is somebody you cannot put in a box, and a box is what you have wanted for four weeks, and I have watched you want one, and I have watched you keep asking me for one, and I am not anybody's witness and neither is Aldis Kerr and neither is a woman of nineteen, and the only thing in this basin that could have given you a witness is a stone on a bank that nobody in this Reach has ever been shown.

"So do not come here for a witness. Come here for a rod, and bring the rod, and leave the witness out of it, and I will help you with the other thing for as long as I am able to, and I am fifty-four and I have a village to run in six weeks' time and I have not got many years of that in me either, and I would rather be asked for the thing I can do."
