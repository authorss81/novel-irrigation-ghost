# Chapter 0086 — A Smaller Number

The shed at the foot of the embankment has a hundred and eighty-one years of a shed in it, and a stove, and two benches, and a smell of oil that is the smell of oil and not of anything else, and on the Wednesday morning of the seventh day of next month there were five men in it and about half of them came in out of the cold.

Iven Marrow came up the mountain service road on the Tuesday in a cart with a man and came down on foot, and he said on the way up that he had come for about a day and that he would go down on the Thursday cart if there was one, and he said it as a man saying a thing he is not going to be asked to say again.

The card was on the nail. The requisition had gone up the road on the Tuesday evening cart of the thirtieth with a boy of nineteen on it, and its counterfoil was on a nail beside the card, and the counterfoil is the top sheet of a pad and by the company's own rule a counterfoil is nothing, and it is the only copy there is.

And in the inside pocket of a coat on the nail-hook was a bench book, nine inches by six, written in from both ends, going back further than the sealing of the works, and a man of thirty-three had carried it in a coat against a bad shoulder for about a month and had not opened it in a shed, because it is not his and it is not a record and it is a working book meant to be written in by whoever is at the bench, and it is the reason a company is worth anything.

Marrow took it off the nail-hook himself and put it on the bench and did not open it.

"That is mine," he said, "and I know it is on a nail in a shed in a valley and I have not come about it. I have come about a plate and a number, and I am going to say both of them in about four minutes, and then I am going to say the reason I came, and it is not the plate."

---

He put a brass ring on the bench. It came out of the gate chamber at the intake in a bag with a clerk's label on it, in a city sixty-one miles east, in the middle of last month, and it has been in his coat since.

"A month ago, in a store on a hill, I said that if you ever want to know what a plate should read, you put a thumb in the ring and you know what it should read. I have carried that thing about the whole basin for a month and I have not put it in a shed.

"Three things. They are all arithmetic, none of them is a discovery, and I want them in a book before I am asked a question about them.

"**A plate is a brass ring with a bore in it, and the number a man takes off a plate is a function of the bore and not of the iron.** A plate of a bigger bore reads a bigger number at the same head. That is the plainest fact in this trade and I have built thirty-one years on it and I am not going to soften it now because of what it is going to cost.

"**The plate in the ring at the intake at Sallow Head is a half-inch plate.** And here is the part that matters, and I have checked it in my own book twice and in the specification book on Tuesday and in a store on a hill a month ago, and it is a half-inch, and the plate it replaced was a half-inch, and the entries are six years apart and neither of them is a note.

"So the high gate at Sallow Head has read dry for six years with the right iron in it, and nobody has swapped a plate, and the reading is **true**, and the reading card is true, and the head on the printed slip of 1902 is **seven point three one metres** and it has never been measured by anybody because the only instrument in this basin that could do it is a hundred and forty years old in a counting-house at Ostre and belongs to a man of eighty-one. And there have been three letters to him this year and the answers are the same and the answer is silence. And I am going to write a fourth from Bexhill on the company's ordinary paper, and not up this road, because a man of eighty-one is not going to answer a letter off a mountain service road and I have been asking him as a stranger when I ought to have been asking him as a company.

"And the water is not coming. I have had that for six years and no reason for it, and a plate and a card and a true reading, and that is the whole of what I have. And I am not going to make a sentence about a man in a county four days away, and I have had a great deal of reason this month to make one, and I have not made it, and I would like it known that I did not make it and not that I could not."

---

Then he opened the bench book and turned it round so that it faced the other way, and put his own finger on a line, and it was a line about a plate and a bore and a date and a man, in his own hand, and it is nineteen years old.

"Every plate in this company has a specification and every specification has a bore, and the reason is that somebody once found a plate of a stated bore was not a plate of a stated bore and wrote it down, and there are two hundred and thirty-nine of them and the writing down of them is the whole of the company.

"And on the thirtieth day of this month, a requisition went up this road in a bundle of forms, and the particulars were written out in a man's own hand in this shed, and one of the particulars is a bore. **Three-eighths. Being the bore of every other plate in this company, and not the half-inch that is in the ring at the intake.** It is the correct bore. It is the most honest instrument requisition I have read in nine years, and I want it entered in this shed's book this morning that I have read it, that I endorse the bore, and that I am not going to have the word *wrong* used about it in this valley by anybody, including the man who wrote it.

"And I am going to tell you what it does, and this is the third thing, and I came up this road for it.

"Eight hundred and fifty gallons an hour is a **lath and a stop-watch** in a slot two inches wide and an inch and a half deep. It is a figure for a Saturday. There is no instrument in this basin that would put that slot on the schedule and there never has been one, and that is written in the particulars of that form in a man's own hand and it is the reason the form says it is honest.

"The day a plate is fitted, that stops. **A plate is permanent and a lath is a week, and the plate goes into the card, and the card goes to Vellum, and in eleven years there is a figure in a shelf with a plate's name on it and the two trials printed under it, and the trials will be the trials of a lath, and the figure will be a plate's.** And the plate will be three-eighths, and a three-eighths plate at the head that is in the slip reads a **smaller** number than a half-inch plate would read at the same head, and how much smaller I cannot tell you, because I cannot tell you the head, because the head has never been measured by a person.

"And I have said all three of those things to the man who wrote the requisition and I am going to say them to a village, and here is what it comes to and it took me a night on a mountain road to get to it.

"**That plate will lower the number. And the number on that card is the only figure about the water of this Reach that has ever been in a return, and in a month a city sixty-one miles east is going to be shown a plate where before it had a lath and a padlock, and it is going to be shown a smaller number, and there is not one person in this basin who will be able to say whether the water went down or the iron changed.**"

Nobody in that shed said anything for about eleven seconds.

"**And I am not going to stop the requisition,**" said Marrow. "I want that in the book in the same hand and in the same minute. Because the alternative is that a thousand two hundred times a year a man with a barrow and a boy of fifteen stand in a lane and time a thing a plate could say, and the plate is cheaper, and does not need anybody to be honest, and will be there after every man in this valley is dead. And I have spent four years watching a company lose a reach to a shut chain and a lock of gravel, and the lock of gravel is not the villain. **The lock of gravel is only what it is when nobody has a plate.**"

---

Sef Ordry put the bar down on the bench, and he did it the way he does, and he said the thing he was going to say before he said it, which is a habit he has and which Tarin Rusk has described in a yard.

"I am going to refuse one thing," said Sef Ordry, "and it is going to be me and not you, and I am going to refuse it in this shed with the door open, and there are five of us in here and that is a room of five and that is the size I want it to be.

"Mr Marrow. You have offered me a figure in plate terms twice this morning. I know what your hands did. I have been in a shed with fitters for nine years and I have watched the trick a man does with his thumb to turn a lath into a plate, and it is a good trick and it is the reason a company has a name.

"And I am not going to have it written in the day-book of this shed, and I am going to give you the reason, and the reason is mine and it is not a rule, and it is not about this works and it is not about you.

"**The company keeps two hundred and thirty-nine plate specifications and it keeps one of each, and it keeps no second copy of anything, and the book travels with a fitter and lives in her head when she is in a lane, and the rule that a fitter may not refuse a certificate or a setting on the ground of a hand is a rule I would defend in front of a bench of my own trade.** And that rule is why the counting-house at Vellum cannot be audited from outside this shed. Not because anybody has hidden anything. Because there is nothing to audit. **The good practice is the reason the question cannot be asked.** And I have spent nine years keeping the Guild out of rooms, and I have never once been in one of them as anything but a man with a bar, and that has been the best thing about my trade and I am not going to give it up for a figure in a day-book.

"And the difference is this, and it is the whole of what I have to say, and then I will be at a gate bed on Tuesday and he will hold the other end of it. **There is a difference between a company that can be asked a question and a company that has never been asked one.** And this company has never been asked one in thirty-one years, which is most of my working life, and I have been proud of it for nine, and I have said in three rooms this month that a calendar year is not a cause, and I have said it about six years, and I have never once said it about the fact that nobody in a hundred and thirty years has had to explain a figure to anybody outside a shed. So convert it in your head if you like. I will not write it down. And in about four years there will be a figure in a book in a village with a plate's name on it and not one specification in the world behind it, and I have decided today that I would rather have a lath on a Saturday for nine hundred years."

"Then the plate comes out and the lath goes," said Tarin Rusk.

"**The plate does not come out,**" said Sef Ordry. "It is not fitted, and the form says it in print on the front of it, and it has said it since before the assize. An instrument is not fitted until a keeper is named. A keeper is named by the assembly of a district supplied. And a particulars column was written out in this shed on the thirtieth with a district supplied on the works' own flyleaf, and not by me, because a particulars column filled in by the man who wants the iron is a particulars column with a man in it, and I have read it every day this week and it is the same district, and it is sixty-one miles east and it has no acting commissioner, and there is no assembly of it in this basin and there never has been.

"So the plate comes when a keeper is named, and the keeper is named when a district assembles, and the district assembles when somebody in that city has to hold a meeting about a thing that is in its own papers, and a card with a hole in it went up on a Friday and came back into a building on the first of this month and there is a paragraph under a cut of a fifth in that building that says the source of supply is not accounted for at this date. And I am not going to make a speech about that because I have said my one sentence for the month and it lasted a day last time and I am not going to have it handed back to me.

"**And I do not want the plate.** That is the thing I have got to say and I have been putting it off for two days. A plate is three-eighths and it is the correct bore and I would put it in myself in a wet week with a keeper standing on this floor. And the day it is fitted the lath stops, and the trials on that card stop being trials and become a history, and in four years the city will be reading a plate and a man at a slit, and neither of them will ever again have to stand in a lane with a boy of fifteen and time something they know is wrong. **I have been the man with the lath for nine years and have never once had to say a number I had not timed twice in front of a witness, and I am not going to be relieved of that by a piece of iron a fitter cannot come and take out and look at the back of.**"

Nobody in the shed contradicted him, and the boy of fifteen, who was in the doorway, said the only thing he said all morning, which was that he would rather the lath stayed, because the lath was a person and the plate was not, and nobody repeated it, and it is in the book because he asked for it to be in the book.

---

The end of it was that a sentence went into the day-book at about half past eleven in the morning on the seventh of next month, in Iven Marrow's own hand and signed by him, and Perdie Ammid has a copy of it in the second book at Brant because Jory Vant copied it out and put the date on it and did not sign it, on the grounds that a copy is a copy and a boy's name on a copy of a master's sentence is a boy in a master's sentence.

> *I have read the requisition of the thirtieth day of the month before and I endorse the bore asked for, being three-eighths, and I will not have the word wrong used about it in this valley by anybody. I record, for the book and not for a room, that the figure now on the card at this work is a lath and a stop-watch in an ungauged slot and is a figure for a Saturday, and that a plate of three-eighths bore at the head given in the printed slip of 1902 will read lower, and that I cannot say by how much and no person in this basin can, and that the day the plate is fitted the reading on that card becomes a plate's reading and the two trials under it become a history and not a trial, and that it will then not be knowable from any document in this basin whether the water went down or the iron changed. I record further that the plate is not fitted until a keeper is named, that a keeper is named by the assembly of a district supplied, that the district supplied on the flyleaf of the Reach's own schedule is supply district 14, North Sallow, that I am the party having the care of this work, and that I have held the reading card for six years and have not been up this road in six years, and that I am not going to stop the requisition, and that this is the whole of my trade and the whole of my fault. — I. Marrow, master, the seventh day of this month.*

It went up to the room over the shop at Brant at about four o'clock on the Wednesday afternoon and was read out in full by a boy of nineteen to about twenty-two people, and what happened afterwards was not a discussion and has never been reported as one.

Perdie Ammid entered it in the second book on a page of its own and then did a thing she has not done in thirty-one years, which is that she entered the *subject* of it, in the column headed *what it cost*, and the subject is: **a plate in a gate, in eleven years, that will read lower than a lath and cannot be got out again.**

And on the counterfoil on the nail in the shed door, which is the only copy there is, there is one more line in a different hand, in pencil, and it is the boy's, and it is not part of anything.

> *The seventh day of this month. Five men in the shed and the door open. Nobody has been put at the back of anything.*
