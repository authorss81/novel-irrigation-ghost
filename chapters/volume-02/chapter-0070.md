# Chapter 0070 — It Is A Tuesday

On the Tuesday morning of the sixteenth day of this month Perdie Ammid read out in the room over the shop what the Sallow Reach knew, as a list and not as a speech, and about twenty people were in the room and nine of them took it down, and she would not let anybody call it a statement, because a statement can be improved on in an afternoon and a list has to be got right.

"Eleven things, and I am going to say them in the order they are worth, and the order is not the order anybody would have chosen.

"One. There is a tank on a mountain holding six thousand gallons of room, and it has been at the same mark to within an eighth of an inch for six years, and it does not empty, and it is being passed through.

"Two. The water that used to go down a flume has been going over the side of a six-inch pipe in the wall of a chamber, out of a joint on the crown of it, and down a throat, into a drain, at about eight hundred and fifty gallons an hour, and the joint has been open for about six years and the pipe was laid to empty a chamber and has been silted in a hard gravel mortar for a hundred and eighty-one.

"Three. Four miles up the same main, on the west shoulder of the service road, there is a break in a conduit that is four and a half inches across with a second weep eighteen inches above it and two courses open above that, and ninety gallons an hour coming out of it into a field, and a lime rind on the lip of it is the only thing in this basin standing between that joint and a face of stone the size of a doorway, and when the rind goes the face will go.

"Four. The flume sluice was shut and chained six years ago, and nobody in this Reach knows who shut it or why, and the second mile of the flume has not been walked since the sixth year of the standard, and a chain is not renewed and there is no keeper and has not been one for six years.

"Five. The card at the culvert is not a toll notice, and there are two numbers on it and a hole where a third one should be, and the reason for the hole is printed under the hole and it is a padlock.

"Six. On the fifteenth day of this month, at four minutes past eight in the morning, the seal on the inspection cover of the flume sluice was broken lawfully with the fitter and the master of the Guild standing at it and the wax whole until the copy of the six-year-old sheet behind it was signed. The gate was opened and the flume ran, and at about half past eight the water came off the end of the last stone into the long drain two miles below the plank, and a woman standing in that drain read out a clause of the Reach's own schedule of 1902 which forbids any other discharge into the long drain, whether from a flume, a waste pipe, a culvert, a spill, a torn joint, or a wet stone. The gate was chained at about ten past ten, and what was in the drain was one hour and forty minutes and one thousand four hundred and seventeen gallons, and the water went into a drain with a village at the bottom of it.

"Seven. The sheet of the sixth year said, in two men's hands, *the chain is to be renewed and a keeper named*, and the chain has not been renewed and no keeper has been named, and the plate that sheet was behind is in three pieces in a barrow at the foot of an embankment and the Guild keeps no second.

"Eight. The Reach's own copy of its own schedule, in a chest in this village, has had a keeper's name struck out in the sixth year and *supply district 14, North Sallow* written in its place in a hand that nobody in this Reach can read, and the writing is six years old and lawful, and the office is in a city sixty-one miles east of nine hundred and forty properties and is not a person and cannot be asked anything.

"Nine. A count is possible. A window is possible. A name is not, and has not been since the twenty-seventh day of the month before, and the refusal is in the fourth book in a man's hand on the page before the notice and it is *I have not said the number is wrong. I have not seen the number.*

"Ten. Eight villages have refused in writing to be a party and one village has signed, and the one is this one, and I signed it, and after Monday I am a document.

"Eleven. There is a boy of fifteen in this Reach who has been paid five shillings a day out of the villages and entered in a wages column in a book that is a hundred and thirty years old, and who ran two miles and four chains on the road beside the flume in eighteen minutes on Monday morning with a lamp and a book, and his name is not written down, and he did not want it written down, and I have put that in the book too."

She put her hand flat on the table.

"Now the other list, which is shorter, and which nobody is going to put on a card, and which I am going to read out anyway because a list of what we know is worth nothing without it.

"We do not know who shut the flume sluice. We do not know whose hand is in the third book and I am not going to have anybody guess and I have had six days to want to and I have not. We do not know where the six hundred and sixty gallons an hour that goes into the ground under a lane between the throat and the plank comes out, and I am not going to have that written as *no loss* anywhere, ever, in my hand or anybody's. We do not know whether the break on the west shoulder at the fourth mile will hold the face for a week or a season or until the fifteenth of next month, and the only instrument in this basin that could put a number on the head at the Sallow Head is a hundred and forty years old in a counting-house at Ostre and belongs to a man of eighty-one who has had one letter this year from a sluicewright at Bexhill and one from a fitter on Friday and one from a master on Monday, and there have been three answers and the answers are the same and the answer is silence.

"And the last thing on that list is the one I would like a room to sit with. There is no keeper. Not this week and not in November. There is a mark in a wall and a card in a shed and a book in a chest and a signature on a notice, and there is not a living man in this Reach who is answerable for that water, and there has not been for eleven years at the works and six years on the flume, and I am the only person in this room who has said that sentence out loud and I have said it in about nine minutes and I would like somebody to stop me saying it again, because I have noticed that I am starting to enjoy it and that is the worst thing about a fact."

---

The bills came in on the Tuesday and they were in hours and shillings and were not in anybody's ledger.

The boy of fifteen had both knees skinned raw on the Saturday and had told nobody on the Saturday and had told everybody on the Monday, twice, at length, and had not once put it on a slate.

Jory Vant had a blister on the heel of his right hand about the size of a farthing and would not say where it had come from and Margit Saye found it on the Tuesday morning and put a plaster on it and said, "That is on the copy," which the boy did not understand and which nobody explained to him.

Sef Ordry had two days of chain on a shed floor and the skin off the inside of both forearms and said that it was the trade and had always been the trade, and would not have it written down anywhere, and had it written down anyway in a ledger in the shed because Margit Saye wrote it in a ledger in the shed whether anybody said so or not.

Wick Venn had lost eight days in a field in a planting window and had told nobody until four hundred people were standing in a yard, and on the Tuesday morning he was in the field at seven o'clock, and the field was behind nine hundred acres of apple that was being set by about a dozen people of whom he was one, and Bess Ilk had put a loaf and a cheese on the wall of the flume field at eight o'clock in the morning and had told nobody, and Wick Venn found it and ate about half of it.

And Tarin Rusk went to the toll bar at half past ten on the Tuesday morning, on foot, with no cart, half a mile down a road he had walked twice in eleven days.

"You said I would be at your bar on the fifteenth," he said.

"You were not at my bar on the fifteenth," said the man.

"I was not at your bar on the fifteenth."

"You had a flume sluice open at eight o'clock in the morning four hundred yards from my bar and I have been sitting in this hut since six," said the man, "and nothing went up that road between Friday morning and this morning except lime and a cart of boards for the flume field, and I have written that, and the entry is *nothing went up, except boards*, and a man who writes *nothing* in a book and a man who writes *nothing except boards* in a book are two different men and I know which one I am."

He shut the book and did not read it out.

"You did not come, and I do not think you forgot," he said. "I think there was a gate open at eight o'clock in the morning and I was a man with a book half a mile down a road, and I am not a party to anything and I would not have come either. And you wrote in a yard on the Sunday that a man cannot get water and be somewhere else at the same time, and you were somewhere else, and so was I, and neither of us is the villain in that, and the Reach has got a reach and not a route, and that is the whole of what I have to say about it, and I have said it about eleven times in forty years to about four people and I have never once said it to a man who was going to do anything about it."

Then he made the tea, out of the churn that comes up the road on the Monday of every week, and it was boiled, and there was a white crust about the width of a thumb in the jar behind him, and Tarin Rusk drank a cup of it standing up and said nothing about it then or afterwards and was never going to.

---

The letter came down with the Bexhill cart on the afternoon of the seventeenth day of this month, which was a Wednesday, and it came to Perdie Ammid at the shop because it was addressed to her and had a Guild wafer on it, and she opened it at the counter and read it and put it down flat and did not say anything for about eleven seconds, and then she read it out loud in the shop to about six people, and she did that because she does not hold paper.

It was one paragraph and there was no other side.

> *Mrs Ammid. I have read the card in the shed door at the foot of the embankment, including the line under the seventh, and I wrote the sixth entry and I have had a shelf in a counting-house for eleven years. There is no seventh of mine and there has not been one for eleven years, and a man of thirty-three has written on the back of a card in my shed that there should have been, and he was right, and I have had two days to think about it and what I have thought about is that I would like to have written the seventh myself.
>
> *I am not asking you for anything and this letter was not asked for. It does not answer the fitter's fourth question, because the answer to the fitter's fourth question is that I have written to the man at Ostre myself and he has not answered me either, and that is now two of us. It does not touch the sluice and I am not going to touch the sluice, and I have had a great deal of reason in nine days to make a sentence about the sluice and I have not made it, and I would like it known that I did not make it and not that I could not.
>
> *The reach has a card, a volume, a schedule and a notice in it, and four of those are true, and one of them is in a village and one is on a nail in a shed and I have no objection to either and the arrangement is a worse arrangement than mine and it is holding better than mine and I have thought about that for two days.
>
> *The offer stands. The bench is on a Tuesday. Nothing I have said in this letter is part of the offer and I will not be told that it is.
> *— I. Marrow, master.*

Nobody thanked her for reading it out. A man of nineteen in the doorway said that the master's letter was the second document in eleven days with a man's name at the bottom of it that a person had asked for, and a woman of forty-six from Bexhill said, "It is the first one in eleven days that a man was not asked for, and I would like to write to him about it and I have not written to him, and I am not going to this month."

---

The card at the culvert was cut on the Tuesday evening and rehung on the Wednesday morning, and the boy of fifteen did the cutting and the nailing because the roster is the only thing in this Reach that has been kept to, and the frame that it went back into was ninety years old and had been cut out of a much larger card for something else, and the nail in it had been driven back in once six years ago by somebody in a temper.

It carried the count of the fourteenth in three rods, the third one empty, the reason under the empty one.

It carried the window of the fifteenth, and the hour and the figure, and the words *the flume discharged into the long drain at about half past eight, and at about an hour and forty minutes from the arrival of the water at the outfall, the discharge was stopped under item 1 of the schedule of the works of the Reach, printed 1902, which forbids any other water whatsoever to the long drain, whether from a flume, a waste pipe, a culvert, a spill, a torn joint, or a wet stone; and the gate was chained at about ten past ten.*

And it carried a line underneath that, in a hand that was not any of the six, on a strip of paper an inch and a half wide, because the card was already full:

> *Brant is a party to the act of the fifteenth. Brant did not ask for the water. Brant was asked and signed and is a document. — P. Ammid.*

And at the bottom, because the card had been full for two days and the date had to go somewhere, in the hand of the boy of fifteen who does not want to be named, because his grandmother could not write and his mother could not write and nobody in his family has ever written a date down:

> *The seventeenth day of this month.*

The third rod was still empty. It was going to be empty on the fourteenth of the month after this one, and on the fourteenth of the month after that, and the reason for it was printed under it.

The reason was a padlock a day and a night off in a village at the bottom of a hill, on a shut gate with a broken seal and a plate in three pieces in a barrow. And there was still no keeper, and there was still nothing in the witness box, and the flume was shut and chained, and the weep on the west shoulder of the road at the fourth mile was under a rind of lime that a boy of fifteen and a woman of forty-six had stood in front of in thirty-six degrees, and the bank on the west side of that road was still soft in a drought.

The window had seven days left in it and the ground after the twenty-fourth of this month would be like a bench, and the sowing in nine hundred acres of apple in this Reach had begun on the Monday morning at seven o'clock with about a dozen people, of whom one was a man of thirty-eight who had been somewhere else that morning and had not been able to be in two places, and had said so in a yard on the Sunday, and had not been thanked.

It was a Wednesday. Nobody took the card down.
