# Chapter 0074 — What It Is Agreeing To

The letter to Wyle went up on the Wednesday morning of the twenty-fourth day of this month and it was carried by the boy of fifteen of Brant, who is on the roster, and who is not named, and who said so before anybody thought to ask.

Wyle is four miles down the Kerrow lane and a boy of fifteen can do four miles in an hour and a half and be back before the light goes if he does not stop, and he did not stop, and he was back at half past three in the afternoon.

The asking itself took about fifty minutes in the front room at Brant and it is four lines long and it is dated and it was read out twice before it was sealed, and it is the cheapest thing anybody did in this month.

> *To the village of Wyle. From T. Rusk, of no office, of no district, and in no standing in this Reach.*
> *On the twelfth day of this month Wyle refused in writing and asked that its refusal be read out, and it was read out, and it said that it would sign if it were asked in writing what it was agreeing to, with a date on it.*
> *This is that asking. Wyle is asked: what is it agreeing to?*
> *It is asked in writing, and this is the date.*
> *No person is asked for and no person's name is asked for. Wyle is not asked for water, is not asked for money, and is not asked to supply or receive anything.*

And it went up the lane with a boy who is not named, and the seal on it was Perdie Ammid's, because Tarin Rusk does not have a seal and has not had one since the eighth day of the month before.

---

Wyle's answer came back in a cart that was going the other way for lime and was turned round by a man at a toll bar who read the address and then read the address again.

It is in the hand of a clerk of about sixty at Wyle, which is the same clerk who wrote the refusal, and it is a whole side of a sheet and it is dated the twenty-fourth day of this month, and at the bottom of it, in the same hand, is the word *Wyle*, and nothing else, and the clerk's name is not on it and was not asked for.

The boy of fifteen brought it down and put it on the table and said that the man at the toll bar had asked him twice what was in the envelope and that he had said he did not know because he could not read, and that that had not been a lie, and that Bess Ilk had given him a bun for it and that he was not going to tell anybody about the bun either.

> *What Wyle is agreeing to.*
>
> *On the twelfth day of this month Wyle refused to be a party to a notice, and gave a reason, and asked to be asked in writing what it was agreeing to. It has been asked. This is the answer and it is the whole of the answer and there is nothing behind it.*
>
> *Wyle agrees to be a party to one thing. The thing is a paper. The paper says that the West Flume sluice at Sallow Head, an installation of the Open Channel Guild, was opened on the fifteenth day of this month at four minutes past eight in the morning, and that the flume discharged into the long drain at about half past eight, and that the discharge was stopped at about an hour and forty minutes from the arrival of the water at the outfall under item 1 of the schedule of the works of the Reach printed in 1902, and that the gate was chained at about ten past ten.*
>
> *That is the whole of it and it is taken from a card at the culvert and a card can be got wrong. Wyle agrees to be a party to a paper and not to the truth of it, and if the card is wrong then Wyle is wrong with it and no more wrong than any other village that signs.*
>
> *Wyle is not asked to supply water. Wyle is not asked to pay. Wyle is not asked to give the use of a drain, a sluice, a tank, a throat or a field. Wyle is not asked for a man.*
>
> *Wyle will not sign the paper itself, and Wyle's reason is its own and is not a reason against anybody. Wyle said on the twelfth that it would sign on the second of the month after this one, and Wyle will do that on the second of the month after this one, and it will keep to that, because a village that signs a paper on a Tuesday because somebody came on a Wednesday is a village that a man in an office can move, and a village that cannot be moved is worth a great deal more to a village than the paper is.*
>
> *So this sheet is not the paper. This sheet is Wyle's answer to a question asked in writing with a date on it, and it is signed and dated, and it goes into a book in a village and not into any office, and if a clerk at a counting-house ever wants to know what Wyle agreed to he can be told and he can be shown this, and if he does not like it he can go and be a clerk somewhere else.*
>
> *One more thing, and it is put here because it was asked of Wyle and Wyle would like it answered in return and does not expect to be.*
>
> *A party is a party and not a person. A village can be asked what it agreed to, and can answer, and can refuse, and can be cross-examined, and can be sued, and can be shut, and can be defended, all without one single person inside it being named. That is the whole of what a village is for. It has been the whole of what a village is for since before anybody now living was born, and it is why nine villages can answer a notice and not one of them had to send a man to do it.*
>
> *And a person is a different thing. A person can be found at a door. A person can be sent for. A person can be held to what he agreed to for the rest of his life, and a person who signs a paper about a door is a person who has agreed to a door. And there is no form in this basin that lets anybody have the one without the other, and anybody who tells you there is, in a hurry, on a Thursday, with a widow in the third mile who is seventy-one and will not be asked, is telling you about a form and not about a village.*
>
> *— Wyle. The twenty-fourth day of this month.*

---

It went into the second book on the Wednesday evening, in Perdie Ammid's hand, with the whole of it, and the boy's carrying it was entered under it, and Bess Ilk wrote the bun out of it.

And then nobody said anything for about a minute, and then a man of thirty-one said:

"That is the cheapest thing anybody has done in this month and it is four miles away and nobody in this room did it."

"It is a clerk of about sixty," said Perdie Ammid. "And he wrote it on the Wednesday and the boy was back at half past three, so the cart went out of Wyle at about eleven o'clock this morning, which means he wrote it at about nine, which means he has had it since the twelfth. He has had it for twelve days. He wrote it in twelve days and we were not asked anything in twelve days and it is the only thing anybody has sent anybody in three weeks."

"It is the answer I asked for," said Tarin Rusk.

"It is the answer you asked for," she said, "and it is also the answer to a question you have not put to anybody and I would like the room to hear the two of them together, because they are the same question and there is one answer to it and it has been sitting in a village for twelve days waiting for somebody to write down what it is agreeing to.

"And here is what it is agreeing to, and you may write it on the back of your hand in about nine words: **a paper, and not the truth of the paper.** Not water. Not money. Not a drain. Not a man. A paper. And there is no person in it, and Wyle has signed that, and it is in a book in this village with a date on it, and it is in a cart at a toll bar going four miles back to a village with about two hundred people in it, and that is the whole of the cheapest thing anybody has done this month and it is worth more than everything on the table in front of us."

---

Then she said the other half of it, and she said it to Tarin Rusk and not to the room, and about nine people were close enough to hear it and none of them moved.

"Now I am going to say what a paper road costs, and I am going to say it before you spend another shilling on it, and I have not slept since Tuesday and I have had the whole of it in my head and I am going to get it out and I am not going to be thanked for it.

"It is on a form that a fitter of forty-six printed in a bundle in a shed and I have read the small type on it and there is one line on it that is not about the water, and it is at the bottom, and it is a hundred and thirty years old, and it asks for a man at a door, and you have read that line four times and you have not once mentioned it in a room, and the fitter asked you to.

"You want that line filled. You want it more than you want the return, because the return is nothing without it. And there is no way of filling that line that does not put somebody's name in a book in two places, and there is no way of putting somebody's name in a book in two places that those people agreed to it.

"And you have four men on a roster. Three of them can write their own names. The fourth cannot, and has said in a wages column in my book that he is not to be asked, and there is one entry in that column and it is two days and a tenth of a shilling, and it is the first entry in that column in a hundred and thirty years and I would like you to think about what happens to that column in about a year, when somebody in this village is looking for a name and finds a column that says a man is not to be asked.

"**And I built that chest.** I built it out of four books that nobody was to turn into a record for twenty-six years, and I have said in this room, and in a yard, and in a book, that a book of customs is not a record and a record is a thing somebody keeps and a chest at Brant is mine and a counting-house at Vellum is not. I said that sentence out loud, on the twenty-seventh day of the month before, in front of forty-one people, and it is the best sentence I have ever said and I have not taken it back and I am not going to.

"You have made a road out of that sentence. Not out of the chest. **Out of the sentence.** I gave you a reason to refuse in a room and you have built a vehicle. And a road built on a refusing is not a road and it is not a refusing, it is a third thing, and the third thing is that in about eleven years the only thing anybody in this basin will be able to see about the four books is that a return was once made out of them, and nobody will be able to tell which of the two came first, and the four books will have become a record without anybody anywhere ever having decided that they should.

"So here is what it costs, and I am right, and I have known it since Saturday, and I have been waiting for Wyle so that somebody else would say it first. **The day that return is subscribed with a name in it, my four books are the Reach's record, and the record goes to Vellum.** Not a copy. The record. That is what a record is and I have said so in this room and I have been right about it for twenty-six years and I am right about it this morning and I am telling you what it costs because I built the thing that is about to be opened and nobody else in this basin is qualified.

"And I am not going to refuse it. I have thought about refusing it and I have decided that I am not going to stand in a room and be proud of a chest again in the one month in which I am going to have to open it, and a woman of fifty-four who has spent twenty-six years on a sentence does not get to have it both ways in a fortnight, and this is me not having it both ways, and it is costing me the sentence, and that is the last time I am going to be able to say that sentence in this village without somebody laughing."

Nobody laughed. About four people had their hands over their mouths and none of them was laughing.

"You may write the whole of that down," said Perdie Ammid, "and I will initial it, and it goes in the second book on a page of its own, and the page is going to be the reason there is a page after it."
