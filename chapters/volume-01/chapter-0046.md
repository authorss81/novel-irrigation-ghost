# Chapter 0046 — A Confession With A Price On It

On the Saturday morning of the ninth day of this month, at about the same hour at which Tarin Rusk had first put his hand into a live channel in this lane in the fourth week of the ration, a circular went out from the office of the acting commissioner with the seal of that office on it, and it was a single sheet, and it was in four buildings by noon and at a penny at the counter by two o'clock, and it was the first document this city's own water office had issued about the works at Ashgate in six years.

It was titled *Condition of works certified closed, released in advance*.

> *Office of the Acting Commissioner, water and channels. Ninth day of this month.*
> *1. The works at Ashgate were certified at decommissioning, six years ago, and again four years ago, as void of standing water, of residual head, and of maintenance requirement. Those certifications have stood in a store and have not been produced to this Assembly. They are released now, on my own instance, and before they are asked for.*
> *2. The condition of that works is therefore void, and has been void throughout, and no part of any water supply of this city has been drawn from it. Any water appearing in the district of the lower terrace road is a contractor's discharge delivered under a works order and is not a supply of this office or of this city.*
> *3. A return of the Open Channel Guild, made on the twenty-second day of last month, describes a chamber and a body of water on that works. This office does not dispute that a chamber exists. A chamber is a void space. A chamber that is certified void is a chamber with no allocation standing to it, and water drawn out of a head that is in no allocation infringes no allocation. The Guild's return is a competent document and is not in conflict with the certifications, because a Guild surveyor is not required to certify an allocation and this office is not required to pay for one.*
> *4. A note, for the avoidance of doubt. The contractors' board at that works reads five and seven for two years and five and nine for the two years above it. The change is explained by the fitting of a plate of a larger bore. A gauge plate is a part and not a work, and a man is paid by the can and will write down what the plate says. This office has thought it proper to state the explanation in its own hand rather than leave it to be found afterwards.*
> *5. The district in question holds no row, no schedule entry and no keeper. The midsummer ration review will proceed on the schedule as it stands.*

Five numbered lines, and no man named, and the whole of six years' arithmetic in them, and it was the work of about forty minutes by somebody who had never been told he was doing anything.

---

Pell read it out in the yard at about seven in the evening, because that is what he does with documents, and about ninety people were there because a circular from that office on that subject on a Saturday evening brings everybody out.

The yard let him get to the third line before it started, and it argued for eleven minutes and won nothing and lost the argument anyway.

"You have not answered the question," said Old Marden. "You have said that a chamber is a void space. Everybody in this yard has been told for a month that there is a chamber under a row of eleven houses, and there is a girl of fourteen who was dragged out of a school to say so—"

"That is not what the circular says."

"It is exactly what the circular says, and a circular is not a sermon, it is a piece of paper, and on that piece of paper a chamber is a hole and a hole is nothing, and there is a gentleman in the city who has had six years to say that and has said it to me in a document and I cannot argue with a document in a yard."

---

The counter was ready on the Monday, and it was ready because a conservator in Vellum had put the sentence in the record four days earlier and a warden's book had it in a fair hand and a woman with a slate had read it out on a step in a lane, and because the Guild's return had said it in item 9 in a form a penny would buy, and because Marrow had said it in a store on a hill on the twenty-second day of last month to a man with a bar, and it had gone up a cattle track in a return and come back down as a list.

*Nobody certifies a chamber void because a plate is the wrong bore. A plate changes a figure somebody wrote in a day-book. A certificate is a document, and a document takes a hand and a decision and a reason, and there is no bore in that.*

It was read out three times in that yard on the Saturday evening, by three different people, and one of them had it off the return and one of them had it off a slate and one of them had it off her own memory, and none of them said where it had come from, and it did not matter, because a sentence that three people in a lane can say without a paper is a sentence that is already in a room.

The fourth line of the circular, with its plate and its bore, was answered in about eleven seconds by a boy of twenty-two standing at the edge of the crowd with a copy in his hand, and then the yard went on arguing about something else.

---

The second sheet came up the cattle track on the Sunday morning of the tenth, in a satchel, and it had a printed head on it and two slots with ruled lines under them, and the first slot said **admission** and the second said **acknowledgment**, and there was nothing else on the page but a schedule of what was offered for it.

> *In consideration of the closure of the matter of the calibration log of the Ashgate intake and of the condition of the district of the lower terrace road, and of the withdrawal of the charge of unauthorized opening of a sealed structure and service of water from a closed work, upon the following admissions:*
>
> *First. That on the first day of the month before the figure recorded in the calibration log of the Ashgate intake was altered by the undersigned.*
> *Second. That the district of one hundred and twenty-six persons at the lower terrace road, which holds no row and no schedule entry, is not a district of this city, and has no existence in the record of this city, and the water drawn and delivered to it was drawn and delivered without right, and was a contractor's discharge of which the district had notice in fact and no notice in writing.*
>
> *And in consideration thereof the midsummer ration review will set the ration of the lower terrace road according to the schedule as it stands, and the matter will be closed, and the undersigned will not be proceeded against further upon either charge.*
>
> *Signed. [ ] Date. [ ]*

Marda Ollan read it out in the yard in daylight with the doors open, twice, and then she told the yard what the two slots were, because about forty people had understood the first and none of them had understood the second.

"The first is him," she said. "He signs it and the two charges fall and he is a man who wrote a false figure in a book on the first day of this month, and that is a lie, and everybody in this yard knows it is a lie, and it is signed by him so it is his."

"And the second is us," said Bel Crale. "It is not him. It is one hundred and twenty-six people signing that we have no existence and that we stole water, and there is no box on that page for the fact that the plant was shut on the second by this office without a letter, or that it was started again on the twentieth by a contractor who asked nobody, or that the only thing any of us has ever done about it is write it down.

And I want everybody in this yard to understand the trick of it. A district with no row cannot be hurt by a ration review. That is what Marda said on Friday and she was right. A district with no row cannot *also* be the injured party in one. A thing that does not exist cannot be compensated. So if they want a clean story for the fifteenth, the clean story has got two people in it: a man who falsified a figure, and a place that took water without right. And the man is real and the place is not, and that is the only version of the fifteenth of next month in which a single line of it is about a chamber."

---

He refused it in the yard at about eleven o'clock on the Monday morning of the eleventh, in daylight, in front of about a hundred and forty people, with the sheet on the step and the pen lying beside it and nobody's hand anywhere near it, and he refused it in nine sentences and it took about a minute and a half, and the ninth sentence is the one that is in four buildings.

"I have not altered a figure in the calibration log of the Ashgate intake," he said, "and I have said that in three buildings already and I am not going to say it a fourth time in a yard, because saying it again does not make it more true and it makes the yard smaller.

The second slot is not about me and I have no standing to speak for a hundred and twenty-six people and I am not going to pretend I have. That slot belongs to this yard and it is going to be answered in this yard and not by me.

"And here is the part I have come down here to say, and then I am going away and I am not going to stand about in a lane being watched.

"Two days ago a piece of paper in this city said that if I sign a lie, then the ration of the lower terrace road is set according to the schedule as it stands. That is a thousand and some people, and six rows and a half of them, and about four hundred households, and there are children on that schedule who are not in this lane and have not been in this lane all month.

"And the only water they have got is the same water. Not the same ration — the same water. A physician told this yard on the sixth day of this month that the salt in that tank has been getting stronger every day since the water came back into it, because the water goes off the top of it and the sun takes the water and not the salt, and that the first water out of a main standing a fortnight is the worst of it, and she has said in this yard three times that she has no instrument in this basin that would test it.

So if I sign that, a thousand people get a ration of a thing that a physician in this lane cannot measure and will not put a number on. And if I do not sign it, they get nothing, and there is no version of me signing it in which a thousand people drink anything else, because the tank in this yard is the tank that fills from the same plant and there is no second plant and there is no other road.

"I am refusing a true thing about myself in exchange for other people's water. That is what is on this table. It is not a sacrifice and I am not going to be thanked for it, and I want the yard to know that it does not put a drop in anybody's bucket, and that on the fifteenth day of this month, if the review is not held, nobody drinks anything that is not already in a tank. I have not saved anybody anything. I have only not signed."

---

The district refused the second slot at about two o'clock the same afternoon, in the same yard, by a vote of about ninety to eleven, and it was Hanne Roeg who put the question, because there was no row and no keeper and somebody had to, and she was seventy-one and had a cough and had said on the twenty-seventh day of last month that she would sign things and cost somebody something every time.

The eleven were not cowards and were named, and one of them was a woman with a barrow who said she had two children in the second row and no water coming, and that if the terrace got its ration out of a piece of paper her children would drink something in the morning, and that she was not going to be the woman who stopped it, and would think about it again tonight. Nobody argued with her. Bel Crale wrote her name down in the district book at her own request, so that the record would show she had voted against it and had not been argued with.

And then, at about four o'clock, Marda Ollan did the sum out loud in the yard, because somebody asked her what was in the tank, and she had the ledger, and she did it the way she has done it all month.

"Four thousand seven hundred at the coping, and it has not gone down by a measurable amount since the afternoon of the twentieth day of this month, because what is coming out of it is going into a lane and a shaft that have been full for thirty years. Three pounds and sixteen, which is nine hundred and twelve pence, and at a shilling the gallon that is seventy-six gallons, and a cart holds forty, and the last man who drove one up this track was Errol Slee on the thirteenth at four in the morning and he has not come back.

And I will say the other half, because I have said both halves every day since the eighteenth and I am not going to stop. That tank stood at four thousand seven hundred on the night of the second, at one thousand nine hundred and fifty on the evening of the ninth and at nine hundred and fifty on the evening of the fourteenth. It lost three thousand seven hundred and fifty gallons in twelve days. The yard's own draw accounts for one thousand four hundred and forty of that. The other two thousand three hundred and ten went out of the gate of this yard in other people's pails at sixpence the gallon, and Bel Crale's book holds the first week of it and nothing at all after the ninth, because a tank at its coping in a district with no keeper has never had a keeper's book."

"I can show you the day it stopped being a hundred and twenty a day. I cannot finish the sum. That is not new and I am not going to pretend it is, and there is nobody in this lane who is going to be able to finish it, and the reason there is nobody is on page eleven of a return anybody can buy for a penny."

---

At about eleven o'clock that night a clerk from the office of the acting commissioner came down the cattle track to say one thing and left again, and the thing he said was that the review was off.

There is no third charge sheet, and has not been since the fourteenth day of this month, and the office's answer to the third written request was in a corridor and is not in anybody's hand; and it was not mentioned that night, and nothing that has happened this month merges it with anything or resolves it, and it is a piece of paperwork that nobody in this city has got to.

The clerk said: the chair has cancelled the midsummer ration review set for the fifteenth of this month. The hearing stands and will be taken at the arch at four of the clock. The chair will state his reasons at the opening.

Nobody in that yard asked him why, because everybody in that yard had known the answer since the ninth, which is that a ration review does not settle a factual conflict, it refers it, and the city now had an Office document and a Guild return in flat contradiction about a body of water under a street, and an examination on the day after tomorrow, and a chair who had been in the post nineteen years and had entered four hearing dates of which three had been adjourned.

The clerk also said one other thing, in the road and not in the yard, to four people, one of them Niko Venn, who wrote it down and gave it to Marda Ollan, and it is on the last page of the district book for that week.

"He said the chair did not decide it this morning," said Niko Venn. "He said the chair had been told in four days by four separate things and does not make a decision like that on one of them. He said — and I am writing it in his words and he did not ask me to — *if the review had been able to sit I would have let it sit, and I would have let it sit with a chamber under a street, because I have done that twice before in this basin and both times I have been glad, and I am not doing it a third time with a Guild return in my papers that says the chamber has eleven feet of water in it.*"
