# Chapter 0019 — The Cut Off The Quarry Side

Niko Venn had been useless for thirteen days, and he had a very clear idea of what that felt like, and he had run out of ways to be cheerful about it by the second.

There is a particular kind of wasted that a young man with no work goes through, and Niko had been through all of it: up before the light, nothing to do by seven, a handcart up and down the terrace road for a man of sixty-eight who called him Niko and did not know there was a famine on, and the walks back to the blue steps at dusk like a dog that knows exactly where it is going and has nothing to do when it gets there.

The truth of it was worse than the walks. He was an apprentice. Apprenticeship in the Water Office was a contract and a wage and a place on the district sheet, and the master had surrendered the district reference on the tenth day of the month along with the seal and the keys, and there was no clause anywhere in the world for what a twenty-two-year-old is when the man holding his contract is charged with tampering with the city's records.

So he was not an apprentice. He was a young man with a roll of tools that were not his and a wage that had stopped and a set of hands that could seat a float in three millimetres, and on the evening of the twenty-eighth he had walked down the Sallow road to a chandler he had bought lead pipe from twice, and had put a coil of rope on the counter, and had had a very short conversation about credit.

He got eight fathoms of hemp, best quality, and a deal for the balance on the first of next month, and he was a man who had never in his life not paid a debt and was now going to find out what that was like.

He got the lamps on the thirtieth: two, hooded, and oil, and a hood for the second one made out of a tin lid because there was no second hood. He got them in a way he was not proud of, which was that the second lamp came out of the old filter house's stores, and Wick knew, and Wick had looked at him for a long moment and then helped him carry it down the hill.

---

He had been listening to the thump since the sixth of the month and he had never once mentioned it to anybody, and on the twenty-ninth night he lay awake and listened to it properly and it was faster.

On the sixth it had been twice a second, and patient, and patient as a miller, the way a man says a thing is patient when it is a machine and not a man. On the twenty-ninth it was three.

He counted it for a full minute with his fingers on the stones of the dispensary floor, twice, because the first count had been the sort of count you do when you have decided something and want to be wrong about it. Three a second, steady, no variation at all, and on the twenty-ninth it had not once missed, and it did not stop. It did not stop at four in the morning. It did not stop at the hour when a fire goes out or a man goes home, because Niko had lain in the lane on the night of the sixth listening to it go on past two, and it was still going on the thirtieth.

Somebody was pulling the head out of Ashgate and pulling it out faster than they had been, and Tarin Rusk, on the evening of the thirtieth, had put a branch on that head and drained a cistern into a lane over a plate, and had done it for nine hours, and had taken about eight thousand gallons out of a body of water that nobody in the city had any idea was there.

The thump getting faster was not a mystery. It was a pump making up for a head it had lost, and it meant one of two things, and both of them mattered.

---

He caught Tarin on the mill wall, which was where Tarin went when he could not be in the lane, and which had been every afternoon for two days, so that in the whole of the lower terrace a suspended engineer and a suspended apprentice were both spending their afternoons on a wall.

"It's faster," Niko said.

Tarin did not ask what was. He put his hand out flat, and they sat for a minute and a half and counted it together, and Tarin's own count was three a second and Niko's was three a second and neither of them wrote it down, and that was the most careful thing either of them had done in a fortnight.

"Well," said Tarin.

"Well."

"That is not the same pump on a heavier draw. That is the same pump on a head that is not there any more, and a pump does not know how to be tired."

"You have not told Yara Sen."

"No. And I am not going to, because Doctor Sen is going to tell me it does not matter that the plant under those works is working harder, because she is a physician and not a sluicewright, and she has nine children with the marks in a lane that cannot drain, and if I go to her tonight with a noise under a floor she will put a book down in front of me and I will not get it back." He looked at the mill wheel going round in the dry race. "I have also not told her that there is a pumping plant down there at all, except in a yard, on the twentieth, in front of a warden, in a sentence I could not un-say."

"Have not you." Niko shifted round on the wall. "Right. Then here is the whole of it and I am going to say it once and you are not going to enjoy it.

"I have got eight fathoms of rope and two lamps and a hood I made out of a tin lid, and I know where the cut is, and it is two miles up the mill road and round by the old quarry, and it is not in this district and it is not in your charge and it is not on any plan in the city, because it was cut by a quarryman before the works were built and the works were built by men who did not know it was there.

"It goes down about forty feet in the rock face and it came out at the quarry wall, and there was a ring of iron in the wall with a rope-eye still in it when I was fourteen, and my father put a load of water down it in a wet year to get a pump going because the cart could not get in and nobody had a key. It is not an entrance. It is a place where a rope has been before.

"The quarry wall is the back of the old filter house, and the filter house is where the noise is, and the only other way into the filter house is the works' front door, and the works' front door has a warden's seal on it that a man put there six years ago and that is still a good seal, and I am not going to be the man who broke it, and neither are you, because you have two charges and one of them is *unauthorized opening of a sealed structure* and if anybody in this basin has to be told no twice it is you."

Tarin Rusk sat on a wall above a dry mill race and looked at his apprentice for a long moment.

"I know," he said.

"You have known since the fourteenth."

"Yes."

"You have written it in the book and underlined it and you have had three weeks to do something about it and you have not, and neither have I, and I am not pretending the reason I have not is that I have been waiting for a permission. The reason I have not is that I am twenty-two and my master is under two charges and there is nobody in the city I could ask to go with me, and that has been true since the tenth of the month, and you have known it as well as I have, and you have let me sit on a wall for a fortnight."

"Yes," said Tarin.

"I am not doing this to be one of the things that happens to this row. I have said that to you in a lane and I am saying it again on a wall." Niko put his hands flat on his knees. "There are nine children in that lane with the marks and four of them are in a book that runs out on the fourth, and the tap on the terrace road main was shut by the warden's office on the first at nine in the morning, on a condition entry, and if you want to know why, it is because the water in it went brackish on the thirty-first, and Doctor Sen put it in writing, and the warden's office does not wait for an examiner. The lane is on its own bucket and a butt and the cistern, and the cistern is being sold at threepence the gallon to men from four miles off, and there are more pounds in this lane than there have been since the flood year, and the ground under the lane came up on the thirtieth and is still up. And none of that is going to be fixed by a pump making more noise, and I know that, and I have thought about it, and I have been lying in a dispensary at night for a week thinking about it.

"But I have got one thing I can do, and it is the only thing, and it is to go and see what is running the pump and where it is running it from. Because if somebody has got a pipe off the Ashgate intake then everything that has happened to that lane is happening because somebody took the head out of the middle, and nobody in this city knows that except me and you, and you have not put it in the book because there is nothing in the book yet that is not a man saying he heard a noise."

---

The argument they had on the wall took an hour and it was the best argument either of them had ever had, and it did not resolve, because it could not.

"You cannot authorise it," Niko said. "Say it. Say it out loud and put a time on it, so that it is a fact on a day and not an opinion."

"You have the standing of a lamp-post. I cannot authorise a descent into a sealed works and neither can any man in the Water Office without a district reference, and the only man in this city who has a district reference for these works is the acting commissioner, and he is the man who read my log back to me in front of a clerk on the tenth of the month."

"So it is unauthorised. Say the other one. Say the money."

Tarin Rusk looked at the mill race.

"I have no money," he said. "There is no fund, there is no district budget, and there has not been a district for this lane since before either of us was born. If I had money I would not pay a man to hold a rope, I would pay Wick, because Wick would refuse, and I would pay a stranger, and I have not got the price of a stranger. So: no authority, no money, and no crew."

"And you cannot be the man on the record for the rope."

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because if I go down or you go down or the boy goes down and it goes wrong, and there is a name on the warden's board, then my name is under it, and the warden's office will read the two charges and the name underneath them and they will not read it as a rescue. They will read it as a continuation." He said it without any self-pity at all, which was the only way he was able to say it. "I am the man on the twenty-second of the ration at 16:00. I do not get to be a second entry in the same month."

"Then I have to find a man in this district who is not you, and who will put his name on a board about a rope, four days after this district put a name on a stamped form, and who does not live here." Niko got off the wall. "That is not a small thing, Tarin. I have been walking round this lane for a fortnight looking for a man who will do it and there is not one. The nineteen who said no on the sixteenth said no for the best reasons in the world, and every one of those reasons is still true, and I have not found one man since who would put his name to a hole in a hill."

---

Hanne Roeg said no, and she said it at half past nine that night on the fourth step, to two men, and she gave them six reasons and five of them were good and the sixth one was the one that won.

They could not take the water to the Fenner district. "You have made us a market," she said, "and I did not decide that and I have not stopped it, and if a man in an office comes into this yard in three weeks with a form there is going to be a barrow, and a bucket, and eighty-six pounds in coin, and a doctor with a book, and now there is going to be a hole in a hill and a coil of rope and a boy of twenty-two with a debt at a chandler's, and I am telling you now that the district I signed for on Saturday is the district that gets taken off the schedule in October, and not because anybody is wicked, and not because anybody lied. Because it will be busy."

She told them about the examiner, and the thirty days, and the fact that a case filed on the twenty-seventh put a district examiner in this lane before the fourth of the next month, and that the examiner's form had a box on it that said *water supply in connection*, and that a yard with a rope in it and a cut at the end of it answered that box in a way that no amount of candour could improve.

"The district has been visible for eleven days," she said, "and I would like to tell you what visibility has cost so that you can both weigh it, and then I am going to bed.

"The tap was shut by a warden on the first of the month because a physician wrote a word on a slate, and the tap was the last municipal water any of us had. The water in the cistern is being sold off this lane to strangers at a penny a gallon four days ago and three pence now, and there is a man from the Fenner district who has been here four times, and the price went up because we gave a man a pail for nothing, and there are now men coming up this lane who do not live here. The floor of the lowest room is out and Ondra's belongings are on the flags and a boy is sleeping in a chair. My own water went into a dispensary on Wednesday and it is written in a book in that room with my name against it. And before the twenty-seventh of this month a man is coming down this track with a form, and the whole of it is in his hand, and it says: the blue steps, one hundred and twenty-six persons, no outfall, plate, no keeper, and now a descent.

"I did not do that. I signed a piece of paper and the piece of paper did that, in eleven days, at the rate it does things. And if the two of you go off a quarry wall in the dark on Friday night then the piece of paper will have done it sooner, and the row will lose the name and the water will go the way the water goes, and I will have been a brave old woman for a fortnight."

Tarin Rusk sat on the step and did not argue with one word of it, and the reason he did not argue was that it was correct and he knew it and he had worked out on Day 30 at four in the morning that he had put the district in exactly this position himself.

"We are not going to ask you," he said at last. "I am not going to stand on this step and ask you, and neither of us is going to come up this lane again on this business without asking you first, whatever happens."

"That is a promise you cannot keep," said Hanne Roeg, "and I am going to hold you to it anyway, because promises you cannot keep are the only kind anybody in this lane has ever got."

---

They went up the mill road at two in the morning on the second of the next month, and they went on foot, and Wick came, and Wick came because Tarin Rusk did not ask him.

Tarin had said: *I need a man who will hold a rope in the dark for a boy of nine's age and who is not a witness and is not a keeper and is not on any sheet, and there is one man in this district who fits that and it is not me and it is not Niko.*

Wick had said: "The bottom room is out. Ondra's boy is in a chair. I've carried pipe past that arch since I was seventeen and I never once asked what was at the end of it, and I have had a fortnight to think about what is at the end of it." And then: "I am not putting my name on anything. I am standing in the dark holding a rope. If I am wrong, I am a man holding a rope in the dark, and that is a thing I can be wrong about in a way I cannot be wrong about on paper."

The quarry was a mile and a half past the mill and up, and the face was cut in a long shallow curve with a spoil bank along the bottom, and there was no water anywhere in it and no wind and the sky over the Thorn Crown was going the colour of a knife.

The cut was at the north end, where the face turned in, and from thirty feet below and two hundred feet back you could not see it at all, which was why it was not on a plan.

Niko went down it first with a lamp in his teeth, forty feet of the rock face in a switchback cut, and Tarin followed, and Wick followed, and at the bottom there was a ring of iron in the quarry wall with a rope-eye in it that had been there when Niko was fourteen and was rusted nearly through.

The spoil at the mouth of the cut was fresh.

Niko put the lamp down and looked at it, and then went four feet to his left and looked at the wall on both sides of the ring, and then came back and crouched down over the ground, and Tarin and Wick watched him do it in the light of one hooded lamp and did not interrupt, because it was his ground and not theirs.

"This is a week old," Niko said. "There has been water down here — look at the rill mark, two fingers, running down the fall of the cut. That is standing water going out of a hole, not a hose. And this is boot prints, and there are four of them going in and one set coming out, and the one set is a man's and it is size nine, and the four going in are size nine." He straightened up. "So somebody went in four times and came out once and has not been back since, and that is not us and it is not anybody from this district, because there is a lantern on a nail by the ring and the lantern is lit and has been lit about two hours, judging by the oil."

There was a tin of lamp oil on the ledge, standing, and it was open, and there was a coil of spare rope on the ledge too, hemp, good quality, and it had been cut.

"That is not Wick's knife," said Niko, quietly, and Wick said nothing at all, and Niko put his own hand flat on the cut end of it and held it there for a moment the way you hold a thing you do not want to be mistaken about.

And then the thump stopped.

Not slowed. Stopped. It had been running, three a second, without a break, for a quarter of a century, and it stopped in the middle of Niko Venn's hand on a coil of somebody else's rope, and the two hundred feet of rock face above them took about a second and a half to say anything about it, and what it said was that they could hear a voice.

"Get the ladder," said a voice, a long way down and to the left, in the flat unhurried way of a man who has been underground a long time and is not surprised by anything. "And tell Hallam to bring the other can, we are running her dry."

Niko Venn did not move. He put the lamp up off the ground and held it above his head, and the light went a long way into the cut and lit a man twenty feet down, on a staging, with a lamp of his own, looking up at three people standing in the dark on a ledge with a coil of rope and a lantern on a nail that he had not noticed.

The man below shaded his eyes.

"Well," he said. "You will be the Water Office, and you will be the wrong week."
