# Chapter 0050 — What Is In The Record

The city did not get better on the Saturday. It got written down.

That is not nothing and it is not everything and Tarin Rusk, who had been in a lane for two months and had wanted a thing he could not name for most of it, was honest enough in the yard on the Saturday morning to say so out loud to about fifty people, and about eleven of them told him he was wrong, and both of those things were correct.

The yard cistern was at its coping, and the lane had six thousand seven hundred gallons of vessel in it and every vessel in the lane was full, and it was the same water. Yara Sen said so at the dispensary door for three hours on the Saturday morning, and said the derivation again, and said the three things about the instrument again. A girl of seven of the second step was at the fourth point on the twenty-seventh day and had not gone down. The district had six thousand seven hundred gallons of a contractor's discharge, eleven gallons of rain in a stone jar, and no municipal supply of any kind.

The notice on the hoarding at the arch said that a bounded supply had been obtained there on the afternoon of the fifteenth, that it was over, that the Assembly was not bound to repeat it, and that no date had been entered for a further supply and none would be until somebody had measured the thing that supplied it.

---

The return came out on the Saturday afternoon and Sluice Warden Pell read it out in the yard at about four o'clock with a hundred and forty people in it, because that is what he does with documents, including his own.

The scheduled rows on the lower terrace road were set at a reduced ration, cut by a fifth, and the reason it was a fifth and not a third was in a ruled line under the schedule: that a bounded supply had been obtained at the arch in the blue steps on the afternoon of the fifteenth of this month out of a works whose two condition certificates were read into the record of a hearing on the same day and have been referred to the registrar; that the source is not established; that the Assembly has entered the fact for the purpose of the ration and not as a supply; and that it will not describe a supply it cannot account for and will not refuse to account for one that a hundred and twenty-six people drank in public in front of a minute clerk.

It was Alderman Tarrant who set it, on the Saturday, in the same room, on the same paper, because a chair who had cancelled a review on Monday night was the only person in that city with the standing to put a number on a schedule that morning, and he did it in about twenty minutes and would have preferred not to, and he said so out loud, twice, in a yard on the terrace road. Nobody thanked him for it, because there was nothing to thank him for. He had not given that terrace a ration. He had found a way of not taking one away.

It is the first line in a hundred and forty years of returns in this city that says the city does not know where a thing came from and has written it down anyway. Marda Ollan read the figure out twice and did not put up a slate about it.

*A fifth off,* said a woman near the back, and nobody answered her, and she said it again later in the week to two people. The cost of the fifteen hours on the Friday afternoon went onto the rest of the city and not onto this lane, which is the only sentence anyone in this lane can honestly say about the sixteenth day of this month.

The district of one hundred and twenty-six persons at the lower terrace road did not appear in the return at all, because a return has one column for it and the column stands empty with a reason written under it in a fair hand, and an empty column in a return is read as a nil return, and a physician said in a street on the Wednesday of last week, in front of a chair of the Basin Assembly, that she would trade a figure she could not measure for a sentence somebody chose to write down, and a minute clerk read the sentence four times.

So the district got a window and a name in a finding and no ration, and the last page of the warden's return for that week, in the district book, in the book with four copies of it in this city, reads:

*row number:* **none held** *— tanks:* three tanks, thirty-eight pails in the yard, the yard cistern, the dispensary churn *— gallons:* *(empty)* *— last serviced:* *(empty)* *— keeper:* **keeper, none held**

Hanne Roeg read it standing in the yard on the Saturday evening because she wanted to read it, and then said one sentence and went inside, and the sentence was that she had spent six weeks telling that yard an empty box a hundred and forty people had looked at was worth more than a full one, and that on Monday morning it would be read in four buildings by a man who had never been in a yard, and that it would say exactly what it was meant to say, and that she was seventy-one and tired.

The petition of the twenty-second day of the month before was not decided and cannot be. A route record wants a named route, a documented source, a witness, and a date. The date passed and was accepted. The source is a chamber and a schedule reference, which is not a document. The witnesses are seven named people. One refused in writing before anybody had asked him anything, and a declination entered under standing order one hundred and fourteen cannot be used against a man in any office of this city. One of the seven cannot read, and asked in a yard that the third line of his own paper be said aloud when it was read, and it was, four times. And one of them gave this district a slate on the twenty-eighth of the month before, which is a thing a person can do and not a thing a form will take, because a slate is not a witness, and she said so in her own hand at the top of it, and she read it into a district examiner's record on the twelfth and will say so again.

The keeper box is the fifth box and it is empty on purpose. A district examiner read it on the twelfth and a minute clerk wrote it into a finding in a ruled margin, and a name in a ruled margin in four buildings is a name in a district book four miles away that says *keeper, none held*. Thirty years, and nobody has ever been able to fill that box without somebody else's hand on it.

---

A letter came up the cattle track on the Saturday from Vellum in a hand Tarin Rusk knew, and it was four lines. The register entry with the struck figure and the correction in the same hand had been read into the record of the hearing by name. The marginal note of a year out remained against it. She had filed a second document and told him first and was not going to be thanked for it. They did not agree about the reference in the fourteenth paragraph of her provenance note of the fourth of this month; neither had withdrawn anything; and condition five was satisfied, and had been satisfied on the ninth in a yard with a district book open.

There was nothing else in the letter. There was no warmth in it and there was not going to be any, and the five conditions made at a dispensary step in the spring were on the same terms they had been on the day they were made, and neither of them had moved an inch toward the other and both of them had put something in a public record that neither could get out again.

The acting water commissioner of Cresset sat in a house in the city on the sixteenth with a key the office would not be needing again, and gave a statement. It took four hours, it is eleven pages, and it is about a plate, a practice, and a day-book with a column for a reading and no column for anything else. It does not help the district, because it cannot: a statement about a plate is not a statement about a chamber, and nobody in this city is going to be able to use it to get a row onto a schedule.

The two certificates have gone to the registrar. He has not been charged with anything, he is not going to be tried, and he is not dead. He is the only evidence anybody has of what anybody intended, and Alderman Tarrant said out loud in a street at midnight why he was being kept rather than examined.

The office has no acting commissioner. The chair has asked for a list and there is no name on it that any person in the blue steps has ever heard of, and Tarin Rusk's name is not on it and could not be, and nobody in the lane has mentioned it and he did not mention it himself.

---

The things that cannot be got out of the record, on the morning of the Sunday, in a lane, he went through like a man reading a return.

His own name is in a transcript of a hearing of the Basin Assembly, four times, in four buildings, with a date on it; it is on a stone at the arch that came out of a plinth on the Friday so that a clerk could read the face of it; and it is in a return at a penny. There is no version of any of it that any person in this city can reach in and take out. That was the price of the eleven hours, and he had known it at about two o'clock on the Friday morning and had said it out loud in a street to nine hundred people and had gone on doing it. And he would do it again on Thursday, and he does not know how. That is the whole of what the street gave him and it is not the water. A route is a route and it needs a person standing next to it saying yes, and he has not got a method for finding one, and every single one he has got so far has come to him by accident, in a lane, where he happened to be standing with a bar.

There is a schedule of eleven names in an office file in this city, the youngest nine and the oldest sixty-one, in a finding that says *justified* and was made without authority. He told a chair on the twenty-fourth of the month before that one thing in that file is incomplete, and would not say what, because he would have to say a name. The second charge is no longer suspended and is not resolved, and neither is the first, and they have not been joined, and a third exists as a conversation in an office with still no sheet of it in anybody's hand.

There is a gate at Sallow that has read dry for six years against a schedule that says it is fed from a catchment that has never failed, and a man in the Guild who has held the reading card for eleven thousand four hundred leaves for six years and cannot put a number on it, and who offered a place, a wage below the one Tarin Rusk is not being paid, every record in the basin, and eleven weeks at a bench at Bexhill — the price being that the blue steps is not in the report. Nobody in this city broke that price. The form kept it, because a Guild return has a field for a supply district and there was nothing to put in it.


---

Niko Venn carried a lamp over a stop-gate for eleven minutes on the Friday afternoon and read figures out to a woman with a slate every ten minutes until three in the morning, and on the Sunday morning he was in a lane doing the same thing with a tank that would be at its coping for some time. He has five pence and no trade and a debt at a chandler's paid to the penny. He is on no list, there is no route to give him, nobody asked him for anything, and nobody thanked him. The four pebbles he cut on a hill this month are in a tin on a shelf in his aunt's old room and he has stopped trying to find out what they are for.

Wick Venn worked three days, on the thirteenth, the fourteenth and the fifteenth, at four shillings a day out of the Assembly's witness fund and not out of three pounds and sixteen, took twelve shillings, and refused a shilling of it offered as a bonus. His left hand is grazed eight inches from heel to thumb, has no feeling in the outer two fingers, cannot hold a bar, a pipe, a rail or a rope, and can tie a bowline with his knee on the line and the tail in his teeth. He said that in a lane on the Saturday morning to one person and to nobody else, and nobody thanked him and nobody will.

Sab Doulton, seven, of the second step, is at the fourth point on the twenty-seventh day. There is no figure in this basin for a child at the fourth stage, and Yara Sen's book has a column of her own invention headed *at risk* and there is no column for that.

And a letter came to the fourth step on the Saturday from a school in the fenner district, in the handwriting of a boy of ten, and it was four lines, and three of them were about a boy of nine at the top end of a lane and a pump that had been shut for two days, and the fourth of them was a question, and it was this:

*Granny — is the water in your yard the same water as the water that made the children ill. You never say. Mother says not to ask you.*

Hanne Roeg read it in the yard at about five o'clock with twenty people there, folded it in half, put it inside her shawl, and said out loud, so that the yard would have it:

"Answered in one line, and I have had it since the twelfth day of the month before and I have not written it down because I did not want it in a book. Yes. It is the same water, and it has been the same water since the seventh, and it is the same water in the tank at the arch tonight that it was in the tank on the seventh, and a physician in this lane has said so in a street in front of a chair of the Assembly and a clerk, and the clerk wrote it down and read it back four times.

"And there is a book in a city four miles away with a number in it, and my name at the top of the hand that wrote it, and my district in it by a reference, and a young woman put it there on purpose and told a man in a road first, and he did not stop her. I have read that and thought about it for a fortnight and I am not going to pretend to any of you here that I have not.

"And a boy of ten has asked me a question in his own hand, and I am going to write back and tell him the truth, because in about nine years he is going to come up these four steps and sit on them, and I would like there to be one thing in this lane's life he has been told before he had to ask anybody."

---

The Sunday morning was cold and clear and the rain had gone off the ground. Tarin Rusk walked out of the second gatehouse at ten past six with a watchman who walked him to the gate and no further, as the regulation requires, and went up the mill road on foot, because a plate had come out of the store at the top of those works on the Friday morning in front of a minute clerk and a district examiner and had not been put back, because it is a part and not a work. A clerk had written *one brass gauge plate, larger bore, removed from the store at the works, retained by A. Trevail, district examiner* into the record, and had read it back.

The rising main off the works stands full and still. It has done since the night of the second, and it is a main and not a channel, and it is charged, and it does not show a thing.

He put his thumb into the brass ring at the head of it, and the ring was bare, because the plate that had been sitting in it since somebody changed it in a works nobody wrote down went out of that store on the Friday morning in a bag with a clerk's label on it and is in a room in the city in a different coat. The bare ring read wet, and it had read wet on the seventh of the month before with a plate in it two sizes smaller, and there is not yet a form in this basin on which a man can write that down. Thirty yards off a chalk board in a contractor's hand read 5·9. Four miles away in four buildings there was a certificate in a deal box saying there was no standing water on that works at all.

The covered channel came out of the head of the main at the top of the arch and went down the terrace road, and it was dry, and it has been dry since the night of the second day of the month before, and he put his hand into it up to the wrist at ten to seven in the morning and it was cold and dusty and there was a leaf in it that had been there since the autumn, and the bare ring at the head of the main was three feet above a channel he could see was dry, and it was wet.

*There is no bore in that*, said Iven Marrow, in a store, on a hill, on the twenty-second day of the month before, about a certificate, to a man with a bar and a book. The man with the bar and the book had it on a slate in his own lane in a fortnight, and it was in a return, and it was in a circular, and it was in a transcript, and it was the truest sentence anybody said this month — and the man who said it was four miles away at Bexhill with a shed and three hundred men and a gate at Sallow that has been dry for six years, and nobody had had any way of telling him.

And then Tarin Rusk went down off the ridge to the arch and got the marker stone out from under the plinth of the low pump with a bar and his one good hand. It is a slab of limestone the size of a bread loaf with two faces. The old weathered face has a name in it weather-gone to nothing forty years ago, a date, the word of the day, and under the date a crooked mark cut by a man who said out loud that he was cutting it; and a white bruise a quarter of an inch across, two lines below where an engineer set a chisel on the seventh day of the month before and put two blows into it and cut nothing at all.

And above the bruise, in chalk, in a hand that was not his and that he did not recognise, somebody had written a second set of names and a date, in the rain, at some hour in the last three days, without asking him.

There were eleven of them and they were in two hands, and he did not know one of the hands, and he did not need to: the first was Nell Drust's, the second a small upright one he had last seen holding a slate up against a bench, the fourth a fifteen-year-old's he had met once on a staging on the fourth day of the month before, and the eleventh was his own name, written out in full, in the small upright hand, with the date under it.

He put the stone down in the mortar of the plinth with the blank face up, because the blank face is the one that faces the weather, and it has been blank since the seventh day of the month before, and the name on the other face of it went weather-gone about forty years ago. It is a piece of limestone out of a quarry that has a twin lying on a pending-disposal slip in a record house in Vellum, and the twin is a different stone, four miles and a day and a night of road away, and he has not seen it and is not going to go and see it. The blank face of the twin has been blank since a hundred and eighty-one years ago and has a white bruise on it a quarter of an inch across where a man he has never met put two blows into it with a chisel and cut nothing at all. That is the whole of the family resemblance between them, and it is the only inheritance anybody in this lane has ever been able to hand anybody.

He did not rub the chalk out, and he did not put his hand over it, and he did not go for the chisel. Ondra put a tin pot in his hand at the top of the arch because that is a thing done in that lane and has been since the Friday of the twenty-fifth of last month, and it had no taste in it, and he drank all of it and said nothing about it then or afterwards and is not going to.

Above him the run came down the wall and went into a yard with a full cistern in it and six thousand seven hundred gallons of vessel behind it, and that water was going to be the same water tomorrow, and the day after, and the day after that, for as long as there was a plant on a hill and a girl of seven at the second step and a man at the top of a lane who would draw a log in a minute and a half if it needed drawing. Nobody had promised a window. The notice said so and the notice was in four buildings, and a figure of sixty-eight gallons a day was standing in a column that was empty, and a minute clerk had read that back four times, and it was in a book, and there was no version of any of it that anybody in that city could reach in and take out.
