# Chapter 0047 — The Deal Box

The examination began at ten o'clock in the morning of Tuesday the twelfth day of this month at the door of the filter house on the works at Ashgate, and there were nine people on the level ground outside it, and of those nine, four had walked the lane in the lower terrace four miles and back, or had come up a cattle track since four in the morning.

Amos Trevail, district examiner, with a printed warrant in an oilskin and a form on a board. Oriel Fenn, minute clerk of the Basin Assembly, sixty-one, with a folding table of his own and a lamp and a bottle of ink. Sluice Warden Pell, who came up the mill road on his own two legs. Ilse Roan, of the registry, with a bundle tied twice. The acting commissioner, in person, with a key.

And at the bottom of the hill in the rain: Hanne Roeg, seventy-one, a shawl and a cough, who had walked two and a half miles uphill at half past seven and would walk it again at six in the evening, and who had said in a yard that she was seventy-one and did not make promises on steps in the dark and was not going to start on a hillside in the rain. Nell Drust, of Ashgate, who had not walked anywhere. Yara Sen, who came because a girl of seven in a lane four miles away is at the fourth point and has been for twenty-three days. Marda Ollan, with the keys and the ledger and the slate. And Tenna Sagg, fourteen, with a lamp.

Tarin Rusk was not at the door of the filter house. He was four hundred yards off, at the top of the adit, with a lamp of his own and a chainman off the terrace road, and he had been there since seven, because an examiner had asked for an engineer to attend and Tarin Rusk had written four words on a card and given them to the warden, and the four words were: *I will show the way.*

---

The store came open at eleven o'clock because a key had to come four miles from a house in the city where a man had decided on the Friday to have it cut, and the cutting of it had taken the whole of the Friday and the Saturday and had cost this office eleven shillings, and that figure is in the office's accounts and is the only number in the world that anybody in that room cared about more than the number in the box.

Two certificates, on board paper, in a deal box, in a locked store, thirty yards from a chalk board that reads 5·9.

Oriel Fenn read them out in the doorway of the store with a lamp on a pail because there was no light in there, and he read them out twice, once slowly, and the second time he read them the way you read a thing out when you want the room to hear the *words* rather than the sense.

> *Certificate of condition, works at Ashgate, level four. The chamber at the end of the drainage adit is certified void. There is no residual head. There is no standing water. No maintenance is required.*
> *Certificate of condition, works at Ashgate, level four, four years after the above. The chamber is void. No residual head. No standing water. Maintained.*

"Both in one hand," said Amos Trevail. "Mr Fenn, will you compare them in front of me and put down what you find."

"Both in one hand," said Oriel Fenn. "I am not a handwriting man and I am not going to pretend to be one. I can tell you that the second is the same hand as the first, because the way a man makes his *d* is the same in both, and because the *maintained* is under the second one in the same pen and a different nib."

"Are they in the hand of the acting commissioner?"

"They are in the hand of a man," said Oriel Fenn, "and I will read out the next four words of the form, and then the man himself will answer the question, and the man is standing four feet from me and has not said anything at all since he arrived."

---

Nobody asked the question. That is the thing about the room and it is the thing that made the room.

For about two minutes the only sound in it was rain on the tarpaulin over the contractor's bench outside, and in that two minutes the acting commissioner of the water and channels of this city looked at two pieces of paper in a deal box that he had produced himself out of a locked store with a key he had paid eleven shillings to have cut, in the presence of a district examiner, a minute clerk of the Basin Assembly, a sluice warden, and a woman from the registry, and he did not say one word about them.

Then he said:

"They are mine. I signed them both. They were made on my instruction and in the ordinary course of the discharge of that office, and I produced them because this office's line three of the fourth of this month said that it would, and it is the duty of the officer responsible for those records to produce them, and I would have produced them whether or not anybody had asked.

"Mr Trevail. You will want to know on what they rest. I will tell you now so that you do not have to ask me in front of a clerk at some later date when I have had a fortnight to think about it.

They rest on the gauge plate in that store and on the contractors' day-book beside it, and on the reading of the plate at the time they were made, and on a certificate of decommissioning in the same series made forty years before my time and in the same words, and on the practice of that office, which is that a chamber certified void is a chamber with no allocation standing to it. That practice is not a trick. It is the reason this office can say what is and is not a supply of this city. I have held this appointment three years and it is a temporary appointment and I have kept it by being able to explain the arithmetic of this basin to men who do not like it.

"And if the plate in that store is of a larger bore than the plate it replaced, then a reading taken from it is larger than a reading taken from the other, and a man paid by the can writes down what the plate says, and a man paid by the can is not going to be asked about a bore by anybody, least of all by me."

He looked at the board on the wall, thirty yards off, and then back at the room.

"I put that explanation in a circular of this office on the ninth, in my own hand, before it was asked for, because a Guild return was going to be in this Assembly's papers on the fourteenth, and a city that discovers on the fourteenth that its own office withheld two sheets for six years does not forgive a body of water. Better it discovers on the ninth, from me, with the documents in my hand. I have made a career of telling a city bad news early. It is the only form of honesty available to a man with a temporary appointment."

Amos Trevail asked the next question flatly, because he had a form in front of him with four questions on it and had been in the post eleven months and had learned that the fourth question is the one.

"Mr Commissioner. Does each of these two certificates state the day on which any measurement was made?"

"No."

"Does either name the person who made any measurement?"

"No."

"Does either refer to a plate, or to a bore, or to a method?"

"No."

"Mr Commissioner, I am required to minute the basis of a certificate of condition. Is there a document in this store, or in the office of this city, in which the measurement on which these two sheets rest is recorded, with its day and its method and the name of the person who took it?"

"There is a day-book in this store," said the acting commissioner, "and the day-book has a column for the reading and no column for anything else, because that is what a contractor's day-book is, and it has been kept by four different men in my time."

"Then the measurement is recorded in a book with no day and no method and no name," said Amos Trevail, "and I am going to minute that the certificate itself carries no date, no measurer, and no method, and that the document in which the figure is recorded carries no method and no measurer, and that both are correct statements about the state of this office's records, and that no person in this room has disputed that they are correct statements."

Nobody put it more plainly than that in a room, and nobody in that room would ever have to do so again.

---

The finding was made at four minutes past four in the afternoon, and it is four paragraphs long, and it is signed by Amos Trevail and countersigned by Oriel Fenn, and the third paragraph is the one that the whole of Volume 1 of this book turns on and it is not a dramatic sentence.

> *3. That the district of one hundred and twenty-six persons at the lower terrace road, of twenty-four houses, holds no row, no schedule entry and no keeper, and is entered in this finding as a place, and not as a supply district, because it has no supply district to be entered in; and that the return of the sluice warden for the district road of the twenty-second day of last month records the row number as* none held *and the keeper as* keeper, none held, *in a fair hand, in a book of which this city holds four copies; and that no person in this city has at any time entered a row for that district.*

There is no box on the form for the number of persons in a place. Trevail wrote it in a ruled margin and initialled the margin, and there is no form anywhere in this city on which a person can object to a sentence in a margin.

Nell Drust read the slate into the record at about half past two, standing in the doorway of the filter house store with the rain running off the tarpaulin behind her and thirty-four people who had walked up a mill road in the rain to stand in a yard at the top end of their own row, and Oriel Fenn read it back to her afterwards word for word and asked her whether it was hers.

"It is mine," she said, "and I dictated it and a girl of fourteen wrote it, and I put my own name at the bottom of it and not hers, and you have read it back to me and that is the first time in thirty years that a thing anybody has written about this row has been read back to this row by a man in a building.

"And I am going to say one thing into this record and I would like it written down in the words I use and not improved on.

There was a district four miles down a hill with a form coming, and they were the first people in thirty years who had ever been in a position to ask us for anything, and what they did with it was write down the question and then write down the answer themselves, and then on a Saturday by themselves they changed the answer and put the reason down. And I have been angry in this yard for a week about being in their paper and I am still angry, and I am going to be angry about it for years, and I am not going to pretend to any of you in this room that I have forgiven a thing because it turned out well.

"And they came up this hill on the twenty-eighth day of last month and told us what was in the paper before the paper was in an office. That is one day. That is worth having and it is worth writing down and it does not make up the other six weeks, and I am not going to stand in a doorway in the rain and pretend that it does."

---

Tarin Rusk was not asked anything.

The fourth line of the Office's own reply of the fourth of this month said that a district examination has no power to take evidence about a person, and Trevail read that line out at the start of the sitting and put it on the record in those words, and so the engineer was not examined, and did not offer to be, and when the chair asked whether the applicant wished to be heard, he said the thing that was on the card four words long, and the four words were *I will show the way*, and that is all he did for four hours and fifty minutes.

Yara Sen was heard at about half past three, and she was heard for nineteen minutes, and she gave the room sixty-eight gallons a day and the whole of the derivation, and said twice that the derivation had stopped, and said three times, unprompted, that there is no instrument in this basin that would test the water she was being asked about, and Oriel Fenn wrote all three of them down and read them back and she made him read the third one again.

And then Trevail asked her the question that nobody had thought to ask her, which was what the number was for.

"It is for two people," she said. "It is for the person who has to hand the water out, and it is for the person who has to be told how much there is. That is the whole of what a clinical figure is for. It is not evidence, it is not a return, and it is not a claim, and the reason I will not put it in a column is that the day I do, it stops being a number I made in a yard with a stick and becomes a number somebody is responsible for, and there is a child in a lane four miles away who is at the fourth point and there is no figure in this basin for a child at the fourth point, and I have said so four times and I will say it a fifth time if you want it in the record."

"It is in the record," said Oriel Fenn. "I have it three times."

---

They went down the two hundred and forty-one steps at about four with two lamps and a chainman, and Amos Trevail took the level himself, with a folding rule and a knuckle of string and a lump of chalk on a stick, and took it twice, and got nine feet four inches above the floor of the adit at its mouth both times, and the floor of the adit at its mouth is the floor inside the filter house door, and the ground outside that door is one foot nine inches above that floor, and the water therefore stands eleven feet one inch above the outside ground at the door of those works.

Oriel Fenn wrote the two readings and the two sums and the hour down, and read them back, and then read them against another figure that was on the same sheet, which Tarin Rusk had written on the fifteenth day of last month in a yard in front of a hundred and forty people and signed.

"Same figure," said Oriel Fenn. "Two instruments, two hands, three months, one number. I have never in nineteen years minuted that and I did not believe it was in the profession."

Nell Drust's well was timed in the doorway of her own kitchen at twenty past five, in front of a district examiner, a minute clerk of the Basin Assembly and a water physician, with her own pail on her own slab and her own watch on her own hand. Six gallons came up in five minutes and twenty seconds, and the clerk wrote it down, and then wrote down, because the physician asked him to, that there was grit in the bottom of the pail, that it had not been identified, and that no instrument was present.

The finding was signed at four minutes past four and it is the first document in six years to say that there is water at Ashgate. The second charge was thereby no longer suspended, the two charges were not joined, nothing in that room resolved either of them, and nobody in that room thought it had. The acting water commissioner of this city put his hat on and went down two hundred and forty-one steps on a bad leg and up a mill road in the rain, to a city where there were eleven hundred and some people on a schedule and a chair who had cancelled a review four days ago and did not yet know what he was going to do about the fifteenth.

At the door of the filter house, on the way out, he stopped and asked Amos Trevail one question, and it was the only question he asked all day that was not about a document.

"Mr Trevail. Friday. Is the branch going to run."

"Mr Commissioner, that is not a matter of this examination and I am not able to tell you."

"No," said the acting commissioner. "It was not a fair question. I have been in this office three years and I have asked a fair question about water about four thousand times and it is the only skill I have, and I have not got one left this afternoon." He got into the cart. "I shall be there at four o'clock on Friday, because I have not got anything else to do and because I signed two sheets, and if that is a foolish thing to do then I would be glad of somebody to say so." Nobody said so, and he went down the cattle track in the rain with a key in his pocket that this office would not be needing again.

Nobody said so, and he went down the cattle track in the rain with a key in his pocket that this office would not be needing again.
