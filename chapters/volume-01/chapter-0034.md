# Chapter 0034 — Proximate And Contributory

The classification came down on the cattle track on the morning of Monday the twenty-first, in an envelope with a works stamp on it and no warden's number on it, and it was on the contractors' paper and not the commissioner's, because a form is on the paper of whoever fills it in, and the man who filled this one in was the man whose job it was to fill it in.

Sluice Warden Pell read it out in the yard at about eleven, standing in the same place he had stood on Tuesday the fifteenth, which was not a decision and was noticed by about six people.

> *Return of supervision, works at Ashgate, twenty-first day of this month. To the district of the lower terrace road, and to the sluice warden of that road, and to such persons as the office may cause to be informed.*
>
> *Supply under report: a contractor's discharge, delivered by gravity from the works named, into a cistern in the district named, and thence to the households of that district.*
>
> *Immediate cause of the condition reported. The pumping plant at the works was suspended on the second day of this month on instruction. The plant was resumed at approximately twenty past three of the morning of the twentieth day of this month without instruction, and by the contractor, and the condition reported in the district is not attributable to any act of this office, and the office records that the office gave no such instruction and was not asked for one.*
>
> *Contributory cause. The district holds no drain and no outfall, its sole outfall having been sealed and plated twenty-six years ago; the discharge has been stored in a single open cistern in the district for a period of twenty days, that is to say for the whole of the ration period; the cistern has not been emptied, vented, or sampled by any person; and the households of the district have continued to draw from that cistern throughout.
>
> *Particulars. A pint of water from the cistern was boiled to a residue in a glazed jar on the twentieth day of this month by a water physician of the district, and the residue was salt. No sample from this cistern has at any time been submitted by this office to any assay, and no assay of any sample from this district has been made by this office within the period, and none was made by any other person in this basin.*

Pell read all of it, twice, at the pace he read things, and then he said the only thing he had ever said in a yard that was about himself.

"I am a warden and that is not my paper and I did not fill it in and I have not seen it before this morning, and I want that said in a yard before anybody says anything about it, because in nineteen years I have had a district hold a sheet against me once and the sheet turned out to be a return I had countersigned without reading, and I read everything."

---

Nobody shouted, and nobody shouted because nobody in that yard had anything to shout with. A man had said the word *resumed* about four times in his own mouth before eleven o'clock and the word was the whole of the afternoon.

Then Yara Sen put a slate on the low house step, which was the third slate that had been on that step in a month, and she did it before anybody asked her a question, because a physician who answers a question has already lost the ground.

"I am not going to call it a lie," she said. "I want that first, and I want it in a yard, and I have thought about it since eight o'clock this morning and I have not slept and I have not come to a different answer.

"The man who wrote that sheet is right that the pump was resumed without instruction. I know it was, because a man on a hill told one girl at eight o'clock on Saturday evening that he was going to do it, and told me in a yard this morning in a stone arch, and told the rest of that row nothing at all. The office gave no instruction and was not asked for one. That is true and I am not going to stand here and pretend that a true sentence in a form is a false one, because it is not, and because in about three weeks somebody is going to point at this page and say a physician said the page was a lie, and I will not have that in a book about me.

"And the office records that the plant was not running for the seventeen days before the twentieth, and that is also true. It is true in a way that is worse, and I am going to take about four minutes to say how, and then I will let anybody say anything they like. It has written twenty days of storage on the same page as seventeen days of a stopped plant, and both of those are correct, and they are not the same claim, and only one of them was ever measured by anybody."

She turned the slate round.

> *The tank lost a hundred and twenty gallons a day for seventeen days, and a hundred and twenty is what I can account for: seventy out of the door, fifty off the surface. Seventy goes to mouths. Fifty goes to the sun and takes nothing with it.*
> *And that is not the whole of what went out of that tank. Between the third and the fourteenth it stood at the lip and it was also being sold at sixpence the gallon to people from another district, and there is no keeper of it and no second book, and I cannot tell this yard how much of that cistern went out of the gate in a stranger's pail, and neither can anybody else in this lane.*
> *The salt does not leave. The salt is left. A pail takes it away out of the tank and the sun leaves it standing in it, and there is only one of those two that puts anything in a mouth.*
> *So for seventeen days this district drank a mixture that was getting stronger, and did not know it, and could not have known it, and had no instrument.*
> *On the sixteenth day of this month, in a yard, I said that in public, in these words, and I said the first water out of a main that had been standing seventeen days would be the worst of it, and I said both of those things before any of this was on any paper anywhere.*
> *It is not in this return. It is in a physician's book, in a yard, and in a slate at the bottom of a lane, and a return is a return.*

"Who is the person claiming to have caused it?" said Old Marden, from his step.

"Nobody is named," said Tarin Rusk. "Read the lines again. *By the contractor.* The return names the works, and the plant, and the hour, and it does not name a firm and it does not name a man, and the sentence *by the contractor* is a phrase and not a person. There is no name in that sheet that anybody in this lane could write on a slate and be answered for."

"Then it is a coward's paper," said Old Marden.

"It is a paper written by a man doing his job in the only words a form has," said Tarin Rusk. "And I would ask you not to say a worse thing about it than that in a yard where it will be repeated, because if a hundred and forty people in this lane say the office is lying, then the office will answer the lie and not the cistern, and the cistern is the thing with the salt in it."

---

Then Yara Sen did the thing she had been getting ready for since eight o'clock, and it was not an accusation, and it was the only weapon she had.

"There is one question in that sheet and it is in the last line and I have read it about nine times this morning, and it is the most useful sentence anybody in this city has written about this lane since the first of the month.

> *No sample from this cistern has at any time been submitted by this office to any assay, and no assay of any sample from this district has been made by this office within the period, and none was made by any other person in this basin.*

"That is a true sentence. It is entirely true. I do not doubt it for a moment and I cannot prove a false thing in it.

"And it says that the return which names the cause of the condition in this district was arrived at without a single measurement of the water in this district having been made by anybody. There is no assay of the cistern on the ninth of the month. There is no assay of the cistern on the sixteenth. There is a jar boiled on a step on the evening of the eighteenth and a jar boiled on a step this morning, and two white crusts, and no numbers on either of them, and I have said in this yard twice that I have not got an instrument that gives me a number, and I was not being modest, and I was telling the truth about a gap.

"So a form has named a proximate cause of a change in a body of water, and no person in this basin has measured the body of water once in the month it changed in.

"And I am not going to say the return is wrong. I am going to say it is unfinished, and I am going to put a figure next to its figure, because that is the only language it has."

She turned the slate over.

> *On the ninth day of this month I gave this yard a figure of the daily requirement of this district
> *On the sixteenth day a mother told me in this yard that one of those five has had cracks in her hands since the spring, of lime, and is not a case of this district.*
> *I took her out. The figure is therefore: four in the third stage at four gallons a head, which is sixteen; four in the second at two, which is eight; a hundred and eighteen at three pints, which is three hundred and fifty-four pints, which is forty-four and a quarter. Sixteen, eight, forty-four. Sixty-eight gallons a day.*
> *I have been reading the wrong number at the top of this yard for three weeks and I have been wrong by two gallons a day and the error has been mine and not the water's.*

Somebody said, "That is better."

"That is *lower*," said Yara Sen, "and it is worse, and I want both of those heard in the same minute, because a figure that goes *down* when you check it is a figure you check twice, and I have not checked this one twice in front of you, I have checked it twice in a book at midnight on Friday, and I would like somebody else to check it now, out loud, with a stick."

Marda Ollan did it on the step in about a minute and a quarter, twice, and got sixty-eight both times, and Hanne Roeg said the answer had been seventy since the ninth of the month in a yard and in a book and on a slate, and that nobody had said anything about a number getting smaller, and that she had been one of the people who had said nothing.

"I have a different arithmetic and I have had it for three weeks and I have not said it either," said Marda Ollan. "The tank stopped falling on the twentieth. It came up to the coping at about two in the afternoon and it has been at the coping since. The ledger has been the truest thing in this lane since the ninth and it is now wrong, and it is wrong because the water is coming in and the ground is taking it, and the ground is not in the ledger and there is no meter on the ground in the world."

"How much came in," said Tarin Rusk.

"Four thousand three hundred and fifty gallons between about twenty to five yesterday morning and two this afternoon, and it is not a rate you can put a figure on, because the tank was also giving seventy gallons a day to a dispensary and fifty gallons a day to the sun, and the interval is not measured, and the tank has a hole in the bottom of it a quarter of an inch deep with a floor under that standing in water since the thirtieth of the month.

"And I will put the cistern's own figure in a yard, because it is the only one anybody can check. Nine hundred and fifty on Tuesday the fourteenth. Out a hundred and twenty a day, and a hundred and twenty and not a hundred: seventy out of the door and fifty off the surface, and nothing coming in since the night of the second. That is eight hundred and thirty on the fifteenth, seven hundred and ten on the sixteenth, five hundred and ninety on the seventeenth, and four hundred and seventy on the eighteenth. Four hundred and seventy divided by seven and a half, and then by seventy-eight and a half square feet, is nine and a half inches on that coping. And then on the morning of the twentieth that tank came up to its coping, which is four thousand seven hundred, in about nine hours, and it has not been measured since, and I would not take a figure off it for a book.

"And I will say the other half of it, because I hold this book and I have never once written a sum in it that I could not finish. Those four thousand three hundred and fifty gallons are what came in on the morning of the twentieth. They are not what went out on the morning of the fourteenth, and a yard is going to hear both numbers in one minute and I would rather say so than have somebody else do it. Between the third and the fourteenth that tank lost three thousand seven hundred and fifty gallons. A hundred and twenty a day for twelve days is one thousand four hundred and forty. The other two thousand three hundred and ten went out of the gate of this yard in other people's pails at sixpence the gallon, and I have got Bel Crale's book for the first week of it and nothing at all for the rest, and nobody has ever kept a keeper's book of that cistern, and I did not know that was a thing a cistern needed until this month."

"Four thousand three hundred and fifty gallons in about nine hours is under five hundred gallons an hour," he said, "and I am not writing that in a book as a figure, because it is a difference between two readings taken by a boy with a chalk and a stick and it is true to within a bucket, and it is the only number in this district that is not in anybody's ledger."

He stood and looked at the yard for a moment with his right hand out, the way he had stood at the coping on the ninth.

"That is going into this yard through a hole eighteen inches across that half of it is silted," he said, "and it took me three weeks, a stopped branch, and a length of oak that a man planed in his own kitchen to put six hundred and ten gallons an hour into one pump and meter it, and I have been proud of that number for three weeks. This yard is taking four hundred and eighty gallons an hour through a hole I never looked at, in nine hours, with no keeper and no setting and no way of stopping it, and the only reason it is not in four feet of water in that yard is a plate of iron with four bolts on it that a city put there twenty-six years ago when it took this district off a sheet."

---

Yara Sen filed something on the twenty-first. It was two pages and it was in the only language available to her, which was the language of a form: a heading, a date, a signature, and a series of numbered lines, each of which was a thing a person could check and none of which was an opinion.

> *Statement of a water physician, twenty-first day of this month, in the matter of the condition of the water supply of a district of one hundred and twenty-six persons at the lower terrace road, and in the matter of a return of supervision of the twenty-first day of this month.*
> *1. I am the water physician for the district named and I have held that office for three years.
> *2. I have made no assay of the water in the cistern of that district, and no instrument in this basin would make one, and I have said so in a yard on the ninth, the sixteenth and the twentieth days of this month.*
> *3. On the twentieth day of this month, in a yard, I boiled one pint drawn from that cistern on the morning of the twentieth to a residue in a glazed jar, and on the eighteenth I boiled one pint drawn from the same cistern in the morning of the eighteenth. Both residues were salt. The residue of the twentieth was greater than that of the eighteenth. I can say that it is greater. I cannot say by how much, and I will not give a figure, and any person may repeat this test at the cost of a pint, a glazed jar, a ring and an hour.*
> *4. I did not make a comparison before the twentieth day of this month, and the return of the twenty-first rests on a comparison of the twentieth with an assumed earlier state. I do not know the earlier state to be as assumed. I have set out the arithmetic of what is known: for the seventeen days before the twentieth the cistern lost seventy gallons a day at the door of a dispensary and fifty gallons a day from an open surface, and I can account for no part of that cistern's loss beyond those two figures, because a tank that stands at its lip is also sold to strangers at a price set in another district and there is no keeper of it and there never has been one; the second of those figures removes water and leaves the dissolved remainder, the first removes water and the dissolved remainder together, and so does a pail. The mixture in that cistern on the sixteenth day was therefore not the mixture in it on the ninth day, and no measurement of either was made.*
> *5. I am not accusing any person of anything and I am not disputing that the plant was resumed without instruction. I dispute the word only, in one place. The return says that the condition reported is not attributable to any act of this office. I have read that sentence eleven times and I cannot make it mean a sentence that would be true. A cistern with no drain is a fact about a district and not an act of an office, and the office had a district book in which that cistern has been described since the first of this month, and the district asked for an examination on the twelfth and the examination has not been held.*
> *6. I request one thing, and I request it in the form in which a thing of this kind may be requested, and it is this: that any person who is about to record a cause for a change in a body of water in this district be required to state, in the same hand and in the same line, whether a measurement of that body of water was made, by whom, on what day, and by what method. A cause without a measurement is an account of somebody's day.*
> *Y. Sen, water physician, lower terrace road.*

She read it out in a yard, because a document nobody has heard read is half a document, and then she signed it on the step, and Marda Ollan wrote the date on the corner of it in her own hand, because Marda Ollan wrote dates.

"Does that go up with Pell's," said Bel Crale from the doorway.

"It goes wherever the other one goes," said Yara Sen. "I have written four hundred words on a piece of paper and I have got no instrument and no clerk and no keeper in the box, and the sum I gave this yard this morning is sixty-eight and not seventy, and I have got that wrong in public three times and corrected it in public once, and I would like somebody here to understand that correcting it has made the district look better, and that is the most frightening thing I have found in a book in three years."

The tank was at the coping and the low room had a hand's depth in it, and a form in a city four miles away said that the cause was a plant and an hour and no man at all, and a physician had answered it with two pages, a jar, and a corrected sum of sixty-eight gallons a day, and both of those sheets were now going up the cattle track in the same bag.
