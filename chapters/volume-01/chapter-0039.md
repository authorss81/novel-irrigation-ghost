# Chapter 0039 — The Door He Did Not Knock On

Ondra had put him in the room over the back stair on the Friday evening without being asked and without telling anybody, and had left a pail of hot water and a blanket on the bed and gone back down to the chair, and Tarin Rusk found out about it by the water.

He had nowhere to be, and had had for five days. Two charges that have not been joined, a hearing suspended until somebody examined a chamber, a tank in a yard at its coping with a hand's depth of grey water in the lowest room of a house at the left of the arch, and a bed in an empty cistern house inside the second gatehouse with a watchman required to walk him to the gate at dawn. He had walked up the lane at about six in the evening because a man with a bar and a book and no commission in this city has two places to be on an evening, and one of them is a step and the other is worse, and the low house was the one with the step.

He got the blue book out at about eleven and put it on his knee and went through it, which he had done every night since the eighth, and which had never once done him any good.

> *Chamber, level four, works at Ashgate. 9 ft 4 in above the floor of the adit at its mouth; that floor is the floor inside the filter house door; outside ground at that door 1 ft 9 in above it. 11 ft 1 in above the outside ground at the door. Unchanged since the seventh. A line cut in the rock at the level, an inch deep, a foot long.*
> *Well at the row, 8th: 6 gal in 3 min 30 sec = 103 gal/hr, twice, never tested, no instrument in this basin that would test it. Well at the row, 20th: 6 gal in 5 min 20 sec = 68 gal/hr, and grit in the bottom of the pail.*
> *Two certificates in a deal box in the store at the works, in the acting commissioner's hand, certifying the chamber void at decommissioning and four years ago, with *maintained* under the second. Thirty yards from a board reading 5·9.*
> *Return of supervision, 21st. Immediate cause, a plant resumed at 20:20 on the twentieth without instruction. No man named. No measurement named.*
> *Physician's statement, 21st. Two pages, a jar, and a corrected sum: 4 at 4 gal = 16, 4 at 2 gal = 8, 118 at 3 pints = 44. Sixty-eight a day, and it was seventy for three weeks and it was mine.*
> *Guild team at the works, 22nd to the 24th. Their report names the works. It does not name the district. Offered on the 23rd: a place, a wage below mine, the counting-house, a bench at Bexhill. Price: the blue steps is not in the report. Refused.*

He read it and shut it and sat in the dark with his hands on the covers, and the thing that came up out of him at half past one in the morning on the Saturday was the one he had been not-thinking since Thursday.

There are a hundred and twenty-six people in this lane, a tank at the coping holding four thousand seven hundred gallons that is not fit to drink, and a pump at the top of the lane with a stone cistern and a queue of ninety people going to it every morning since the twentieth. The man who keeps that pump has two hands, a boy of nine, and a white scar down his forearm from a cistern lid in 118, and he said no to me in front of eighty people and a warden on the twentieth of the month before, and he was entirely right to.

And the water is coming off the hill at about three thousand gallons an hour and it has nowhere to go except the plate.

He went down to the arch at two in the morning with a lamp he did not light, because he did not need it, because he had walked that lane since he was six.

The branch was a hand's width across behind a wall that had been built a foot in front of the channel in some point in the last century, and it had been dug out to bare stone on the sixth of the month before by two men with a bar, and it was running.

He got his right forearm into it to the elbow.

The water went past his arm the way water goes past an arm, at the rate of something being let through and not something being allowed, and the bed under it was cutting, the way it had cut an inch deep in four minutes on the thirtieth day of the last month, before it had gone smooth twice, once in a doorway at dawn with one man and a stone and once in a yard with a warden and a crowd and a man walking out of it.

He stood in a channel with a head of five point eight nine metres behind it and worked out what he was doing there, and it took him about a minute.

The seventh, in a doorway, at dawn, one man and a stone. The twentieth, in a yard, with a warden and a crowd and a man walking out. The thirtieth, in this channel, at four in the morning, the bed cut and a floor up, and it is the third of those that has gone into a family of five and I have never been able to say which.

And to make any of that a route and not a hole, a man needs three things, and he has had them twice in his life, and both times somebody else stood on the stone.

---

He went up the lane at half past three in the morning with no rope in his hand and no bar on his shoulder and no authority in his coat, and he stopped at the bottom of the four steps and did not knock, and Hanne Roeg was on the top one with a shawl over her shoulders and a lamp beside her, because she had been up since one in the morning and because the low room on the left of the arch had water in it again.

"You have not knocked," she said.

"No."

"I told you, on the first day, in a doorway, that I would do something about a man coming up these steps in the dark, and I have done it, and here I am, and you are standing at the bottom of them at half past three in the morning with nothing in your hands, and I would like you to say what you have come for, and then I would like you to go away, because it is cold and I have a cough and I am not going to have this in my chest for a fortnight on top of everything else."

"I have not come to ask you a question," said Tarin Rusk.

"Then you have come for nothing."

"Yes."

That was the whole of the exchange for about a minute and a half, and she let it sit, and he let it sit, and the lamp on the step made a small ring on the flags and the lane was as quiet as a lane can be with a tank at its coping and a floor coming up in it.

"There is a channel at the arch with a head on it," he said, "and it has no keeper and no witness and no record, and there is one clean account of the night of the breach within two miles of that arch, and it is yours, and I have come up four steps at half past three in the morning to tell you that I am not going to ask you for it."

"You have come to tell me you are not going to ask."

"Yes."

"And you want me to notice that you have come to tell me."

"Yes."

"Then I will notice it," said Hanne Roeg, "and I will tell you what I notice, which is that a man who has come to say he will not take a thing is still a man who has come, and it took him the same two miles, and I am not going to give you the credit of thinking that was a different sort of coming. But I will notice it, because you have come to tell me and not to ask, and nobody has ever done that on these steps."

She put her hand out flat on the stone of the step, the way she had on the fourth, and she did not get up.

"And I am going to answer you, because you have not asked me a question and a woman of seventy-one at four in the morning can do as she likes with a man who has not asked her one. So: yes. I remember it. I was forty-five, and I was the oldest of the four signers, and I stood in a doorway in the wet and read a list of names out loud and a man copied them and a man struck them, and the whole of it took about an hour. And I have not said the word *breach* out loud in twenty-six years, and I have said it once to you tonight, and I would like to be left alone about that.

"And if you had asked me I would have said yes. I am telling you that so that you do not spend the rest of the week telling yourself that you did the right thing, because you did not do the right thing. You did the careful thing, and the careful thing and the right thing are not the same, and I have watched men in this lane confuse them for thirty years, and the ones who confused them were always the ones who had a bar over their shoulder while they did it.

"And I am not going to be relieved about it, and I want you to go down those steps with that in your pocket, because what I am going to tell you now is the reason, and the reason is not pride and it is not stubbornness and it is not a sulk, and you would know a sulk when you got one."

"And I have not given it to anybody. Not to a conservator, who came down a hill in a warrant with a good lamp. Not to a warden, who came down a track with a form. Not to a boy of twenty-two who cut the fifth of his own pebbles in a hole in the ground. Not to my own grandson, and he is ten, and he is at a school in the fenner district, and I have not told him and I have not written it down.

"There are a hundred and seventeen people in this lane who were not born and nine who were, and nine of them were in that doorway in the wet. And there is a boy in a chair at the low house who is eleven and cannot walk, because a floor came up under him on the thirtieth of this month in a lane that has a plate over its only drain, and when he is thirty he is going to say *what happened that night, ask the old woman*, and he is going to come up these four steps, and I am going to be here, and that is the only thing I have got that a piece of paper does not have.

"And here is the other thing and then you will go.

"You are not the first. There have been three men in thirty years who wanted something out of this lane, and every one of them wanted it for a good reason, and the reason was always good, and that is how it works. Good reasons are how a place gets emptied out. I have watched it twice. A road and a chapel and a whole hamlet in a wet year, for a good reason.

"So go down the steps. And I am not going to say I will do it another time, and I am not going to say I will not, because that would be a promise, and I am seventy-one, and I do not make promises on steps in the dark.

"And I will say the last thing, and then I am going to take my lamp in.

"You did not knock. That is on you and I am not going to take it off you, and I am not going to give you anything for it either, because you did not knock at half past three in the morning on the twenty-fifth of this month, and in about nine years somebody who has not been born yet is going to ask me what you did that night, and what I am going to say is *he stood at the bottom of my steps at four in the morning and he did not knock*, and that will be the whole of what there is, and it will have to be enough."

---

He went down the steps.

He stood in the yard for about ten minutes and then went back down to the branch with a knuckle of string and a chalk, because a man who has stood at the bottom of four steps at half past three in the morning can still measure.

He tied the string across the branch a foot above the bed, marked ten feet down it, dropped a chip of limestone into the water, and timed it on his own pulse.

Ten feet in seven seconds, which is one and three sevenths of a foot a second. The channel is a hand wide and about three inches deep at the string, which is a twelfth of a square foot, and one and three sevenths of a twelfth is a little under an eighth of a cubic foot a second, and a cubic foot of water is seven and a half gallons, so that is about fifty-three gallons a minute, and a minute is a sixtieth part of an hour, and the number at the end of it is about three thousand two hundred gallons an hour.

He wrote it on the back of a ward notice and looked at it.

> *Branch, arch, 03:40, Saturday. String across at 1 ft above bed. 10 ft run. 7 sec. About 3,200 gal/hr.*
> *The gauged oak with the pin at the setting Roke found on the nineteenth puts 610 gal/hr through this same channel. The log is in my pack and it is not in the gate and I have not put it in.*
> *So I can tell you what is in the branch. I cannot tell you what is not in it. There is no stop-log in the run and there never has been and there is no place in four hundred yards to put one, and the run came off a shut gate with five point eight nine metres behind it on the first day of this month and nobody in this city has ever measured it, and I am not going to measure it tonight in the dark with a string and a piece of limestone.*

The water went on.

He was still in it at twenty past five when Niko Venn came down the lane with a lamp he had not lit either, and neither of them said anything about that, and they stood in a channel together for forty minutes because a head of water going into a closed district is a job and there is a job.

And at about ten past six the tank in the yard stopped rising.

That was how they knew. The ground had come up to meet it. There is a shaft under the plate and it has been full for thirty years, and on the thirtieth of the last month it took the whole of the lane's water and then let four hundred and seventy gallons of it come up through a floor, and on the Saturday it did it again, and the water in the branch began to get thicker and the bed under it began to take silt up out of the ground instead of putting it down. A hand's width, three thousand two hundred gallons an hour, and a hundred and twenty-six people standing on top of a body of water that had nowhere to go.

---

Tam Roke came out at about seven.

He had been up since four, which he did every morning now, and he had a bucket and a dipper and his right hand and a look on his face. The tank at the top of the lane was full to its kerb and had a board jammed in the branch above it, because the branch had come back to life on the Sunday night when the run came back, and the queue had been at that pump since six.

"I have shut it," he said, to Tarin Rusk, from about ten feet away, before anybody had said anything to him.

"You have not got to—"

"I have shut it and I am going to keep it shut and I am going to tell you why, and I am telling you in the road and not in front of a warden because a warden would make it a thing and it is not a thing, it is a pump.

"On the seventh of the month before you put water into that tank for about two hours and the bed went smooth, and I stood on my own step and watched it happen, and you had a stone and your hand on it.

"It has been running since Sunday night with a board in it. Yesterday afternoon I put my hand in that channel, which is a thing I can do, and I felt the bed. It is a quarter of an inch down in six hours and it will not stop while that much is coming. And the water going into that tank is five times what you told me you were going to put in it, and I am not going to stand in this road and pretend you did anything wrong, because you have not been near it since Tuesday and you have been in a room with a barrow in it all week.

"But there is a boy of mine at the top end drinking out of that tank, and a man in a chair in the low house who cannot walk, and a queue down that lane that turns, and I am the man on the pump and I cannot see the bottom of the channel that is filling it, and I have nineteen years of laying pipe in a hand that I have never once been asked to look at the bottom of anything. And I am not going to run a pump off a bed I cannot see with a tank of nine-year-olds drinking out of it.

"Not for a day. Not for two days. Not for you with a bar and a book, and not for a physician, and not in November."

There were about forty people in the lane behind him who had come for that pump since six, and they were standing there watching the man on the pump tell a man with a blue book that he would not take it, and not one of them said anything, and it was the third time in five weeks that somebody in that lane had said no to Tarin Rusk out loud in front of other people, and the third time was the only one of the three where every one of the people who heard it was standing there about their own business.

---

Ondra came out at about nine with a lamp, and put it on the step, and then went and got the tin pot and filled it at the butt, and put a new length of string on it, and put the chalk in a tin so it would not go to dust, and oiled the folding rule, and set it on the wall.

None of that had been asked of him. None of it would ever be mentioned by anybody in this lane.

"You went up the steps in the night," he said.

"Yes."

"You came back down them."

"Yes."

"I have not slept," said Ondra, "and I was awake, and I did not hear you knock."

Tarin Rusk stood in the road with a pack with a planed length of oak in it and a string in his pocket.

"No," he said. "I did not knock."

"No," said Ondra. "You did not."

He looked at the ground for a while.

"Right," he said. "I am going to say two things and then I am going to open the low house and get the flags up, because they will be under water again before the end of the week and there is nothing else for anybody to do.

"The first is that I have had four men through that door this month and three of them wanted something. One came with a rope on the first and I would have had him in my house till the sun came up, and that is the same man who took my door frame off its hinges and stood it in my yard, and I have not forgiven him and I am not going to, and it is Wick Venn, and it does not make us even. One came on the fifth with a lamp and he was right to be on my hill. And the one that went up at half past three this morning and did not knock came down at ten past four in a lane where I have not slept since the thirtieth of the month before, and he went away again with nothing.

"And the second is the one I have been turning over since six o'clock this morning with a lamp in my hand instead of a bar, and I am going to say it plainly because I have not got the way round it and I am fifty-one.

"I have never once in my life been able to keep a thing. Not a job, not a rent, not a boy, not a door frame, not my father's tools when he died. Everything I have ever had has gone out of that door and gone down that lane, and I have made my peace with that about four times and it has not once been any use.

"And you went up those steps at four in the morning for the one thing out of that door that would not have gone. And you did not ask. And I am telling you, in this road, that you were right, and I know it because of the other three men and the door frame: a man who takes it is a man who has it, and then it is gone, and then he has not got it either, and by the time he finds that out there is nobody in the room to ask.

"I do not know what it was. You have not told me and I am not going to ask you, and I want you to hear me say that so that you know it was on offer and you left it, because I do not think you will get another night like this one and I would rather you knew where it went.

"That is all. Get your breakfast. I am going to open the low house."

He went up the step and went in, and the boy was in the chair by the window in the front room with a blanket over his knees and the light on his face, and Ondra picked him up without being asked and carried him four feet to the good side of the room and put him down, and it was the two-hundred-and-fiftieth time he had done that in twenty days,

The marker stone was still under the plinth of the low pump with one face cut on it and the other face blank, where it has been since the seventh of the month before, and he did not touch it that night.

The pump at the top of the lane was shut, and it would be shut on the Sunday and on the Monday, and the man who had shut it had been entirely right, and the only thing that had happened on the night of the twenty-fifth of the month to any of them was that a man went up four steps at half past three in the morning and did not knock.
