# Volume 01: The Empty Gauge

Chapter range: 1–50

## Central pressure

Cresset is three weeks from the midsummer ration review, the city has had no real rain for fourteen months, and the Assembly's Standing Ration is the only thing keeping the lower terrace alive. The Water Office's own records say the Ashgate Reservoir has been empty for six years. When Tarin Rusk opens a seized inspection gate at the abandoned Ashgate works and pressurized water runs *uphill* through a dry stone channel, the city has to decide whether its worst shortage is a lie it has been maintaining or a route nobody is allowed to name.

This volume answers its own question. The water is real, the reservoir is real, and a bounded service window can be won in public. What the volume cannot answer is where the flow comes from, why the same channel is dry upstream, or why a district full of living people has no name.

## Starting state

- **Cresset:** Under Standing Ration. Cistern rows measured in days, not weeks. Public fountains on a posted schedule. The Assembly is short of money and impatient.
- **Tarin:** Stage 0, Wrench and rule. Thirty-three, senior flow engineer, four years in the lower terrace district. Nobody in the Office doubts he can read a gauge; nobody has ever asked him what he thinks the water *means*.
- **Mara Quill:** Provenance conservator at the Municipal Memory Office, three weeks into a scheduled check of the Ashgate maintenance volume. She is not looking for a conspiracy. She is looking at a page weight that does not add up.
- **The gap between them:** Tarin knows a district on the lower terrace that no map names. Mara knows a file that has been altered. Neither has told the other anything, and each assumes the other is the source of the problem.

## Starting relationships

- **Tarin / Mara:** Professional suspicion, no personal contact. Mara has flagged the Ashgate volume; Tarin has been asked, politely, to stop requesting it.
- **Tarin / Niko Venn:** Mentor and apprentice. Niko is twenty-two, quick, and useful in a way that irritates Tarin because Niko moves through the terrace by song and habit where Tarin needs a map.
- **Tarin / Yara Sen:** Professional and cordial. She signs off his exposure paperwork and argues with him about what counts as an acceptable risk to a patient.
- **Tarin / Halden Roe:** Employer. Roe has promoted Tarin's competence and resented it for years.
- **Tarin / Orrin Sedge:** Distant colleague, downstream at the Meltwater Verge, the only person who returns Tarin's letters promptly.
- **Glassstep residents:** People Tarin grew up among, none of whom know he is an engineer now, all of whom are absent from every document he has ever signed.

## Starting power level

Stage 0, **Wrench and rule**. Tarin can survey, calculate, repair, and command a crew. He has no reliable perception of a wet line, and the Gauge has never appeared. He must be competent on the page before anything supernatural is allowed to happen to him.

## Major locations

- The **Ashgate inspection works** — a decommissioned intake and pressure gallery, three hours' walk above the city, officially sealed for six years.
- The **lower terrace** and its cistern rows, the poor-district service fountains, and the pump house Tarin rents above.
- **Glassstep** — populated, lived-in, and absent from every official map. Blue doors, hand pumps, a row of cisterns that nobody can find on a plan.
- The **Municipal Memory Office** in Vellum — card catalogue, provenance benches, restricted stacks.
- The **sluice warden's station** on the terrace road, where service windows are posted and disputes are heard.

## Major factions

- **Cresset Water Office:** Underfunded, over-worked, and quietly ashamed of its own records. Tarin's employer. Contains reformers and fatalists in roughly equal measure.
- **The Office of the Acting Commissioner (Halden Roe):** Controls inspection, promotion, and who gets blamed. Roe's authority is a temporary appointment he is actively trying to make permanent.
- **Open Channel Guild:** Sluicewrights and contractors. Sends an inspection team in Movement IV because someone above them wants Ashgate looked at by people who can be relied on to find nothing.
- **Municipal Memory Office:** Mara's institution, competent and frightened, caught between its own supervisor's rule about unverified records and a Quiet Archive practice of pulling contested volumes.
- **Glassstep residents:** Not a faction in anyone's document. The most organized group in the volume anyway.
- **The Basin Assembly:** Distant, budget-minded, and about to become the most important room in Cresset.

## Escalation sequence

### Movement I — Rain on a Dry Channel (Ch 1–10)

Tarin recalibrates a decommissioned intake gauge. The gate is seized; he forces it, and residual pressure sends a stream uphill through a channel that has been dry for six years. He does not create the water and the volume must never suggest he did.

Walking the lower terrace afterward, he finds a district of occupied houses, lit windows, and working hand pumps that appears on no plan, warden's map, or Office record. He grew up there. Nobody there is a ghost.

He opens a narrow blind branch to Glassstep's low pump. The opening needs a physical trace, a willing witness, and a record: a resident signs as witness, and Tarin re-cuts a record stone himself with a borrowed chisel. The pump runs for a bounded window. The water is crosscurrent-tainted; Yara Sen treats three children with early salt fever, and Tarin pays the first name debt — a practiced hand motion he has used for twenty years is simply gone, and his bitter tea tastes of nothing.

Mara, independently, finds that the Ashgate maintenance volume has a replaced leaf. Halden Roe's men convert Tarin's own calibration log into the evidence that he is the man who altered it. At the end of the movement, in front of a crowd at the warden's station, Tarin names the branch aloud as a witness act. The water answers for eleven hours. The accusation becomes formal the same afternoon.

### Movement II — The Replaced Leaf (Ch 11–20)

Tarin is stood down from the lower terrace. The flow stops almost at once, which proves the point he has been avoiding: the route is not his property and it does not belong to him because he opened it.

Mara is instructed to close the file. Her supervisor invokes the rule that an unverified memory must never decide a public route. She builds provenance anyway, in her own time, and finds that the substituted ink is court-supply ink from a specific year. She also finds a notation in a hand she learned at her mother's kitchen table.

Niko, mapping the terrace by movement and song, describes a descent to the Ashgate works that no survey records and that he insists his father used to run. Halden offers Tarin a quiet transfer to a district that has no problems. Tarin refuses, badly, and the refusal costs him a reference he will need later.

The movement turns when Mara and Tarin, forced into the same room, discover that the Ashgate log's redaction and the Glassstep survey redactions use the same substitution pattern — the same hand, the same year, the same court ink.

### Movement III — Under the Terrace (Ch 21–30)

The midpoint. Tarin, Niko, and a small crew descend into the Ashgate inspection works along Niko's route.

The reversal is physical, not political: **Ashgate is not an empty ruin.** The reservoir is intact. It sits *beneath a newer settlement* — a district built on top of it after the official closure, its residents living above a pressurized void they have never been told about. Its water is not drained; it is being pushed sideways into a pressure chamber that appears on no public survey at all.

The ghost's voice is clarified in the same descent. It is a crowd, not a woman. Tarin hears a dozen voices where he expected his mother's, and none of them will tell him anything useful. This is the volume's central promise of a collective ghost, established early and without revelation.

The movement closes on cost. The descent damages a party member's ability to work, the return route is unstable, and the settlement above learns that its houses stand on a chamber nobody has surveyed. Halden's falsified measurements are placed in context: he is concealing illegal contractor pumping through a dead reservoir, not operating an ancient machine. He is the volume's villain and he is no more than that.

### Movement IV — The Pressure Chamber (Ch 31–40)

A partially opened blind route produces a brine crosscurrent through the lower terrace. Yara's clinic is the battlefield, and the sickness is genuinely physical, not a metaphor. The Office attempts to classify it as contractor negligence.

A Guild inspection team arrives. Iven Marrow offers Tarin work, access, and the beginning of a real education in what the mountain gates actually are — in exchange for leaving Glassstep out of the record. Tarin does not accept, and Marrow does not press, because he is a patient man.

Tarin's old flood closure — the one that saved the riverside hospital and trapped people in a lower courtyard — is raised against him at an internal hearing as evidence that he cannot be trusted with a route. Mara testifies about his competence and is asked, by Tarin's own advocate, whether his judgment is sound. She answers honestly and it costs him.

The movement's real turn is a refusal. Desperate for a clean anchor, Tarin considers using a Glassstep resident's private memory of the breach. He does not. The route splits, the pressure is lost, and Glassstep's pump fails for two days while the family that hosted him keeps his tools fed and tells him plainly that he was right not to use it. The refusal costs the volume its convenient solution and buys the trust the ending will require.

### Movement V — One Name, One Window (Ch 41–50)

The climax is political before it is hydrological. The midsummer ration review collapses. Halden releases the falsified Ashgate figures publicly and offers Tarin a confession in exchange for the lower terrace keeping its ration — the clean official story, which is a lie that keeps people alive on paper.

The only real water in the district is the uphill branch. Tarin takes the route instead. He does it in public, before the Assembly, with named witnesses from Glassstep who are free to refuse and one of whom does refuse, which is what makes the rest of it credible. The service window is twelve hours, bounded, and announced as temporary.

Halden's falsified Ashgate measurements fall apart in the same proceeding, because the water that answers the Gauge is in a reservoir his own ledger declares empty. He is removed from the acting commissioner's office and becomes a cooperating witness rather than a dead man.

## Volume climax

Chapters 46–49. Tarin stands before the Basin Assembly and names the branch, publicly, with a witness who is allowed to walk away. The water arrives for a bounded window and Glassstep's pump runs in front of the whole city. The same hearing dissolves Halden Roe, whose falsified measurements cannot survive a gauge that reads wet. The cost is immediate and permanent: Tarin's name is now tied to the route in the record, the city knows what it is, and the whole basin can read the transcript tomorrow.

## Concrete resolution

- Cresset's lower terrace receives a bounded service window and survives the midsummer review at a reduced ration rather than at zero.
- The Water Office formally acknowledges, in a public record, that Ashgate Reservoir physically exists. It does not acknowledge who diverted it.
- Halden Roe is removed from the acting commissioner's office, retains his knowledge as a witness, and is never redeemed.
- Tarin reaches **Trace**, with one incomplete and unrepeatable use of **Name**. He cannot re-run the opening on his own; it requires another witness and he does not yet have a method for finding one.
- Tarin and Mara agree to share evidence and continue to distrust each other's methods.
- Glassstep has water, a signed witness, and no legal existence whatsoever.

## Rules this volume must not break

- Tarin never creates water. Every restored supply is residual pressure, groundwater, an existing channel, or the rain that opens the volume.
- The first Gauge statement is incomplete and slightly wrong. It is not a tutorial.
- The ghost is many-voiced from Chapter 24 onward. It is never Lira, and it never becomes an advisor.
- Niko and the Glassstep residents are competent at their own practice. Tarin is not the only person who can move water, and in Movement IV a local keeper is better than he is.
- Halden Roe is a liar with a motive, not a mage. He never uses magic and never learns to.
- The drought is not cured. The volume ends a season, not the drought.
- No character is compelled to forget. Every name-debt payment in this volume is a choice or a documented accident.

## New question for the next volume

The upland Guild reports that the high Sallow gate has been dry for six years, while a village two days west reports water moving uphill under a channel that the Guild's own engineers say is solid. The uphill water did not come from Ashgate. Something upstream is running a second, contradictory copy of the same route.

## Final chapter image

At dawn, Tarin stands at the Ashgate intake with a brass gauge that reads a wet line in a channel he can see is dry. On the record stone beside it, someone has chalked a second set of names under his own, in a hand he does not recognize, without asking him first.
