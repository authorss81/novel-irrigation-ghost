## Batch goal

Open Volume 2 by putting the protagonist on the ground in a second place with a different set of people, and establish four things on the page rather than in a state file:

1. **The village half of Volume 1's closing question is now spoken aloud by somebody other than a Guild man**, by a woman of forty-one with a plank and a pail and a book her mother kept, and the water is real and substantial and has been carrying for six years.
2. **The name is refused.** A route assembly, in a room with the doors open, in daylight, refuses an outsider's route name, in a hand that is not the engineer's, because the word already means two incompatible things. **This is the batch's central beat and it is Chapter 56.**
3. **Tarin goes west as a working man at a stated wage and is shown to be the worst man in a shed.** He can read a head and he cannot scrape a gate bed, and a crew of four tells him so and then trusts him for one stated reason.
4. **Two people who have never met measure the same water from opposite ends and get numbers that are six and a half times apart, and the chapter that shows the gap does not close it.**

**Nothing is opened. Nothing is named. No window is won. No route is moved. One Gauge panel, in Chapter 57, and it is the fourth in the series.**

## Chapter cards

### Chapter 0051 — A Notice With Two Boxes Filled
**POV and location:** Tarin. The blue steps and the sluice warden's station on the terrace road. The 18th and 19th.
**Immediate goal:** Decide whether the thing Wick Venn has come back up the hill to tell him about is a job.
**Resistance:** The notice is a form with five boxes and only two of them are filled. The district cannot pay him. A man with two live charges is a risk to an employer, and the notice names a reach and not a place. He has three pounds sixteen shillings' worth of nothing and a bad shoulder and a room he is given.
**Major turn:** He reads the empty boxes instead of the wage, and asks for the notice to be amended in two places — *no keeper* and *no account of the plant* — and he says out loud that he will not be the keeper of a route he has not opened, and that is the first time in six weeks he has said a rule and meant the whole of it.
**Emotional or practical consequence:** He goes down to the warden's station and has the amendment entered in the district book in a fair hand, in a district that is on no schedule, because that is the only public book in Cresset that will take a word about a place with no row. Niko reads the amendment to Ondra twice. Wick says out loud in a yard that he is recommending a man who got him three days' work and that he is not to be thanked for it.
**Required continuity facts:** 6,700 gallons in the lane; the ration cut by a fifth; the keeper box still reading *keeper, none held*; two charges live and not joined; the third with no sheet; tea with no taste in it; the two-finger press gone.
**Ending hook or completed beat:** The leave form. One line for the district of residence. He writes *none held*, and reads the sentence twice, and it is the same two words that are in the empty keeper box in four buildings. Completed beat: he posts it on the Saturday and cannot post it, because the registry has no acting commissioner and the last determination in it is a vacancy.

### Chapter 0052 — Five Shillings And A Cottage
**POV and location:** Tarin, Wick, Perdie Ammid by letter, Iven Marrow by form. A yard in the blue steps and the Guild shed at Bexhill. The 20th.
**Immediate goal:** Establish what five shillings a day and a cottage is worth, and what it costs.
**Resistance:** The wage table is a printed slip and it is unambiguous: labourer four, sluicewright five, keeper six, inspector six and eight, foreman seven and six, master by contract. Wick's winter work at the Reach was a shilling and six a day and a quid in a week, and is gone since June. The two charges must be disclosed to an employer and a disclosure is a permanent thing. Marrow's recommendation is a trade and not a favour and it is priced.
**Major turn:** Marrow's sealed form, filled in his own hand, with a fourth reason on the back that he wrote for nobody: that a plate can address a name, and a name is a plate, and a district with two names for one reach has to choose which plate is right, and if it chooses, the other one is shut. He also states, unasked, that he has not raised the Sallow Head in the Guild for six years, and why, and what it has cost him. **He is not a villain and the chapter must not let him become one.**
**Consequence:** The route to Sallow Head is a Guild installation. A stranger does not go into a Guild chamber without a man present, and the chapter's last scene is four men loading a cart: Sef Ordry, Margit Saye, Jory Vant, and Wick Venn, and Tarin, who is a passenger.
**Ending hook:** A rider comes back up the road nine days later with a note that says the notice has been read out at a meeting in a room over a shop, and the answer is four words, and the four words are not yes and not no.

### Chapter 0053 — Two Days West
**POV and location:** Tarin, the party, the road. The 21st to the 23rd.
**Immediate goal:** Reach the works and see the gate that has read dry for six years.
**Resistance:** Two days of mountain road in a heat that has not been kind to anybody in this volume, a toll bar, and a Guild crew that treats a suspended officer as a man who may not be left alone in a chamber. The village at the end of the lane does not want him and has said so in four words.
**Major turn:** The culvert notice, nailed to a post, dated six years ago, in the shape of a public notice, and it is a **road notice, not a water notice** — *no water, no entry, under penalty* — and nobody can find who issued it, and the sluice it refers to was shut a year before the notice was written. **That is the whole engine of the volume and it is a nail in a post.**
**Consequence:** Because the notice exists, the village has a secret and the secret has a shape: a plank, a pail, a watch, an hour before the cart can go, and a bucket, and no name. Tarin is asked to do nothing and watches it and does the arithmetic, and **the village takes 38 gallons an hour and the water is 193 and he is told the difference is a barrow and the hours people are awake.**
**Ending hook:** The four words are: *we want the water counted.*

### Chapter 0054 — Where It Comes In, And Where It Goes
**POV and location:** Nether Sallow. The 24th and 25th.
**Immediate goal:** Understand a water system that has no name, no gate, no schedule and no keeper, and works.
**Resistance:** Aldis Kerr, seventy-one, says the drain ran the other way in her mother's time, and she is right, and she is the only person alive who can say so. And the field books: a reach kept in two documents for four hundred years, a canal renamed in 1902 by copying a heading forward instead of carrying it back, and **two men in 1903 who each carried half a page.**
**Major turn:** Perdie's account of the split, in about nine hundred words, in a cottage, with four books on a table, and the chapter's own line: *the water has an address and the address has two spellings, and a route that is written down twice is not twice as safe, it is twice as hard to hold.* **Nobody in this volume says that sentence first. The Reach's own water did.**
**Consequence:** Nesta Sarg and Perdie Ammid are put in the same room and they have never met, and the chapter ends with them agreeing on one figure — **the take, 38 gallons an hour** — and on nothing else at all.
**Ending hook:** The fifth box, and what it is for.

### Chapter 0055 — Solid
**POV and location:** The Guild shed at the foot of the embankment, and the works. The 26th.
**Immediate goal:** Get a rod into the covered line the Guild's return calls solid.
**Resistance:** The return of eight years ago, in the Guild's own words, and it is a good return: *stone, no void, solid to the rod at both ends, to remain so.* And the specification book: the plate at the Sallow Head should be a **three-eighths bore** and a **half-inch** plate is in the ring, and a half-inch plate cannot read dry at any head, and the first thing that means is that the plate is above the water. And the crew will not put him in the chamber, and the reason is not contempt and is stated out loud.
**Major turn:** The sediment. Nine feet of it in a chamber that is nineteen deep, and it is not a mystery and it is not a conspiracy, it is a hundred and eighty-one years of a catchment nobody cleaned, and the reason nobody cleaned it is **on a printed card pinned inside the door of the shed**, in Marrow's own writing from eleven years ago, and it says *cleaned, sixth*, and there is no seventh. **A card, a seventh line, and eleven years.**
**Consequence:** Sef Ordry, thirty-one, four men and a bucket and a rod and a scraper, and the chapter's best work is the crew working a chamber on hand tools, and Tarin is left above ground and given the job nobody wants.
**Ending hook:** The specification book, and the two entries that are inconsistent with the works, and the one nobody has looked at.

### Chapter 0056 — The Word Is The Document
**POV and location:** Brant. A room over a shop. The 27th and 28th.
**Immediate goal:** Get a route name agreed.
**Resistance:** **This is the chapter the batch exists for.** The word *the Sallow* means a gate with a schedule and a catchment that has never failed to the men who work for the Guild, a wet common that has never once been supplied by a gate to the people of Nether Sallow, and a spelling to the bookshop at Brant that has been wrong since 1805. **A village refuses an outsider's route name because the same word has two incompatible histories, and it refuses in daylight, in a room, in its own hand, and it is entered in Perdie Ammid's fourth book on the page before the notice.**
**Major turn:** Perdie Ammid's four sentences about a name, which are the volume's thesis and are spoken by a woman of fifty-four who has never been to a school: a name is for saying who decides; two names is not two routes, it is one route and one argument; an argument can be carried; and nobody has to be right.
**Consequence:** No name is agreed. **A date is.** The assembly will hold a window on the fourteenth of next month — the day before the fifteenth — with a rod, a pail, a watch, and a witness, and the witness will be read out.
**Ending hook:** The printed slip off the pin, dated 1902, one line struck through in ink and another written above it, and two miles four chains where four miles two chains was, and the number on the card is not a correction of a length but a different length. **And a third line under both, in the same type and untouched by the later ink, which is the only one of the three nobody has ever asked about: *the Sallow Head intake, with a plate, at a head of 7·31 metres.*** This is where the volume's `7·31` / *seven point three one* convention lands on the page, in a document, with a man failing to say it out loud the way it is written.

### Chapter 0057 — A Thumb In A Brass Ring
**POV and location:** The Sallow Head gate chamber. The 29th.
**Immediate goal:** See the inside of the thing that has been dry for six years.
**Resistance:** The chamber is full of silt to ten foot three, it is nineteen deep, and it takes six men and a bucket and a scraper four hours, and the man who has to go down it is Jory Vant, nineteen, because the plate is at the bottom. **And the plume on the long drain at Nether Sallow is eight hundred and sixty gallons an hour, which is six and a half times what anybody has ever measured out of that drain, and Tarin works it out in a yard from two figures and a cross-section and it takes him a whole morning and a bit of paper.**
**Major turn:** **The fourth Gauge panel in the series**, and the plate, and the finding: the ring is nine feet above the sediment line, so the plate is not measuring anything, and it is a **half-inch plate where the specification says three-eighths**, and the difference between a three-eighths and a half-inch at seven point three one metres is not nothing and is nowhere near enough to be the answer.
**Consequence:** Below the silt, by rod and then by hand, there is a **six-inch branch leaving the intake chamber below the seat on no plate list at all** — a drowned connection, laid in a wall, discharging into the covered line under the road. **He does not open it. He puts his hand on it, and he says out loud that he is not going to open it, and that he would like it recorded that he had a hand on it and did not.**
**Ending hook:** **The plume is the branch.** Eight hundred and sixty gallons an hour of it into a drain that has been carrying three hundred and fifty, and a nineteen-year-old boy at the bottom of a silted chamber in a heat, and a man with a bar and a bucket, and a week to think about it.

### Chapter 0058 — The Jobs Nobody Wants
**POV and location:** The Guild shed and the Reach. The 30th.
**Immediate goal:** Become useful to four men who do not need him.
**Resistance:** **The specific, ugly, ordinary resistance of a craft.** He is the fifth man in a shed and the worst one in it, and the chapter does not skip a single day of it: three days of hand-scraping a gate bed in thirty-eight degrees, and his two hands and a bad shoulder, and Sef Ordry, who does not shout, and a bucket brigade of five men and one wheelbarrow, and Jory Vant's back.
**Major turn:** The crew's one stated reason for trusting him, said once and never repeated: *you counted the water in a lane at the top of your hill when everybody in your city was telling you there was none.* And his bad answer, and Sef's reply, and the chapter's other turn: **Margit Saye tells him the specification list is Guild property and he has not paid for it and she has not been asked to give it, and she is going to have to write to somebody about it, and he offers to pay and she says no.**
**Consequence:** The **level staff**: a graduated stave, chalk, a watch, two men, twice. **870 gallons an hour over the working range.** And **Tank-card Day: one hundred and fifty-three working days a year, every tank's level on a board at the foot of the tank, on a Sunday, in a fair hand, in a book, and the year given and a fair copy going to the counting-house at Vellum once a year, on a cart, and nobody has to believe anybody about anything.**
**Ending hook:** The second crew meeting, and the argument about who goes into the chamber, and the volume's first hint that the crew will find out what he did not tell them.

### Chapter 0059 — A Window Is A Date And Not A Wish
**POV and location:** Brant and the fields. The 31st.
**Immediate goal:** Find out how much water a planting window needs, in a currency that a room will accept.
**Resistance:** Everything in the Reach measures its world in **gallons a household a day**, and the Reach's figure is four, and Cresset's figure is four and a half, and **the two are the same figure and they were arrived at separately by people who have never met**, and that coincidence is the most useful thing in the chapter and the one nobody in it believes for a fortnight.
**Major turn:** **1,700 acres, of which nine hundred are apples; the window is the first ten days; and the Reach's own four years under Cromwell give the number that a room will believe: a fifth of a household's water for four days, four times, is eighteen thousand nine hundred gallons.** And beneath it, from the roadside stone, the **lower Sere**, three miles below the intake, in a face two hundred and fifty years old, cut on purpose to be read: *he that takes the water of the Sere carries the oath of the Sere.* **Tarin cannot take the water of the Sere and he is going to want to, and the stone is the reason he does not, and the Reach does not know it exists and nobody has told them.**
**Consequence:** A letter of four lines to Mara Quill, and **the first thing he has written to her that is not about evidence.**
**Ending hook:** The first night a duty roster is kept, in a book, in six hands, on a page that has been ruled for something else.

### Chapter 0060 — Two Ends Of One Water
**POV and location:** Brant, Nether Sallow, and Vellum, in the same four days. The 1st and 2nd of next month.
**Immediate goal:** Close the volume's central measurement honestly, which means closing a gap rather than a sum.
**Resistance:** **850 against 193, and 850 is four and a half times 193, and the chapter must not make that a surprise, because the volume has already shown the reason: a gravel bed that is not lined.** And the reach-record split is now a sheet on a table with two entries side by side and a copy of each, and the Reach's four books and the Guild's volume forty-four are the same reach in two documents, and a route written down twice is not twice as safe.
**Major turn:** **Mara Quill refuses to certify the village's own number.** Nesta Sarg put **thirty-eight gallons an hour** in Perdie Ammid's field book, in her own hand, as a figure she could be held to, and it would help the village enormously, and it is an estimate and not a measurement and a custom and not a record. **Nesta agrees with her against her own village's interest in one sentence and Tarin, who wanted it certified, says nothing at all**, because the condition of their agreement says they disagree in public with the disagreement recorded, and this is that.
**Consequence:** **The first duty roster in six hands.** Eight hours on, eight off, four in the yard, two at the plank, somebody's mother, somebody's boy, and **a man of thirty-three who is paid five shillings a day and is on the roster in the fourth week of the six.** And a private line in Wick's hand, which he shows to nobody, which reads *in the field 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. and down the lane 3 p.m. to dusk, there and back, and a man cannot get water and be somewhere else at the same time, and I have been trying to say that since June.*
**Ending hook:** The **front** of the notice. The post at the culvert is to be cut and rehung, and six hands turn it over, and the reverse is a record, and the front is dated — and the front is dated the **fifteenth**.

## Hard mechanics

- **The head at the Sallow Head is `7·31` metres, spoken *seven point three one*, and nobody measures it.** Write `7·31` in a document and *seven point three one* in a man's mouth, exactly as `5·89` / *five point eight nine* was kept in Volume 1.
- **The tank is 6,000 gallons of room.** Twenty feet by fifteen feet is three hundred feet of floor; two feet eight inches is eight hundred feet of water; and a foot of water is seven and a half gallons in this basin, so 300 x 2.667 x 7.5 = 6,000. The mark on the wall is at two feet six and a quarter inches and the level on the thirtieth was two feet six and an eighth. **Six years of tank card and a variation under a quarter of an inch.**
- **There is no tank flow rate in this volume, because the tank does not flow.** It is fed by the Cold Shoulder line, its flume sluice is shut, and it stands at the same mark every day of the year. The surplus leaves through the six-inch waste pipe. **The three flow figures in the batch are 860 and 833 at the stone throat and 193 and 191 at the plank, and the printed figure is 850.**
- **The sluice at the Sallow Head is shut and has been for six years. Nobody in this batch opens it and nobody says who shut it or why.** That is Volume 3 and later.
- **A gauged stop-log meters a branch; an ungauged one does not; a channel stops at the balance point.** All three still hold and the tank's balance point — the level at which the flume stops of its own accord — is why the fourth hour of the window gives nothing, and it is not discovered in this batch.
- **The figures in Chapter 60 are 850 against 190, which is four and a half times, and the difference of 660 an hour has gone into a gravel bed. Margit Saye's arithmetic in Chapter 57 and again in Chapter 60 must agree with itself and be reproducible from a lath, a watch, a rule and the volume's factor of seven and a half gallons to the cubic foot.**
- **The notice at the culvert is six years old, is a road notice, and refers to a gate that was shut a year before it was written.** Nothing else is true of it and it is never explained.
- **No chapter ends on a cut-off. Every chapter lands on a completed beat.**
