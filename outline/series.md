# Series Outline: The Irrigation Ghost

## One-sentence premise

When Cresset's missing reservoirs begin to answer from inside the municipal records, a water engineer who trusts instruments more than people must learn to restore a whole basin's routes without forcing anyone to surrender their name, history, or claim to water.

## Series promise

This is a regional water fantasy and a long mystery. Every volume contains a complete local crisis: water is found, diverted, contaminated, stolen, or withheld; a community makes a decision; the immediate problem is answered; and the solution changes the balance of power. The mystery grows from one vanished reservoir to the origin of the One Map, but no volume can postpone its own central promise.

The distinctive difference from other basin stories is that the “system” is not a game interface or a personal mana tree. It is a physical and civic relationship between water, maintenance, and the public meaning of a place. A correct solution must account for a gate, a recipient, a downstream floodplain, a witness, and a cost.

## Protagonist: Tarin Rusk

Tarin is a thirty-three-year-old senior flow engineer in the Cresset Water Office. He is already highly competent with pumps, survey tools, crews, and pressure mathematics before he develops any unusual ability.

- **External want:** Prove that Ashgate Reservoir still exists, reopen its route, and keep Cresset alive through the first municipal water deadline.
- **Internal need:** Accept that responsibility cannot mean sole ownership. He must learn to work through other people's authority and preserve disagreement rather than turn every community into a line on his private map.
- **Central flaw:** He hoards the final decision. He believes that if he carries enough responsibility, he can prevent another flood or another disappearance.
- **Fear and false belief:** He fears becoming a useful authority who decides which people may be sacrificed. He falsely believes a perfect calculation can replace consent.
- **Personal history:** He grew up in Glassstep, a neighborhood erased from official maps, and signed a flood closure as a young engineer that saved a hospital while trapping people in a lower courtyard. His mother Lira vanished while trying to preserve Glassstep's route.
- **Final change:** He becomes a temporary anchor rather than a permanent ruler of the water network. He accepts a partial, plural map and gives up his private right to keep one perfect memory of home.

## Relationship engine: Tarin and Mara Quill

Mara is a record conservator and the daughter of Glassstep canal workers. She can establish provenance and read damaged records, but she cannot move water. Her independent goal is to restore Glassstep's legal and civic existence, not to make Tarin the hero of its story.

Their one slow-burn relationship develops through:

1. suspicion over a missing Ashgate file;
2. forced cooperation in a field investigation;
3. mutual disclosure of shame and family history;
4. a public conflict when Mara publishes evidence against Tarin's office;
5. Tarin refusing to use her private memory without consent;
6. separate assignments that prove neither partner is only a support character;
7. a public, bounded partnership in the Many Rivers Charter;
8. a chosen relationship after the crisis, not a romance used to cure grief.

## Central mystery and planned answer

### Surface question

Why do Cresset's reservoirs appear empty, and why does water run through places that official records say are dry?

### Escalating question

Why do the same unlisting process appear in villages, floodplains, mountain weather routes, and buried ruins?

### Final answer

The One Map is a basin-wide calibration lattice built after the Ninefold Flood. It cannot recognize more than one authoritative address for a contested route. When a community's history or shared practice conflicts with the official map, the system unlists the route, stripping the water of a usable social address and forcing it toward the Blind Reservoir beneath the old Orisene works. The lost water is real and physically dangerous. The Irrigation Ghost is the accumulated pressure of unlisted routes and their people, not a single dead soul.

Chancellor Boryn Vey quietly reactivated the system's current version six years before the opening after concluding that a controlled sacrifice of outer districts was the only way to avert a predicted regional famine. He expanded the program while the public drought worsened. Tarin's mother, Lira Rusk, discovered the system's maintenance history and created the counter-map, a distributed set of local records. She died inside the system after choosing secrecy and delay over asking her family and neighbors for help. Her choices are part of the answer, not evidence that the system is benevolent.

The final solution is to break the One Map's monopoly without erasing its records or forcing a new common memory. Many local route assemblies create the Many Rivers Charter and maintain a shared emergency network through plural, contestable records. Water returns unevenly at first, but it returns without anyone being compelled to forget who they are.

## Reveal schedule

- **Volume 1:** The Ashgate Reservoir exists below a later settlement. The ghost is not a normal person. Halden Roe's local falsification is exposed.
- **Volume 2:** The same route can be dry in one record and wet in another. The Open Channel Guild knows enough to exploit the rule.
- **Volume 3:** Unlisting can cause both drought and flood. The missing water is not always in the same physical direction.
- **Volume 4:** Glassstep is a living community, not a ghost town. The Irrigation Ghost is collective. The Quiet Archive has erased counter-records for decades.
- **Volume 5:** The Meridian Compact controls the source gates; Boryn Vey's six-year-old recalibration and rationing plan are publicly exposed.
- **Volume 6:** False records create salt fever and brine surges. Evidence begins pointing toward the Blind Reservoir.
- **Volume 7:** The One Map's origin and operating rule are revealed. Lira's trail leads to the counter-map; she does not return as a resurrected guide.
- **Volume 8:** No single faction controls the system. The crisis is a contest over consent, authority, and maintenance.
- **Volume 9:** Weather corridors can be opened only through shared memory, and moving rain toward one basin can dry another.
- **Volume 10:** Boryn offers Clearwater, the forced centralization that would save Cresset at the cost of other communities.
- **Volume 11:** The counter-map is assembled. The apparent requirement for one common name is revealed as the One Map's trap, not a genuine magical necessity.
- **Volume 12:** The basin breaks the central anchors, releases stored water, and builds a plural emergency network.

## Power progression across the series

- **Chapters 1–50, Wrench and rule to Name:** Tarin establishes his ordinary competence, passes through Trace on perception alone, survives a first route-reading failure, and makes one narrow opening that he cannot repeat. Name debt costs him a practiced hand motion and the taste of his tea.
- **Chapters 51–150, Name to Bend:** He learns to redirect a real current, and his name becomes tied to a route he cannot walk away from. Name debt next costs him a lasting professional association — a crew he had begun to trust.
- **Chapters 151–300, Bend to Braid:** He learns that a route cannot be held by one keeper. Floods, Glassstep, mountain politics, and Pale Teeth force him to share timing and information.
- **Chapters 301–400, Braid:** The One Map's physical system and Lira's counter-map are revealed. His ability remains dependent on teams and records.
- **Chapters 401–500, Sky braid:** He helps guide an existing weather corridor, accepts unequal consequences, and refuses a convenient lie about the weather.
- **Chapters 501–600, Open channel:** He can stabilize a basin-wide emergency network only by giving up exclusive control. The final power is institutional and distributed, not a personal superhuman surge.

## Antagonist ladder

Each rung represents a different answer to the same question: who should be allowed to decide where water goes?

1. **Halden Roe, acting commissioner:** hides illegal pumping behind a small lie to prevent immediate panic.
2. **Iven Marrow, Open Channel Guild master:** believes specialized knowledge should be owned and sold by those who maintain it.
3. **Cael Dorrin, Fair Copy magistrate:** treats legal recognition as proof of physical reality and uses forced relocation as correction.
4. **Liora Tarn, Quiet Archive keeper:** protects communities by removing dangerous or contradictory memories, allowing censorship to look like care.
5. **Boryn Vey, Meridian Compact chancellor:** knowingly sacrifices outer districts to prevent a larger famine and activates the One Map.
6. **The One Map:** the inherited system that cannot accept plural addresses. It is the final external pressure; Boryn is its final human operator, not a surprise villain introduced at the end.

## Twelve-volume progression

### Volume 1: The Empty Gauge (Chapters 1–50)

- **Central pressure:** Cresset has a public water deadline, a missing Ashgate Reservoir, and a first apparently impossible flow through a dry channel.
- **Starting state:** Tarin is a respected but isolated field engineer; Mara is investigating a missing record; their relationship is professional suspicion.
- **Power state:** Wrench and rule, moving through Trace and into a first, incomplete use of Name. The first Gauge statement is incomplete, slightly wrong, and never a tutorial.
- **Major locations:** Cresset's pump houses, the Ashgate inspection works, Glassstep terraces, the Municipal Memory Office.
- **Major pressure:** A citywide rationing schedule creates immediate stakes, while Halden Roe's falsified readings hide a contractor scheme.
- **Escalation:** Tarin finds an unlisted pump route; his first attempt to open it saves a neighborhood but leaves a crosscurrent and a missing sensory memory. Mara proves the official maintenance log was altered after the flood.
- **Midpoint reversal:** Ashgate is not an empty ruin. It lies beneath a newer settlement, and its water is being diverted toward a pressure chamber that does not appear on any public survey. The ghost's voice contains more than one person.
- **Climax:** Chapters 46–49. A posted Basin Assembly hearing forces Tarin to choose between a clean official story and a dangerous but real route. He opens the narrow branch in public with named witnesses who are free to refuse and one of whom does, the water answers for the same eleven hours the courtyard got, and Roe's falsified measurements cannot survive a gauge that reads wet in front of the city that was told the reservoir was dry. The earlier openings in Chapters 6 and 10 are smaller, unannounced, and leave his lie untouched.
- **Concrete resolution:** Cresset receives temporary water and the Water Office acknowledges Ashgate's existence, but the city cannot sustain the supply and upstream demand remains unexplained.
- **Relationship change:** Tarin and Mara agree to share evidence while distrusting each other's methods. The agreement is made in Chapters 11–20, on conditions attached, and is never repaired into trust.
- **Power change:** Tarin reaches Trace by perception in Chapter 5, uses Name for the first time in Chapter 6, and repeats the opening once in Chapter 10 while suspended and in front of a crowd. Each use costs a debt or a charge, and he cannot repeat it again because he has no method for finding another witness. Bend is not reached in this volume.
- **Next-volume question:** Why is the upstream Guild reporting a dry gate while a Sallow Reach village reports water moving uphill?

### Volume 2: The Dry Names (Chapters 51–100)

- **Central pressure:** Sallow Reach villages need a source before the next planting window, while the Open Channel Guild sells priority at the mountain gates.
- **Starting state:** Cresset has a fragile emergency supply; Tarin and Mara are pursuing a joint record, not a romance.
- **Power state:** Name, with Tarin learning that a route name is a negotiated address rather than a magical command.
- **Major locations:** Sallow Reach villages, mountain service roads, the Guild hall, the lower Sere channel.
- **Major factions:** Route Assemblies, the Open Channel Guild, and Cresset's Water Office.
- **Escalation:** Iven Marrow offers Tarin a place in the Guild if he accepts the official map. A village refuses an outsider's route name because the same word has two incompatible histories.
- **Midpoint reversal:** A supposedly dry canal contains a substantial, physically present flow. The water was not lost; it was made blind by splitting one name across two records.
- **Climax:** Tarin and the village keepers open the channel for a measured service window while Cresset gives up part of its new supply. The choice prevents a crop failure and costs the city a visible reserve.
- **Concrete resolution:** The Sallow Reach assembly gains a recognized route right and the Guild faces a public inspection. The immediate water crisis is solved, but the mountain gate remains politically closed.
- **Relationship change:** Mara sees Tarin accept a cost he cannot personally measure; Tarin sees her refuse to falsify a village's account.
- **Power change:** Tarin reaches Bend for a short operation and his name becomes tied to the route in a way he cannot undo. His name debt takes his standing with a Guild crew he had started to trust. The practiced hand motion lost in Volume 1 does not come back.
- **Next-volume question:** The freed water is flooding a downstream neighborhood that official records claim has no inhabitants.

### Volume 3: The Flood Ledger (Chapters 101–150)

- **Central pressure:** Meltwater Verge is flooding while Cresset remains dry, exposing the false choice between saving one city and abandoning another.
- **Starting state:** The rural route is open but unstable; Tarin is trusted by a few crews and blamed by city residents for reducing Cresset's reserve.
- **Power state:** Bend, with a growing risk that his private route-sense is becoming a liability.
- **Major locations:** The floodplain, old flood-control terraces, the hospital channel, and a buried record house.
- **Major factions:** The Fair Copy under Cael Dorrin, local flood wardens, and the Water Office.
- **Escalation:** Dorrin seals a downstream village to protect a legal boundary, creating a new wave. Tarin's old flood-closure history is raised by opponents and Mara discovers the inquiry's missing pages.
- **Midpoint reversal:** The old flood ledger shows that the Ninefold Flood began with conflicting gate orders, not an unmanageable natural disaster. The system used to prevent repetition is now making order impossible.
- **Climax:** Tarin opens an abandoned spillway and lets a controlled flood occupy a former military terrace, saving homes but destroying a physical record house. Mara secures fragments of the ledger from residents who never trusted the court.
- **Concrete resolution:** The floodplain is kept habitable and Dorrin is forced to show the legal basis for the unlisting. The route remains politically dangerous; some land will not be rebuilt.
- **Relationship change:** Mara publishes the missing pages while Tarin admits his old closure. Their trust is damaged by honesty, not repaired by confession alone.
- **Power change:** Tarin's Bend ability becomes socially constrained: he can redirect water only when crews and residents share the risk.
- **Next-volume question:** Why do the records call Glassstep empty when people are still living there?

### Volume 4: The Unlisted City (Chapters 151–200)

- **Central pressure:** Returning to Cresset, Tarin and Mara find a populated neighborhood absent from every official map, while the Quiet Archive moves to erase its counter-records.
- **Starting state:** Tarin is publicly blamed for the flood, Mara has lost trust in his office, and their relationship is professionally and personally under strain.
- **Power state:** Bend with the first lessons in Braid; Tarin can coordinate two local routes but cannot make them agree.
- **Major locations:** Glassstep, the old blue-door rows, the Memory Office, and a quiet house beneath Vellum's archive district.
- **Major factions:** Glassstep residents, the Quiet Archive, the Fair Copy, and a small council of Water Office reformers.
- **Escalation:** The archive removes a household record that could prove Glassstep's water right. Tarin wants to force a public opening; Mara wants to protect witnesses and organize a legal claim.
- **Midpoint reversal:** Glassstep residents have survived by maintaining a route without one central name. Their practice is not a failed version of official water management; it is a different, more resilient system.
- **Climax:** A public assembly and a dangerous service window force the city to acknowledge Glassstep's inhabitants and restore provisional route rights. The Quiet Archive removes the remaining proof, and Mara leaves the Memory Office after publishing what she can.
- **Concrete resolution:** Glassstep receives water, legal presence, and a public counter-record, but it remains vulnerable because the archive has not surrendered its custody.
- **Relationship change:** Tarin and Mara choose separate public responsibilities while remaining partners in the larger work.
- **Power change:** Tarin's first Braid is brief and costly; it works because residents choose to hold different ends of the route.
- **Next-volume question:** The source-country gates are being closed by the Meridian Compact, and a mountain village says its rain songs have been declared obsolete.

### Volume 5: The Source Countries (Chapters 201–250)

- **Central pressure:** Thorn Crown communities face a choice between protecting their cloud forest and sharing the runoff that feeds the lowlands.
- **Starting state:** Glassstep's provisional right is recognized, but Cresset is still dangerously short of water; Boryn Vey enters as a credible, dangerous negotiator.
- **Power state:** Braid, expanded from two communities to a regional arrangement.
- **Major locations:** Thorn Crown terraces, cloud-forest paths, source gates, and the first Meridian Compact hearing.
- **Major factions:** Mountain villages, the Open Channel Guild, the Meridian Compact, and lowland route assemblies.
- **Escalation:** Mountain communities reveal that lowland maps erase seasonal weather paths. Boryn offers a source treaty that protects cities but leaves the villages with no formal vote.
- **Midpoint reversal:** The rain songs are not primitive decoration; they are a distributed weather record. A single official forecast cannot replace them without causing a local dry season.
- **Climax:** Tarin, Mara, and the mountain assemblies negotiate a shared source window rather than a permanent transfer. Boryn's six-year-old recalibration is exposed; when the wider drought worsens, he expands the One Map protocol and closes the source gates to the outer districts.
- **Concrete resolution:** Thorn Crown receives a recognized maintenance role and the lowlands receive a season of water. The cost is that the Compact now knows the counter-map is possible.
- **Relationship change:** Mara and Tarin disagree over whether to publicize Boryn's program; they choose disclosure despite the risk to the mountain alliance.
- **Power change:** Tarin becomes useful as a braid coordinator, but cannot order weather or guarantee a fair result.
- **Next-volume question:** In Pale Teeth, buried aqueducts are producing salt fever and every official record says the water is naturally undrinkable.

### Volume 6: The Salt Memory (Chapters 251–300)

- **Central pressure:** Salt sickness spreads in Pale Teeth as false records and stolen route stones turn groundwater into brine.
- **Starting state:** The source treaty is temporary, Boryn's drought policy is expanding, and Tarin's name debt is beginning to affect his ability to work.
- **Power state:** Braid under pressure; local readers, not Tarin, make the route work in several villages.
- **Major locations:** Pale Teeth salt pans, buried Orisene ruins, a sealed cistern, and a mobile clinic.
- **Major factions:** Salt-trading contractors, Quiet Archive field readers, village healers, and the Meridian Compact.
- **Escalation:** A forged route record creates a moving brine river. Yara Sen proves the illness is physical, while Mara traces the substituted ink to a court-approved source.
- **Midpoint reversal:** The salt is not the source of the poison. Water is being pulled through an unlisted route into a pressure chamber below the old imperial works. The first physical evidence of the Blind Reservoir is found.
- **Climax:** The team opens a narrow outlet and floods a useless salt pan to prevent a town from drinking brine. The route cannot be named safely, so Tarin accepts a severe name debt and the locals keep their own records.
- **Concrete resolution:** The salt fever is contained, the Blind Reservoir's existence is public, and the Compact declares Tarin a security threat.
- **Relationship change:** Yara sets a boundary with Tarin after he tries to hide the cost of a route operation. He begins to ask for help before he is forced to.
- **Power change:** Tarin's Braid expands, but his private route-sense becomes less reliable; a local reader performs a key operation he cannot.
- **Next-volume question:** The only map showing an entrance to the Blind Reservoir leads beneath the old One Map, where Lira Rusk left a mark.

### Volume 7: The Water Beneath (Chapters 301–350)

- **Central pressure:** The team enters the One Map's maintenance works to find a way to return the missing water before the reservoir fails.
- **Starting state:** The drought is now regional, Boryn controls the source gates, and Tarin's personal history points toward a place the official map denies.
- **Power state:** Braid, with an early proof that a plural network can hold when the physical gates and records are maintained by different people.
- **Major locations:** The Orisene maintenance galleries, the Blind Reservoir, record-stone chambers, and a counter-reader's refuge.
- **Major factions:** A small expedition, the Quiet Archive's last readers, Compact enforcement crews, and the nonhuman pressure of the unlisted routes.
- **Escalation:** The One Map rejects any route name that is not singular. Each attempt to open a gallery causes a memory bleed in the expedition and wakes larger portions of the irrigation ghost.
- **Midpoint reversal:** The One Map is not a person and not a curse. It is a calibration lattice enforcing the founders' rule. Lira is not waiting as a living guide; her notes and the physical traces of her work show that she entered the galleries and died after choosing delay over consultation.
- **Climax:** The team recovers a counter-map fragment and deliberately opens a small, plural set of records. The central system responds by pulling more water into the Blind Reservoir, causing a regional dry pulse.
- **Concrete resolution:** The expedition escapes with evidence and a route fragment, but the One Map is now awake and the drought has widened. No hidden shortcut remains.
- **Relationship change:** Tarin stops treating his mother's choices as instructions and begins to mourn the person rather than search for her resurrection.
- **Power change:** He can briefly hold a network of several galleries, but only while Mara and the readers maintain the records below him.
- **Next-volume question:** If the One Map cannot accept several names, can the communities build a shared network without asking Tarin to become the next central keeper?

### Volume 8: The River That Remembers (Chapters 351–400)

- **Central pressure:** Regional communities need a common emergency network, but none trusts the others enough to surrender local control.
- **Starting state:** The counter-map fragment is incomplete; Tarin is a public suspect, Mara has left the Memory Office, and the Compact can close the source gates at any moment.
- **Power state:** Braid, now tested at regional scale.
- **Major locations:** Sallow Reach, Meltwater Verge, Cresset, Vellum, and the disputed middle Sere crossings.
- **Major factions:** Provisional route assemblies, the Open Channel Guild reformers, Quiet Archive dissidents, Compact authorities, and Cresset's Water Office.
- **Escalation:** Tarin attempts to coordinate the middle basin in real time. His habit of taking the final decision causes two communities to withdraw their records, and a transfer arrives at the wrong village.
- **Midpoint reversal:** The failure is not a lack of power. It is the centralization of attention. A less capable but openly shared braid keeps the routes alive longer.
- **Climax:** Tarin relinquishes central timing authority to the local assemblies and uses his own route-sense only as a temporary check. The network holds, but the Compact retains the only mountain source gate.
- **Concrete resolution:** A provisional Many Rivers network is established. The public understands that the basin can cooperate, but immediate water remains insufficient.
- **Relationship change:** Mara and Tarin work in separate places and trust each other's reports without needing to control each other.
- **Power change:** Tarin reaches the boundary of Braid and loses the ability to maintain more than a few local traces at once.
- **Next-volume question:** The only remaining way to rain on the basin is to borrow a weather corridor remembered by the mountain villages, and using it may dry another region.

### Volume 9: The Weather Borrowers (Chapters 401–450)

- **Central pressure:** A real storm front is approaching, but no basin can receive it fairly without another losing rain.
- **Starting state:** The provisional network is fragile, the source gate is closed, and Boryn publicly blames the route assemblies for the drought.
- **Power state:** Sky braid, learned through failed forecasts and regional negotiation.
- **Major locations:** Thorn Crown storm terraces, the high cloud forest, the Sallow Reach fields, and the open air above the Meltwater Verge.
- **Major factions:** Weather-callers, mountain assemblies, the Compact, lowland cities, and farmers facing a visible choice.
- **Escalation:** Tarin opens a corridor toward Thorn Crown and Cresset, causing an unseasonable dry spell in a distant basin. He cannot hide the consequence behind a technical explanation.
- **Midpoint reversal:** The weather record shows that no basin has a moral monopoly on rain. The question is not who deserves everything, but who has been carrying the emergency cost alone.
- **Climax:** The basin assemblies negotiate a shared, variable weather service. They use a smaller rain front instead of the largest one, and some farms receive less water than they wanted. The Compact admits that Clearwater is the next emergency plan.
- **Concrete resolution:** A real rain front reaches the Thorn Crown and the middle fields. The drought slows, but Cresset still cannot survive without its own source.
- **Relationship change:** Mara's public testimony makes the weather cost legible; Tarin accepts that being useful does not give him the last word.
- **Power change:** Tarin can guide a natural storm only with many weather witnesses and a prepared receiving basin. He cannot control the sky.
- **Next-volume question:** Boryn offers Tarin a place in the final solution: activate Clearwater, save Cresset, and let the One Map erase the competing routes.

### Volume 10: The City of One Map (Chapters 451–500)

- **Central pressure:** Cresset is surrounded by dry reservoirs, a refugee population, and factions competing to control the last serviceable gates.
- **Starting state:** The city has a temporary charter, Tarin is a public engineer with a dangerous name, Mara is an independent archivist, and Boryn controls the mountain standards.
- **Power state:** Sky braid approaching Open channel, but only through institutions rather than personal power.
- **Major locations:** Cresset's civic assembly, Glassstep, the Ashgate works, the city wall, and the upper Sere gates.
- **Major factions:** Cresset's Assembly, Water Office reformers, Compact troops, Guild contractors, Glassstep residents, and the Quiet Archive.
- **Escalation:** Boryn makes Clearwater a public offer. He can save the central city within days if the competing routes are unlisted. Tarin's old flood signature is released as political evidence, and Mara's archive is attacked.
- **Midpoint reversal:** Tarin discovers that Clearwater would not merely redirect water; it would use his past emergency closure as a legal template. The system would call the resulting deaths a correction, making his own guilt part of the operating procedure.
- **Climax:** Tarin refuses the central activation. The city, Glassstep, and surrounding villages use a distributed emergency plan with lower capacity than Clearwater. Cresset survives with rationing and visible sacrifice; Boryn loses public consent but retains the mountain gate.
- **Concrete resolution:** Cresset does not receive unlimited water. Its Assembly publishes the water ledger, recognizes route assemblies, and accepts a temporary public network. The city is poorer and less certain, but it is not owned by a single keeper.
- **Relationship change:** Tarin and Mara testify together, disagree in public, and refuse to let either personal history become the authority.
- **Power change:** Tarin can hold a temporary city-wide braid only when local keepers control the gates. His route-sense begins to fracture under the attempt.
- **Next-volume question:** The counter-map can be completed, but it appears to require one common name. If Tarin supplies his own, will he recreate the One Map?

### Volume 11: The Counter-Map (Chapters 501–550)

- **Central pressure:** The team must assemble enough local records to replace the One Map's central authority before Boryn activates Clearwater.
- **Starting state:** Cresset is stable but rationed, the source gate is closing, and Tarin's name debt is severe.
- **Power state:** Open channel in theory, but not yet in practice.
- **Major locations:** Glassstep, Vellum, Thorn Crown, Pale Teeth, the middle Sere, and the One Map's public control rooms.
- **Major factions:** Many Rivers organizers, Liora Tarn's final custodians, Compact officers, local readers, and Boryn's Clearwater command.
- **Escalation:** Every local record is incomplete, contested, or dangerous to publish. Mara's archive is confiscated. Tarin is offered his mother's remaining route in exchange for completing the common-name version of the counter-map.
- **Midpoint reversal:** The apparent requirement for one common name is a trap written into the One Map. A true plural counter-map can work only when no community can revise or silence the others.
- **Climax:** The organizers open the central galleries from several directions while local assemblies maintain their records. Boryn triggers Clearwater, forcing stored water into the basin. The central anchors split, but the release creates a dangerous flood and leaves several routes physically damaged.
- **Concrete resolution:** The One Map's monopoly is broken, not its memory. The counter-map survives, Boryn's command is exposed, and the basin enters the final flood-drought transition.
- **Relationship change:** Mara refuses to give Tarin a private memory as an anchor; Tarin refuses to let her sacrifice her archive. They choose a public, imperfect record over a personal guarantee.
- **Power change:** Tarin reaches Open channel only as a temporary central braid. He cannot hold it alone and must trust local keepers when he cannot see the route.
- **Next-volume question:** With the central anchors broken, who will open the Blind Reservoir safely, and what will remain dry if no one is allowed to possess the whole map?

### Volume 12: The Open Channel (Chapters 551–600)

- **Central pressure:** The basin has a few days before the released blind water causes an uncontrolled flood, while every community wants a guaranteed share.
- **Starting state:** Cresset is alive through a rationed public network; the Blind Reservoir is rising; Boryn's Clearwater order is still active in the upper control rooms; the counter-map is incomplete but plural.
- **Power state:** Open channel, requiring a watershed-wide chain of local custodians and the acceptance that no one can see the whole flow.
- **Major locations:** The Blind Reservoir, the One Map galleries, Glassstep, Cresset, Meltwater Verge, Sallow Reach, and Thorn Crown.
- **Major factions:** The provisional Many Rivers assemblies, Cresset reformers, mountain communities, Quiet Archive custodians, Compact officers, and the people maintaining the physical gates.
- **Escalation:** Boryn argues that a temporary return to one central map is the only way to prevent mass death. His offer is rational and dangerous. Local communities cannot all be in one place, so the team must build trust across incomplete reports.
- **Midpoint reversal:** The warning in Lira's final record becomes actionable: the common-name requirement is a trap, not a necessary magical sacrifice. Tarin understands that the final act is release, not perfect control.
- **Climax:** Boryn attempts to complete Clearwater while the basin is already in motion. Tarin and the local custodians open the Blind Reservoir through many named outlets, sacrifice the remaining private anchor built from his Glassstep memory, and keep the flood within habitable service limits. The One Map loses its ability to call one route authoritative.
- **Concrete resolution:** Water returns to Ashgate, Glassstep, Sallow Reach, and downstream farms through a negotiated, uneven network. Boryn is stopped and placed on trial rather than killed; his authority and the Compact's monopoly are dismantled. The Many Rivers Charter is adopted. Some routes remain closed, some records remain disputed, and no community receives a perfect guarantee.
- **Final change:** Tarin loses the exclusive ability to sense one private route to his mother and accepts a future in which he can be useful without being final. Mara becomes the first public keeper of a contestable archive. Orrin, Yara, and Niko each retain independent roles in the new system.
- **Next question:** The water is restored, but the future will still require people to maintain, argue over, and repair the routes. The final story closes the drought mystery while leaving the ordinary work of a plural world alive.

## Long-form pressure rotation

The series should alternate pressure types rather than repeat a larger flood or a stronger enemy:

1. **Volume 1:** Practical survival and first mystery.
2. **Volume 2:** Rural politics, guild economics, and training.
3. **Volume 3:** Action, flood consequences, and recovery.
4. **Volume 4:** Character conflict, public history, and relationship rupture.
5. **Volume 5:** Source-country politics and negotiation.
6. **Volume 6:** Environmental investigation and public-health consequences.
7. **Volume 7:** Physical exploration, origin mystery, and grief.
8. **Volume 8:** Regional alliance, character separation, and institutional change.
9. **Volume 9:** Weather ethics, uneven consequences, and political pressure.
10. **Volume 10:** Urban siege, moral choice, and relationship public test.
11. **Volume 11:** Investigation, infiltration, political reversal, and cost.
12. **Volume 12:** Climax, action, institutional resolution, and aftermath.

Each volume must pay off its stated local promise before the next mystery question is emphasized. Training is always tied to a decision; relationship work changes permissions or institutions; action reveals a cost; political pressure changes who can use water.

## Ending constraint

The ending is fixed in `outline/ending.md`. No late volume may introduce a new final enemy, a higher god, a second system, or a convenient resurrection. The One Map and Boryn Vey are present from the opening planning structure, and the final victory is to prevent forced forgetting while restoring physical water.
