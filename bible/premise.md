# Premise: The Irrigation Ghost

## Core premise

**Tarin Rusk, a municipal water engineer, discovers that the drought strangling Cresset is not a natural failure: vanished reservoirs are still physically present, but their routes have been removed from the living record of the world. A ghost made from the memories of unlisted places asks him to move water through a history the city has chosen to forget.**

To deliberately name or open a route, Tarin must find a physical trace, a witness, and a record that acknowledges who bears the cost. He can sense a raw wet line earlier, but a trace alone is not a route he can move. The power is useful, limited, and dangerous. It can restore a neighborhood's water, but it cannot decide which people deserve a place on the map.

## Genre and scale

- **Genre:** Water fantasy, regional mystery, civic adventure, slow-burn relationship drama.
- **Setting:** The drought-stricken Marrow Basin and the five polities that share its water.
- **Target length:** Approximately 600 chapters in twelve volumes of about fifty chapters.
- **Narrative scale:** A complete local water crisis in each major arc, with the mystery widening from a missing reservoir to the history of the Basin.
- **Relationship policy:** One slow-burn relationship, Tarin Rusk and Mara Quill. No harem structure and no instant romantic resolution.

## The protagonist's engine

- **External want:** Reopen the missing **Ashgate Reservoir** and keep Cresset alive through its first municipal water deadline.
- **Internal need:** Stop treating other people's memories as resources he is entitled to spend, and accept that trustworthy stewardship requires shared, revisable authority.
- **Central flaw:** Tarin is competent, careful, and exhausting. He believes that if he carries the final responsibility, he can prevent another catastrophic flood. He hoards decisions, distrusts incomplete testimony, and confuses usefulness with moral permission.
- **Fear:** Becoming a benevolent official who saves the present by quietly deciding which people, places, and versions of the past may be sacrificed.
- **Final change:** Tarin stops trying to be the single anchor for the world. He helps build a network in which many communities can name and maintain their own water routes, even when no single map can be perfect.

## The central mystery

The drought is connected to **the One Map**, a basin-wide hydrological instrument built after the Ninefold Flood. It was intended to stop conflicting water orders from causing another disaster. Instead, when local histories refuse to fit one approved version, it **unlists** their routes. The water is not destroyed; it loses its social address and gathers in an inaccessible **Blind Reservoir** beneath the old imperial works. The unlisted routes accumulate as the **Irrigation Ghost**, a many-voiced pressure of places that still live but have no accepted address.

The mystery has a planned answer, but the truth is not a single villain or a secret chosen bloodline:

1. The water is still moving; it is being stored and misaddressed by the One Map.
2. The One Map is not a conscious monster. It enforces one rule: only one official route can remain authoritative.
3. Chancellor Boryn Vey quietly reactivated the system six years before the opening because he believed a controlled sacrifice of outlying communities was the only way to prevent a larger famine. He expanded the program as the drought worsened.
4. Tarin's mother, Lira Rusk, discovered the system and became a counter-reader. Her trail explains the hidden counter-map, but she is not a saint and her choices cost her family and herself.
5. The final solution is not to destroy every record. It is to build a shared emergency network that preserves conflicting accounts and makes no community's water dependent on one private keeper.

## The central promise

Every major crisis answers a practical question—where is the water, who controls its route, what will a restored channel cost, and who is absent from the official account—while advancing the larger mystery. A solution may save a village and still change who owns the sluice, who receives compensation, or whose story is allowed to count.

The story promises that restoration will not require a clean slate. Water can return to places that have been abandoned, divided, or misnamed, but only if the living people of those places are allowed to describe the return in their own terms. The final answer is not perfect memory or perfect infrastructure. It is the choice to keep multiple, honest routes open.

## First opening pressure

At the beginning, Cresset has measurable rain for the first time in fourteen months, and every drop falls on a city whose largest reservoir has officially been empty for six years. The municipal intake is still wet. When Tarin opens the abandoned Ashgate inspection gate, a concealed pressure shaft releases a residual stream that runs uphill through a dry stone channel; Tarin did not create the water. In the same moment he hears a voice made from the names of erased streets.

He has enough engineering skill to investigate, but not enough authority to open the route. The first small victory will save a neighborhood pump; the first cost will be a sensory memory and a public accusation that he has tampered with city records. The immediate mystery is believable because it begins with a gauge, a missing file, and a flow that appears physically impossible—not with a prophecy.

## Guardrails

- The protagonist is never the only person who can read water. Other communities have readers, engineers, weather-callers, and local practices.
- Memory magic cannot manufacture water, read private thoughts, or make a false record harmless.
- Physical hydraulics, negotiation, maintenance, and ordinary labor remain necessary even after the power appears.
- The ghost is a local and collective phenomenon; it is not a conveniently omniscient guide.
- No volume may postpone its own promise. The larger mystery can open only after the immediate crisis has been answered.
- The final conflict is the already-planned One Map and Boryn Vey's attempt to activate Clearwater, not a new cosmic enemy.
