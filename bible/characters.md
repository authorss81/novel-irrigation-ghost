# Characters

## Protagonist: Tarin Rusk

### Basic identity

- **Name:** Tarin Rusk
- **Age at opening:** Thirty-three
- **Occupation:** Senior flow engineer, Cresset Water Office
- **Home:** A rented room above a disused pump house in the lower terrace
- **Public reputation:** Reliable with tools, difficult with excuses, increasingly unpopular with anyone who wants a faster answer than the evidence allows.

### Want, need, flaw, and fear

- **External want:** Prove that Ashgate Reservoir still exists, reopen enough of its route to keep Cresset alive through the summer deadline, and restore the water supply he was trained to protect.
- **Internal need:** Learn that responsibility is not ownership. He must let other people hold authority over routes and memories that matter to them.
- **Central flaw:** He takes final responsibility too literally. He tries to become the last person who can be blamed, which means he quietly takes decisions away from everyone else. He is also impatient with stories that cannot be reduced to a measurement.
- **Fear:** Becoming the person who saves a city by deciding which other people were allowed to disappear. His deepest fear is not death but becoming useful enough that others let him make choices they never would have accepted from a stranger.
- **False belief:** If he can calculate the safest route, he can make a moral choice without asking anyone to carry its memory.

### Skills and limits

Tarin is competent before the power appears. He can survey a channel, read a pressure gauge, repair a pump, calculate head loss, calm a frightened crew, and notice when a measurement has been altered. He understands the practical consequences of a bad gate order because he lived through one.

He cannot:

- read a person's intention from a magical trace;
- create water;
- safely open a route without a physical anchor and a willing witness;
- solve a political problem by proving that a document is true;
- carry every route at once;
- undo the consequences of his own past decisions.

He has a dry sense of humor, a habit of repairing small things when he is frightened, and an inconvenient memory for exact measurements. He drinks bitter tea and forgets meals. He is not naturally charismatic, but people trust him with tools because he tells them what is likely to break.

### Personal history

Tarin grew up in Glassstep, a lower-Cresset neighborhood removed from the municipal map twenty-six years before the story. His father, **Bevan Rusk**, was a sluice mechanic who died when Tarin was twelve. His mother, **Lira Rusk**, was a municipal copyist. She left him a brass sluice key and the sentence, “A name can be a lock or a bridge,” then disappeared during the Glassstep breach. The official record says she died in a landslide and never mentions Glassstep as a place. Tarin remembers a street, a row of blue doors, and her voice giving directions through a storm.

When he was nineteen, Tarin signed an emergency closure that sealed a side channel during a flood. The closure saved the riverside hospital but trapped several people in a lower courtyard. The official inquiry calls it a justified choice. Tarin has never been able to forget the knocking. This history is not a secret that magically clears his guilt; it is a practical proof that his instinct to contain a problem can move danger somewhere he cannot see.

### Arc

Tarin begins as a man who believes competence means carrying the whole map. He becomes a man who can hold a route only by making sure no one is forced to carry it alone. By the end, he can work inside a shared water network without becoming its ruler, and he has accepted a partial truth about his mother rather than a comforting resurrection story.

## Mara Quill

### Identity and independent want

- **Age:** Thirty-one
- **Occupation:** Provenance record conservator in the Cresset Municipal Memory Office
- **Background:** Daughter of Glassstep canal workers; one of the families that kept old route records after their neighborhood was unlisted
- **Want:** Restore Glassstep's legal name, land claims, and share of basin water. She does not want a symbolic apology. She wants the city to stop treating the people who live there as an accounting error.
- **Need:** Trust a shared public record without mistaking transparency for safety. She must accept that exposing a hidden truth can endanger the people whose truth it contains.
- **Flaw:** She can become so exacting that a messy living community looks like a corrupt one. She hides fear behind procedural language and keeps dangerous documents alone so no one can use them against her.
- **Ability:** She can establish provenance, identify substituted ink, read damaged records, and follow changes in handwriting and paper. She cannot move water by magic. Her expertise is the counterweight to Tarin's field sense; seeing a route and proving who may claim it are different problems.

### Relationship with Tarin

Mara and Tarin begin on opposite sides of a missing Ashgate file. She suspects he altered a maintenance record; he suspects she intends to seal a working route in the name of historical accuracy. Their slow-burn relationship is built through disagreement, shared danger, and repeated choices to make their work visible to the other.

The relationship never requires Mara to become magically capable or Tarin to abandon his responsibility. It requires them to keep separate skills, separate histories, and separate permissions. They become partners without one becoming the other's cure.

## Supporting cast

### Orrin Sedge

- **Age:** Fifty-four
- **Occupation:** Head sluice keeper for the Meltwater Verge
- **Want:** Keep his crews alive and clear the name of his son, who died during a flood caused by a misordered gate.
- **Need:** Accept that an old personal grievance may be the least useful fact in a dangerous situation.
- **Flaw:** He judges people by whether they show up when the water rises.
- **Function:** Tarin's practical instructor and a forceful advocate for people whose lives are made difficult by elegant plans. He distrusts memory magic but understands that pumps, rope, mud, and maintenance are what keep it alive.
- **Planned outcome:** He survives the final crisis, loses formal authority under the new public water structure, and becomes a teacher whose authority comes from chosen trust rather than inherited office.

### Yara Sen

- **Age:** Forty-one
- **Occupation:** Water physician and public-health examiner
- **Want:** Prove that salt fever and memory bleed are physical hazards, not superstition or excuse-making.
- **Need:** Accept that a diagnosis can be uncertain and still require action.
- **Function:** Supplies the bodily consequences of failed magic. She treats Tarin's memory debt without romanticizing it and keeps the story grounded in patients, clinics, sanitation, and frightened families.
- **Planned outcome:** She becomes the chief medical witness for the Many Rivers Charter and establishes treatment for people who have been exposed to blind water.

### Niko Venn

- **Age:** Twenty-two
- **Occupation:** Apprentice waterwright and former route runner
- **Want:** Get a formal place in the Water Office without having to pretend he was born with a master's training.
- **Need:** Stop treating every dangerous place as a test of his worth.
- **Function:** Niko remembers routes by movement, song, and habit rather than documents. His ordinary ambition keeps the story from becoming a procession of exceptional readers. He makes mistakes, survives consequences, and eventually becomes a route keeper in the new network.

### Lira Rusk

- **Status at opening:** Missing and officially dead; her later counter-reader history is hidden.
- **Occupation:** Cresset copyist and later counter-reader.
- **Want:** Keep Glassstep's route and the names of its residents from being legally erased.
- **Flaw:** She believed secrecy was the safest way to protect a cause and made decisions for her family without their consent.
- **Function:** The absent moral pressure in Tarin's life. She is not a perfect guide, a ghost who can solve problems, or a resurrection promised to the reader. Her surviving notes and final deliberate record reveal that she entered the One Map's maintenance chambers to delay a purge and died there. Her final answer is incomplete: she asks Tarin to choose between a clean map and a dangerous plurality.

## Relationship milestones

1. **Professional suspicion:** Mara catches a discrepancy in Tarin's Ashgate measurements. He is certain she is hiding a reason; she is certain he is hiding a method.
2. **Forced cooperation:** He needs her provenance work to open a route; she needs his field access to locate a living street. Neither gets what they want without the other.
3. **Private vulnerability:** Tarin admits that he signed the flood closure. Mara shares that Glassstep's remaining residents have been threatened for keeping copies of the old map.
4. **Public fracture:** Mara publishes evidence that Tarin's office altered a service log. He considers it a betrayal even though the evidence is accurate.
5. **Shared limits:** In a flood, Tarin refuses to use Mara's private memory as an anchor without her consent. This costs them a route but preserves her autonomy.
6. **Separate work:** During the regional crisis, they coordinate different basins without constant contact. Their relationship survives because both retain lives beyond the quest.
7. **Public partnership:** They testify together, disagree in public when necessary, and design a water charter that assigns neither of them final authority.
8. **Ending:** They share a home only after choosing each other as partners; the final story does not treat romance as a cure for grief or a reward for saving the world.

## Antagonist ladder

The antagonists are successive answers to the question of who should control water, not a parade of increasingly powerful monsters.

### 1. Halden Roe — the practical liar

Cresset's acting water commissioner falsifies Ashgate measurements to conceal illegal pumping by contractors. He is not a magical mastermind. He believes a controlled lie will prevent panic and keep the city functioning until the next council election. His betrayal teaches Tarin that a practical problem can still be a political crime. He is exposed in Volume 1 and becomes a cooperating witness rather than a final threat.

### 2. Iven Marrow — the guild monopolist

Head of the Open Channel Guild, Iven controls the technicians who know how to maintain the mountain gates. He sells priority, withholds maps, and uses a true route name to make a fraudulent claim. He offers Tarin a place in the Guild if he leaves Glassstep out of the public record. He represents the belief that expertise deserves ownership. His defeat is negotiated, not simply killed: the Guild accepts public inspection and a route-right process.

### 3. Cael Dorrin — the legal eraser

A regional magistrate and keeper of the Fair Copy, Dorrin enforces the One Map Accord as though law and physical reality must agree. He closes villages, seizes records, and authorizes forced relocation under the doctrine of necessary correction. He is rigorous, public, and willing to be challenged in court. His defeat exposes the difference between legal authority and consent.

### 4. Liora Tarn — the careful censor

Leader of the Quiet Archive, Senn removes unverified and contradictory histories to prevent renewed violence. She preserves the evidence of the Ninefold Flood and genuinely protects vulnerable people. She also becomes the administrator of the archive that can erase a community without touching a single stone. She is a major ideological antagonist and later a difficult ally, never a secret final villain.

### 5. Boryn Vey — the rational sacrificer

Chancellor of the Meridian Compact and the person who quietly reactivated the One Map's newest function six years before the story, Boryn uses regional models to argue that the basin's northern cities can survive only if the outer districts receive almost no water. He knows the cost in people and chooses it because he believes uncontrolled sharing will kill more. He is persuasive, transparent about his calculations, and responsible for the drought's current spread. He enters the public story in Volume 5, is developed through Volumes 6–10, and is confronted in the planned ending.

### The One Map — the inherited antagonist

The One Map is the final external pressure. It is not a new villain and does not think or want in human terms. Its central rule is that only one official address can remain authoritative. It can be resisted by exposing its rules, distributing its inputs, and removing its central anchors. Boryn is the person currently trying to use it; the system is the force that makes the final choice impossible to avoid.

## Supporting antagonist pressures

- **Guild contractors:** Bribes, delayed repairs, and false service windows create immediate crises.
- **Village gatekeepers:** Some refuse outsiders for good reasons; some profit from the same corruption. The story does not treat every local authority as a villain.
- **The Blind Reservoir:** When a blind route is opened carelessly, the stored water can return as a sudden flood, brine surge, or pressure collapse. It is a physical hazard, not a monster.
- **Public panic:** False claims that magic is creating water can turn small restorations into violence. Tarin must sometimes choose a slower, less impressive truth over a dramatic lie.

## Relationship and team rules

- No character exists only to praise Tarin or explain the mystery.
- Mara does not become a passive reward for solving the mystery.
- Orrin and Niko disagree about how much risk a community should accept, and both positions can be morally defensible.
- Yara's patients are not props; treatments and deaths affect the choices available to the team.
- Antagonists can change positions when evidence changes their incentives. A defeated person is not automatically redeemed.
- There is one primary romance and no harem or competing romantic endgame.
