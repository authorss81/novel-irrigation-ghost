# Terminology

The story favors concrete water-work language over invented magical jargon. These terms should remain stable across chapters.

## General water works

- **Sluice:** A controllable gate or channel used to admit, release, or divide water. Sluices need maintenance and a named keeper during an emergency.
- **Weir:** A low barrier that measures or regulates flow. A weir can flood a neighborhood if its downstream channel is blocked.
- **Head:** The vertical height of water above a point. Higher head creates more pressure and danger at a gate.
- **Service window:** A bounded interval during which a community receives or releases water. A service window is both a physical schedule and a political promise.
- **Route:** A connected water path from a source to a use, drain, or destination. A route includes its gates, sediment, maintenance, and human custodians.
- **Route right:** A recognized claim to be included in decisions about a route. It does not guarantee unlimited water or automatic control.
- **Basin:** A drainage region organized around shared water movement. The Marrow Basin is both a geography and a political argument.
- **Sallow Reach:** A western agricultural region of villages, orchards, and canal fields.
- **Meltwater Verge:** A downstream floodplain where excess water becomes a public and agricultural danger.
- **Thorn Crown:** The northern and eastern source mountains, including cloud forest and high terraces.
- **Pale Teeth:** A dry salt basin containing ruins and buried aqueducts.

## Remembered hydraulics

- **Remembered hydraulics:** The practice of reading, naming, and maintaining water routes through material traces, witnesses, and durable records.
- **Wet line:** A physical or sensory trace showing that water once moved, or is still moving, through a place. A wet line proves contact, not ownership or intention. A raw wet line can be sensed before a route has a witness or durable record; it cannot, by itself, be deliberately moved.
- **Route name:** The shared address of a water path. It includes the physical course, the people affected, and the conditions under which the account was made.
- **Name:** In practical shorthand, a route name. A name is not a possession and should not be used as one.
- **Witness:** A person who has used, maintained, witnessed, or refused a route and accepts responsibility for speaking about it. A witness is not automatically a public official.
- **Counter-record:** A record preserved outside the official archive to challenge omissions, substitutions, or conflicting accounts.
- **Record stone:** A durable marker naming a route or a major junction, with dates and witnesses when available.
- **Blind water:** Water without a usable social address. It may be physically present but cannot be reliably directed by remembered hydraulics.
- **Unlisted:** A place, route, or community removed from an official or working record. Unlisting does not erase the place's people, but it makes its claims difficult to recognize.
- **Underlisted:** A place that survives physically and socially while being increasingly invisible to official maps, budgets, and memory magic.
- **Name debt:** The personal and communal cost of holding a route open against its proper conditions.
- **Consent fracture:** The failure pattern caused when a private memory is used as an anchor without informed consent. The route may split, the target may suffer a crosscurrent, and the person whose memory was used may lose orientation or a sensory detail.
- **Crosscurrent:** The dangerous result of forcing a route whose records, memory, and physical gates disagree.
- **Salt fever:** A physical illness caused by drinking or bathing in blind or badly addressed water. Symptoms include burning skin, confusion, metallic taste, fever, and loss of orientation. It can be treated, but severe exposure leaves lasting sensory damage.
- **Name fracture:** A severe name-debt condition in which a practitioner can no longer serve as a dependable route anchor.

## The ghost

- **Irrigation Ghost:** The pressure or voice that appears when an unlisted route, lost neighborhood, or buried water record remains physically present but socially unrecognized. It may contain fragments of many people and places. It is not automatically benevolent, omniscient, or a single dead person.
- **Ghostwork:** The practical work of identifying unlisted routes, restoring their witnesses, and reconciling their records. Ghostwork is engineering, local history, negotiation, and grief.
- **Counter-reader:** A trained person who preserves routes that an official system has rejected. Counter-readers are vulnerable to being called false, dangerous, or possessed.

## The One Map and related institutions

- **One Map:** A basin-wide hydrological calibration lattice built after the Ninefold Flood. It couples record stones and copper address plates to physical gate controls, so an official route name can close an inlet and open a pressure-relief overflow. It tries to keep one official route address authoritative. It is the series' central inherited system and final external pressure.
- **One Map Accord:** The legal and engineering agreement that authorized the One Map after the flood.
- **Blind Reservoir:** The physical storage beneath the old Orisene works where unlisted water gathers. It contains real water under extraordinary pressure and cannot be treated as a limitless reserve.
- **Clearwater:** A final emergency procedure that would force water toward Cresset's central basin and suppress all competing routes. Chancellor Boryn Vey intends to activate it unless he can force a more controlled outcome.
- **Counter-map:** A distributed collection of local, household, oral, and public records that can address more than one valid route at once. It is not a replacement single map; it is a method of keeping uncertainty visible.
- **Many Rivers Charter:** The final public structure for sharing routes and emergency authority among local assemblies. It is created through negotiation, not summoned by magic.

## Factions and offices

- **Cresset Water Office:** The city's public engineering department.
- **Basin Assembly:** The regional public council that approves major budgets and emergency service. Its authority is contested.
- **Open Channel Guild:** The contractor and sluicewright guild that maintains many high gates and controls specialized maintenance knowledge.
- **Route Assembly:** A local council with a recognized role in a specific route.
- **Quiet Archive:** A network of vaults and readers that removes unverified or dangerous memories from public circulation.
- **Meridian Compact:** A regional coalition controlling source-country gates and emergency standards.
- **Fair Copy:** The official regional record and court system that treats the One Map's address as legally authoritative.

## The Gauge

- **The Gauge:** A limited interface experienced by a practitioner in contact with a wet route, record stone, or strong trace. It can state an immediate physical condition, a missing witness, or a conflicting record. It cannot know the future, read minds, or solve political questions.
- **Gauge panel:** A brief in-world presentation of the Gauge's immediate statement. Keep it rare, plain, and tied to a scene. It should create a decision rather than deliver exposition.

A representative panel, not chapter prose:

> **The Gauge:** One route remembers. It asks for a witness.

## Naming conventions

- Characters use given names in ordinary speech; titles are used by institutions, not as constant honorifics.
- Places are named by residents first and official maps second. A place may have an official name, an older name, and a household name. The difference between them is often the mystery's evidence.
- Avoid using a new invented term in prose unless it is immediately defined through use. The world has enough water-work vocabulary already.
