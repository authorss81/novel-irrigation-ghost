# Power System: Remembered Hydraulics

## What the magic is

The world's magic is a natural relationship between water, material traces, and shared memory. A moving body of water carries traces of the routes, hands, weather, and substances it has touched. Repeated acts leave a pattern that can be felt by a sensitive person or read from a record stone. Magic does not read private thoughts, rewrite morality, or create matter.

People call the practice **remembered hydraulics** because a water route becomes practically addressable when three things agree. These conditions are required for a deliberate naming or transfer. A practitioner can sense a raw wet line with only physical contact; that trace is evidence, not yet a route he or she can move.

1. **A physical trace:** a channel, mineral stain, tool mark, instrument reading, or repeated journey.
2. **A witness:** someone who has used, maintained, or refused the route and accepts responsibility for speaking about it.
3. **A record:** a durable account that names the route, the people affected, and the conditions under which the account was made.

A fourth condition is required for deliberate redistribution: **legitimacy**. The people who will receive the water, bear flooding, pay for maintenance, or lose access must have a real route right. This does not require unanimous agreement. It requires that no affected community is hidden from the decision.

If a route is physically present but lacks an accepted name, it is **blind**. Blind water is still water. It cannot be directed reliably by memory magic, and opening it can produce a pressure surge, brine contamination, or a flood in the wrong place.

## The Gauge

The **Gauge** is the name for the limited interface that appears to a practitioner who is touching a wet route, a damaged record stone, or a sustained memory trace. It is not a sentient assistant, a complete database, or a source of free answers. It translates a local pattern into a few plain statements.

The Gauge can say:

> One route remembers. It asks for a witness.

> The water is here. Its name is not.

> The record was changed after the flood.

> A second hand is already holding this route.

The Gauge can be wrong when the record it is reading is forged, when several people remember the same place incompatibly, or when a more powerful route masks a smaller one. It never provides exact probabilities, item lists, quest instructions, or a solution to a political dispute. Panels should be rare, brief, and connected to the physical scene.

## Core rules

### Conservation

No stage creates water. A concealed shaft, a sealed gate, or a difference in hydraulic head can make water appear to run uphill; that is ordinary pressure movement, not magic creating a new source. The protagonist can:

- reconnect a dry channel to groundwater, a river, a reservoir, or snowmelt;
- move existing water through a known route;
- open an atmospheric corridor that lets distant moisture arrive;
- release water held in the Blind Reservoir.

A large restoration always has a source, a distance, a pressure cost, and a receiving basin. If the story shows a well refilling, the refill must be supported by water already present, a rainfall, or an opened connection.

### Address, not ownership

A route name tells water where it is going and what conditions it passes through. It does not make one person the owner of the water. A name can be held by a household, a village assembly, a public office, or a temporary emergency coalition. A route with one permanent keeper is more vulnerable to that keeper's death or manipulation, which is why the final system distributes authority.

### Consent and coercion

Opening a route without the consent of affected people can physically work, especially under pressure, but it creates a **crosscurrent**. Consequences may include:

- water that reverses direction near a person carrying an unconsented record;
- salt fever and skin burns in the receiving basin;
- loss of common dreams or a temporary inability to recognize a familiar person;
- gates that fail open or fail closed when the route is next used.

Coercion is not magically impossible. It is politically and physically expensive, and the damage is not erased when the offending keeper repents.

A private memory is not a public record. A practitioner cannot use another person's private memory as the sole anchor without informed, explicit consent. If someone is coerced, the operation may run through physical force, but it creates a **consent fracture**: the route splits, the receiving community bears the crosscurrent, and the person whose memory was taken loses orientation or a sensory detail. A coerced anchor is never a clean shortcut.

### The memory cost

Every deliberate memory operation spends **name debt** from the practitioner and, when a route is mishandled, from the surrounding community. The cost is not a simple experience bar. It is specific and personal.

- **Minor debt:** temporary loss of smell, taste, a recent date, a familiar route, or a practiced hand motion; headaches, tremor, fever, or inability to read fine instruments.
- **Severe debt:** a lasting loss of a face, a word, a skill, a year, or a beloved sensory association. A practitioner may become unable to remember a route they once opened.
- **Name fracture:** the practitioner can no longer serve as a reliable anchor. They may be mistaken for a ghost or refuse to lead a route because their own identity has become unstable.
- **Community bleed:** a repeatedly forced route causes shared memory fragments to blur, crops to fail in patterned rings, or wells to produce brackish water. It can be repaired, but the repair takes years and cannot restore every lost detail.

Rest does not automatically erase debt. A lost detail can sometimes return when a trusted person helps the practitioner reconstruct it, but the initial failure remains part of the record. Consequences are most dramatic when a character chooses to pay a cost they understand rather than when a surprise punishment appears.

### Failure and false records

A false route can move water convincingly. That is why forged records are dangerous. A contradictory record produces a crosscurrent: the water follows the most emotionally forceful version while the physical gate follows the most recently maintained one. A practitioner can be technically correct and still cause a flood if the people relying on the record were never told the truth.

The system has no moral detector. It responds to material, physical, and relational consistency. This is why evidence, maintenance, and consent matter more than willpower.

### Natural and magical limits

Ordinary gravity, evaporation, soil, elevation, sediment, and weather remain active. Magic can alter access and direction, not abolish physics. A route can be opened for minutes, hours, or as long as its physical infrastructure holds. A high-stage network still needs pumps, gates, keepers, repair crews, and political agreement.

## Power stages

The stages are earned through practice, failure, relationships, and changed decisions. A higher stage is not automatically more powerful in every situation; it gives a wider range of choices and larger obligations.

### Stage 0: Wrench and rule

Tarin's ordinary competence.

- **Capability:** Surveying, pump repair, pressure calculations, gate operation, crew leadership, and practical reading of forged measurements.
- **Advantage:** He can solve many problems without magic, so he is useful in a world where the power is rare.
- **Limit:** He can move only the water his physical plant can reach. He has no dependable way to perceive a route's social history.
- **Story requirement:** The opening must establish this competence before the first supernatural event.

### Stage 1: Trace

Tarin can perceive a **wet line** left by a route, ghost, or hidden water source.

- **Capability:** Identify the direction and approximate age of a trace, feel a pressure anomaly, recognize a route's unresolved place-memory, and locate likely record stones.
- **Example:** In a dry channel, he can feel that water passed uphill recently, but he cannot open the channel or know who used it.
- **Cost:** Nosebleed, vertigo, altered taste, and temporary confusion. A false memory can make the trace feel convincing; observation must be checked against tools and other people.
- **Limit:** One route at a time; no reliable long-distance sensing; cannot distinguish grief from physical flow.

### Stage 2: Name

Tarin can anchor and open one narrow route by combining a trace, a physical record, and a willing witness.

- **Capability:** Move a limited amount of water through a named channel, usually a gate, culvert, well, or small canal. He can briefly stabilize a route while the infrastructure is repaired.
- **Example:** He reopens a small branch to Glassstep for one service window, then must close it before the pressure rises.
- **Cost:** A specific sensory or personal memory is placed at risk. The route may remember him as its keeper even if he refuses the role.
- **Limit:** The route must be known enough to name. He cannot open a reservoir from a map alone.

### Stage 3: Bend

Tarin can redirect a substantial local current, reservoir outlet, or underground flow when the physical route is sound and a crew can maintain the gates.

- **Capability:** Move water between two named basins over a planned interval, with a visible pressure and flood risk.
- **Example:** He reroutes part of the Sere through a restored branch, but the unused water must go somewhere real.
- **Cost:** His name becomes tied to the route. A failure in the route damages his ability to sense nearby water and can force a lasting memory loss. He cannot abandon a route while it remains open without transfer or collapse.
- **Limit:** A single practitioner cannot coordinate more than a few gates, and a legal or community refusal can make a physically correct route unusable.

### Stage 4: Braid

Tarin can coordinate several local routes and assemblies into a temporary network.

- **Capability:** Balance water among multiple named watersheds, share a limited amount of groundwater, and make a route that exists in several records speak as one operational network.
- **Example:** Sallow Reach and Cresset exchange a carefully timed service window instead of fighting over a single source.
- **Cost:** He must give up exclusive control over timing and information. Every participating community can see his decisions and withdraw consent. Prolonged use causes identity bleed: he may briefly mistake another route keeper's grief for his own.
- **Limit:** A braid is only as honest as its least protected participant. Propaganda and unequal power can distort it.

### Stage 5: Sky braid

Tarin can use remembered storm corridors to guide existing moisture toward a named basin.

- **Capability:** Open a window for rain or snow to arrive from a distant weather system, increase the chance of a natural front crossing a remembered source, and temporarily hold a moisture path open.
- **Example:** The team uses an old mountain rain record to give the Thorn Crown a storm that would otherwise pass over the wrong ridge.
- **Cost:** The weather route is not owned by one settlement. If the team manipulates it without regional agreement, another basin can lose its rain. A severe use can erase a unique local weather memory from living tradition, though surviving records can slow that loss.
- **Limit:** He cannot command temperature, create a storm from clear sky, or make rain fall evenly. The result depends on real weather and real terrain.

### Stage 6: Open channel

At the final stage, Tarin can anchor a watershed-wide emergency network only by giving up his right to be its sole anchor.

- **Capability:** Coordinate the One Map's former inputs through many local records, route assemblies, and keepers. He can stabilize a shared flow during a flood or drought, but he cannot use it by command.
- **Example:** The basin restores water through dozens of local custodians, each able to refuse or amend a transfer. Tarin holds only the temporary central braid and loses it when the network is stable.
- **Cost:** He must accept loss, shared blame, and incomplete records. His ability to sense individual routes becomes less reliable; in exchange, the system no longer dies with one person. The final cost is a permanent personal change: he can no longer keep a private, perfect mental map of the places he has saved.
- **Limit:** It works only when the local records are not identical copies of one authority. A network made of one map is still a One Map in another form.

## How stages are earned

- **Stage 1** comes from a dangerous accident, tools, and a failed attempt to understand a trace.
- **Stage 2** requires an apprenticeship with a reader, a physical repair, and a cost Tarin cannot immediately repay.
- **Stage 3** comes from a flood operation, a team, and the consequences of a route he chose to open.
- **Stage 4** requires political trust and a willingness to share authority, not a longer training montage.
- **Stage 5** is learned through weather failure, regional negotiation, and accepting responsibility for unequal consequences.
- **Stage 6** is not a final explosion technique. It is the result of building institutions and relationships that can function without him.

## Power ceiling and ending constraint

Tarin never becomes a solo god who can reverse drought by thinking. His strongest version can make a shared route work for a limited time, but only while people, gates, records, and sources continue to cooperate. The final victory must remain physically and politically possible without his presence.
