## The job

Write **Chapters 31–40** as complete finished scenes. This movement has four jobs and one that is fixed.

1. **The crosscurrent is genuinely physical, and Yara Sen's clinic is the battlefield.** A partially opened blind route puts brine through the lower terrace and the sickness is not a metaphor. **The Office attempts to classify it as contractor negligence** — its own contractor, the pumping plant, paid by the can, who stopped being paid on the 2nd and did not cause any of it. The classification is convenient, it is administratively correct in form, and it is how an office protects itself.
2. **The only water available to the blue steps is the same discharge that made the children ill.** This is the movement's central dilemma and it is already built: the run stopped when the pump stopped, the run is a contractor's rising main, and the chamber it draws from has never been tested by anybody. **Restarting the plant does not make the water safe; it makes it move harder, and a body of water that is drawn harder gives up more of what is on its own surfaces.** A district that has run out of clean water and a child at the fourth stage must now choose between no water and the same bad water, and neither of those is a solution.
3. **A Guild inspection team arrives, and Iven Marrow offers Tarin work, access, and a real education in exchange for leaving Glassstep out of the record.** Tarin does not accept. **Marrow does not press, because he is a patient man.** He is the second rung of the antagonist ladder and no more: he believes expertise should be owned and sold, he is not a mage, he does not know the One Map's name, and he must not become a bigger threat than the man who signed two certificates. He is set up here for Volume 2, where his offer is still on the table.
4. **Tarin's old flood closure is raised against him at an internal hearing as evidence that he cannot be trusted with a route.** He signed an emergency closure at nineteen; it saved the riverside hospital and trapped people in a lower courtyard. **Mara Quill testifies about his competence and is asked, by Tarin's own advocate, whether his judgment is sound. She answers honestly and it costs him.** She does not soften it to protect him and he does not ask her to.
5. **THE FIXED BEAT — Chapter 39 is a refusal.** Desperate for a clean anchor, Tarin considers using a Glassstep resident's private memory of the breach. **He does not.** The route splits, the pressure is lost, Glassstep's pump fails for two days, and the family that is hosting him keeps his tools fed and tells him plainly that he was right not to use it. **It is not a third performance of the naming set piece: he never gets as far as naming anything, and the event of the chapter is a door that stays shut.** See the differentiation table in `outline/volume-01.md` and read Chapters 6 and 10 before writing it.

**The midsummer review is not forced until Chapter 46.** It may be quoted, deferred, or argued about, and Movement IV is a good place to argue about it. Movement V is the review, and it is the last ten chapters of the volume.

## Fixed starting position — Day 46, Tuesday the 15th evening

Everything below is already on the page. Do not re-establish it; start inside it.

- **The lane's water: 950 gallons in the yard cistern, one foot seven inches**, on the evening of the 14th. The three tanks and the pails are empty. The dispensary has **80 gallons** of clean water bought at a shilling the gallon on the 13th and will draw 70 a day, so it is **gone on the 18th**. **£3 16s remains in the bucket — 912 pence, which at the price the district actually paid on the 13th is 76 gallons: a day and a bit, and a cart, and a man willing to drive it. No municipal supply is restored in this movement.** The 950 is the same crosscurrent water that has been in the lane since the 1st, and its price has been falling all month because there is less of it.
- **Sab Doulton, seven, of the second step, is at the beginning of the fourth stage** — palms cracked, sick twice, twice asking her mother where the front door is. Nine children with the marks, four at three points, five at two. The marks last a fortnight. The fortnight ended on the 4th. It is the 15th. **Yara Sen's 70-gallon figure (16 + 10 + 44) is still arithmetically correct and is no longer a usable clinical figure, and she has said so in a yard.** She has said it out loud so that nobody can use it against her later.
- **The pump has been stopped for thirteen days.** The run has been dry since the night of the 2nd. The discharge was a contractor's, from a beam pump and a horse gin on the works at Ashgate, run against a certificate certifying the chamber void, and shut by an office on the Wednesday of the second with no letter. **A boy of about fifteen has been on a staging every night since the second and has not been paid since the second.**
- **The chamber is intact and measured.** Level four, at the end of an adit off the settling tank. Water standing **9 ft 4 in** above the adit floor; outside ground at the filter house door is **1 ft 9 in** above that; **11 ft 1 in above the outside ground at the door of the works**, unchanged, with a line cut in the rock at the level. Its roof is a flagged floor with a hearth, a flue, a chimney breast, a cut lead stub of a floor drain, and eleven steps of stair hanging with nothing at the top — and it is a street, not a house. **Two hundred and forty-one steps up to a door under a store.**
- **Above it: Ashgate.** Eleven houses, thirty-four persons, about thirty years, no row, no schedule entry, no keeper. They call the place Ashgate; on every sheet in this city Ashgate is a structure with a closure date and a warden's seal. **Nell Drust, 39,** has kept the row for sixteen years and what she keeps is the water. The water comes up out of the ground under her own kitchen floor at about **103 gallons an hour** — six gallons on the well-head slab in three minutes thirty seconds, checked twice — and **it has never been tested by anybody and no instrument in the basin would test it.** Tarin has said in a signed public document that he cannot promise it will be the same water tomorrow. **They do not know they are on a chamber and nobody is to tell them who put them there or why, because nobody knows and the text says so.**
- **The paper.** Pell's condition is in the district book, in the commissioner's registry, and on its way to a third copy in Vellum and a fourth somewhere he cannot reach. Tarin's engineer's statement — six numbered paragraphs, signed on the 15th, the first thing he has signed since the 10th — is with it. Four boxes on the warden's return are filled; **the fifth will read *keeper, none held*.** The date passed on the 22nd and was accepted. The source is a schedule reference and a chamber, and neither is a document. The witness cannot be found.
- **A district examiner** was promised before the 27th of next month and has been asked for *early* by the district's own letter of the 12th, which the district expects to succeed. **The letter has been in the office five days.**
- **Hanne Roeg's letter asks, in her own hand, a question nobody in this city has ever been asked in a city document: whether a district with no row may ask a settlement with no row for water.** It is unanswered. And it is the best question in the book, because a hundred and twenty-six people with 950 gallons and a seven-year-old at the fourth stage are about to have to answer it, and the answer is a form of words that will be written down in a yard.
- **Halden Roe.** Two gauge certificates in a deal box in a store at the works, one at decommissioning and one four years ago, both certifying the chamber void, no residual head, no standing water, no maintenance required, both in his hand, both with *maintained* beneath in the same pen, thirty yards from a chalk board reading 5·9. **He is not threatened in this movement and must not be removed, confronted, or spoken to. He does not know the certificates have been read. Nobody is currently asking who falsified them.** He is removed in Chapters 46–49, by arithmetic, in front of the city, and not before.
- **The condition of the man:** suspended since the 10th, **two open charges and never joined** (tampering with city records; unauthorized opening of a sealed structure and service of water from a reservoir the ledger records as closed), a third forming over the descent and not yet charged, no office, no money, no crew, a bad left shoulder, a hand that will not seat, a cup of tea with no taste in it since the 7th, and a blue book containing three candidate explanations for the worst morning of his life that he refuses to guess between, four and not one about his mother, and a chamber he has measured and cannot use.
- **The thump** under the terrace ran three a second, without variation, and never stopped at any hour, right up until it stopped on the night of the 2nd, in the middle of Niko's hand on a coil of somebody else's rope. **It has not restarted and nothing in this batch should restart it or explain it.** It is a plant, not a mystery. Nobody in this movement is asking who owns it.

## Required character changes

- **Tarin** must be further from certainty than at any point in the volume so far, and closer to a person. He has a measured chamber, a read certificate and a signed statement, and none of them is a route. **Chapter 39 is his refusal, and it must be a rule he lives by rather than a rule the plot introduces** — Chapters 6, 10, 17, 21 and 24 have all already shown the pattern, and the batch may *point at* the pattern and may not *establish* it.
- **Yara Sen** is the movement's battlefield and it is her clinic, her book, her arithmetic and her refusal to be anybody's ally. **She must not become a symbol of conscience, and she must not be right about everything.** There should be at least one thing in this movement she gets wrong, or cannot do, or is told by a patient that she has.
- **Mara Quill** testifies and it costs him. She answers honestly, including the part that is against him, and she does not soften it to protect a man she does not trust. **She does not become fond, and she does not become a reward.** If she is on a page in this batch it is because she is doing a job.
- **Iven Marrow** is credible, patient, specific, and not frightening. He has read more about the mountain gates in a week than Tarin has in a year and he is right about most of it. He offers a real education. **He must not press after the refusal, he must not threaten, and he must not be caught out.** He is the Volume 2 problem and he is set up here.
- **Niko Venn** has said out loud that he is not going to chase the Water Office. **He must not go back on it to be useful, and he must not be given a route.** What he is now is a man with a cut, a chalk, a set of notched pebbles, and an adit nobody in this city is going to fund. ## Hard mechanics — the prose must not contradict these

- **The head is 5·89 m** and it is a real pressure head. It has not changed and Tarin cannot increase it. **Nothing in this batch makes more water appear.**
- **The gauge log reads `5·89` in Office notation and the engineer says "five point eight nine."** Keep that split. Record keepers write it; the engineer says it. **The contractors' tally board at the works reads `5·9` and `5·7` in their own hand, and that is a different instrument in a different notation and is not a contradiction.**
- **A channel stops at the balance point.** Any attempt reasoned in this batch is reasoned from the same physics.
- **A gauged stop-log meters a branch; an ungauged one does not.**
- **The eleven-hour window does not repeat and no third window is won in this movement.** The number stays eleven because the source does not grow. The Chapter 46–49 window is the same eleven hours.
- **Two charges remain open, never merged, upgraded or resolved.** A hearing is mentioned in Chapter 37 and must not be held to a conclusion. **The third charge forms but is not filed in this batch**, and if it is mentioned it is mentioned as paperwork nobody has got to.
- **The Ashgate maintenance log runs about a hundred and eighty years** of a shut gate at 5·7 and a bit, four entries a year, 724 leaves. The removed reading is the last line, the year before the flood, and it ends in **ref. 118**, which is the closure entry that plated Glassstep's terrace outfalls and took the row off the distribution schedule. The substituted leaf is twenty-six years old; the reading on it is a hundred and eighty-one years old.
- **The marker stone's other face is blank and stays blank for the whole volume.** Chapter 17's second chisel attempt cut nothing and the book entry says *Blank face not cut.* Chapter 24's third attempt, on a different stone at the works, also cut nothing. **Do not write it as cut, and do not put the crooked mark on it.**
- **Every cistern figure is counted forward and re-derived, never carried.** The standing figure is **950 gallons = one foot seven inches** on the evening of the 14th, in a cistern ten feet across, twelve to the coping, **eight feet of room, 4,700 gallons of room.** 78.5 sq ft × 8 ft × 7.48 = 4,697 gal; gallons ÷ 7.48 ÷ 78.5 = depth. **Evaporation is 50 gallons a day off the open vessels and is not a per-inch figure. Yara's requirement is 70 gallons a day — four children at 4 gallons = 16, five at 2 = 10, 117 others at 3 pints a head = 44 — and a fortnight of it is 980, rounded on the page to a thousand.** **£8 at 8d = 240 gallons; at 9d = 213; at a shilling = 160. 15s 4d = 184 pence, at 8d = 23 gallons. The bucket now holds £3 16s = 912 pence, which at 8d is 114 gallons and at a shilling — the price the district actually paid on the 13th — is 76 gallons, which is a day and a bit, and a cart, and a man willing to drive it. The binding constraint is always the cart, never the money.** If you restate any of these, restate the derivation.
- **Nothing in this batch may be described as the reservoir, the Blind Reservoir, the One Map, or Boryn Vey by those names.** What Tarin can name is a gate, a head, a channel, a plate, a chamber, a settlement, a shaft, a stair, a filter house, a beam pump, a horse gin, a gauge, a certificate and a form.
