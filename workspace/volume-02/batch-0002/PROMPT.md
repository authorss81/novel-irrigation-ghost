## The job

Write **Chapters 61–70** as complete finished scenes. Six jobs and one that is fixed.

1. **The weep hole is a break and not an overflow.** Four miles up the Kerrow valley, above the lime kiln, on the same main. Two days, four men, a heat, a boy of fifteen's knees. They find the crown joint **four and a half inches** across, a **second** weep above it, the stone open above the first, and **the bank on the west side of the road soft in a drought.** There is no villain, no conspiracy, and nobody to catch. **Eleven years of an unvisited chamber and six years of a shut sluice, and a joint that opened.** The volume's crisis is on the page and it is ordinary.
2. **The road is the evidence.** The man at the toll bar keeps a book that is not a toll book and has had **six years of a road with no inspection on it** in it. He will not read it out and will answer if he is asked. **Tarin asks him.** It is the cheapest and best piece of evidence in the batch and it costs a day and a half-mile walk and nothing else, and the book has been in his hut the whole time, which is the volume's thesis in one object: a route is a chain of people who open, close, inspect and question it.
3. **The amendment is in the Reach's own copy of its own schedule, in a chest at Brant, in a hand nobody in the Reach can read.** A keeper's name struck out six years ago and **supply district 14, North Sallow** written in its place. Twenty-six years in a chest. **Four documents for one reach**, and this batch names all four.
4. **Yara Sen comes west for four days and refuses to certify a figure that would help the Reach, and leaves a girl of seven at the fourth point sixty-one miles east.** She comes because of the fourth line of a letter that was not about evidence. **She is not a reward and she is not the volume's conscience and she is not right about everything.**
5. **THE FIXED BEAT — the fourteenth of next month is the count, and it comes out incomplete, and the incompleteness is published in a yard by the woman who asked for it, without being asked.** Three rods: the plank, the tank, and the flume sluice. **The flume sluice is chained and sealed and is not going to be opened, so the third rod has nothing to measure**, and Perdie Ammid says so in front of four hundred people, and the figures are printed and nailed up anyway, incomplete, with the reason under them.
6. **THE FIXED BEAT — the fifteenth of next month the window opens lawfully and is stopped at about an hour and forty minutes by a person.** The seal is broken properly, with two men present and the six-year-old inspection read out first, and the gate is opened, and the flume runs. And **Perdie Ammid stands in the long drain and stops it with the third book in her hand**, and the clause she invokes is of the Reach's own schedule of 1902, and it forbids any other discharge into the long drain, and **she is right, and Tarin Rusk had been in the Reach ten days and had not asked for that book.** Nothing supernatural happens. A woman with a document stops a route, and it is the best scene in the batch.

## The fixed starting position — Tuesday the 2nd of next month

## Hard mechanics — the prose must not contradict these

- **The head at the Sallow Head is `7·31` metres, spoken *seven point three one*, and nobody measures it in this volume.** Keep the `7·31` / *seven point three one* split exactly as Volume 01 kept `5·89` / *five point eight nine*.
- **The tank is 6,000 gallons of room**: 20 ft by 15 ft is three hundred feet of floor; 2 ft 8 in is eight hundred feet of water; **a foot of water is seven and a half gallons in this basin, so 800 × 7½ = 6,000.** The mark is at 2 ft 6¼ in. **There is no tank flow rate, because the tank does not flow.** Any chapter that gives the tank gallons an hour is wrong.
- **The chamber is nineteen feet deep and the silt is nine feet two inches.** The card's *silt 2 ft 4* is the outlet-channel layer below the sill and is a different place.
- **The throat: 860 on the twenty-ninth, 833 on the first, 850 printed, and the card says in ink that it is not a figure anybody should lean on.** The plank: 193 and 191. **850 against 190 is four and a half times and 660 gallons an hour is in the ground.** Every new figure in this batch is measured twice and printed with both trials, or it is not printed.
- **A plate is either in water or it is not. A bigger bore reads a bigger number. There is no head in this basin at which a plate reads dry.** Any new gauge or plate reading must obey that, and a dry plate means the plate is above the water and nothing else.
- **A channel stops at the balance point.** The flume in Chapter 69 is gravity off a tank and there is nine inches of head on it and it is not a wizardry: a six-inch launder running about three-quarters of an inch deep at 850 gallons an hour takes almost no head, and the chapter should say so, and the reason the flume can be dry while the tank is full is **the level of the flume's own invert at the sluice, not the tank's level**, and Chapter 67's walk of five miles should establish that as a fact about the flume and not as a surprise.
- **A gauged stop-log meters a branch and an ungauged one does not; a channel stops at the balance point; a route with only a man in it cannot be held.** All three still hold and none of them is stretched.
- **The Guild's day rates do not change:** labourer four, sluicewright five, keeper six, inspector of high gates six and eight, foreman seven and six, master by contract. Tarin's wage is five shillings a day, six days, and the cottage at no rent, and by Chapter 70 he is in week seven of fourteen.
- **No chapter ends on a cut-off. Every chapter lands on a completed beat.**
- **Nothing in this batch may be described as the reservoir, the Blind Reservoir, the One Map, or Boryn Vey by those names.** A Guild document may use the city and the Guild's own words. What a character can name is a gate, a head, a channel, a plate, a chamber, a pipe, a joint, a silt, a seal, a padlock, a schedule, a clause, a book, a card, a return, a notice and a witness.
