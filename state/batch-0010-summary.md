## The document that was the volume's door, and its exact words

**Item one of the schedule of the works of the Reach, printed in this village in 1902, forbids any other water whatsoever to the long drain, whether from a flume, a waste pipe, a culvert, a spill, a torn joint, or a wet where.** A window down the flume is the fifteenth of the month before this one again, two and a half times over, so it is not lawful without a thing in writing from the assembly of the villages, on a day it names.
**Sef Ordry asked for a DECLARATION and not an amendment, on the page, in Chapter 91, and the reason he gives is the strongest argument in the volume: a narrow thing can be got through nine men in a morning and a wide thing cannot, and a hole cut in the strongest document the Reach owns in a drought is worse than a second document beside it.**
**The declaration does four things and no more.** It says **item one is not amended and stands as printed.** It says **the West Flume is a channel from the works to the fields and the long drain is the drain of the fields, and these were two different things in 1902 in the words used by the men who wrote them, and they are two different things now in the words used by the people who have used them, and the assembly says so in those words and not in its own.** It says **it does not resolve, and does not need to resolve, and does not admit, whether the six-inch waste pipe is a drain of the fields or a channel from the works, and that no person shall take anything in it as an admission, a concession, or a precedent upon that pipe.** And it consents to **one window and no other**, on one named night, for four hours and six minutes, with the chain back on before daylight, and it does not survive the night, and it is not a precedent, not a rate, not a supply, and it appoints nobody to anything.
**Eight of nine signed. The ninth refused in the room, out loud, and gave a reason that is his own and not a rule: a declaration of what a thing is is not an amendment, and he does not believe it, and there are nineteen houses at the end of that drain.** He was not thanked, nobody argued with him, and the man of thirty-eight with one hand said afterwards that it was the only honest thing said at that table and that it cost that man more than the eight men would ever pay.
**Armist Sarg signed for himself. Nesta Sarg was not asked, because there is no form in this basin that would put her in it, and she had said so in front of eleven people on the fifteenth. He did not look at her and she did not look at him, and neither said one word, and this is the fourth year and it is not reconciled and it must not be.**

## The do-not-stage list, and what this batch did instead

**The batch has one room of about nine** (the shop at Brant on the fourteenth), **one room of about twenty with nine men at one end of it** (the assembly), **one board in a city with about a hundred people on the pavement**, and **one press in a town of about four thousand people with about nine people in the room.** There is no second room of a hundred and forty, no second public document read out in full, and no hearing.
**The volume's smallest ending beat was spent once, in Chapter 98, and the strike-off of Chapter 60 and the village recut of Chapter 70 were not staged.**

## The ending image, which is on the page and is not negotiable

**At dusk on the Friday the twenty-third, at the top of the lane at Nether Sallow, a man is standing in a field of turned earth with a six-gallon pail and a watch and a roll of tarred twine, filling a butt at the plank.** The pail is Nesta Sarg's, the one with the mark inside it and the split in the handle repaired with a wire and a bit of pitch, and it is not lent. The butt is about two hundred and forty gallons of room and takes about an hour and sixteen minutes and forty trips up a bank, and it will be filled again in the morning. The watch is in his left hand and he does not use it.
**The twine came off the stake at a culvert, out of a tin frame ninety years old, off a notice that said CLOSED. No water. No entry. Under penalty, and that notice named no office and named no person, and it was fetched and turned over for the first time on the twenty-seventh day of the month before this one by a man who could not read and who had walked two miles and four chains with it and had not known what was on it. It was not cut off. It has been on the pail's handle through a count, a window, a chain, a card and a declaration.**
**A man is going to do that every evening now. There is a day when somebody has to say his name. Tonight nobody had to.**
**And the notice's date is the fifteenth. And it is the fifteenth.**
**No month is named on the image, the batch's last day was not moved to reach a fifteenth, and the last sentence of the volume is that he could not leave.**

## The two books, in two hands, in two places

**Perdie Ammid's last entry in that valley is four lines on the seventh sheet, and it is the volume's last document:** his name is in a column headed *days lost, and by whom, and to what* in the four field books of this Reach, in her hand, which says one day, taken by T. Rusk, of no office, out of a Saturday morning, and is in a cart; and in a day-book in a shed at the foot of an embankment, in a man of thirty-one's hand, which says four men at three points on a launder and a boy with a slate. **Four hundred yards apart, going in opposite directions, and neither of them is a record of a route.**
