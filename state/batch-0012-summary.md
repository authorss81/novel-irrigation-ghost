## WHAT THIS BATCH IS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH

**A magistrate measures a bank four people made good on a Sunday, writes down truly that they made it good, and says in the same breath that the reason he is writing it down is so that it can be charged to the door they live in — which is the whole of the batch in one sentence.** The row is counted mouth by mouth, door by door, on the court's condition of *if willing*, and Corrie Lille gives the count of eleven off his own hook and declines to give his name, once, and is not argued with, and the refusal is recorded as unwilling and bought him nothing whatever, because a rate comes through a door and not through a name. The Memory Office establishes that the pencil line inside the back board of the flood book is the hand of a **desk** and not a man, in court-supply pencil from a batch that was exhausted inside a fortnight fourteen years ago, and that **no return was ever recorded because the book went out on a writ and the writs are kept in another office in another ward**, and a requisition to that office is entered and read back by Oriel Fenn and is not answered. Mara Quill puts her own name in a register against a withheld sheet and cannot say why. A man is asked, on a form, how many papers were read out to him in his hearing, and writes one, and it is true, and three days later a woman of fifty-four reads out the other number in a shop in front of nine people. **And every evening a man fills a butt at six, and on the seventh of next month he fills it at four in the morning in the dark so that he can be a hundred minutes late to a bank, and four other men fill it for him at half past three and the shutter says so.**

## The five jobs, and where each stands

1. **The bank view and the willing tally, without a victory.** Done Ch 111–112, 117, 120. **The bank view finds the bank partly held by residents' work and says so on paper, and the truth is the instrument of the charge.** The tally comes mouth by mouth, door by door, with one refusal that stands and is not argued with. Countable costs a rate on six doors, a valuation in a year by a man who will not stay the night, a number a stranger will write against a door, and a door that can be sold for arrears. **The rate is nineteen shillings and twopence and Sella says the point is not the money and the point is that it never ends.**
2. **The pencil line, one step and no more.** Done Ch 113. Desk-hand (a docket, series 411, the Receipts desk of the examination table), ink batch (court-supply, batch eighteen, exhausted in the fortnight of issue), date (that fortnight, fourteen years ago, agreeing with the line's own date), and **no return ever recorded, because the book went out on a writ and the writs are in the Low Ward.** **The missing pages' content is not delivered, the taker is not named, no man at the desk is named, the 1843 book is separated once in Ch 114 and joined to nothing, and the volume's midpoint is not touched.**
3. **Bend socially conditioned.** Done Ch 115 and 116. Shared-risk work succeeds modestly: six people lay the stone to a line, with a bond, a joint and a date, and the boy's own closing observation is that nobody chose the hour — a clerk named six o'clock because the drain is lowest then, and the man who fills nineteen butts at six in the evening is the one man certain to be elsewhere. Knowing-without-showing is refused twice, in two different registers: **timing** in Ch 115 (*six o'clock is a butt*) and **scope** in Ch 116 (*a well is a hole, and a pump is a machine*). **No new technique, sense, range or name.**
4. **Relationship damage compounded, nothing repaired.** Done Ch 113, 118, 119. The drawer's pressure is now visible in a register with a name in it: *one sheet withheld from publication, officer M. Quill, reason withheld by the officer.* **Tarin does not learn that she holds it.** The consequence she did not intend is that publishing made him a witness of record: *of no district, of no office* is now in a fair copy in Vellum, and he has had to give evidence in writing at a wall because he cannot leave a valley, and he wrote a smaller number than the truth in a box, and a boy of fifteen noticed, and Perdie Ammid wrote the other number down in a shop and read it out twice, and he said *yes* out loud in front of nine people. **Mara is not told the eleven names and not told the number.**
5. **The butt every evening, and once in collision.** Shown or named in all ten chapters. **The collision is Ch 115 and it is physical: the filling is an hour and sixteen at six in the light, and three hours and a quarter at four in the dark, and it cost him the shoulder he carried the hod with for two hours on the Saturday, and it cost him the butt itself, which four men filled at half past three and the shutter records.**

## Three things a next writer must not undo

1. **The two numbers on the witness schedule, and which is which.** *One* is the number of papers read out to that witness in that hearing, and it is the answer the form asked for, and it is true. *Three* is the number of numbered sheets read out in a stone hut at the culvert on the thirty-first, and Perdie Ammid is the only person who can say so, and she said it in a shop in front of nine people and put it in a numbered sheet. **Tarin said the word *yes* out loud on the eleventh. Nobody argued. The boy knows. Tarin does not know that Perdie has already decided what she will do about the letter.**
2. **Corrie Lille's refusal bought nothing, and that is the point, not a failure.** He gave the count and withheld the name on the fourth, it was recorded as unwilling and not argued with, and on the twelfth a clerk told him at his own door that a rate comes through the door and not through the name, so there is no box on any form in which a refusal could have done anything. **Do not give him a late compensation, and do not let anybody in a later batch say the refusal was in vain in a register sense. It was in vain in exactly one sense and that sense is the batch's whole argument.**
3. **Perdie's letter is four lines and unsent, and the fourth line is not for Iven Marrow.** It is hers, and she has said it in a shop, and she will not send a sentence with a counting in it to a man whose trade is counting. **It is not resolved, he is not thanked, and the two lines of it that have existed since the sixteenth are still the first two.**

## The trims, and the one chapter that was written twice

**The batch stood at 29,760 with Chapter 0120 at 3,926 and Chapter 0113 at 3,581, and the overage was taken out by deletion and not by rewording.** Two things happened that a later pass should know about.

**Chapter 0118 was written once entirely wrong and was rewritten whole.** The first version was contaminated with Volume 01 material — a shed day-book at the Sallow Head, a man in a cupboard, a launder in February — none of which belongs in this valley or to this man, and it invented that the closure was signed on a particular day of a particular month and that the schedule was a number of men, which is the disclosure the volume refuses. The rewrite is the one on the page. **Nothing from the first version of 0118 survived, and no fact in this batch about the Riverside closure is stated in Chapter 0118 except that it was signed at nineteen and that the witness declined both the schedule and the number, which was already on sheet 33 before this batch began.**

**The trims, all of them deletion.** Chapter 0113 lost the second half of one sentence and nine words of a store keeper's sentence. Chapter 0120 lost two paragraphs of door-by-door restatement, the third door's answer was cut by a third, and the closing catalogue was halved because Chapter 0110 already owns that cadence. Chapter 0111 lost a line of Cobb's. Chapter 0112 lost a line of the tallyman's. **No scene was removed and no beat was moved; the batch is 29,437.**

**One stylistic note a reviewer will raise, recorded here so it is not raised as a fault.** *Read out* and its variants appear about thirty times in 29,437 words, which is roughly twice the density of Batch 0001. That is deliberate and it is earned: this batch is about documents entering a district one at a time, and eleven of those instances are the same form being read at six doors, or the same column being filled by three witnesses, or the same clause of a register. **The motif is never stated as a maxim in this batch.** It is carried in Chapters 0113 (*a thing in this building that is not read out is a thing in this building*) and 0117 (*a bill that is not read out is a bill that arrives on a door without anybody having told the door it was coming*), and those are the only two places where the words *not read out* are joined to a consequence. **An earlier state-file claim that a longer formula appeared twice was checked against the page and is false, and the claim is retired here.**

## The external review on this batch, and the repair pass it caused

**An external review was run on the delivered batch (`reviews/volume-03-batch-0002.md`) and it returned one blocker, eight majors and a long minor tail. All of the blockers and all of the majors are fixed, and the minors that changed a fact are fixed. The durable artefact is the review file; this is the short version. The batch is 29,437 words after the repair pass, and every trim was deletion.**

**The blocker was real and it was the worst kind of fault in this book: a prohibition broken in the middle of a chapter without anybody noticing.** Chapter 0113's closing scene had Tarin at the plank saying *I was at Vellum*, in a batch whose standing rule is that he cannot leave a valley, and whose only mileage is sixty-one miles east and two days of road each way. **Fixed whole.** He is now at the plank all day — he did the noon trial and the four and has been on the bank since half past four — and **Perdie Ammid is the one who went down the road on the Monday and came back on the Thursday**, and what she tells him at the bottom of the bank is the whole of what the session established, in her words, in about a hundred words, and the chapter ends better for it. The state files that asserted the opposite are corrected here, in `state/current.md`, in `state/open-threads.md` and in `state/continuity.md`.

**The four majors that were calendar impossibilities.**

1. **"Thursday the seventh" appeared three times and the seventh is a Saturday.** It is a Saturday everywhere now, in Chapter 0111 twice and in Chapter 0114 once, and the coping stone and the district book follow the page.
2. **The month turn was three weeks away and the batch put it a week away, four times.** The month after next begins Day 187, so Harl Bray is not due back until the middle of that month. **He now comes home on the Wednesday the eleventh, three days short of a run, because Jess Marr put a boy on the flour boat on the Monday with a folded paper that had a door number on it and not his name — and Jess says so, at the door, in front of the lane.** That is better than the version it replaces, because it is the count reaching out of the valley for the first time and doing something nobody planned.
3. **Chapter 0114 re-printed, in a shop, the sentence the magistrate had struck out in the lane two days earlier.** Perdie is the one character who would catch it, in a chapter whose whole subject is a record not lying. **She now reads out the finding as it stands and then says, without looking up, that a fourth clause was struck in the lane on the Tuesday by the magistrate and that the striking is on the record.**
4. **Two one-day errors.** Harker had "nine days" to think about an instruction that came on the second in a chapter dated the fourth; it is two days. Cobb invented a rate on a Sunday in a Monday chapter.

**The two majors that were arithmetic conflicts.**

5. **Chapter 0115 priced the materials at about eleven shillings and Chapter 0117 priced them at four shillings and eightpence, and the eleven collided with the labour.** Chapter 0115 now prices nothing — a lane on a working morning does not know what a clerk's schedule will say — and states only what was carried: nine loads of stone off the ford bank, a cart of clay, a bushel of lime. **The figure is introduced once, in the bill form, where it belongs.**
6. **The leather washer was charged to the seventh of next month in a bill form and cut out of a boot on the eighth.** It is not in the bill form now. The washer belongs to the pump and the pump is not the bank.

**The two majors that were document faults, and the two majors of prose.**

7. **"Ten names given" was one more than the doors yield.** Counting the doors as written: widow, Jess, the boy, Wyn, Sella, Mr Trew, Mrs Trew, Nell, Ada — nine. **The tally now says nine names given, one withheld by the person himself and recorded unwilling, and one person absent and not named for him because his mother would not be asked for it.** The figure appears in Nye's return line, in Harker's note and in the district-book entry, and all three agree, and the machine's inability to name an absent man is now a second, quieter thing the row learned about itself on the same morning.
8. **The bank view named four persons "of the row" and one of them is a man of Nether Sallow.** The finding now reads *by Mr C. Lille, Mrs S. Marr and Mrs J. Marr, of the row, and by Mr W. Venn of Nether Sallow* in the chapter and in the sheet Perdie reads out on the Friday, and Dorrin says so in the lane, **and Corrie Lille notices on the Monday that the row is going to be charged for the man from across the ford, and Cobb looks at the sheet for a while and says that it is, and that he thought about it on the water on the way over.** The sheet is still true and it is still charging the row, and that is the argument of the batch working the way the argument of this book works.
9. **Chapter 0112's ending was stitched out of order** — the butt full at the fortieth trip and then the count restarting at thirty-five, with the same beat played twice. **The reflection now falls at about the thirtieth trip and the last ten are carried without counting, because a man does not count the last ten of forty.**
10. **A provenance claim by the boy of fifteen went uncorrected inside the one passage that polices provenance** — he said Tarin gave eleven on the first of next month "off the same man's hook", which the same chapter contradicts. **Tarin now corrects him, at length and in front of nobody: *on a hook, and a woman with a slate, and nine people who heard it. That is the whole of what a count is in this valley and it is a poor thing, and it is all there is.***

**The minors that changed a fact.** *The volume* is gone from Tarin's sworn evidence and reads *the book*; *the reader of all three* is gone from sheet thirty-nine; a pot went onto a shop hob in Chapter 0119 and came straight off it, so the batch has no tea; Mrs Halward is **about fifty** and forty-one years in the valley, not seventy-one, which is Aldis Kerr's age and came from Chapter 0101; **Nell Trew is the girl of the fourth door and not Sella Marr's granddaughter**, and Sella now says *my daughter's boy*, which fixes the relationship the batch had been floating; the store book's entry now carries a month and a year at the head of its page, so the fortnight inference is supported by the evidence quoted and not only by assertion; the form has nine boxes and not eleven; the hammer man says nine loads, not eleven shillings; and Chapter 0120's boy has thirty-eight trips behind him and not forty-one, and the second of this month is an evening and not a Tuesday.

**One inherited fault that was deliberately not propagated and not re-cut.** Chapters 0101, 0106 and 0116 date the removal of the notice from the frame to **the Tuesday of the second of this month**, and the second of that month was a Friday. Three delivered chapters and a state file carry it. **The finished chapters were not re-cut, on this project's own rule that re-cutting a weekday in a delivered chapter is a bigger injury than a documented fault, and Chapter 0120 no longer names the weekday at all** — it says *the evening of the second of this month* — so the error is neither multiplied nor corrected, and it is recorded here once.

**One finding the reviewer raised that the review itself got wrong, recorded because a later pass will otherwise re-raise it.** Chapter 0118 says the reading of the thirty-first was *ten days ago*, in a chapter dated the tenth. Day 155 to Day 165 is ten days. **It is ten, it is correct, and no chapter may change it to eleven.**
