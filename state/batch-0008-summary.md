## WHAT THIS BATCH IS, IN ONE PARAGRAPH

**A paper road is built, lawfully, on a printed Guild form, out of four true figures about four different reaches, and the man who builds it cannot be named, so the form's fifth line is left empty for eight days while he decides who to put there, and he puts three men in it on the Saturday morning without asking any of them, and the three men find out at eleven o'clock and the fourth finds out at two o'clock in a field, and the form is torn up in a room in the afternoon and the four lines stay in a book in a village for ever. And on the Monday the Reach finds, in a clay in its own lowest field, a channel that two records in two centuries call a drain and nobody has ever measured, running clear at seven hundred and thirty-five gallons an hour — a rate somebody takes four times, in a lath, with a watch — and the water in it is not lost, it is blind, and it cannot be used, and taking it drowns the field it is standing in. The window shut on the twenty-fourth and the count was not taken on the twenty-seventh and the next count is on the third of next month and there is a new column in a book in a village headed *Days lost, and by whom, and to what. To be added to and not taken back.***

## The one thing a next writer must not un-write

**The fifth line is on a printed Guild form and it is the Sere's third line, and nobody in the Reach knew until the twentieth day of this month.** It reads, in types, in a bundle that comes up from Bexhill twice a year: *Takers, named, so that a man who is thirsty may come to the door of the man who has the water.* Perdie Ammid said in a room of thirty-four people on the twelfth of this month that the third line was the only witness clause in the basin; **Margit Saye read the same sentence off a bundle of forty unused forms on the Saturday and said the only thing she had to say about it: *it is not a stone, that is the difference and it is the whole of why it is worth a sheet of paper.*** Neither the stones nor the form are dated against the other and the volume does not say which came from which. **Nobody may now say that the Reach's four lines on two kerb stones are the oldest witness clause in the basin. They are not. They are the same sentence in a village, and the form is a hundred and thirty years old.**

## The arithmetic, all of it on the page and all of it derived

- **The Sallow Dyke, second bridge. A new figure, measured four times.** The cut is **one foot wide and one foot deep**, which is **a square foot of section**, and a foot by a foot is 144 square inches, which is one square foot, and **a cubic foot is seven and a half gallons in this basin** and it has never once been wrong. **Twenty feet in twelve seconds** is a foot and two-thirds a second, which is a foot and two-thirds of a cubic foot a second, which is **twelve and a half gallons a minute = 750 gallons an hour. Twenty feet in twelve and a half seconds** is one and three-fifths of a foot a second, which is **twelve gallons a minute = 720 gallons an hour.** Both pairs are the same pair and are said to be the same pair. **The figure is 735** and both trials are printed, about four per cent apart.
- **The reason 735 is given on the page by Tarin Rusk and not by a fitter, and it is the batch's growth.** A fitter would take 750, because 720 is the figure that is true and 750 is the figure that is generous and a card has to be generous for thirty years after everybody in the room is dead. He takes the middle one **because there is nobody in this Reach left to check him.** He says it once in a field where a woman of seventy-one can hear him and says he is not going to say it again.
- **735 × 24 = 17,640 gallons a day.** The fourth book's 4 gallons a household a day × 290 households = **1,160 a day**, and 17,640 is **fifteen times** that. The assize's 5,481,000-gallon season at 17,640 a day is **three hundred and ten days.** Check: 5,481,000 ÷ 17,640 = 310.7.
- **The addition that is refused.** 735 at the dyke + 190 at the plank = **925**, against the throat's **850**, so **seventy-five gallons an hour out of nothing.** Tarin Rusk does it in a field in front of eleven people, says he will not fix it, gives the one bound he will go to — **the tank has not moved an eighth of an inch in six years, so the works pass about what the throat passes and not a millimetre more, so the two figures must be the same water counted twice** — and then says he cannot prove it, and refuses to stand in a lane with a ladle and call it the same.
- **The hours.** The count of the fourteenth was **four and a half hours** (Ch 68). The twenty-seventh was **five and a half hours of Wick Venn's barrow and a lath and a boy of fifteen**, and Tarin Rusk was in a room with fourteen people in it for about three and a half of them. Ten hours is **a day and an hour.** Both figures are entered in a new column in the second book.
- **The distance arithmetic of the Reach, all already on the page and not re-derived:** the Long Drain is **4 miles 2 chains** from the wet common at Nether Sallow; the **Sallow Dyke is 2 miles 4 chains**; the **West Flume is 2 miles 4 chains**; the throat is **340 yards** from the plank; the tank and the flume sluice are **400 yards** apart; the flume's outfall is **two miles below the plank**; the flume is on the service road for **about the first mile only.**
- **NOTHING FROM THE PREVIOUS BATCH WAS RE-DERIVED.** The 850 and its trials of 833 and 860, the 660 and the 667 and the reason they differ, the 844, the 1,417, the 1,785 and the 368, the tank's 2 ft 6¼ in, the plank's 193 and 191, the weep's 90 and 89, the 4½ inches, the 1,417 against 1,160, Yara Sen's 120 gallons an acre, and the 7·31 that is never measured — **all are quoted once at most and none is argued again.** The 660 is not re-derived; what is new is that Chapter 78 offers a hypothesis for where it goes and **refuses to prove it on the page**, which is the open thread and is now a live question with a figure attached rather than a figure with a hole in it.

## The three things the writer found and fixed in this pass

1. **925 − 850 is seventy-five, not a hundred and seventy-five.** Chapter 78's refused addition was wrong in the first write and is right on the page.
2. **Every reference to the witness box and the takers list was dated the eleventh of this month.** It is the **twelfth**: the notice went up on the Wednesday the tenth, the eight refusals came back by the Friday the twelfth, the ninth signature was given on the Friday morning, and the witness box was put on a table in front of about thirty-four people **on the Friday the twelfth.** Six chapters carried the wrong date and are corrected. **Aldis Kerr's refusal is also corrected: she refused the box in a room of forty-one people on the twenty-seventh day of the month before and has not been asked since, and she was not in the room on the twelfth.**
