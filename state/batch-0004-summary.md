## What the batch proves

1. **The central dilemma is on the page and it has no answer.** The Office's form asks the district a question it cannot truthfully answer: is the discharge continued, suspended, or permitted to continue without comment. The only water is the same water that made nine children ill. A tank whose salt is concentrating because the sun takes the water and not the salt. A child at the fourth stage. **A district that has run out of clean water must choose between no water and the same bad water, and the choice is written down in a printed line and it is nobody's professional opinion.**
2. **The crosscurrent is genuinely physical and the mechanism is evaporation.** Seventy gallons a day leaves by mouths, carrying its salt; fifty leaves by the sun, carrying none. A fortnight of that is a fortnight of a tank getting stronger, and the physician who says it in a yard on the 16th is proven on the 20th by two white crusts on a glazed jar and a ridge up the glaze. **The classification that follows is administratively correct and materially wrong, and it is wrong in a sentence the Office itself printed:** no sample from that cistern has ever been submitted to any assay, and none was made by anybody in this basin.
3. **Chapters 39 is a door that stays shut and nobody is asked.** He goes to the fourth step at half past three in the morning and does not knock, and the whole of the scene is that he has come to say he will not ask. **He never gets as far as naming anything. No route is opened, named, moved or shut anywhere in Chapters 31–40.** The cost is real: the branch runs ungauged at about 3,200 gal/hr, the ground takes it, the tank stops rising, and Tam Roke shuts the low pump for two days and will not open it because he cannot see the bottom of the channel that is filling it.
4. **Iven Marrow is the second rung and no more.** He is patient, specific, right about most of it, wrong about one of his own three reasons and says so first, does not press after the refusal, and gives away the one thing that is not for sale — a bench book out of a shed at Bexhill. *Anything I know, I will teach you in a shed. Anything I hold, I will sell you.* He is not a mage, he does not know the name of anything, and he hands Tarin back a sentence about a brass ring in a man's hand and lets it stand.
5. **Tarin is further from certainty and closer to a person.** He cannot read what is in the room with a hundred and nineteen in it, cannot say what is missing from a fourteen-year-old schedule, cannot gauge a well, cannot move a branch without a witness, and cannot ask a woman on a step for the one thing in the lane that would hold. He carries a bench book in his coat inside a bad shoulder and opens it on a gatehouse step at half past one in the afternoon.
6. **Roe is in context and not in danger.** A chair at a preliminary inquiry says out loud, in front of the room, that a certificate *is* a finding, that it is in a deal box in a store this office has never opened, and that he will not rule on it in anybody's absence. Nobody threatens Roe, nobody asks who falsified them, and he is not spoken of in a single line of this batch except as *the acting commissioner* on a piece of paper.
