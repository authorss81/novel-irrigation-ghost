# Open Threads

These are bootstrap promises, not unresolved chapter events. Each should advance through the volume noted without replacing a local promise with the larger mystery.

1. **Ashgate and the first Gauge:** **Paid in Chapter 24, and it is now a fact on the page:** the reservoir is physically present, it is full, it is under a street, and the opening stream was residual pressure and not created water. The immediate crisis is *not* paid — the district has 950 gallons and no inlet — but **Volume 1's own promise is paid at the climax in Chapters 46–49**, where the Office formally acknowledges that Ashgate physically exists without acknowledging who diverted it. The larger route mystery is untouched.
2. **The collective ghost:** The Irrigation Ghost is the accumulated pressure of unlisted places and people, not Lira alone. **The many-voiced reading was spent in Chapter 24, once, plainly, and is not explained.** Nine voices, then eleven, then not countable; work, a boundary, a price, a stock counted in tens, a child's question with no answer, and four women giving directions about pumps at once; no addressee, no response to a name, to speech, or to the stone, before or after. **Tarin's private certainty about his mother is the least reliable instrument he brought down the hill and he writes down four and not one.** No character in Chapters 25–30 names it, theorises it, or is told what it is. **Do not re-use it as a sound, a pressure or an image in Movement IV; if it appears again it must appear as something a person remembers, and it must not do a job.** Answer its identity by Volume 4 without a resurrection, and keep Volume 4's *Glassstep is a living community, not a ghost town* for Volume 4 — **Ashgate on top of the chamber is the same method and gets no more explanation in Volume 1 than Glassstep did.**
3. **The One Map and Blind Reservoir:** The system uses address plates, pressure relief, and rain shutters to make water unreachable. Reveal the physical mechanism progressively; the final answer remains the planned monopoly, not a new entity.
4. **Boryn's hidden chronology:** Boryn reactivated the newest protocol six years before the story and expanded it afterward. Introduce his public role in Volume 5 and make the earlier, smaller operation part of his credible motive.
5. **Glassstep and Lira:** Tarin's childhood route, mother's choices, and the unlisted neighborhood are a personal consequence of the system. **Chapter 24 spent the private half of this and it must not be spent again:** he heard four women giving directions about pumps at once and could not tell which of the four was his mother's, and he wrote down four and not one. Do not turn the quest into finding a returned mother and **do not let a later chapter decide which of the four it was.**
6. **Tarin and Mara:** Their relationship must pass through professional suspicion, public disagreement, consent boundaries, and shared institutional work before any romantic resolution. **Chapter 27 is shared institutional work under load and it did not soften anything:** she refused to help him write his purpose, she caught his misstated count in a public register, and condition four was said out loud. **They still distrust each other's methods. There has been no romantic beat and none may be manufactured in Movement IV.**
7. **Local readers and other routes:** Ensure at least one major crisis is solved by another community's reader or keeper. Tarin must never be the only person who can make water move.
8. **Ending constraints:** Track the physical cost of every route operation, the source of every restored water supply, and whether consent is real. The final restoration must not require anyone to be compelled to forget.
9. **Glassstep's petition and the physical source:** **The petition was filed on Day 22 and returned on Day 34.** It fails: the warden's office will enter the district only on a route record — named route, documented source, witness, date — and the only document that could supply the source is the restricted Ashgate folio, so the district's legal existence and the volume's central document are the same object. **Both halves of that bind have now been worked.** The reading day produced **ref. 118** and a schedule entry that plated the row's drain and took a hundred and four names off a survey in the same year, in the same office, by the same hand — *and it is not a source and cannot be filed as one.* The descent produced a measured chamber and a settlement on top of it — *and a chamber is not a document either.* **Two of the five boxes on the warden's return are now filled in a book with four copies in it — *row number*, as *none held*, and *tanks* — and the three empty ones are *keeper*, *gallons* and *last serviced*, the keeper box to read *keeper, none held*.** Do not let the petition succeed in Volume 1: the reservoir is acknowledged, the district is not, and that difference is the point the ending will need. **The date passed and was accepted; the witness cannot be found; the keeper has not been asked; and the named route is still a name cut on a stone beside a low pump.**
10. **The settlement on top of the chamber.** **Ashgate: eleven houses, thirty-four persons, about thirty years, no row, no schedule entry, no keeper, on top of a chamber standing eleven feet one inch above the outside ground at the door of the works.** Published as a fact on the 15th of the month and in four buildings. **Who put those people there and why is not said anywhere in Volume 1 and must not be said.** Nobody decided anything: there was a works, then a closure, then empty houses, then people, then a well, then thirty years. **The row's well rises at about 103 gallons an hour out of the chamber and has never been tested by anybody, and Tarin has said in his own engineer's statement that he cannot promise it will be the same water tomorrow.** That is the live problem and it belongs to whoever is asked to be a keeper.

It is beneath the houses.* It is a condition about a place, it is plain, and it is neither a rule nor a solution. The ticking was water round the bolts. The shaft below the plate is full and has been since before the register. Not the reservoir. **The cistern has fallen to 950 gallons and there is no municipal supply in this movement, so the plate and the lane are now a live public-health problem and not a mystery.**

- **Why a true reading survives a record that was built to discredit it.** The leaf was cut out and replaced, and the only surviving copy of the reading is a pressure shadow that nobody intended to preserve. Nobody has asked why. **Someone needed that number to stay where it could be found** — and Chapter 27 shows the same office, twenty-six years later, certifying the same chamber dry twice more in its own hand, and Chapter 25 finds both certificates in a deal box thirty yards from a chalk board that says 5·9. The question is now *why the shadow survived*, and it has been answered in the sense that a reader can now check the connection; the question nobody has asked is why nobody removed the certificates too.
- **Wend Quill.** The hand on both redactions and Mara's mother's brother. **Chapter 16 moved this**: Mara wrote to her mother, and the certificate she filed describes the hand in terms only one person in the commissioner's registry could read. The registry's marginal note says no further action is proposed at present and is dated a Tuesday in the second month of next year, and nothing whatever happens on it. **Chapter 27 files a second document — a schedule reference, a second office, a public comparison — and not a second copy of the first, which is the only reason the note does not apply to it.** He remains evidence of an office, not a villain, and is not the reason Roe's lie falls.
- **Niko's descent.** Named in Chapter 10, set up in Chapters 14, 17 and 19, walked to the mouth of the cut in Chapter 19, and **paid for in Chapter 22.** What the descent bought is on the page and checkable: the plant, the tally board, the chamber, the roof, 11 ft 1 in, two hundred and forty-one steps, and a stone with a name cut twice. **What it cost is Wick's left hand and the four-day water in a dispensary.** Niko's own change is a spoken decision with no paper in it: he is not going to chase the Water Office, and the thing he is going to do has no name in this city, and he asked for it to be remembered by one man and refused to have it cut in a yard.
- **The hundred and four names.** Mara read them aloud in a yard on Day 28 and stopped twice, at the eleventh and the fourteenth, both times because the household identified itself. She has not transcribed either. The certificate records the identifications as events and not as evidence, and the fourth step is now a custodian of a record in its own hand and in her own hand a third time. **Chapter 28 has her write a third sheet, in which she records a refusal of her own people, and Chapter 30 has a district examiner promised inside a fortnight and an empty keeper box in a warden's form that says *keeper, none held*.**
- **Mara Quill's unlogged drawing.** Still unlogged, still in her drawer under a tray of brass weights, and still the only copy that nobody can check. She did not destroy it and she did not enter it. Condition two of the Chapter 16 agreement exists because of it and **Chapter 27 puts it under load and holds it by putting three names on the desk list.**
- **The keeper.** The volume's live institutional problem at the end of Batch 0003. A route with a name, a record and a keeper can be held; a route with only a man in it cannot. The stone has one name and one blank face, the date is accepted, the source is a chamber and not a document, and **the box that would make the district fileable is empty on purpose and will be read by a district examiner who has been brought forward by the district's own letter.**

These are living-world uncertainties, not missing central-mystery answers.
