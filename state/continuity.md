# Continuity State

## Baseline

- The story opens in drought-stricken Cresset, in the Marrow Basin. **Chapters 1–20 exist and are canon** (see `state/chapter-summaries.md`, `state/batch-0001-summary.md`, and `state/batch-0002-summary.md`).
- Tarin Rusk is a 33-year-old senior flow engineer and the male protagonist. He is competent with ordinary hydraulics before any memory magic, and Chapters 1–4 show it on the page with no magic present.
- Glassstep was unlisted **twenty-six years** before the story, when Tarin was seven. Tarin's mother Lira Rusk disappeared during the breach; she later entered the One Map's galleries as a counter-reader and died there. No resurrection or return-as-guide is planned.
- The first opening event is residual pressurized water released behind a sealed inspection gate when Tarin opens it with a bar. Tarin does not create the water. **The head is 5·89 m and it is a real pressure head; the uphill run is a channel laid at a foot in the hundred.**

## World and magic

- The drought is real. Unlisted routes are physically rerouted by the One Map's copper address plates and pressure-relief gates into the Blind Reservoir beneath the old Orisene works.
- A raw wet line can be sensed with physical contact. Deliberately naming or moving a route requires a physical trace, a willing witness, a durable record, and route legitimacy.
- Remembered hydraulics never creates water. It cannot read private thoughts or make false records harmless.
- Every deliberate use costs name debt. A private memory cannot be used as another person's anchor without informed consent; coercion creates a consent fracture.
- The Gauge is a limited, potentially wrong interface. It may state an immediate physical condition, never a complete solution or future probability.

## Relationships and factions

- Tarin and Mara Quill are the only primary romantic relationship. Mara is a non-magical record conservator with an independent legal and civic goal.
- Halden Roe is the local liar; Iven Marrow is the guild monopolist; Cael Dorrin is the legal eraser; Liora Tarn is the careful censor; Boryn Vey is the rational sacrificer and final human antagonist.
- The One Map is the inherited final external pressure. Boryn quietly reactivated its newest function six years before the story and expanded the drought protocol afterward; this is revealed publicly in Volume 5.

### Water and hydraulics, as fixed in Batch 0002

- **The head is 5·89 m and has not changed.** Tarin cannot increase it. Nothing in this batch makes more water appear. Written `5·89`; spoken *five point eight nine*.
- **A channel stops at the balance point.** The Chapter 30 branch stopped itself at the correct level in the correct tank, and the fact that it worked is the point of the chapter.
- **A gauged stop-log meters a branch.** The oak Roke planed, with a groove and a pin hole, was set to the pin found on Day 19, at **610 gallons an hour**, on Day 30, and the flow rate was right. The failure was not the flow rate.
- **What a stop-log does not do is address a route.** Spelled out on the page in Chapter 14 and Chapter 17: a route with a name, a record, and a keeper can be held; a route with only a man in it cannot. And Chapter 14 makes the sharper discovery: **the missing second hand is not a neighbour, it is the other end of the pipe** — a man at the wheel, on the works, at the gate, at four in the morning. There is no such man, because the works is sealed and every man between the lane and the wheel draws a wage out of the office that has charged Tarin twice.
- **The eleven-hour window is not repeated and no second window is won in Movement II.** The Chapter 30 attempt is not a window: it is unnamed, unwitnessed, and it opens nothing that anybody can file.
- **The run's own flow has never been measured by anybody, in the city's history, for one physical reason:** there has never been anywhere for it to go. The branch taking a third of it is measured only as a sound — the run's note dropped by about a third when the log went in on Day 20. Tarin had that number in his book for ten days and did the sum without it, and that is the compounding error in Chapter 17.
- **The plate under the yard is a lid on the terrace overflow shaft, and the shaft below it is full.** The district has been a closed drainage system for thirty years: water in, nothing out, all of it into the ground. That is why Bel Crale's well still produced a bucket a day in the spring. **The ticking every ten or twelve seconds, first audible in Chapter 10, was water finding its way round the bolts**, and it stopped at 09:25 on Day 30 when the ground came up. It is not the reservoir and must not be revealed as one.
- **The pumping plant under the terrace** (Chapter 6) was patient at two a second on the sixth. By the 29th it was **three a second, without variation, and it does not stop at any hour.** A pump making up for a head it has lost is not a mystery. It is the reason Niko goes down. It is *not* a reason to ask who owns it, and nobody in Movement II asks.

### The three live pressures, and what they produced

1. **Glassstep filed for recognition on Day 22** and **was refused on Day 34 in a way worse than a refusal.** Box two names a household (Roeg, Hanne, of the fourth step). Box five names the source, the gate, the head, the engineer and the stone, which puts Tarin and his second charge into a stamped public document. Twenty-two signed, nineteen asked and said no, and both numbers are on the card; 104 people were never asked and are recorded as not asked. The cost of the day: the warden's own note, filed in the district book on the 23rd, is **the first city document in which the words "the blue steps" appear, and it says there is no row, no keeper, no schedule entry, and that the plate is weeping.**
2. **Folio eleven went into restricted handling on Day 17**, on rule nine of the reading rules — *an unverified record may not decide a public route* — and Mara learned on Day 23 from the shelf-marker, which is the only part of the procedure that touches the case officer. The public finding aid now carries the finding in the Office's hand and without her name. **Her problem was durability, not nerve, and she solved it legitimately**: a quarterly collation warrant (five boxes, eleven words of purpose), a field book collated in the open in the yard, and a **second attestation** — one tracing in her hand, one in Hanne Roeg's, both from one original in one room in one sitting by two named hands, with witnesses. **Hanne Roeg is now a custodian of a record, which is the institutional form this whole series generalises.**
3. **Tarin forced the branch on Day 30 without a witness and it failed.** Physics, on the page: a vessel has to be emptied before it can be a vessel, and the drained 4,700 gallons has no drain; the branch is metered and the tank fills twice as fast because the run's unmeasured inflow comes in through an eighteen-inch lead pipe half silted; the run comes into the yard over the flags because the cistern's inlet cannot take it; the ground is a thirty-year closed sponge; and the floor of the lowest room comes up. He is right about the vessel, the head and the balance point, and none of it is any use. **He cannot afterwards tell whether he lost it at the witness, at the record, or at his standing, and nobody tells him.**

## The turn, and what Movement III starts from

The petition is answered and the answer is worse than a refusal: the warden's office will enter the blue steps in the book **only on a route record** — a named route, a documented source, a witness, and a date. The date passes. The other three cannot be filed by anybody. And the only document in the basin that could supply the source is folio eleven, which is locked in a strong room in Vellum on the rule that an unverified record may not decide a public route. **The district's legal existence and the volume's central document are the same object, and neither can be got first.** There are two routes to the same answer and the chapter names both in the yard: the **scheduled reading day on the 4th of the next month** (a named applicant, a countersignature, a date, and eleven days to prepare) and **the quarry cut** (a warden's seal, a crew nobody can pay, and a twenty-two-year-old who has already gone once). **Movement III therefore begins from a choice, not a summons.**
