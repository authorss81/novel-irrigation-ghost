# Continuity State

## Baseline

- The story opens in drought-stricken Cresset, in the Marrow Basin, before any chapter prose exists.
- Tarin Rusk is a 33-year-old senior flow engineer and the male protagonist. He is competent with ordinary hydraulics before any memory magic.
- Glassstep was unlisted twenty-six years before the story, when Tarin was seven. Tarin's mother Lira Rusk disappeared during the breach; she later entered the One Map's galleries as a counter-reader and died there. No resurrection or return-as-guide is planned.
- The first opening event is residual pressurized water released from a concealed Ashgate shaft when Tarin opens an inspection gate. Tarin does not create the water.

## World and magic

- The drought is real. Unlisted routes are physically rerouted by the One Map's copper address plates and pressure-relief gates into the Blind Reservoir beneath the old Orisene works.
- A raw wet line can be sensed with physical contact. Deliberately naming or moving a route requires a physical trace, a willing witness, a durable record, and route legitimacy.
- Remembered hydraulics never creates water. It cannot read private thoughts or make false records harmless.
- Every deliberate use costs name debt. A private memory cannot be used as another person's anchor without informed consent; coercion creates a consent fracture.
- The Gauge is a limited, potentially wrong interface. It may state an immediate physical condition, never a complete solution or future probability.

## Relationships and factions

- Tarin and Mara Quill are the only primary romantic relationship. Mara is a non-magical record conservator with an independent legal and civic goal.
- Halden Roe is the local liar; Iven Marrow is the guild monopolist; Cael Dorrin is the legal eraser; Liora Tarn is the careful censor; Boryn Vey is the rational sacrificer and final human antagonist.
- The One Map is the inherited final external pressure. Boryn quietly reactivated its newest function six years before the story and expanded the drought protocol afterward; this is revealed publicly in Volume 5.

## Volume 1 canon (planning, not yet drafted)

- Cresset is three weeks from the midsummer ration review under a posted **Standing Ration**. Fourteen months without real rain.
- **Halden Roe** falsified the Ashgate measurements to conceal illegal pumping by contractors through a reservoir his own ledger calls empty. He is a liar about arithmetic, never a magic user, removed from the acting commissioner's office in Volume 1, and alive thereafter as a witness.
- **The falsified figure is a true one.** Roe did not invent a number to cover a dry reservoir. A genuine head reading was lifted off the original Ashgate leaf so a clean leaf could stand in its place, and the true figure survives in the pressure shadow. This is the mechanism behind the Chapter 5 reveal, and it is why a figure older than Tarin's involvement can incriminate him. He does not know the One Map's name and is not its architect.
- **Ashgate Reservoir** is intact and lies beneath a settlement built on it after the official closure. This is the Volume 1 midpoint reversal (Chapters 21–30, set out as its own field in `outline/volume-01.md`).
- The uphill flow in Chapter 1 is residual pressure behind a seized inspection gate. It is not created, summoned, or redirected by Tarin.
- **The terrace outfall under Glassstep is sealed and iron-plated**, shut when the district was written off. Named in Chapter 2 by warden Pell, confirmed in Chapter 3, and it is why the courtyard cistern stops at a third. Nothing in this world fills, stops, or reverses without a named cause; the plate is a standing pressure risk for Chapters 6, 10, and beyond. It is not the reservoir.
- **Glassstep** is inhabited, unnamed on every official map, and recognized in no document. Residents are witnesses and participants, never a symbolic constituency.
- Chapter 5 is the first Gauge statement and the first reach of **Trace**. It states a physical condition, asks for a witness, is incomplete, and is slightly wrong. It never offers a solution, and it never explains the substituted leaf — Mara does that with ink and pressure.
- Chapter 6 is the first deliberate route opening and the first reach of **Name**, not Trace. It requires a physical trace, a willing witness, and a record Tarin re-cuts with his own hands. Volume 1 ends at the beginning of Name; Bend belongs to Chapters 51–150.
- The Gauge never states a rule, a lesson, or a solution. In Chapter 10 it shows only a physical condition — a witness present, the stone blank without one — and Tarin reasons out the rule about a witness being free to refuse, in public, where the crowd can argue with him.
- The collective ghost is **not** in play in Chapters 1–10. The many-voiced reading is held for Chapter 24; a Volume 1 batch may not confirm it or let a character name it. Tracked for Volume 2 in `state/open-threads.md`.
- Chapter 7 is the first name debt: a practiced hand motion of twenty years, and the taste of his tea. It does not heal inside Volume 1, and Volume 2's debt is a different thing — a professional association, not a second lost hand technique.
- The Chapter 10 window is **eleven hours**, bounded and insufficient. The Chapter 46–49 Assembly window is also eleven hours: the volume does not grow the number, because the source does not grow. The branch keeps running for nine hours after warden's men remove Tarin, then stops on its own at three in the morning with nobody in it. It does not stop because he is gone, and it does not come back when he asks.
- The ration review is quoted as roughly three weeks away at the opening, is still not held when the cistern rows run out, and is finally forced in Chapter 46. Chapters 1–10 are Days 1–20 and are governed by the cistern rows. Push the review date rather than compress scenes.
- Tarin never uses a private memory as an anchor. The Chapter 39 refusal costs the plot its convenient solution and must read as an established pattern.
- No chapter may end on a cut-off mid-action. Each lands a completed beat and opens a question.

## Ending constraints

- The final conflict is Boryn's attempt to activate Clearwater while the basin must release the Blind Reservoir.
- The final answer is a Many Rivers Charter and a distributed, contestable network of local route records.
- Water returns without forced forgetting. Tarin voluntarily releases his private Glassstep/Lira anchor; he loses exclusive certainty, not his identity or love for his community.
- The final enemy is seeded now. Do not add a later cosmic threat or substitute a surprise villain.
