# Continuity State

## Baseline

- The story opens in drought-stricken Cresset, in the Marrow Basin, before any chapter prose exists.
- Tarin Rusk is a 33-year-old senior flow engineer and the male protagonist. He is competent with ordinary hydraulics before any memory magic.
- Glassstep was unlisted twenty-six years before the story, when Tarin was seven. Tarin's mother Lira Rusk disappeared during the breach; she later entered the One Map's galleries as a counter-reader and died there. No resurrection or return-as-guide is planned.
- The first opening event is residual pressurized water released from a concealed Ashgate shaft when Tarin opens an inspection gate. Tarin does not create the water.

## World and magic

- The drought is real. Unlisted routes are physically rerouted by the One Map's copper address plates and pressure-relief gates into the Blind Reservoir beneath the old Orisene works.
- A raw wet line can be sensed with physical contact. Deliberately naming or moving a route requires a physical trace, a willing witness, a durable record, and route legitimacy.
- Remembered hydraulics never creates water. It cannot read private thoughts or make false records harmless.
- Every deliberate use costs name debt. A private memory cannot be used as another person's anchor without informed consent; coercion creates a consent fracture.
- The Gauge is a limited, potentially wrong interface. It may state an immediate physical condition, never a complete solution or future probability.

## Relationships and factions

- Tarin and Mara Quill are the only primary romantic relationship. Mara is a non-magical record conservator with an independent legal and civic goal.
- Halden Roe is the local liar; Iven Marrow is the guild monopolist; Cael Dorrin is the legal eraser; Liora Senn is the careful censor; Boryn Vey is the rational sacrificer and final human antagonist.
- The One Map is the inherited final external pressure. Boryn quietly reactivated its newest function six years before the story and expanded the drought protocol afterward; this is revealed publicly in Volume 5.

## Volume 1 canon (planning, not yet drafted)

- Cresset is three weeks from the midsummer ration review under a posted **Standing Ration**. Fourteen months without real rain.
- **Halden Roe** falsified the Ashgate measurements to conceal illegal pumping by contractors through a reservoir his own ledger calls empty. He is a liar about arithmetic, never a magic user, removed from the acting commissioner's office in Volume 1, and alive thereafter as a witness.
- **Ashgate Reservoir** is intact and lies beneath a settlement built on it after the official closure. This is the Volume 1 midpoint reversal (Chapters 21–30).
- The uphill flow in Chapter 1 is residual pressure behind a seized inspection gate. It is not created, summoned, or redirected by Tarin.
- **Glassstep** is inhabited, unnamed on every official map, and recognized in no document. Residents are witnesses and participants, never a symbolic constituency.
- Chapter 5 is the first Gauge statement. It states a physical condition and asks for a witness. It never offers a solution.
- Chapter 6 is the first deliberate Trace operation, requiring a physical trace, a willing witness, and a record Tarin re-cuts with his own hands.
- Chapter 7 is the first name debt: a practiced hand motion of twenty years, and the taste of his tea. It does not heal inside Volume 1.
- The Chapter 10 window is eleven hours, bounded and insufficient. The branch continues for nine hours after Tarin is removed and then stops.
- Tarin never uses a private memory as an anchor. The Chapter 39 refusal costs the plot its convenient solution and must read as an established pattern.

## Ending constraints

- The final conflict is Boryn's attempt to activate Clearwater while the basin must release the Blind Reservoir.
- The final answer is a Many Rivers Charter and a distributed, contestable network of local route records.
- Water returns without forced forgetting. Tarin voluntarily releases his private Glassstep/Lira anchor; he loses exclusive certainty, not his identity or love for his community.
- The final enemy is seeded now. Do not add a later cosmic threat or substitute a surprise villain.
