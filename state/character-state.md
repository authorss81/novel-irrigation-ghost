# Character State

Updated after Batch 0003 (Volume 01, Chapters 21–30, Days 34–46). Compact and factual; use this for the next batch rather than re-reading the chapters.

## Tarin Rusk — protagonist

- **Age 33.** Senior flow engineer, Cresset Water Office. Rents nothing; sleeps in a dry corner of an empty cistern house inside the second gatehouse, under a watchman who is required to walk him to the gate at dawn and no further.
- **Power:** still **Stage 2 (Name)**, and **not a step further, and not a step back.** The Chapter 24 panel is a *perception* and he perceives a place: a chamber, a roof, a street, and a level in feet and inches. **No route is opened, named, moved or shut anywhere in Chapters 21–30. He has a trace, a cut record he cannot add to, a stone he cannot cut, and no method for finding a witness.** **Bend belongs to Chapters 51–150.**
- **Status:** Suspended since **Day 10** under **charge one — tampering with city records**. Charged again on **Day 20** under **charge two — unauthorized opening of a sealed structure and service of water from a reservoir the ledger records as closed.** Two open charges, two subjects, two dates, **never joined.** The hearing on charge one was set for the 22nd and **adjourned without a date entered**. Charge two's hearing has still not been set. **A third is forming: an unauthorised descent on a sealed works from the quarry side, discovered by the same office that reported the lamp on the ledge, and nobody has charged him with it yet.**
- **Lost:** desk, seal, district reference, keys, access to the lower terrace. Keeps the blue field book, the bar, the folding rule, the chalk stub, a knuckle of string, the tin pot, and **Roke's gauged oak stop-log with the pin.**
- **Name debt (minor, Chapter 7):** the two-finger press and the taste of his bitter tea. **Neither has healed and both are shown three more times in this batch** — it costs him the ladder rail in Chapter 23, the chisel on the chamber stone in Chapter 24, and in Chapter 21 he names it in his own pack list and does not try it. Chapter 25: a woman he has never met makes him tea and it has no taste in it and he drinks all of it and says nothing to anybody then or afterwards.
- **The three candidate explanations of the 30th are still apart and are not joined.** Chapter 24 produced no measurement that separates them and he did not write a fourth guess. The book now also holds: the chamber, 9 ft 4 in / 1 ft 9 in / **11 ft 1 in**; the roof, which is a floor; the tallies; and the four sentences about the voices.
- **He has come back from Chapter 17 without certainty.** He is the man who has been down a hole and cannot yet say what the hole is for, and who has found that his private certainty about his mother was the least reliable instrument he brought with him. **He wrote down four and not one, and that stands.**
- **He can do his own trade and cannot do the thing he came for.** He gauged a well to 103 gallons an hour with a pail and a watch and checked it twice. He has no instrument that would test that water and said so in a yard, in a signed document, in public.
- **He is on the record twice more and it is his choice.** His name is in Vellum's register of restricted consultations against the blue steps, with a struck figure and a correction in the same hand. He signed an engineer's statement on the 15th, the first thing he has signed in thirty-six days, and it goes up to the office whose signature is on the certificates.
- **He has refused on the page again.** Four people asked to put a hand on a route; four refusals; he thinks three of them were right and he will not argue with the fourth. He did not use his mother's name in a hole. He wrote *four and not one.*
- **He has not yet been to Ashgate with the news.** He has been there once, and Nell Drust gave him tea, and the thirty-four people in that row do not know they are on a chamber. **That is the next thing he has to do and he knows it.**
- **Left shoulder:** torn on Day 1, re-torn on Day 30, and in Chapter 23 he is told by three people, twice, that he is holding a lamp he should not be holding.
- **Open wants:** to get water to a district with 950 gallons; to read what is in the keep box at the works; to find a keeper; to be on a hill with an instrument and a crew. **New and specific: to get a district examiner to arrive before a doctor runs out of clean water, and he has just made that more likely on purpose.**
- **His fear, restated in his own words on the 15th in a yard:** *you have all had six weeks of me standing in this yard telling you things are possible, and I have been wrong in this yard and it cost a man his hand and it cost a child a fortnight.*

## Mara Quill — the slow-burn relationship

- **Age 31.** Provenance record conservator, Municipal Memory Office, Vellum. Eleven years in service. Non-magical and stays non-magical for the series.
- **Power:** none, and never any. Her expertise is provenance — nib cut, gum, batch stamp on the tin lid, sizing, watermark, pressure shadow, pressure of the stroke. **In Chapter 24 she spends four minutes reading the pressure of a three-digit number under a raking lamp and gets 118, and she says out loud that a shadow of a number is a shadow of the pressure of a number.**
- **Wants:** Glassstep's legal name, land claims, and basin share. Unchanged, and now impossible by a different route: the district's name is in Vellum's register and the warden's book and a store at a works and a letter in a bag, and it is in none of them as a *row*.
- **Holds:** the unlogged one-to-one drawing, still in her own drawer under a tray of brass weights, still the only copy nobody can check. Two one-to-one tracings of the Glassstep redaction folio, one in her hand and one in Hanne Roeg's, both certified as made from one original in one room in one sitting by two named hands. **And now, since the 11th: a certificate recording the existence, form and date of a pressure shadow and a schedule reference, countersigned by a second reader, which does not transcribe the reference and can be checked by any person against a public document in a warden's office.** That is the first thing she has ever produced that another person can falsify.
- **Career cost added in Chapter 27:** she files the sentence about the *hand* a second time, and says out loud that she knows the registry's marginal note of a year out exists and that she filed anyway, because the note was against a provenance note about two documents and *this is not that*. She cannot get a man from Vellum to come to a warrant faster than the public room, and she did not try.
- **Family:** wrote to her mother. Wend Quill is her mother's brother and the hand on both redactions, and the certificate she filed on the 28th and the certificate she filed on the 11th are the two sentences that will make a registrar examine him. **He is not spoken of in Movement III.**
- **Vulnerability:** folio eleven is still in the strong room. **She has now been in the room with it.** Rule nine is still correct. The restriction is no longer what is stopping the district; the district's problem is that a schedule reference is not a source.
- **Relationship position:** **the Chapter 16 agreement, on conditions, with distrust intact, and unrepaired.** Chapter 27 loads conditions two and four and holds both. Three names on the desk list and none alone on the certificate. Condition four said out loud in a public room: *I told you the first time, and I am telling you now.* **And the chapter's best beat is hers, and it is not warmth:** she stops him misstating his own district in a register.
- **She is the only character who has heard the word *Gauge* spoken** and her position on it is unchanged. In Chapter 24 she is a day and a night away and does not know what happened and would say she has never been told anything by a piece of paper.

## Sluice Warden Pell

- Counting channels, not stories. Nineteen years in the post. Three chapters of his own, and the third is the institution working.
- Holds the Day 1 notice with the empty cause box, the Day 20 entry timed at 16:00, the Day 22 return with blank boxes and a filled remark, the stamped district application, the returned application with four grounds, **Hanne Roeg's letter of the 12th, and Tarin Rusk's engineer's statement of the 15th, signed in a yard in front of a hundred and forty people.**
- **He fills in two of the five boxes on the Day 22 form** — *row number*, as **none held**, and *tanks* — and leaves **keeper, gallons and last serviced** empty. **There is no box headed *time* on the form at all,** and that is the spine of his remark: *a warden enters the time a condition begins and this warden was not present and will not enter a time he did not witness, and this form has no box headed time, and it is the only box I have ever wanted in it.* His own Chapter 10 rule, turned against himself, and the most honest sentence in the batch.
- **He explains the other two empties as one reason and not two:** a *gallons* box can hold only one of the cistern, the dispensary and the seventy gallons a day going out of the door, so a single number in it would be true and a lie; and *last serviced* is a date a keeper writes, and this office has no keeper.
- **He adds a line nobody asked for:** the Day 1 notice with the empty cause box is the reason the condition could be entered at all, **and he puts his own six days on the record next to it** — reported to this office on the 9th, entered on the 15th, fourteen days from the notice. *The six are the office's and nobody else's.*
- **He will write *keeper, none held* in the empty box, in a fair hand, in a book with four copies in it,** so that a man arriving with a form in six weeks finds those two words and knows a district looked at a box and left it empty on purpose.
- **In his closing speech he refuses the blame the yard wants to hand him.** Wick Venn's hand was lost on a rope on a mill road in the small hours of Sunday morning, a day and a night after the descent set out, and Pell had no part in it: *it was not this office and it was not me, and I am not going to stand here and pretend to you that I would have stopped him.* He turns it into the chapter's argument instead: had there been a name in that box a fortnight ago there would have been a person a man with a form could have come and find.
- He has not been threatened and has not been offered anything. The only weapon he has is that nothing he writes has ever been found to be made up, and on the 15th he nearly spent it by reading his own opinion into a book and caught himself.

## Halden Roe — the volume's antagonist

- Acting water commissioner. **In context on the 15th and not in danger, not confronted, and not spoken to.** Two gauge certificates in a deal box in a store at the works, one at decommissioning and one four years ago, both certifying the chamber void, no residual head, no standing water, no maintenance required, both in his hand, both with *maintained* beneath in the same pen — and thirty yards away a chalk board reading 5·9.
- **The logic of the lie, which is arithmetic and not conspiracy:** a chamber recorded void is a chamber with no allocation, and abstraction out of a head that is in no allocation infringes no allocation. That is what a false void buys. He falsified the gauge certificates and the decommissioning certificate over six years. **The substituted leaf of twenty-six years ago is not his; that is Wend Quill's, in the registry.**
- **Nobody in Movement III asks who falsified them.** The certificate is read by a lamp in an unlit store by a suspended engineer with two charges, who writes four lines and can do nothing with them because the only man who can act on it is the man who signed both sheets and is not standing in that lane.
- **He never uses magic, never learns to, and does not know the One Map's name.** He survives to be a cooperating witness in Movement V and is not redeemed.

## Yara Sen

- 41. Water physician and public-health examiner. Third chapter of her own, and it is the best one.
- Holds a quarto account book with three columns and forty-one names, nine with the marks — four at three points, five at two — and a fourth column of her own invention headed *at risk*. **One of the four is now a fourth-stage case: Sab Doulton, seven, of the second step.** Palms cracked, sick twice since the ninth, twice asking her mother where the front door is. **The fourth stage is the one that kills, this is the district's first, and she does not die in this volume.**
- **Her formula has stopped being a formula and she says so in a yard:** sixteen and ten and forty-four is seventy a day, and a child in the fourth stage does not take four gallons, and there is no figure for that in the basin. **Restating 70 in Movement IV must restate the derivation and must also state that the derivation has stopped.**
- **She put the word *discharge* on a slate in a yard in daylight, before anybody could ask her a question**, because a physician who answers a question has already lost the ground. She told a yard to go on selling its only water to strangers and wrote the hour on a slate so that in four weeks somebody can ask her why. She refused the arithmetic of singling out one child.
- **She put the row at Ashgate on the table before anybody asked her for it**, so that nobody could say afterwards that a physician kept it back: *I have a right to that answer only if somebody who is not a physician asks me for it and not if I go and take it.* A man near the arch asked, and she said *I know you would.*
- Her requirement stands at **70 gallons a day** and the money to meet it is **£3 16s, which is 912 pence and at a shilling the gallon is 76 gallons** — a day and a bit, and the cart is the constraint. The dispensary has 80 gallons of clean water, gone on the 18th. She has refused to be anybody's ally.
- **She entered Niko's spoken decision in her book without being asked**, on the grounds that a thing remembered by one man is a thing that dies with him. A small boundary of her own, not commented on.

## Niko Venn

- 22. Not an apprentice, and there is still no clause in the world for what he is. **He has now given up on the Water Office out loud and meant it.**
- **Knows the terrace by body and the works by his father's hill. Second chapter of his own, and the descent is paid for.**
- **His error is on the page and it is not a punishment:** he took the second lamp and went ninety feet down the adit alone, because it is his father's hill and there is a figure on a board somebody paid not to write down. He made a camp, swept it, rationed the oil, and came back up both times, and he said all of that before anybody accused him of anything. **The cost of it is Wick's hand.**
- **He counted the stair: two hundred and forty-one steps.** He copied the contractors' head column onto the back of his hand because he does not trust a figure he cannot put down, and he wrote on that hand that the ladder was not a kindness — out loud first, and he was right.
- **His change:** he is going to be a thing that has no name in this city and no paper and no wage, because his aunt kept a low pump for nine years and *when she died there was nothing to hand on, because there was no name anywhere*. **He asked for it to be remembered by one man rather than cut in a yard, and refused a slate and a chalk when Wick offered them, because it is a rule in this lane and he is not breaking it with a thing nobody asked for.**
- Still has a debt at a chandler's, and the chandler is owed on the first.
- He is not a keeper, not a witness, and not on any sheet, and he has stopped trying to be.

## Wick Venn

- 38. **Two chapters, and the second is where he is the engine of the scene.**
- **The left hand, and it is the cost of the movement.** Full thickness off the heel of the hand and the base of the thumb, grazed eight inches on a quarry face at about ten past one in the morning on the 6th while holding a ladder. **No sensation in the outer two fingers, and none in the other three except under pressure.** Cannot close the hand. Cannot hold a rope, a bar, a pipe, a rail or a cistern lid.
- **He can tie.** A becket, a bowline and a half hitch, with his knee on the rope and the tail in his teeth. *I can do knots to the end of my life with this hand. What I cannot do is carry a bar, hold a pipe, take a cistern lid off a hinge, or go down a ladder. And that is the whole of what I have done for nineteen years.*
- **What he has lost:** the Reach's winter work, which is half the year, and the **journeyman's paper at the lodge of the Reach waterworks at the fenner turn**, offered by Osgar Pike in a doorway nine years ago and never withdrawn. The door frame is still on Ondra's wall and still on him.
- **He blames himself on the page and rules Niko out of it**, and nobody lets him off and nobody lets Niko take it: *it is nobody else's. It is mine, and I have written it on a board.*
- **He is not a symbol and is not one.** He is a man who will not be thanked, who carried eighty gallons of water four miles at four in the morning, who pushed his cart the last three hundred yards with his shoulder, and who said nothing at all to Errol Slee about the thirtieth and has been the better of two men for it.
- He held the rope on the 2nd and on the 5th, and rigged the only thing that was rigged, and is on nothing else, and refuses his own name for it.

## The row at Ashgate — new, and a fact rather than a faction

- **Eleven houses, thirty-four persons, about thirty years, no row, no schedule entry, no keeper.** They call the place Ashgate, and on every sheet in this city Ashgate is a structure with a closure date and a warden's seal. **They have blue doors and so does every terrace in Cresset, and Tarin does not comment on it, and it must not be made the same thing.**
- **Nell Drust, 39,** keeps the row and has for sixteen years. **The water comes up out of the ground under her own kitchen floor at about 103 gallons an hour and has always done.** Four people in the row cannot be got to the far well and the far well is dry. Her verdict: *it is not a discovery, it is a kitchen.*
- **The well has never been tested by anybody and no instrument in the basin would test it.** Tarin has said in a signed public document that he cannot promise it will be the same water tomorrow. **Thirty-four people do not know they are on a chamber.**
- **Coll Preece, about 50**, four years on the pump, paid by the can, not frightened of anybody, has never met a signature and does not believe the commissioner knows the hill exists.
- **The boy on the staging, about fifteen, never named.** Paid weekly, on the staging every night since the 2nd, **not paid since the 2nd**, not evidence, not a man on a staging, and the person in this batch most harmed by a piece of paper. He brought Wick a box of clean linen and a jar of oil because a Mrs. Drust sends it down on Mondays, and Wick refused to have his name taken.
- **Nobody in this row has been asked anything by this city in thirty years, and the district's question about whether it may ask them for water is written on a piece of paper in a bag on a cart going up a cattle track.**

## Mara Quill — the slow-burn relationship

## Aldis Kerr, 71 — the fourth thing, and the word, and not the box
