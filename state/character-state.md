# Character State

Updated after Batch 0003 (Volume 01, Chapters 21–30, Days 34–46). Compact and factual; use this for the next batch rather than re-reading the chapters.

## Tarin Rusk — protagonist

- **Age 33.** Senior flow engineer, Cresset Water Office. Rents nothing; sleeps in a dry corner of an empty cistern house inside the second gatehouse, under a watchman who is required to walk him to the gate at dawn and no further.
- **Power:** still **Stage 2 (Name)**, and **not a step further, and not a step back.** The Chapter 24 panel is a *perception* and he perceives a place: a chamber, a roof, a street, and a level in feet and inches. **No route is opened, named, moved or shut anywhere in Chapters 21–30. He has a trace, a cut record he cannot add to, a stone he cannot cut, and no method for finding a witness.** **Bend belongs to Chapters 51–150.**
- **Status:** Suspended since **Day 10** under **charge one — tampering with city records**. Charged again on **Day 20** under **charge two — unauthorized opening of a sealed structure and service of water from a reservoir the ledger records as closed.** Two open charges, two subjects, two dates, **never joined.** The hearing on charge one was set for the 22nd and **adjourned without a date entered**. Charge two's hearing has still not been set. **A third is forming: an unauthorised descent on a sealed works from the quarry side, discovered by the same office that reported the lamp on the ledge, and nobody has charged him with it yet.**
- **Lost:** desk, seal, district reference, keys, access to the lower terrace. Keeps the blue field book, the bar, the folding rule, the chalk stub, a knuckle of string, the tin pot, and **Roke's gauged oak stop-log with the pin.**
- **Name debt (minor, Chapter 7):** the two-finger press and the taste of his bitter tea. **Neither has healed and both are shown three more times in this batch** — it costs him the ladder rail in Chapter 23, the chisel on the chamber stone in Chapter 24, and in Chapter 21 he names it in his own pack list and does not try it. Chapter 25: a woman he has never met makes him tea and it has no taste in it and he drinks all of it and says nothing to anybody then or afterwards.
- **The three candidate explanations of the 30th are still apart and are not joined.** Chapter 24 produced no measurement that separates them and he did not write a fourth guess. The book now also holds: the chamber, 9 ft 4 in / 1 ft 9 in / **11 ft 1 in**; the roof, which is a floor; the tallies; and the four sentences about the voices.
- **He has come back from Chapter 17 without certainty.** He is the man who has been down a hole and cannot yet say what the hole is for, and who has found that his private certainty about his mother was the least reliable instrument he brought with him. **He wrote down four and not one, and that stands.**
- **He can do his own trade and cannot do the thing he came for.** He gauged a well to 103 gallons an hour with a pail and a watch and checked it twice. He has no instrument that would test that water and said so in a yard, in a signed document, in public.
- **He is on the record twice more and it is his choice.** His name is in Vellum's register of restricted consultations against the blue steps, with a struck figure and a correction in the same hand. He signed an engineer's statement on the 15th, the first thing he has signed in thirty-six days, and it goes up to the office whose signature is on the certificates.
- **He has refused on the page again.** Four people asked to put a hand on a route; four refusals; he thinks three of them were right and he will not argue with the fourth. He did not use his mother's name in a hole. He wrote *four and not one.*
- **He has not yet been to Ashgate with the news.** He has been there once, and Nell Drust gave him tea, and the thirty-four people in that row do not know they are on a chamber. **That is the next thing he has to do and he knows it.**
- **Left shoulder:** torn on Day 1, re-torn on Day 30, and in Chapter 23 he is told by three people, twice, that he is holding a lamp he should not be holding.
- **Open wants:** to get water to a district with 950 gallons; to read what is in the keep box at the works; to find a keeper; to be on a hill with an instrument and a crew. **New and specific: to get a district examiner to arrive before a doctor runs out of clean water, and he has just made that more likely on purpose.**
- **His fear, restated in his own words on the 15th in a yard:** *you have all had six weeks of me standing in this yard telling you things are possible, and I have been wrong in this yard and it cost a man his hand and it cost a child a fortnight.*

## Mara Quill — the slow-burn relationship

- **Age 31.** Provenance record conservator, Municipal Memory Office, Vellum. Eleven years in service. Non-magical and stays non-magical for the series.
- **Power:** none, and never any. Her expertise is provenance — nib cut, gum, batch stamp on the tin lid, sizing, watermark, pressure shadow, pressure of the stroke. **In Chapter 24 she spends four minutes reading the pressure of a three-digit number under a raking lamp and gets 118, and she says out loud that a shadow of a number is a shadow of the pressure of a number.**
- **Wants:** Glassstep's legal name, land claims, and basin share. Unchanged, and now impossible by a different route: the district's name is in Vellum's register and the warden's book and a store at a works and a letter in a bag, and it is in none of them as a *row*.
- **Holds:** the unlogged one-to-one drawing, still in her own drawer under a tray of brass weights, still the only copy nobody can check. Two one-to-one tracings of the Glassstep redaction folio, one in her hand and one in Hanne Roeg's, both certified as made from one original in one room in one sitting by two named hands. **And now, since the 11th: a certificate recording the existence, form and date of a pressure shadow and a schedule reference, countersigned by a second reader, which does not transcribe the reference and can be checked by any person against a public document in a warden's office.** That is the first thing she has ever produced that another person can falsify.
- **Career cost added in Chapter 27:** she files the sentence about the *hand* a second time, and says out loud that she knows the registry's marginal note of a year out exists and that she filed anyway, because the note was against a provenance note about two documents and *this is not that*. She cannot get a man from Vellum to come to a warrant faster than the public room, and she did not try.
- **Family:** wrote to her mother. Wend Quill is her mother's brother and the hand on both redactions, and the certificate she filed on the 28th and the certificate she filed on the 11th are the two sentences that will make a registrar examine him. **He is not spoken of in Movement III.**
- **Vulnerability:** folio eleven is still in the strong room. **She has now been in the room with it.** Rule nine is still correct. The restriction is no longer what is stopping the district; the district's problem is that a schedule reference is not a source.
- **Relationship position:** **the Chapter 16 agreement, on conditions, with distrust intact, and unrepaired.** Chapter 27 loads conditions two and four and holds both. Three names on the desk list and none alone on the certificate. Condition four said out loud in a public room: *I told you the first time, and I am telling you now.* **And the chapter's best beat is hers, and it is not warmth:** she stops him misstating his own district in a register.
- **She is the only character who has heard the word *Gauge* spoken** and her position on it is unchanged. In Chapter 24 she is a day and a night away and does not know what happened and would say she has never been told anything by a piece of paper.

## Sluice Warden Pell

- Counting channels, not stories. Nineteen years in the post. Three chapters of his own, and the third is the institution working.
- Holds the Day 1 notice with the empty cause box, the Day 20 entry timed at 16:00, the Day 22 return with blank boxes and a filled remark, the stamped district application, the returned application with four grounds, **Hanne Roeg's letter of the 12th, and Tarin Rusk's engineer's statement of the 15th, signed in a yard in front of a hundred and forty people.**
- **He fills in two of the five boxes on the Day 22 form** — *row number*, as **none held**, and *tanks* — and leaves **keeper, gallons and last serviced** empty. **There is no box headed *time* on the form at all,** and that is the spine of his remark: *a warden enters the time a condition begins and this warden was not present and will not enter a time he did not witness, and this form has no box headed time, and it is the only box I have ever wanted in it.* His own Chapter 10 rule, turned against himself, and the most honest sentence in the batch.
- **He explains the other two empties as one reason and not two:** a *gallons* box can hold only one of the cistern, the dispensary and the seventy gallons a day going out of the door, so a single number in it would be true and a lie; and *last serviced* is a date a keeper writes, and this office has no keeper.
- **He adds a line nobody asked for:** the Day 1 notice with the empty cause box is the reason the condition could be entered at all, **and he puts his own six days on the record next to it** — reported to this office on the 9th, entered on the 15th, fourteen days from the notice. *The six are the office's and nobody else's.*
- **He will write *keeper, none held* in the empty box, in a fair hand, in a book with four copies in it,** so that a man arriving with a form in six weeks finds those two words and knows a district looked at a box and left it empty on purpose.
- **In his closing speech he refuses the blame the yard wants to hand him.** Wick Venn's hand was lost on a rope on a mill road in the small hours of Sunday morning, a day and a night after the descent set out, and Pell had no part in it: *it was not this office and it was not me, and I am not going to stand here and pretend to you that I would have stopped him.* He turns it into the chapter's argument instead: had there been a name in that box a fortnight ago there would have been a person a man with a form could have come and find.
- He has not been threatened and has not been offered anything. The only weapon he has is that nothing he writes has ever been found to be made up, and on the 15th he nearly spent it by reading his own opinion into a book and caught himself.

## Halden Roe — the volume's antagonist

- Acting water commissioner. **In context on the 15th and not in danger, not confronted, and not spoken to.** Two gauge certificates in a deal box in a store at the works, one at decommissioning and one four years ago, both certifying the chamber void, no residual head, no standing water, no maintenance required, both in his hand, both with *maintained* beneath in the same pen — and thirty yards away a chalk board reading 5·9.
- **The logic of the lie, which is arithmetic and not conspiracy:** a chamber recorded void is a chamber with no allocation, and abstraction out of a head that is in no allocation infringes no allocation. That is what a false void buys. He falsified the gauge certificates and the decommissioning certificate over six years. **The substituted leaf of twenty-six years ago is not his; that is Wend Quill's, in the registry.**
- **Nobody in Movement III asks who falsified them.** The certificate is read by a lamp in an unlit store by a suspended engineer with two charges, who writes four lines and can do nothing with them because the only man who can act on it is the man who signed both sheets and is not standing in that lane.
- **He never uses magic, never learns to, and does not know the One Map's name.** He survives to be a cooperating witness in Movement V and is not redeemed.

## Yara Sen

- 41. Water physician and public-health examiner. Third chapter of her own, and it is the best one.
- Holds a quarto account book with three columns and forty-one names, nine with the marks — four at three points, five at two — and a fourth column of her own invention headed *at risk*. **One of the four is now a fourth-stage case: Sab Doulton, seven, of the second step.** Palms cracked, sick twice since the ninth, twice asking her mother where the front door is. **The fourth stage is the one that kills, this is the district's first, and she does not die in this volume.**
- **Her formula has stopped being a formula and she says so in a yard:** sixteen and ten and forty-four is seventy a day, and a child in the fourth stage does not take four gallons, and there is no figure for that in the basin. **Restating 70 in Movement IV must restate the derivation and must also state that the derivation has stopped.**
- **She put the word *discharge* on a slate in a yard in daylight, before anybody could ask her a question**, because a physician who answers a question has already lost the ground. She told a yard to go on selling its only water to strangers and wrote the hour on a slate so that in four weeks somebody can ask her why. She refused the arithmetic of singling out one child.
- **She put the row at Ashgate on the table before anybody asked her for it**, so that nobody could say afterwards that a physician kept it back: *I have a right to that answer only if somebody who is not a physician asks me for it and not if I go and take it.* A man near the arch asked, and she said *I know you would.*
- Her requirement stands at **70 gallons a day** and the money to meet it is **£3 16s, which is 912 pence and at a shilling the gallon is 76 gallons** — a day and a bit, and the cart is the constraint. The dispensary has 80 gallons of clean water, gone on the 18th. She has refused to be anybody's ally.
- **She entered Niko's spoken decision in her book without being asked**, on the grounds that a thing remembered by one man is a thing that dies with him. A small boundary of her own, not commented on.

## Niko Venn

- 22. Not an apprentice, and there is still no clause in the world for what he is. **He has now given up on the Water Office out loud and meant it.**
- **Knows the terrace by body and the works by his father's hill. Second chapter of his own, and the descent is paid for.**
- **His error is on the page and it is not a punishment:** he took the second lamp and went ninety feet down the adit alone, because it is his father's hill and there is a figure on a board somebody paid not to write down. He made a camp, swept it, rationed the oil, and came back up both times, and he said all of that before anybody accused him of anything. **The cost of it is Wick's hand.**
- **He counted the stair: two hundred and forty-one steps.** He copied the contractors' head column onto the back of his hand because he does not trust a figure he cannot put down, and he wrote on that hand that the ladder was not a kindness — out loud first, and he was right.
- **His change:** he is going to be a thing that has no name in this city and no paper and no wage, because his aunt kept a low pump for nine years and *when she died there was nothing to hand on, because there was no name anywhere*. **He asked for it to be remembered by one man rather than cut in a yard, and refused a slate and a chalk when Wick offered them, because it is a rule in this lane and he is not breaking it with a thing nobody asked for.**
- Still has a debt at a chandler's, and the chandler is owed on the first.
- He is not a keeper, not a witness, and not on any sheet, and he has stopped trying to be.

## Wick Venn

- 38. **Two chapters, and the second is where he is the engine of the scene.**
- **The left hand, and it is the cost of the movement.** Full thickness off the heel of the hand and the base of the thumb, grazed eight inches on a quarry face at about ten past one in the morning on the 6th while holding a ladder. **No sensation in the outer two fingers, and none in the other three except under pressure.** Cannot close the hand. Cannot hold a rope, a bar, a pipe, a rail or a cistern lid.
- **He can tie.** A becket, a bowline and a half hitch, with his knee on the rope and the tail in his teeth. *I can do knots to the end of my life with this hand. What I cannot do is carry a bar, hold a pipe, take a cistern lid off a hinge, or go down a ladder. And that is the whole of what I have done for nineteen years.*
- **What he has lost:** the Reach's winter work, which is half the year, and the **journeyman's paper at the lodge of the Reach waterworks at the fenner turn**, offered by Osgar Pike in a doorway nine years ago and never withdrawn. The door frame is still on Ondra's wall and still on him.
- **He blames himself on the page and rules Niko out of it**, and nobody lets him off and nobody lets Niko take it: *it is nobody else's. It is mine, and I have written it on a board.*
- **He is not a symbol and is not one.** He is a man who will not be thanked, who carried eighty gallons of water four miles at four in the morning, who pushed his cart the last three hundred yards with his shoulder, and who said nothing at all to Errol Slee about the thirtieth and has been the better of two men for it.
- He held the rope on the 2nd and on the 5th, and rigged the only thing that was rigged, and is on nothing else, and refuses his own name for it.

## The row at Ashgate — new, and a fact rather than a faction

- **Eleven houses, thirty-four persons, about thirty years, no row, no schedule entry, no keeper.** They call the place Ashgate, and on every sheet in this city Ashgate is a structure with a closure date and a warden's seal. **They have blue doors and so does every terrace in Cresset, and Tarin does not comment on it, and it must not be made the same thing.**
- **Nell Drust, 39,** keeps the row and has for sixteen years. **The water comes up out of the ground under her own kitchen floor at about 103 gallons an hour and has always done.** Four people in the row cannot be got to the far well and the far well is dry. Her verdict: *it is not a discovery, it is a kitchen.*
- **The well has never been tested by anybody and no instrument in the basin would test it.** Tarin has said in a signed public document that he cannot promise it will be the same water tomorrow. **Thirty-four people do not know they are on a chamber.**
- **Coll Preece, about 50**, four years on the pump, paid by the can, not frightened of anybody, has never met a signature and does not believe the commissioner knows the hill exists.
- **The boy on the staging, about fifteen, never named.** Paid weekly, on the staging every night since the 2nd, **not paid since the 2nd**, not evidence, not a man on a staging, and the person in this batch most harmed by a piece of paper. He brought Wick a box of clean linen and a jar of oil because a Mrs. Drust sends it down on Mondays, and Wick refused to have his name taken.
- **Nobody in this row has been asked anything by this city in thirty years, and the district's question about whether it may ask them for water is written on a piece of paper in a bag on a cart going up a cattle track.**

- **Age 33.** Senior flow engineer, Cresset Water Office, under a stand-down since the 10th. Sleeping in the room over the back stair of the low house at the left of the arch, which Ondra gave him on the Friday of the 25th without being asked and without telling anybody.

## Mara Quill — the slow-burn relationship

- **Age 31.** Provenance record conservator, Municipal Memory Office, Vellum. **Came up a cattle track on the 25th with a printed summons to be a witness in a room over a sluice store on the terrace road, a day and a night each way.**

Do not thank me for it.*** He said nothing to that and she told him not to.

## Sluice Warden Pell

- Counting channels, not stories. Nineteen years in the post.
- **Four chapters of his own now.** In Chapter 31 he read out the Office's reply and said three things first, the third being that **a sheet asks this district a question, in a printed line, and the answer is yes or no, and he has been on this road nineteen years and has never had to explain to a yard what a yes means.** In Chapter 34 he read a return of supervision that is not his paper, said so before he read a line of it, and put the cause box of a warden's form up against a contractor's form in a yard.
- **His five boxes are unchanged: two filled, three empty, and *keeper, none held* is going into the empty one in a fair hand in a book with four copies in it.** And he has now said in a room, in front of a hundred and forty people, that the third box is the *keeper* and *that is the whole of what a warden may put in a box and I am not going to put the end of it in a box.*
- **He has not been threatened and has not been offered anything.** The only weapon he has is that nothing he writes has ever been found to be made up, and he spends it by reading other people's documents out in daylight.

## Halden Roe — the volume's antagonist

- Acting water commissioner. **In context on the 27th and not in danger, not confronted, not spoken to, and not named in a single line of Chapters 31–40 except as *the acting commissioner* on a piece of paper.**
- Two gauge certificates in a deal box in a store at the works, thirty yards from a chalk board reading 5·9. Both his hand. Both *maintained*.
- **Two things have happened to him in this batch and neither is a threat.** A chair at a preliminary inquiry said, in a room, that a certificate is a finding, that it is in a store this office has never opened, and that he will not rule on it in anybody's absence. And Iven Marrow said, to Tarin, in a lane, *nobody certifies a chamber void because a plate is the wrong bore — a plate changes a figure somebody wrote in a day-book, and a certificate takes a hand and a decision and a reason, and if you are ever in a room where somebody is explaining those two sheets with a brass ring in their hand, that is the man and not the ring.* **Marrow is not a conspirator and does not know what is in the box. He must not resolve it.**
- He never uses magic, never learns to, and does not know the One Map's name. **He survives to be a cooperating witness in Movement V and is not redeemed.** The officer whose name is on the requisition answers the district's request in Ch 40 and does not refuse it.

## Yara Sen

- 41. Water physician and public-health examiner. **Second chapter of her own in the batch, and the best writing in it, and she is wrong in it.**
- **She is corrected by a patient's mother.** A woman at the top end of the lane says *Petra is not one of yours* — Petra Olask, nine, cracked hands since the spring before last, of lime — and Yara has to stand in a yard and say that she has had five in that column who was four, four times, in her own hand, and that the reason she is in it is that she is a child with bad hands and she has been counting bad hands. **The entry is struck and not replaced, because there is nothing else she can measure.**
- **Her requirement is now 68 gallons a day and she re-derives it aloud on a step in a yard in front of a hundred and forty people and gets it twice with a stick:** 4 at 4 = 16, 4 at 2 = 8, 118 at 3 pints = 354 pints = 44¼, and 16 + 8 + 44 = 68. **And the line that is the chapter's thesis: a figure that goes down when you check it is a figure you check twice, and correcting it has made the district look better, and that is the most frightening thing I have found in a book in three years.**
- **The only test she has is a boiled-jar residue**, and she will not give a number: *both crusts are white, the one on the right has more of it and has run up the glaze in a ridge, and I would not stake a child's dinner on that with a pint and a ring and an hour's boiling in it, and I will not invent a number to be impressive in front of nine children.* **Any person may repeat it at the cost of a pint, a glazed jar, a ring and an hour.** She has no instrument and says so three times in a yard.
- **Her central public claim, made on the 16th and proven on the 20th, is the mechanism of the whole movement:** the salt in that tank is not being used up, it is being left, because seventy gallons a day goes out of mouths carrying its salt and fifty gallons a day goes to the sun carrying none. And the first water out of a main that has been standing a fortnight is the worst of it.
- **She fights the classification in the only language it has, and she does not call it a lie**, because the plant was resumed without instruction and that is true, and because *a physician who answers a question has already lost the ground.* Her file is six numbered lines, a heading, a date, a signature, and one request: that anybody about to record a cause for a change in a body of water be required to state, in the same hand and the same line, whether a measurement was made, by whom, on what day, and by what method. *A cause without a measurement is an account of somebody's day.*
- **She refuses to write a clinical question into the examiner's box**, because she is the only person in the lane with a title and filling it would fill the box she has spent a month saying nobody should fill. She will say it in a room with a clerk writing, in her own time, on the record.
- **She is nobody's ally.** She declines to tell a yard which of the three answers on the form it should give, and puts the reason in public: *that is not a physician's sentence, and I am not going to stand in front of nine children and make it look like a professional opinion.*

## Niko Venn

- 22. Not an apprentice, and there is still no clause in the world for what he is.
- **He has said out loud that he is not going down the hill and he has not gone back on it**, in Chapter 31 and again in Chapter 33. **He is not given a route and he is not asked for one.**
- **What he is now: a count.** Two figures in chalk on the cistern coping with the date under them, every morning since the ninth, the fifteenth day, the sixteenth, the seventeenth, the eighteenth, the nineteenth, and on the twentieth **two numbers in one day, one over the other, which is the first time a tank in this lane has had that.** *No log in. No setting. No meter of any kind. This is not the same as the twentieth.* And on the twentieth: *the tank came up a hand's width in an hour, which is a rate I have not seen since the twentieth day of the month before in a yard with a warden and a stone.*
- **He has finished the fifth of five notched pebbles**, which are a part-day count of time spent on a hill nobody is going to fund, and he does not know what they are for, and he has not been given a name for them and has stopped trying to find one. *A pebble is not a keeper.*
- **He asked Yara Sen in a yard to say out loud that his chalk is the only instrument in the district, and she refused, in front of everybody: *a boy with a stick is not an instrument, and the day this lane starts calling things instruments instead of instruments is the day a man comes down that track with a form and gets told the form is one of them.*** He took it and said that was fair. **And then, four days later, at the dispensary, in a room, he said: *I will, and I cannot read a word, so I will have to get somebody to read it to me and I will have to trust them, and I have already been told by a woman in this lane that that is how things are done here.*** He has a cut, a chalk, a stick, five pebbles, and an adit nobody in this city is going to fund.
- **He came down the lane at ten past five on the 26th with a lamp he had not lit, and neither of them said anything about that**, and stood in a live channel with a man for forty minutes because a head of water going into a closed district is a job and there is a job.
- **He carried the district's request up the hill on the 27th**, because a man who has said out loud that he is not going to chase the Water Office is not going to start now, and because somebody has to tell a girl of about fourteen with a pail in each hand that a document is coming with her name on it.
- Still has a debt at a chandler's, and the chandler is owed on the first.

## Wick Venn

- 38. **His trade is a third of what it was, and the number is on the page: nine years at the Reach was a shilling and six a day for six days, and in the winter a man could get a quid, and he does not get a quid any more, and he is not going to get one out of a bonus.** A Guild day rate is four shillings and he refuses a fifth as a bonus, in front of a surveyor, and says the day a bonus is a shilling and six is a day he is going to be glad about is a bad day.
- **The left hand, and it is Wick's and only Wick's.** Left palm, full thickness off the heel and the base of the thumb, grazed eight inches on a quarry face at about ten past one in the morning on the 6th of the month before. No sensation in the outer two; none in the other three except under pressure. Cannot close it. Cannot hold a rope, a bar, a pipe, a rail or a cistern lid. **He can tie.** What he has lost: the Reach's winter work, which is half the year, and the journeyman's paper at the lodge of the fenner turn, offered by Osgar Pike in a doorway nine years ago and never withdrawn. The door frame is still on Ondra's wall and still on him, and **Ondra names it out loud in Chapter 39 and says it does not make them even.**
- **He is competent, ordinary, and not thanked.** He pushed a barrow up a switchback cut in three pitches. He found out that the first pitch is fine and the second is where you need a right hand, and he said so once, halfway up, to a man with a level, and never said it again. The woman at the top end asked what he was paid and he told her and gave the reason, and nobody in the blue steps thanked him and nobody will.
- **He offered a slate and chalk in Chapter 29 and was refused, and the rule in that lane has not moved.** He is not on any sheet, he is not a keeper, and **Ondra's Chapter 39 speech is the chapter's answer to him: three of the four men who came through that door this month wanted something, and the one who did not is the one who came back empty.**

## Iven Marrow — new, and the second rung

- **54.** Sluicewright first, thirty-one years in the trade, master of the Open Channel Guild. Not a mage. Does not know the name of anything. **Not caught out, and not frightening, and patient.**
- **What he offers, in this order, in a cleared filter-bed store at the top of the works:** Grade two, inspector of high gates, **£31 a year against the Office's £40**, six men, a tools chest, a mule, a boat. The reading card at the counting-house at Vellum for the whole of **eleven thousand four hundred leaves**; **three men have held it, two of them are seventy, and one of those is dead, and he has had it for six years and has not got time to read it, and he calls that his guilt.** **Two hundred and thirty-nine plate specifications — bore, date, gate, the man who set it — and with the list he can put a thumb in a brass ring in a store and know what it should read.** And **eleven weeks at a bench at Bexhill on Tuesday mornings, nine years of Tuesday mornings, and in a year he will ask Tarin to check a setting and be annoyed about it in front of people.**
- **The price: the blue steps is not in the report,** for three reasons that are all good and one of which is the best argument Tarin has heard in a month — *a district's water supply on a schedule is a thing that can be closed, and I have watched a road go onto a schedule and come off it eight years later by a man who had never walked it, and I have never once in my life seen a thing go onto a schedule and stay there.*
- **He is wrong about his own second reason and says so before Tarin can:** he has believed for twenty years that a party who cannot be named cannot be given anything, and he has watched it be the reason a village got nothing twice. He does not finish the sentence, because the rest of it is *and then I will change my own practice, and I have three hundred men who will want to know why a sluicewright from a terrace in the lower fold has changed the articles.*
- **He does not press.** He says the offer does not expire, that he will make it again, and that he will not enjoy it. He says the *thing* he is a Guild man for is his records and the *thing* he is a teacher for is his work: *anything I know, I will teach you in a shed; anything I hold, I will sell you.*
- **What he gives away: the Bexhill bench book.** A working book meant to be written in by whoever is at the bench. Nothing restricted, nothing a record, nothing for sale, and if he put it in a report Tarin could take it back. It is in Tarin's coat inside a bad shoulder and it was going for longer than the works at Ashgate has been sealed.
- **What he knows and says first, unasked:** the plate explanation for the board, and then the correction — *nobody certifies a chamber void because a plate is the wrong bore.* And that he has known about the two certificates since the 21st, from a counting-house, and did not ask for them and would not have opened them if he had not been sent to that works at all.
- **The dry gate at Sallow, six years, unanswered, and it is Volume 2's question from the Guild's side.**
- **Set up and not resolved. His offer is still on the table.**

## The row at Ashgate — told, and not yet answered

- **Eleven houses, thirty-four persons, about thirty years, no row, no schedule entry, no keeper.** They call the place Ashgate, and on every sheet in this city Ashgate is a structure with a closure date and a warden's seal. **They have blue doors and so does every terrace in Cresset and the two are not the same thing and the text has not said otherwise.**
- **Told on the 17th, in daylight, in Nell Drust's yard, in front of twenty-eight people, and nobody shouted.** The measurement twice, the roof that is a floor and a street, the hearth and flue and the cut lead stub of a floor drain, the stair in eleven steps to a door under a store, and the two sentences that mattered: **the well may change, and he cannot promise it will be the same water in a month.** It is on a slate in that lane with a signature on it, and he had put the same sentence in a signed document two days earlier where a hundred and forty people heard him say it.
- **Nell Drust, 39,** keeps the row and has for sixteen years. **The well is at 68 gallons an hour against 103, and there is grit in the bottom of the pail, and the pail has never had grit in it in her life.** She timed it herself with her own watch, twice, and chalked it on the back of a shutter. **The horse trades the well.** She will not send water down that hill while that plant can be started by anybody with a horse, and if there is a day when there is no horse she will come, and will not send it in a barrow, *because I am the keeper of it and I have never once been called that by anybody and I am not going to be called it by a piece of paper.* **Her name is on a slate in the lane and she made a girl of about fourteen write it and made her put Nell's name on it and not her own.**
- **There is a cistern at the bottom end of that row, filled once in the year before the flood, never opened since, standing in the ground under eleven houses with a lid on it, and the plant is drawing off the same water it is standing in. She has asked for a man with a bar to come and look at the lid of it before anybody in that row is thirsty, and has not been answered.**
- **Coll Preece, about 50**, four years on the plant, paid by the can, started it at twenty past three on Sunday morning with a horse out of his own pocket and told one person about it. He is not a villain and he is not the man who gave the order, and the yard knows both of those things.
- **Nobody in this row has consented to anything. A document went up a cattle track on the 27th that will put them in a file. Chapter 41 owes them a visit.**
- **The boy on the staging, about fifteen, never named**, has been sent home on the Friday evening at eight with **a day and a night** of warning and his own wages for the week in Coll Preece's coat, and Coll Preece walked the horse up the mill road himself on the Saturday evening and started the plant himself at twenty past three on the Sunday. **He is owed weekly, on the staging every night since the 2nd, unpaid since the 2nd, not evidence, and the person in this batch most harmed by an office.**

## Ondra, 51 — new, and he hosts him

- Landlord of the low house at the left of the arch, whose boy has a fever and has been in a chair since the 30th of the month because a floor came up under him. **His boy is eleven.** **His door frame came down on the first day of the month, taken by Wick Venn, and he has not forgiven him and says so and says it does not make them even.**
- **He cannot read**, which is why the district's letter and the warden's return are the same catastrophe to him, and why the ask for a rate he cannot pay in this weather sits on the same Monday as a landlord's inspection.
- **He put Tarin in the room over the back stair on the Friday of the 25th without being asked and told nobody, and Tarin found out by the hot water.**
- **He keeps the man's tools fed, and nobody will ever mention it in this lane:** the tin pot filled at the butt, a new length of string on it, the chalk in a tin so it will not go to dust, the folding rule oiled and set on the wall.
- **He says the two things Chapter 39 needs him to say.** That he has had four men through that door this month and three of them wanted something, and that **he is telling him in the road that he was right, and he knows it because of the other three men and the door frame: a man who takes it is a man who has it, and then it is gone, and then he has not got it either, and by the time he finds that out there is nobody in the room to ask.**
- He is a symbol of nothing. He is a man who carries his son four feet every hour he can and has done it two hundred and fifty times.

## Hanne Roeg of Glassstep

- 71. Four steps, a cane across her knees, a mat over her shoulder.
- **She has asked a question in a city document that no office in this city has ever been asked: whether a district with no row may ask a settlement with no row for water. It is unanswered and nobody has treated it as a question yet.**
- **The 17th: she answered it herself, in her own hand, and said no.** They will not ask a settlement with no row for water while the well at Ashgate has never been tested by anybody and while a signed document says he cannot promise it will be the same water tomorrow. The reason is given in the same hand, because a refusal without a reason is a sulk. It was read out at the top of the mill road in front of twenty-eight people, and it is on a slate in her own lane, and it is a document going up a cattle track.
- **The 27th: she reversed it on the record, in the same hand, with the reason, because a person who says *I was wrong* and does not say why is a person who has been frightened.** *On the seventeenth day there were eighty gallons of clean water in a churn and the tank in the yard had five hundred and ninety gallons in it and was going down at a hundred and twenty a day, and there is no municipal supply in any form coming down this road. On the twenty-seventh day there is nothing clean in this lane.* She is being out of water, and she says so, and she is seventy-one and has known better than to be sentimental about this lane.
- **What she asks for is narrow and what she does not ask for is the decision:** the condition of the works, the condition of the ground beneath the district, and that the two may be heard together in one room by one examiner. **Not a schedule. Not a route. Not water. Not a keeper. And not the setting out of a single charge standing against the man who has carried her water up and down this lane since the fourth day of this month with a bar on his shoulder.**
- **The price, in her own hand, read out separately because a document nobody has heard read is half a document:** the examiner is coming on the twelfth of next month, the sheet is a file, the file is in four buildings, **and there is a boy of ten at a school in the fenner district who does not know any of this is going on.** *That is the fifth thing I have signed in this lane's life and every one of them has cost somebody something, and this is the first one I have chosen on a Saturday by myself with nobody asking me.*
- **And on the night of the 25th, on a step in the dark, she did not give him the night of the breach and did not refuse it, and told him that if he had asked she would have said yes.** There are a hundred and seventeen people in that lane who were not born and nine who were, and nine of them were in that doorway in the wet, and a boy in a chair at the low house is eleven and cannot walk and will be told about that night by her, and she is the only person alive who can tell him.
- **She is not a symbol and she was never made comfortable by anybody.** She was frightened of an examiner before the twelfth and she is more frightened of him on the twenty-seventh.
