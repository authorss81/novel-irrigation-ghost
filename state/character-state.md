# Character State

Updated after Batch 0002 (Volume 01, Chapters 11–20, Days 21–34). Compact and factual; use this for the next batch rather than re-reading the chapters.

## Tarin Rusk — protagonist

- **Age 33.** Senior flow engineer, Cresset Water Office. Rents nothing; sleeps in a dry corner of an empty cistern house inside the second gatehouse, under a watchman who is required to walk him to the gate at dawn and no further.
- **Power:** still **Stage 2 (Name)**, at the beginning. The Chapter 30 attempt is the same stage used wrong: unnamed, unwitnessed, and it opens nothing anybody can file. He is at no further stage and he is not closer. **Bend belongs to Chapters 51–150.**
- **Status:** Suspended since **Day 10** under **charge one — tampering with city records**. Charged again on **Day 20** under **charge two — unauthorized opening of a sealed structure and service of water from a reservoir the ledger records as closed.** Two open charges, two subjects, two dates, never joined, and he has now said so in a warden's note and in a yard. The hearing on charge one was set for the 22nd and **adjourned without a date entered**. Charge two's hearing is not even set, because the warden's entry has to be read first.
- **Lost:** desk, seal, district reference, keys, access to the lower terrace. Keeps the blue field book, the bar, the folding rule, the chalk stub, the tin pot, and **Roke's gauged oak stop-log with the pin**.
- **Name debt (minor, Chapter 7):** the two-finger press — the tap he has used since he was thirteen on spindles, floats and page edges. Also the taste of his own bitter tea. **Neither has healed.** Chapter 11's list records the blank face and *I cannot cut it*; Chapter 17 records a second failed chisel attempt and the tea, drunk on a step at seven in the morning, still tasteless, with the word *hoped* doing the damage.
- **What he has learned and cannot use:** naming requires a trace, a cut record and a willing witness; it does not require legitimacy, and without it the receiving side takes the crosscurrent. And the harder thing, from Chapter 14: **the missing second hand is not a neighbour, it is the man at the other end of the pipe.** A route with a name, a record and a keeper can be held. A route with a man in it cannot be shut.
- **The failure (Chapter 17, Day 30).** He emptied the cistern to make a vessel, opened the branch at the pin without naming it and without a witness, and the ground under the lowest room came up and the tank filled twice as fast as his sum. He was right about the vessel, the head and the balance point and none of it was any use. **He cannot say afterwards whether he lost it at the witness, at the record, or at his standing, and the batch must not tell him.** The three candidates are kept apart in the book and he refuses to write a guess.
- **He has refused twice on the page, and a third time in Chapter 17 when the refusal came back at him:** his own private memory of the lane as an anchor (Chapter 6), the boy of nine as a witness (Chapter 14), and then offering that same private memory again in the open channel on Day 30 and finding it does not work, because a private memory is not a public record and nobody offered.
- **He has refused to stop Niko twice** and has said in a yard of two hundred people that he will not try a third time. He is aware that this is the beginning of losing an argument he is right to lose.
- **Left shoulder:** torn or badly pulled on Day 1, not healed in twenty-one days, and gone again on Day 30 lifting a stop-log out of a loaded gate.
- **Open wants:** to find out where the water comes from (he has said it aloud in a yard twice now); to keep the district out of a classification that erases it; to read folio eleven on the 4th of the next month; to be on a hill with an instrument and a crew.
- **His stated fear, in his own words, Chapter 10:** that being useful enough is what lets him decide for people who never would have accepted it. **Chapter 16 adds the professional version:** that he watched a tank for an hour and ten minutes when the thing that could have gone wrong was under a floor thirty yards away, and that a man who can do that is a man who should not be handed a route.

## Mara Quill — the slow-burn relationship

- **Age 31.** Provenance record conservator, Municipal Memory Office, Vellum. Eleven years in service. Non-magical and stays non-magical for the series.
- **Power:** none, and never any. Her expertise is provenance — nib cut, gum, batch stamp on the tin lid, sizing, watermark, pressure shadow, pressure of the stroke.
- **Wants:** Glassstep's legal name, land claims, and basin share. Unchanged, and now much closer: she has put the district's own hand on a record of its own removal.
- **Holds:** the unlogged one-to-one drawing, still in her own drawer under a tray of brass weights, still the only copy nobody can check. She did not enter it and did not destroy it. Plus, since Day 28: **two one-to-one tracings of the Glassstep redaction folio, one in her hand and one in Hanne Roeg's**, both certified by her as made from one original in one room in one sitting by two named hands. The Glassstep copy is a second attestation and she says on the page that it is not proof.
- **Career cost paid in Chapter 16:** a self-proposed district travel warrant, recorded as self-proposed, the fourth note in her file in a quarter; a certificate whose hand characteristic is the only sentence she writes that is not falsifiable by anybody who can read, so filing it means the commissioner's registry will examine a man who is her mother's brother; a disclosure of a living household's name in a yard; and a deliberately weaker evidence base, because she chose not to transcribe two names she could have transcribed. She considered filing the weaker certificate before Friday and did not. **The certificate is filed on the 4th of the next month.** The registry's marginal note says no further action is proposed at present and is dated a Tuesday in the second month of next year, and nothing whatever happens on it.
- **Family:** wrote to her mother. Wend Quill is her mother's brother and the hand on both redactions, and the certificate she filed is the first thing anyone in the registry will look at with that in mind.
- **Vulnerability:** folio eleven is in the strong room in Vellum with her initials on the shelf-marker. She cannot get in. Rule nine says an unverified record may not decide a public route, and rule nine is correct, and it is also the reason the district cannot have its source.
- **Relationship position:** **the Chapter 16 agreement, on conditions, with distrust intact.** Five conditions: no public use of the other's method; **nothing on paper with one name on it**; no private memory taken without an answer that is not silence; no producing the other's evidence without telling them first; disagree in public with the disagreement recorded. He gets her method in a form a clerk could check. She gets field access she has no other way to obtain. Neither got what they wanted, and neither pretended otherwise. She told him in front of nine people that it is not friendship and that she will deny in writing any suggestion otherwise.
- **She is the only character who has heard the word *Gauge* spoken**, and her position on it is unchanged: she has never in her life been told anything by a piece of paper.

## Sluice Warden Pell

- Counting channels, not stories. Nineteen years in the post. First chapter of his own is Chapter 20.
- Holds the Day 1 notice with the empty cause box, the Day 20 entry with the right-hand column timed at 16:00, the Day 17 return with blank boxes and a filled remark, the stamped district application, and the returned application with four grounds and a warden's note.
- **He filed the first city document in which the words "the blue steps" appear, and it says there is no row, no keeper and no schedule entry.** He knows what that did and he wrote it anyway, having thought about it for eleven days.
- **He closed the district's last municipal tap on the 1st of the next month** on a condition entry, because a physician put brackish water in writing. The tap was the last municipal water the district had.
- He told Hanne Roeg, out loud and off the record, that the district's legal existence and one leaf in Vellum are the same object, and then wrote down everything else instead, on the grounds that a man who only writes down the comfortable parts is a keeper of opinions.
- He will write to the district book every week for as long as he holds the post, and some of it will be very bad for this lane.
- He has not been threatened and has not been offered anything. The only weapon he has is that nothing he writes has ever been found to be made up.

## Halden Roe — the volume's antagonist

- Acting water commissioner. **Untouched and unthreatened in this movement.** His file on Tarin is finished, formal, and correct enough to hold. He appears in Movement II only as the office that a refusal now comes from, and as the holder of two open charges.
- His falsification is arithmetic and his own work over six years. He never uses magic, never learns to, and does not know the One Map's name.
- **Nobody in Chapters 11–20 is asking who removed the pressure figure.** The certificate Mara files produces a registry marginal note that postpones that question by a year.

## Yara Sen

- 41. Water physician and public-health examiner. **Second chapter of her own in Chapter 18.** She has been in the blue steps since the spring and has never been able to file a case, because the row has no name.
- **Now she can, and she did.** Nine cases filed on Day 27, dated findings, in a district with no schedule entry — and she said out loud, to Bel Crale, before she did it, that filing will bring a district examiner inside thirty days and that the examiner's form has a box headed *water supply in connection*, and that this will end the bucket and the kettle. Bel Crale said do it, deliberately, with the water on the table.
- Holds a quarto account book with three columns — date, name and age, hands — with forty-one names in it, nine with the marks, four at all three points, eleven at two, and a fourth column of her own invention headed *at risk*. It is the only document in the district that will not be lost.
- Her requirement is **70 gallons a day of clean water**. The dispensary could only ever afford 70 a week off a cart at twopence. Hanne Roeg gave 200 gallons on the evening of Day 31 and the fortnight ends about the 4th of the next month.
- The district has started selling its own cistern to strangers and has £86 it cannot spend on a child, because everything it has to sell is the one thing nobody can use. She entered that in the book.
- Still not Tarin's ally and still not a reader's ally. She has no use for the word *reader* and treats none of it as evidence.
- **She has told Hanne Roeg that a district cannot be findable and unfindable at once**, and has said in writing that the tap is fit for nothing and that the fourth step is giving its water away regardless.

## Niko Venn

- 22. Apprentice, eleven months, **and the contract died on Day 10 with the district reference.** He is not an apprentice now and there is no clause in the world for what he is.
- Knows the terrace by body. **First chapter of his own is Chapter 19.**
- **He has the kit:** eight fathoms of hemp rope on credit off a chandler he knew from a pipe requisition, due the first of the next month; two hooded lamps and oil; and a hood for the second lamp made out of a tin lid. Wick helped him carry them and looked at him a long time first.
- **He has counted the thump.** Two a second on the sixth of the month, patient as a miller. Three a second by the twenty-ninth, without variation, and never stopping at any hour. A pump making up for a head it has lost is not a mystery, and he says so.
- **He will not be the man on the record for a rope**, and he has worked out that there is nobody in the district who will be: the nineteen who said no on the sixteenth said no for reasons that are still true, and every person in the lane is employed.
- Hanne Roeg refused him on the 1st of the next month with six reasons, and the sixth one — that a hole in a hill and a coil of rope in a district under examination will get the district taken off the schedule in October, not out of wickedness but because it will be busy — was the one that won, and he could not argue with it.
- He went anyway, on the night of the 2nd, with Wick holding the rope and Tarin on the bar. He found fresh spoil, four prints in and one set out, a lantern lit about two hours, an open tin of lamp oil, and a coil of good hemp cut by somebody else's knife. He put his hand flat on the cut end of it so that nobody would mistake it later.
- The thump stopped, and a man twenty feet down on a staging with a lamp of his own said *get the ladder, and tell Hallam to bring the other can, we are running her dry*, and then *you will be the Water Office, and you will be the wrong week.*
- **His flaw is on the page and is not excused:** he treats this as a test of his worth, he says so, and he went anyway after being refused. Nobody let him off it.
- He wants a formal place in the Water Office and knows he cannot get one while his master is charged.

## Glassstep residents — not a faction in any document

- **24 houses, 126 persons**, counted by the district on Day 22, with two people entered at the margin as declining enumeration. One of those two holds a ration card in the Fenner district and did not want to be written down.
- **Hanne Roeg, 71.** Four steps, a cane, a mat. Named the district in Chapter 6. **On Day 22 she signed the application as the named representative and put a household on paper** — her own, the fourth step — and she was the one who decided. She refused to be made a brave old woman, refused to let Tarin soften box five, had box two rewritten to add *answerable for the truth of it, and not having been put forward by any office in this city*, and read the hundred and four struck names aloud in a yard and stopped twice because households identified themselves. She is now a **custodian of a record in her own hand**. She knows exactly what it costs and she priced it on Saturday evening with a cup of Bel's water. She gave the dispensary 200 gallons and had it written down. **She refused the descent and she was right, and she said the second time that a promise Tarin cannot keep is the only kind anybody in this lane has ever got.**
- **Tam Roke, 44.** Keeps the low pump. White scar, 118. **The first witness (Chapter 6) and the first refusal (Chapter 10), and the refusal is not reversed and must not be.** He is the standing reason the Ch 17 attempt had no keeper in the lane at four in the morning. He is the man who is at this moment in a house at the top end with his own boy in it. He is also, still, the only person in the basin with a mark on a stone.
- **Marda Ollan, 31.** Keeps the keys to the two rooms at the top end, since her mother died. Took the count on a slate. Signed the witness list with four conditions. Signed the application and told Tarin to his face that she had made herself the easiest person in the row to find. Four conditions on her list: I count as keeping the route; I witness the houses, not the running; I am the one they come to; write the actual date.
- **Old Marden, 81.** Top end. Asked for his name to be cut in two and got it: a witness to where the water comes from, not to what it does. Kept a household's books in an unlisted lane for fifty years.
- **Wick, 38.** Took Ondra's door frame down on Day 1 and the row has held it against him since; refuses the witness list for that and for working the Reach six days; held a rope in the dark on the 2nd on the condition that he is not on anything.
- **The Tall boy.** Signed as the person who could not find the top of the lane on the seventh, which is the only thing he can answer for.
- **Bel Crale.** Runs the dispensary, opened in the spring with £41. She has kept the building deliberately illegible for a year, on the grounds that illegibility is a wall thirty years thick, and she stopped the water-selling for an hour and moved when a woman with a low barrel asked her to. Tarin gave her his word twice that the dispensary would not go on the witness list.
- **Ondra and his boy.** The lowest room's floor came up on Day 30. The bed frame is leaning against the outside wall and the boy is sleeping in a chair in the low house and his belongings are on the flags under a tarpaulin.
- **The boy of nine**, Roke's son, still unnamed, still working the cistern coping with a bit of chalk. He told Tarin at 03:10 that it was not his. He volunteered to sign the witness list on Day 25 and was refused, out loud, in front of his mother, on the page.

## Working relationships to carry

- **Tarin / Mara:** a conditional agreement with five terms, made on a dispensary step in front of nine people, with continuing distrust of each other's methods and no repair.
- **Tarin / Niko:** mentor and apprentice, and Niko has now been refused twice and gone anyway. Tarin cannot stop him and has said so in a yard.
- **Tarin / Yara:** professional, adversarial, and she has now told him twice that his method is right about arithmetic and wrong about people, and he has put her criticism in his own book.
- **Tarin / Pell:** a warden who has now written him down four times and who is the only reason the district has a document in a city file.
- **Tarin / Roe:** public conflict, unchanged, and no worse.
- **Mara / Kreft:** professional friction with a rule behind it, and four notes in her file in a quarter.
- **Mara / Hanne Roeg:** a conservator and the custodian of the record, and the only other person in the world who can read a second attestation.
- **Yara / Hanne Roeg:** a physician and a woman who gave away her household's water on the record, and who were both very clear with each other about what it cost.
