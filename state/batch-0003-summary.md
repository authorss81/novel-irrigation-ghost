## What the batch proves

1. **Ashgate is not an empty ruin, and it is underneath a street.** The chamber is intact, full, and eleven feet one inch higher than the outside ground at the door of the works, and its roof is a flagged floor with a hearth and a chimney breast in it. The district of occupied houses Tarin found on Day 1 has been standing on top of a pressurised void for thirty-one days of ration and for the whole of its life, and nobody who lives there has been told.
2. **It is not a conspiracy, and the text says so on the page.** There was a works, and then a closure, and then houses with nobody in them, and then people, and then a well. Nobody put thirty-four people on top of a chamber. Nobody decided anything. That is the last line of Chapter 25 and it is the most frightening sentence in the batch.
3. **The ghost is a crowd, and it is established once, plainly, and not explained.** Nine voices, then eleven, then not countable. Work, a boundary, a price, a stock being counted, a child asking one question. No addressee, no response to a name, to speech, or to the stone, before or after. Four women are giving directions about pumps at the same time and Tarin cannot tell which of the four is his mother, and he writes down four and not one.
4. **The cost is specific, physical, and paid by named people.** Wick Venn's left hand cannot grip. The plant is shut and the lane's only supply has stopped. The clinic's arithmetic is five days, and then eleven days, past the fortnight Yara Sen wrote herself. And the district spends its entire spendable money — £4 of £8, at a shilling the gallon, for eighty gallons, which is four days.
5. **The villain is placed in context and left standing.** Two gauge certificates in a deal box in a store thirty yards from a chalk board that says 5·9. Both in Halden Roe's hand. Both certifying a chamber void. Nobody in this batch threatens him, asks to see him, or gets near him, and the only thing that happens to him is that a piece of paper now exists in a district book in four buildings.

## New canon recorded in this batch

- **The chamber.** Level four of the works at Ashgate, at the end of a drainage adit driven off the settling tank. Tooled rock, a dressed ledge six feet wide on the north-east side with a kerb set nine inches high, water clear to the bottom, standing, with no drift, no weed, and no wetting of the ledge. A line cut in the rock at the level, one inch deep and a foot long.
- **The measurement, derived twice on the page:** water **9 ft 4 in** above the floor of the adit at its mouth; that floor is the floor inside the filter house door; outside ground at that door is **1 ft 9 in** above it; therefore **11 ft 1 in** above the outside ground at the door. It has not moved.
- **The roof of the chamber is a floor.** Flagstones on putty beds, a hearth and flue, a chimney breast, a cut lead stub of a floor drain, and a wooden stair of eleven steps left hanging with nothing at the top. The flagging changes direction where the light reaches and the level of the floor is not the same all the way along, so it is a street, not a house.
- **The works' own record stone** is in situ at the head of the ledge, not on a plinth, set into the rock of the wall at the height of a man sitting. The name is cut once and then cut again in front of the first set, shallower, half an inch out of register, the second cut the better of the two. It is the twin of the cracked stone on the pending-disposal slip in Vellum. **Its second face is blank and nothing is cut on it**, because Tarin's hand will not seat. This is not the Glassstep pump marker stone, which is unchanged.
- **The plant is ordinary.** A single-cylinder beam pump: walking beam on a frame, pump rod into a bore, and at the near end a horse gin — squared timber, a vertical spindle, a four-bar sweep about eleven feet across, and a pinion. It is stopped by a **fresh-sawn oak block under the beam's heel** and the horse has gone out of the basin on a cart. Nobody has broken it. Somebody was told, and the telling came from an office, and there is no letter.
- **The tally board.** Columns of date, cans filled, and *head at wheel*. The head reads **5·7** as far back as the lamp goes, and at a point about a third of the way up the board the 7 becomes a 9 and everything above it reads **5·9**. Nobody has ever written down why the figure changed. It is the contractors' own instrument in their own notation and it is not Office notation.
- **The certificates.** Two sheets in a deal box in a store at the works, one dated at decommissioning six years ago and one dated four years ago, both certifying the chamber void, no residual head, no standing water, no maintenance required, both in the acting commissioner's hand, both with the word *maintained* beneath in the same pen.
- **The settlement is Ashgate.** Eleven houses, thirty-four persons, thirty years, no row, no schedule entry, no keeper. **Nell Drust, 39,** has kept the row for sixteen years and what she keeps is the water.
- **The well comes up on its own.** Six-gallon pail on the well-head slab: **3 min 30 sec** — 6 ÷ 3.5 min = 1.71 gal/min = **103 gal/hr = 2,466 gal a day.** Checked twice. It has never been tested by anybody and there is no instrument in the basin that would test it. It is the whole of a row's water and it is out of a chamber eleven feet under the kitchen floor. Nell Drust's verdict: *it is a kitchen.*
- **The ladder arrived because the man sent for it.** On the second he said *get the ladder*, and on the fifth there was a good new timber ladder leaning on the staging. Niko writes on his hand that it is not a kindness. Wick says that out loud in the filter house.
- **The boy on the staging** is about fifteen, is paid weekly by somebody, has been on the staging every night since the second, has not been paid since the second, and is not evidence and is not a man. He brings Wick a box of clean linen and a jar of oil because a Mrs. Drust sends it down on Mondays. He is never named.
- **Coll Preece**, about fifty, four years on the pump, not frightened of anybody, paid by the can, has never met a signature. He said what he said on the second to see what a man with a lamp would do.
- **Wick's hand.** The left palm, grazed eight inches on a quarry face at the edge of a staging at about ten past one in the morning on the sixth while holding a ladder. Full thickness off the heel of the hand and the base of the thumb, the outer two fingers white and cold, no sensation in the outer two, no sensation in the other three except under pressure, cannot close the hand, cannot hold a rope or a bar. **He can tie.** A becket, a bowline and a half hitch, with his knee on the rope and the tail in his teeth. What he has lost is the Reach's winter work, which is half the year, and the journeyman's paper at the lodge at the fenner turn, which Osgar Pike offered him nine years ago and which has not been withdrawn.
- **Errol Slee**, the water cart from the fenner turn, stopped coming on the thirtieth because a man's speech in the road told him the price was going to three. He came back on the thirteenth at a shilling the gallon and took the money and would not look at anybody.
- **Sab Doulton, seven**, of the second step, is the fourth child in Yara Sen's book and is at three points for the third time in a fortnight, with the skin of her palms cracked, sick twice since Tuesday, and twice asking her mother where the front door is. She is at the beginning of the fourth stage. **The fourth stage is the one that kills and it is the first one this district has ever had.**
- **The three candidate explanations of the fault are closed.** The blue-book entry of the 30th, the three of them kept apart, is still the record. This batch produced no measurement that separates them and none is offered.

## The arithmetic, all of it derived on the page

- **Yara Sen's requirement, unchanged in derivation and stopped being true in fact:** four children at 4 gal = 16, five at 2 gal = 10, a hundred and seventeen at 3 pints a head = 44. **16 + 10 + 44 = 70 gal a day.** A fortnight of it is **980**, rounded on the page to a thousand. From the ninth of the month it is no longer a usable figure, because a child in the fourth stage does not take four gallons and there is no figure for that in the basin.
- **The cistern, evening of the 9th:** standing at the lip on the night of the 2nd, **4,700 gal**. Sold 3rd–9th at 340, 340, 300, 280, 240, 220, 190 = **1,910**. Lane drawing 7 × 70 = **490**. Evaporation 7 × 50 = **350**. Out **2,750**. Standing **1,950 gallons**, which is **three feet four inches** in a tank of 78.5 square feet with 4,700 gallons of room. Check: 1,950 ÷ 7.48 = 260.7 ft³ ÷ 78.5 = 3.32 ft.
- **The cistern, evening of the 11th:** 1,950 − (70 + 50) × 2 = **1,710 gallons**, which is **two feet eleven inches**. Sales stopped after the ninth.
- **The cistern, evening of the 14th:** 1,710 − 400 handed out − (70 + 50) × 3 = **950 gallons**, which is **one foot seven inches**.
- **The money.** £8 in a bucket. 4s to a reading-day fee on the fourth, leaving **£7 16s**. **£8 at 8d = 240 gallons**; at 9d = **213**; at a shilling = **160**. **15s 4d = 184 pence, at 8d = 23 gallons.** The district spent **£4 at a shilling for eighty gallons**, leaving **£3 16s**, and Bel Crale wrote *entered in this book* twice. Eighty gallons is **four days** at seventy a day. A cart holds forty gallons, so 240 gallons is six cart-loads and two days each way, and the money was never the binding constraint: the cart was.
- **The head is 5·89 m and has not changed**, and nothing in this batch makes more water appear. The chamber's level is a separate measurement in feet and inches and is not a head. The contractors' board says 5·9 in their own notation and 5·7 for two years before that.

## Corrections applied in the review pass

- Two name typos (`Tar in Rusk`, `Nko`) and a duplicated adjective in Chapter 22.
- Chapter 25 used *abstraction out of a reservoir*, which the brief forbids in this movement. Corrected to **abstraction out of a stored head**.
- Chapter 21 accidentally echoed the eleven-hour figure in an unrelated sentence. Corrected to *about four days*.
- Chapter 23 had the staging girl drawing from *the works' own well by a bucket and a windlass*, which collided with the row's rising well in Chapter 25. Corrected to a butt in the store filled twice a day off the well-head.
- Chapter 27's register entry carried **124 houses and 119 persons** where canon is 24 and 126. Rather than silently correct the number, the error was dramatised: the clerk reads back a figure Tarin has misstated, Mara Quill catches it, he recognises it as the number a man invents about a place that is not on a sheet, and the clerk strikes it out in ink and enters the correction in the same hand. It is now the best beat in the chapter and the count is on the page twice.
- Chapter 28's price ladder put a shilling the gallon at 190 gallons. It is **160**, and the whole passage was rewritten around the discovery that the cart, not the money, is the binding constraint.
- Chapter 29's ledger entry contained an uncorrected self-correction artefact and a mis-derived tank depth. Both fixed; the tank is 950 gallons and one foot seven inches.
- Chapter 30's statement six said *I have no method for finding a witness* and then contradicted it in the same sentence. Rewritten to: four people asked, four refusals, three of them right, and he will not argue with the fourth either.
- Chapter 24 ran six consecutive one-line paragraphs opening *He tried / He said*, which read as a list. Rewritten to vary the rhythm while keeping the escalation.
- Chapter 22's notch count and Chapter 24's time markers were reconciled to a single overnight: down at four on the sixth, Niko found at five, the ledge on the morning of the seventh, the stair on the evening of the seventh, the door out on the eighth.
