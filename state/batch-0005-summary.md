## What the batch proves

1. **The volume's central question is answered in public and the answer is a person, not a victory.** The Water Office's public record now says, in a district examiner's finding and in a Basin Assembly transcript, that there is standing water at Ashgate — **eleven feet one inch above the outside ground at the door of the works** — and does not say who diverted it, and does not say that a district of 126 people lives on the roof of it. The district is named in a finding *as a place and not as a supply district*, which is the whole of what the district asked for and none of what it wanted.
2. **The antagonist's lie falls in the same room as the water, by arithmetic and not by accusation.** Nobody asks who falsified the two certificates. **The certificates answer the question themselves**, because the Office's own reply of the fourth agreed to open the deal box on the condition that the officer responsible for the records would produce them, and Halden Roe produced his own hand in front of a minute clerk on the twelfth, and a chair laid the two sheets on a chair under a board with the arithmetic on it and asked whether two documents can both be true. They cannot. He is suspended, referred to the registrar, kept as a witness, and **not redeemed**: *enter that I have not known for six years, in those words and not in a better one.*
3. **The window is the same eleven hours and it is a different scene.** Same log, same pin, same setting, same division: **6,700 of room ÷ 610 an hour = 11 hours.** It is posted in advance, minuted in one book across three sittings, argued in a street with nine hundred people in it, and the whole city can check every figure because the clerk reads each entry into the street and posts it in four buildings the same night. **It stops itself at 03:00** and the minute clerk says the date aloud four times.
4. **A refusal delivered into the record is what makes the rest of it credible.** Coll Preece is put on the witness list by the district's own instrument without being asked, refuses before anybody has asked him anything, in his own hand, and says in the street, into the transcript, before the window opens: *I am not refusing the water.* His refusal is not noble. The plant has to be stopped, the plant is the only wage on that hill, and a boy of fifteen has been owed since the second.
5. **Being named in somebody else's document is played as worse than being refused, and nobody is compelled to forget or consents by silence.** The row at Ashgate is told a day before the paper is in an office. Nell Drust's anger is entirely accurate and she is not mollified: a slate in her own hand is not a paragraph in a district's paper, and she cannot take herself out of it. Tenna Sagg, fourteen, asks *is my name on it* and is told the answer is that it is not her name and it is going to be her. **The row's answer is neither consent nor refusal and says so in its own hand.**
6. **Yara Sen says what a number is for, in public, without an instrument, and refuses the column.** *It is for the person who has to hand the water out, and it is for the person who has to be told how much there is.* She refuses the *water supply in connection* column of the midsummer return, states the price out loud — **a blank column is read as a nil return** — and trades the figure for a sentence somebody chose to write down, which is then read into the transcript four times. She still cannot test the water. She says so three times in a room and once more in a yard.
7. **Mara Quill publishes against Tarin's office, they disagree in public with the disagreement recorded, and neither is the other's ally.** She names the district in Vellum **by referring to a schedule that covers it**, which is the exact move he refused in Marrow's report. He is right about it. She filed under the second reason. Neither withdraws anything, and condition five is satisfied on the page in a yard with a district book open.
8. **Iven Marrow keeps every word he said and gains nothing and loses nothing.** **Nobody broke his price. The form kept it**, because a Guild return has a field for a supply district and there was nothing to put in the field. His report is the best document about that chamber in the basin, it names no district, it is in four buildings, and a copy of the hearing transcript went down a mountain road to Bexhill on a cart because public documents go where they go. He is not in the room when Roe falls. He is not thanked, not blamed, and not on the list.
9. **The district gets its name by the route it refused to ask for.** Its *matters wished examined* box was left empty on the 27th of last month and is full on the fourth of this, with one line, and the price of that is Hanne Roeg's own argument against her own position, said out loud in a yard by Marda Ollan.
10. **The season ends and the drought does not.** Six thousand seven hundred gallons of the same crosscurrent water at the coping, a girl of seven at the fourth point on the twenty-seventh day, sixty-eight gallons a day unmet, no window promised, a notice that says so in four buildings, and *keeper, none held* on the last page of the return.
