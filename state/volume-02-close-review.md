NOT REPAIRED BY THIS PASS AND NOT TO BE REPAIRED.** **Chapters 56, 58 and 59** run on the other weekday chain, where the twenty-seventh is a Wednesday, the twenty-ninth a Friday and the thirtieth a Saturday — that is, they agree with `outline/volume-02.md` and not with Chapter 80. Chapters 56–60 are internally consistent among themselves and Chapters 61–100 are internally consistent among themselves, and **the two blocks do not agree.** It is a pre-existing condition inherited from Batch 0002, it is recorded in `state/continuity.md`, and re-cutting a weekday in a finished chapter is a bigger injury than a documented fault. **Note it, do not fix it, and do not let any new chapter inherit the wrong chain.**

**A day count is never written as an ordinal.** In Chapters 51–90 narrative carries *next month* forward from Chapter 80 and *this month* in a dated entry means the month the hand is in. **In Chapters 91–100 "this month" is the month that began on the first, a Thursday, and "the month before this one" is the month that ended on the thirty-first.** A writer who uses *the month before last* for the month immediately behind is out by thirty-one days, and that was the most common error in the volume.

**Tarin's day count runs from Day 86. His fifty-third day is the fourteenth of next month and his sixty-second is the twenty-third. His fourteen weeks of engagement end in the month after next. There is no "ten days" anywhere in the volume as a count of his days, and there is no "twenty-four years of plates" in Chapter 58 that is anywhere consistent with him being thirty-three, both of which are flagged below.**

**The window runs from eleven o'clock at night on the twenty-second to six minutes past three on the twenty-third, and the batch ends on the twenty-third and the image is at dusk on the day after. No month is named on the image. The image's fifteenth is the notice's date and not a day of the calendar.**

## 2. The state at Chapter 100, and every item is load-bearing

- **The flume sluice is SHUT AND CHAINED** on a chain a man bought with his own money, the old chain is in a shed, the inspection cover's padlock is on and the key is in his pocket, and the record of all of it is in a shed day-book in his own hand, nine inches by six, written in from both ends.
- **The Sallow Head gate is shut and chained and was not touched**, and the flume is not the gate and the flume does not feed the gate, and that is printed on a public board in a city.
- **The third rod is empty, the reason is a padlock, there is a new line on the card with a heading and nothing under it, and a printer of sixty-eight has written at the foot of the card that there is no figure for it and he will not print one.** The plate is not fitted, is not to be fitted, and cannot be until a keeper is named by an assembly of a district sixty-one miles east with no acting commissioner in it. **Do not fit the plate in Volume 03's opening.**
- **The fourth rod is a drain and is on the card. The fifth rod is the flume at the third pier, is promised, is worded in Nesta Sarg's own words, and is NOT cut.** They are not the same rod and the volume does not confuse them.
- **The crew of four is gone, the standing is spent, and the reason is on the page and it is not treachery.**
- **Perdie Ammid has no book.** Everything from the sixteenth is a numbered sheet with the word *copy* at the top. There is no *days lost* column and no *what it cost* column in that valley. **The column stood at twenty-seven and a half days in five lines in a cart; the figure that exists anywhere is thirty-four and a half days, in seven lines, on a sheet**, and there is nothing at the foot of it and a number is due there in about a year. **The seven are 4½, 5½, 4½, 1, 12, 5½ and a day and a half, and they add to thirty-four and a half. 27.5 + 5.5 + 1.5 = 34.5. The two one-day entries of the seventeenth are on a sheet and are NOT a line of that column and are NOT going to become one, because the column is in the cart.**
- **Item one stands as printed and was not amended.** The declaration of the twentieth is signed by eight of nine, is narrower than what was asked for, expressly does not resolve or concede anything about the six-inch waste pipe, consents to one window that does not survive the night, and appoints nobody to anything.
- **The volume's next question is on a numbered sheet going to a counting-house at Vellum:** the tank is not the Reach's, the book of 1843 does not exist in the valley, and nobody has asked for it in a hundred and thirty years. **It is not resolved and Volume 03 must not resolve it in its opening.**
- **A requisition to inspect the counting-house at Vellum is on the road**, entered and read back by Oriel Fenn, a minute clerk of nineteen years, on the ground of the master's own sentence, and the master is four days away and cannot be sent for and is not thanked.
- **Perdie Ammid's letter to Iven Marrow is three lines, unfinished, and not sent**, with four days of the fortnight left on the twenty-third. **It must not be resolved in Volume 03's opening and it must not make him a villain.**
- **A row number has been entered for a district that had none**, by Mara Quill, with her own name on the form, and a paragraph in four buildings says it is for a count and not for a supply. **A row number makes a place countable, and countable is not the same as served.**
- **A city on a cut of a fifth has a drop on a public board** and nine hundred and forty cisterns filling more slowly for about a day and a half, and nobody can say by how much.
- **Mara Quill has still not been told about the eleven names and must not be told in Volume 03's opening either.**
- **Aldis Kerr, seventy-one, has not been asked for the witness box and has not been thanked once in that lane in her life.**
- **Nesta Sarg's fifth rod is promised, worded, and not cut.** She will cut it herself in her own hand when there is a sheet in front of her instead of a shutter.
- **A butt behind a low wall at Nether Sallow has to be filled every evening by a man, and nineteen butts in a lane with forty-three people in them is not enough, and there is a day when somebody has to say his name.**

## 3. The thirteen things Volume 03 must not do

1. **It must not answer who shut the sluice, or whose hand is in the schedule, or who was struck out of the keeper column.** Aldis Kerr raised the first of these on the eighteenth and refused to answer it, and a man of sixty-one put the second of them on a sheet on the twentieth.
2. **It must not turn the name into a command.** A route is never shown behaving better because it has been given a name.
3. **It must not resolve the split name's method**, and it must not name it, and it must not say which came first, the stones or the form.
4. **It must not assert that the Sallow Dyke's water is the waste pipe's water.** The declaration of the twentieth expressly does not resolve it, on purpose, and that non-resolution is now a document.
5. **It must not make Iven Marrow a villain, and it must not have him rescued, thanked, or exposed, and it must not resolve Perdie Ammid's letter in the opening movement.** He is fifty-four, he gains nothing, he is four days away, and his price has not moved.
6. **It must not cure the drought, and it must not cure the crop.** The window prevents one field failing and does not prevent the Reach's season, and that was said out loud on a coping at midnight. Nine hundred acres of apple are not sown; eleven acres of beans are standing about nine inches deep in a trench two feet wide; the next sowing is not a thing anybody in this basin has a date for.
7. **It must not tell Mara Quill about the eleven names.** That is a Volume 03 thread and Volume 02 is not the place.
8. **It must not pay a name debt twice and it must not heal the Chapter 7 debt.** There is no tea in Volume 02 after Chapter 63 and the two-finger press is not used anywhere in the volume. **The Volume 2 debt is a lasting professional association and a route, and neither comes back.**
9. **It must not open a fifth thing.** Volume 02's one opening was the flume, for four hours and six minutes, on the twenty-second. **The Sallow Head gate stays shut and chained. The flume sluice stays chained, on a new chain. The weep at the fourth mile stays under its rind. The plate stays in three pieces, unfitted, and the third rod stays empty with a padlock printed under it. The third rod's new line stays empty.**
10. **It must not un-tie Tarin from the route and it must not let him leave.** He cannot leave. That is the last sentence of Volume 02 and Volume 03 opens with a man who cannot get out of a valley.
11. **It must not resolve the 1843 book, and it must not find anybody who was in the room in 1902.**
12. **It must not fit the plate, and it must not name a keeper, and it must not produce the figure the printer of Culm would not print.** The figure he would not swear to is the date the plate can be fitted, and that date does not exist.
13. **It must not merge the two unnamed men of sixty-eight, and it must not name either of them.** The printer of Culm opened a locked press on the strength of nothing and printed a card with a hole in it; the man at a toll bar sixty-one miles away keeps a book that is not a toll book. They are two different men of the same age in two different places and naming either was left to a later batch on purpose. **For the same reason it must not merge the unnamed fourth master of Chapter 75, a man of fifty-four whom Sef Ordry has worked for two years, with Iven Marrow, who is also fifty-four and has been the master for eleven years. They are not the same man.**

**The four of these thirteen that a Volume 03 opening will break within a chapter if nobody is watching: it must not answer who shut the sluice; it must not resolve the 1843 book; it must not let Tarin leave; and it must not tell Mara Quill about the eleven names.**

## 5. The figures, and there is one correct value for each

- **850 gallons an hour** is the launder's figure the Reach agreed in the second week of the month before and agreed to print, and it is a lath and a stop-watch in a slot two inches wide and an inch and a half deep. Its trials are **833** on the first of this month and **860** on the twenty-ninth of the month before. **844** is the launder's own figure from a section of six inches by about three-quarters of an inch, derived twice in Chapter 87. **They are not the same quantity and the twenty-five gallons between them are NOT resolved.** The window is **3,485**.
- **Four hours and six minutes is four point one hours. 850 × 4.1 = 3,485. 844 × 4.1 = 3,460.** **The window closed at six minutes past three in the morning and every closing time in the volume says six minutes past three.**
- **3,485 is about one part in fifteen hundred of the Reach's own season of 5,481,000; about three quarters of one household's day at 4,725; about a fifth of one household's whole one-fifth share of 18,900; about a fifth of one day of the Sallow Dyke's 17,640; and 3.71, three and three-quarter gallons, a property, across 940 properties.** All five are on the page and none is to be derived again. **NOT 861 AND NOT 3,540.**
- **The volume's arithmetic against the window, said out loud on a coping:** the Reach's assize at one fifth is 5,481,000; the window is 3,485; **the window prevents one field failure and does not prevent the Reach's season**; 850 an hour for ten days over 1,700 acres is a hundred and twenty gallons an acre, and a physician has said out loud that a hundred and twenty gallons an acre is not a crop; and the Sallow Dyke is putting 17,640 gallons a day out of the Reach's own lowest field into the Sere and therefore into Cresset, which is three hundred and ten days of the Reach's own season a year, for six years, and it cannot be used and cannot be stopped.
- **The plank: 193 and 191, off six gallons in 1 min 52 s and 1 min 53 s; figure 190.** **The throat: 860 on the twenty-ninth, 833 on the first, printed 850, and the card carries the reason it is not a figure anybody should lean on.** **The two subtractions, 850 − 190 = 660 and 860 − 193 = 667, are both on the card with the reason they differ.** **The Sallow Dyke: 735 gallons an hour, from trials of 750 and 720, and its times are in minutes.** **The 1,417 and 1,785 and 368 of the fifteenth** are on the page and are not re-derived. **The money: 850 × 24 = 20,640 a day; at a penny £86 a day and £31,390 a year; at threepence £258 a day and £94,170 a year.** **The two ratios: 4,725 against 4 gallons a household a day is about a thousand two hundred times; 4,725 against a good day's cart of sixty gallons is about seventy-nine times.**
- **The tank is a tank and not a channel: 6,000 gallons of room, a mark cut in the wall at 2 ft 6¼ in, standing at 2 ft 6⅛ in, and NO CHAPTER IN THIS VOLUME GIVES IT A RATE.** The prohibition is said out loud in a yard in Chapter 68. **The chamber is nineteen feet deep and the silt is nine feet two inches, and the card's *silt 2 ft 4* is a different place.**
- **The `7·31` metres is on the page in Chapters 55, 56, 62, 85 and 86 and is not spoken anywhere in Chapters 91–100. Chapter 91 refers to the slip of 1902 without naming it. Chapter 86 is the last place the volume states the number. It is never measured and no chapter in Volume 03 may measure it.**

## 6. The ending, which is fixed and is the volume

**The flume sluice shut and chained; the Sallow Head gate still shut, untouched, and no plate in it; the third rod still empty with a padlock under it and a new line under that with a heading and nothing under the heading; the card printed at Culm with a hole in it and a printer's own line at the foot; the Chapter 7 debt not healed; the crew of four gone; Tarin at Bend for one operation of four hours and six minutes and tied to the route; and the volume closing on a name in two books in two hands in two places, neither of them a record of a route, both of them his.**

**At dusk on the Friday the twenty-third, Day 147, at the top of the lane at Nether Sallow, a man is standing in a field of turned earth with a six-gallon pail and a watch and a roll of tarred twine, filling a butt at the plank.** The pail is Nesta Sarg's, with the mark inside it and the split in the handle repaired with a wire and a bit of pitch, and it is not lent. The butt is about two hundred and forty gallons of room and takes about an hour and sixteen minutes and forty trips up a bank. The watch is in his left hand and he does not use it. **The twine came off the stake at a culvert, out of a tin frame ninety years old, off a notice that said CLOSED. No water. No entry. Under penalty, which named no office and named no person, and it was the reason a village's water had been a secret for six years. It was cut off that stake, and turned over, and the tin cut out of the frame, on the Tuesday evening of the second of this month by six hands in daylight, and the frame went back on the post. It was not cut off. It has been on the pail's handle through a count, a window, a chain, a card and a declaration.**

**A man is going to do that every evening now. There is a day when somebody has to say his name. Tonight nobody had to.**

**And it is the twenty-third, and the fifteenth was a day like this one, and there is no rod on the card at the culvert for a butt in a field, and there is going to be a fifth rod on that card and it is not this.**

**He could not leave. That is the last sentence of Volume 02.**

## 7. The Volume 3 opening question, and it is physically available

**The water that came out of the Sallow Head tank went down the West Flume and the Flume is on the lower Sere, and there is a district on the far bank whose return has said *no inhabitants* for nineteen years and whose ground is four feet under at the mouth of the Flume. The freed water is flooding a place that official records say has nobody in it, and the reading of the Flume in a channel that everybody can see is not a document problem. It is a person problem, and Volume 3 is a Fair Copy problem.**

**This pass confirms all three of the things the opening needs, and that nothing in Chapters 51–100 has closed any of them:**

1. **The water down the West Flume ends in the long drain, and the long drain is the sole discharge of the Reach.** Item one of the schedule of the works of the Reach, printed in this village in 1902, says so in terms: *the Long Drain, from the wet common at Nether Sallow, with the sluice at the head of it, is the sole discharge of this Reach.* **The flume's outfall is on that drain two miles below the plank, and the flume's water never comes back to it, and the nineteen households are four miles two chains from the head of the drain.** All four are on the page, in Chapters 69, 91 and 99, and Chapter 99 says the whole of it in nine lines.
2. **The tank is a tank and not a channel, and Volume 02 has given no chapter a rate for it.** It is a vessel with a mark in a wall, it is being passed through, and Chapter 68 refuses the rate out loud in a yard of four hundred people.
3. **Nobody in Volume 02 has said anything about a district on the far bank, or about a return that says *no inhabitants*.** Grep across all fifty chapters returns nothing for *no inhabitants*, *far bank*, *far side* or *opposite bank*. **A reader meets it cold in Volume 03.**

**And the consequence, which is the one that matters: Tarin is a man who cannot leave a valley, and Volume 03 opens by sending the water he freed into a place that a document says is empty. That is the volume's first sentence's worth of pressure and it is on the page at the end of Chapter 100, and the ending and the opening do not cancel each other out — the ending is a man filling a butt in a lane with forty-three people in it, and the opening is a flood going somewhere with a return that says there is nobody there. Neither of them is cancelled by the other and a Volume 03 writer must not let a chapter cancel them.**

- **It did not rewrite anything for prose.** Where a fault was only a prose fault it was recorded and left. The four such faults are listed at the foot of the new section in `state/chapter-summaries.md`.

- **It did not touch the recorded weekday divergence in Chapters 56, 58 and 59.**
- **It did not touch the weep on the west shoulder at the fourth mile.** It is under a rind of lime, it is the clock of the whole Sallow Dyke question, Margit Saye said in a yard that she is not going and said it in a yard so that it could not later sound like a thing she decided not to do, and **it was not visited in this volume and is not to be.**
