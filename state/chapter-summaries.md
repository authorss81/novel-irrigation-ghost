## Volume 01 — The Empty Gauge

### Chapter 0001 — The Gauge at Ashgate (Tarin, Day 1, dawn, Ashgate works)
First measurable rain in fourteen months. Tarin climbs the sealed works three hours early to recalibrate a decommissioned float gauge before the monthly warden check. The float pins at the top stop; the stilling tube is live from below. Head above the gate seat: **5·89 m**. He forces the seized inspection gate with a bar and his shoulder and the residual head runs **uphill** down the gallery outlet channel, a channel laid at a foot in the hundred, dry and furred with six years of dust. He cannot shut it: the main wheel is loaded and a foot of head in the intake channel. He writes *by bar, at 06:11, before the warden's check* in his own hand. The kit's brass float comes out of the water with a grey gritty bloom that is not rust. He walks the covered terrace run downhill and finds it emerging at a low arch on the lower fold where the warden's sheet says *level four, no settlement beyond* — and hears a door being worked on behind it. He turns back up the hill to get the row sheet.

### Chapter 0002 — The Warden's Road (Tarin, Day 1, midday, warden's station)
Pell will not act on a story, only on a condition. Tarin is made to give the head, the gate state, and the direction, and the giving of it puts his own opening in a document. Pell cannot find a receiving district on any sheet he owns. The plate arrives as a work fact on a schedule: outfall **14**, *terrace overflow, row 7–12, lower fold — SEALED, PLATED, NO KEY ISSUED, OUT OF SERVICE*, closure entry 118, dated **twenty-six years**, the same entry that removed the row from the distribution sheet. No keeper, no key, no service window. Tarin takes the notice of uncontrolled channel condition, reads the empty **cause** box, and does not write in it — a decision, not a slip. He asks Pell for an hour and walks the line instead. Niko Venn, his apprentice, tells him the fold has people in it whom nobody has ever asked about.

### Chapter 0003 — A District of Blue Doors (Tarin, Day 1, late afternoon, Glassstep)
The water has nowhere to go. The row is occupied, washing, a woman beating a mat, a cistern filling to a third and stopping. Tarin names the cause in his working voice: the yard's outfall is a plate of iron three-eighths thick over four fist-sized bolts, so a courtyard cistern is the only vessel the district has. He gives Hanne Roeg his own name and his father's, and tells her what happened to the branch in his own hand, and asks for help. He is a stranger to the row he was born in. A Water Office runner catches him with requisition eighteen: the Memory Office has exhibited the Ashgate maintenance volume, which says the gate was shut before the Ninefold Flood and dry for a hundred and eighty-one years, and the commissioner has put Tarin's name against it.

### Chapter 0004 — The Weight of a Leaf (Mara, Day 2, morning, Municipal Memory Office, Vellum)
Routine re-binding of folio eleven. One leaf is a different weight, a different sizing, and has no watermark. Raked light shows a **pressure shadow** on the reverse: an intake head at the main wheel, gate state shut, *5·8_*, dated before the flood, and — in a second, heavier hand — **maintained**. The reading is real and the page has been removed and replaced. Mara refuses to certify, enters the discrepancy in the requisition book in her own hand, and keeps working after hours; the supervisor notes her hours. She reads the missing figure as **5·89** under raking light and a press-glass mirror. The requisition goes to the Water Office with the figure attached, and Cresset will pick the engineer who was nearest.

### Chapter 0005 — Two Professionals, One File (Tarin and Mara, Day 5, morning, provenance room, Vellum)
Batch midpoint. Three days of delay on purpose, and a day and a night of mountain road each way. She suspects he altered a record; he suspects she will seal a working route under cover of historical accuracy. He shows her his notebook — **5·89 m**, measured at 06:11 on the first — knowing it hangs him. She tells him the mechanism: not a forged number but a *true* one lifted off the original leaf so a clean leaf could say *chamber void*, with the second-hand word *maintained* under it. He confesses the notice with the empty cause box; she calls it a complaint with a route in it. In the lower room she shows him the cracked Ashgate record stone, carted down six years ago and left on a *pending disposal* slip. He puts his hand on it. **First Gauge statement:** *One route remembers. It asks for a witness.* Incomplete (does not say which route) and slightly wrong (the stone has two cut lines and he counts one; the request is given to the water rather than to the person). A second pressure sits underneath it, unnamed, with a mundane explanation available. He perceives and can do nothing. The hook: the only witnesses to Ashgate live in a district that does not legally exist.

### Chapter 0006 — The Low Pump on Glassstep (Tarin, Day 7, dawn, Glassstep low pump)
**First reach of Name.** He writes the requisition answer on the coach overnight and puts 5·89 in a second document, knowingly. He hears the low pump knocking under the flags at night — a pumping plant that is on no sheet he has ever signed. With Wick he digs out the blind branch and its stop-gate. **Niko Venn** knows which stones on the terrace are markers and which are only stones, brings the chisel, and beats Tarin at his own district; he refuses, out loud, to be Tarin's reason. Half a mile down the lane Tarin considers and **refuses** to use his own private memory of the row as an anchor, because a private memory is not a public record and nobody offered. **Tam Roke**, who keeps the low pump, is asked to witness and is told the full terms, including what Tarin does not know. Residents argue the name; Hanne Roeg supplies *Glassstep*. Tarin re-cuts the weathered face of the pump's marker stone with the borrowed chisel; Roke cuts a crooked mark. The water stops fighting and runs. Roke's mother drinks and says: *That is not our water.*

### Chapter 0007 — What the Water Brought With It (Tarin with Yara Sen, Day 7, daytime)
**Salt fever is physical, fast, and caused by badly addressed water.** Yara Sen finds early salt fever in three children and says to his face that the crosscurrent is his opening's consequence. She shows the three diagnostic points on the pad of the fingers. He confesses the cause before she proves it — the route was opened with no legitimacy condition, and the people it runs past were not in the decision. He closes the branch at 11:20 in front of the row, and tells Tam Roke it was his doing and not the water's, on Hanne Roeg's instruction. **First name debt, minor:** the two-finger press he has used since he was thirteen — the tap, on spindles, floats, and page edges — is gone, and his hand will not make the notation he has made ten thousand times. His bitter tea tastes of nothing.

### Chapter 0008 — The Replaced Leaf Has a Cousin (Mara, Day 9, after hours, restricted stack, Vellum)
Same day the low pump goes dry on the count Tarin gave Yara. Court-supply ink dates by nib cut, gum, and the **issue stamped on the tin lid**. The tin is the same batch for the Ashgate replacement leaf and for the Glassstep survey redactions — a batch issued **twenty-six years** back, from a tin the Office has never had cause to empty. Same hand, same ink, one hand in service across the whole period. The Glassstep redactions strike out one hundred and four household names; the Ashgate leaf removed true pressure figures and set a clean leaf in their place. Mara draws a one-to-one key of both in her own ink, and **does not enter it in the hand-drawing register** — choosing to be the only person in the basin who can read the evidence on condition that nobody can check her. The hand belongs to the Commissioner's registry and to her mother's brother, who was nineteen at her mother's table when she learned to read it. Vellum's district return shows the terrace cisterns at twelve days and the low pump not running.

### Chapter 0009 — The Commissioner's Kindness (Tarin, Day 10, morning, Water Office)
Halden Roe is gracious, exact, and better at this than Tarin is. He offers North Sallow pumping district — a real post, nine hundred and forty properties, a finished cistern programme — before he says anything else. He reads Tarin's own log back to him. The case: the certified discrepancy shows a figure identical to one a hundred and eighty-one years old, therefore Tarin's log must be a copy, therefore the copying is the tampering. The blank-cause notice of the first is in the file. Tarin refuses the transfer and **accuses Roe of falsification in front of Deryn Fenn and Osla Vint** — that the decommissioning certificates and the gauge certificate are Roe's own work over six years, and that a pumping plant in the old filter house has been running at night for the better part of a year, and that a dead reservoir has no lawful allocation. **Charge one, filed and read on Day 10: tampering with city records.** Stand-down, seal, keys, district reference and desk taken; he keeps the blue book, the bar, the rule, the chalk, the tin pot.

### Chapter 0010 — One Name, One Window (Tarin, Day 20, 15:40–03:00, Glassstep courtyard)
Batch climax. Ten days of suspension, a letter refused by the Memory Office, nineteen of twenty-three people asked to stand on a stone saying no, and Niko naming a descent into the old filter house that no survey records. Tam Roke leaves a gauged length of oak at the wall. Pell arrives at 15:40 with a clerk and leaves the right-hand column of his entry book blank, because a warden records the time a condition **begins**. Yara Sen states the charge aloud before he reaches the stone. **He cannot cut his own name on the blank face** — the lost two-finger press from Chapter 7 — so the naming has to be spoken. **Second Gauge statement:** *The stone is blank. Two hands are on it.* He works out the rule himself, aloud, where the yard can argue: a witness who cannot walk away is not a witness, and the second hand has to belong to somebody free to refuse. Roke refuses, out loud, and is right to. He lays out the terms: no second hand, the charge, salt fever, the plate, the empty cause box, the arithmetic (**6,700 gallons of vessel, 610 gallons an hour, eleven hours**), and that it stops by itself at the balance point. A man walks out. Then Tarin says the name. **16:00** — the water settles and runs. Pell's entry is timed and dated. **18:00** — the warden's men carry him out; Pell tells him there will be a **second charge, distinct and unjoined**. He tells the yard plainly: *I do not know where it comes from.* At **03:00** a nine-year-old walks up to the second gatehouse to say it stopped, and that it was not his. The run is dry, the cisterns are full, and the plate under the yard is ticking.

### Chapter 0011 — A Yard Full Of Water (Tarin, Day 21, dawn to late morning, the blue steps and the warden's station)
The stand-down order is nailed to the second gatehouse wall, two numbered charges on one sheet of paper, the hearing set for the 22nd and adjourned without a date. Down in the lane the row has put the water out — 38 pails along the low house wall, the cistern at its coping, washing on the line. Tarin walks the vessels with a stick and gets 6,700 gallons, the same figure as the 20th, and understands that the window bought a cistern with no headroom in it. **Yara Sen's finding:** the cistern is the worst, the fourth pail the best, evaporation is the only good news; it is not fit to drink and not fit to wash in, and the row has been living on two ounces a head a day since the 21st. Old Marden asks how long the cistern has left with nobody drinking it, and Tarin does it in front of the yard: six thousand seven hundred gallons at fifty a day, which is a hundred and thirty-four days, a hundred and thirty. Yara says the row is not short of water, it is short of water it is allowed to give anybody. A district-return runner comes down with a form with four boxes — row number, keeper, tanks, gallons — and cannot fill any of them, and takes it back blank, having written on it: *if there is no row, then no row can ever be wrong.* **Pell** comes down on foot and states the whole of his power: he can enter a condition, not a district, and offers a return with blank boxes and a filled remark, knowing it does nothing. Tarin writes his list — water, head, trace, record, keeper, standing — and sees that five of the six lines are ticked. *Witness* is deliberately absent from the page: he does not yet know what he is looking for. Hanne Roeg, from the fourth step: *This row is not a row.* And then: **"Then we will be a row."** Asked who signs: *"Then one of us signs it."*

### Chapter 0012 — A Named Representative (Tarin and the lane, Day 22, warden's station and the dispensary step)
Pell reads out the six numbered boxes of an application for entry of a district row: name, representative and household, houses and persons, water works maintained, source of supply, date of continuous maintenance. Four shillings, not refundable, paid by Bel Crale. **The count is taken by the district itself** (Marda Ollan on a slate, a day and a half, door by door) because a man under a stand-down may not take a census: **twenty-four houses, one hundred and twenty-six persons**, with two people entered at the margin as declining enumeration, one of whom holds a ration card in the Fenner district. Tarin objects twice and is refused twice. His first objection is the correct one: a name on that form is a name in the commissioner's registry by the end of the week. Hanne Roeg's answer: *Then in eleven months we will be a thing that can be refused, instead of a thing that cannot be.* She refuses to be made a brave old woman: four signers, she is the oldest and the only one with a legible hand. **Twenty-two sign, nineteen asked and said no**, and both numbers are read out twice into the card. Box two is rewritten at Hanne's insistence to add *answerable for the truth of it, and not having been put forward by any office in this city.* Box five states the source truthfully — a branch off the Ashgate intake at 5·89 m through a channel cut by Rusk, Tarin, named on a stone, at a gate the city ledger records as shut — which puts the engineer and his charge into a public document. She refuses to let him add *source not known to the district*. Pell warns that the application will get a file number, that the file number is a handle, and that the answer will come from the commissioner's office and not from him. Marda Ollan signs and says she has made herself the easiest person in the row to find.

### Chapter 0013 — The Shelf-Marker (Mara, Day 23, morning, lower room and reading supervisor's office)
Folio eleven is gone from the ninth slot. The **shelf-marker** says *ASHGATE INTAKE, MAINTENANCE SERIES, FOLIO 11. WITHDRAWN. R. QUE.* with the date **17/4** under it — six days ago, while she was in the low court at the Fair Copy. **Hollis Kreft** invokes **rule nine** of the reading rules: an unverified record may not decide a public route. He is right, and he is also the mechanism, and he has done the rule *publicly*: the monthly finding aid now reads, in the public reading room, *contains one substituted leaf, uncertified* — Mara's finding converted into an Office posture, in the Office's hand, without her name. She gets no thanks and no argument. Kreft then tells her to go and get a second instance independently, somewhere it is not this building. She writes out what one would have to be: the same substitution of a whole leaf, the same institutional correction wording from a different archive, the same ink from a dated tin. The only survey originals of the blue steps in the basin are in the blue steps — the field book went to the house the surveyor lodged in, and her mother told her the name of that house when she was eleven: *the Roegs have the book, and mind it.* She takes the **legitimate route** — a quarterly **collation** warrant, five boxes, eleven words of purpose — and Kreft signs it and records that she proposed it herself, which is the fourth note in her file this quarter. The district application comes down with the morning's tray and the second box stops her dead. She copies box two and box five into the **public** requisition book, with a footnote that the applicant's consent to the copy is not held.

### Chapter 0014 — Nineteen Said No (Tarin, Day 25, all day, up and down the lane)
Tarin builds a witness list properly: name, exactly what each person would be answerable for, date, and a ruled line for a mark. Every refusal is sound. **Wick** took the door frame down on the first and the row has held it against him since, and he works the Reach six days a week. **Bel Crale refuses and explains why the dispensary has been unfindable for a year** — *illegible* is a wall, thirty years thick, and it is why forty-one pounds has lasted. Tarin gives her his word, twice, that the dispensary will not be on the list. **Old Marden asks for his name to be cut in two**: a witness to where the water comes from, not to what it does. Tarin discovers that a partial witness is a category. **Marda Ollan signs** with four conditions, including *you write the actual date under my name*. **The Tall boy signs** as the one who got lost on the seventh. And the **boy of nine** asks to sign, and Tarin refuses, and says why on the page: a witness must be able to answer for ten years, and a route resting on a nine-year-old is a route resting on a family. The turn: three names and two refusals, with the condition stated five times on the way up the lane by people who have not met, and it is the same condition — *I will not be the second hand for a route I cannot stop.* That is Roke's reason from the twentieth, and it has walked round the lane by itself. Tarin understands that the missing second hand is not a neighbour but **the other end of the pipe**, which is up a sealed hill behind a chained wheel, and every man between pays a wage out of the office that charged him twice. Niko makes him say the money out loud: no authority, no money, no crew.

### Chapter 0015 — Six Thousand Seven Hundred (Yara Sen, Day 27, six in the morning to five in the afternoon, dispensary and yard)
Yara's own book: forty-one names, nine with the marks, four at all three points, five at two, and a column of her own invention headed *at risk*. The dispensary's whole clean supply is eleven gallons of rain in a stone jar. She argues the six thousand seven hundred gallons out in the open, in four pieces. **The crust:** a jar boiled down to a finger of white ring; boiling takes water out and leaves the salt, so it makes it worse per gallon. **The skin:** touch the wet stone inside the lid, then look at the pads of your fingers — a day early, the same three points, and there is no washing in it and no drinking it. **Cooking:** boiled, kept out of cups, the best the row has, and the arithmetic has changed. **The arithmetic:** four children in the third stage at four gallons a day, five in the second stage at two gallons, a hundred and seventeen people to keep alive on a floor of three pints a head — sixteen, ten, and forty-four, which is **seventy gallons a day, and nine hundred and eighty over a fortnight** — and there is no figure in this basin for it: a cart twice a week off the Sallow road at twopence, and Bel has only ever been able to afford seventy gallons a *week*, and that is the whole of the reason those four children are where they are. And the six thousand seven hundred is nearly seven times the fortnight they need and they cannot touch a drop. A woman at the back names it: *it is not a cistern, it is a cistern with a hose on it.* Yara agrees and explains that there is no route to move it and no row to move it on. **She files the case** — nine cases, dated findings, in a district with no schedule entry — and says out loud, to Bel Crale, that filing will bring a district examiner and that the examiner's form has a box saying *water supply in connection*, and Bel Crale says do it now, with the water on the table, and *do it deliberately and do not do it by accident.* A man from the Fenner district walks four miles with a barrow, is given a pail, and Yara tells him plainly in front of a yard full of people that it is not drinking water and not wash water and cannot be mixed because he has no water to mix it with. He asks what it is for. Nobody answers.

### Chapter 0016 — A Hundred And Four Names (Mara, Day 28, half past eight to six, Glassstep, with Tarin from five)
Mara arrives on a **collation warrant** with three rules written on the back of her hand, the third being *do not leave this lane with anything in your hand that only you can read.* Hanne Roeg tells her at the door that the field book is not hers — it is the lane's, and there is a difference. Forty people stand round a trestle table and look at two pieces of paper about themselves, and being allowed to look is the only thing Mara has that nobody has given her. She reads the fair copy against the field book aloud — 230 properties, 109 returned for closure, 31 for exclusion, 90 retained, and a folio promised to carry the list of a hundred and forty. Then the trouble: twenty-six years under a floor has foxed the folio and taken the ink *into* the fibres, so **a stroke-for-stroke comparison is impossible** and she will not pretend otherwise. She can give batch, margin, schedule reference and the fact that it is one hand — the pattern, not the hand. Marda Ollan points out that an uncheckable claim about a hundred and four houses is what Mara has spent four days refusing to be. Old Marden solves it: make it checkable with one. **Hanne Roeg offers the fourth step** — one name, read aloud, in a yard, by the fourth step herself, on a date. The folio carries a hundred and four strokes where the field book promises a hundred and forty, and that gap is now on the page as the first thing in twenty-six years anybody in this lane has been in a position to notice. Mara reads the hundred and four. At the eleventh an old man says *that's mine* — the second house, his mother's — and at the fourteenth Hanne says *mine* and Mara stops both times and writes down that she stopped. Then nine thousand separate little agreements of the retained properties, all in the open, twice corrected by people in the crowd. **The two tracings:** Mara makes one, then hands the second sheet and the pen to Hanne Roeg and does not dictate it, and the old woman writes a hundred and four house numbers in her own hand over four hours. Mara certifies both as made from one original in one room in one sitting by two named hands. *That is a second attestation. It is not proof.* Hanne Roeg is now a custodian of a record. The **Tarin–Mara agreement**, on the dispensary step in front of nine people: neither uses the other's method in public; **nothing goes on paper with one name on it** (she catches him spotting her own Chapter 8 decision in four seconds and neither pretends otherwise); neither takes a private memory without an answer that is not silence; neither produces the other's evidence without telling them first; they disagree in public and the disagreement is recorded. Neither gets what they want. She will not write down the sentence Rusk wants — *the district's legal existence and the restricted folio are the same object* — because a sentence in a conservator's hand is not a record. The coda: the certificate's hand-characteristic is the only sentence she writes that is not falsifiable by anybody who can read, and reading it means the commissioner's registry will examine a man who is her mother's brother and who was nineteen at her mother's table. She could file the weaker certificate before Friday. She files the real one on the eleventh of the next month, and writes to her mother.

### Chapter 0017 — What There Was Room For (Tarin, Day 30, four in the morning to half past two, the blue steps)
The arithmetic is on the back of a ward notice: 6,700 gallons of vessel, 610 gallons an hour at the pin found on the 19th, eleven hours. And under it, in his own hand, on the 30th: **there is no room, so make the hole.** He wakes Wick and gives him the whole of it at four in the morning, including the price: 4,700 gallons is going to come out of the bottom of the cistern into a lane with no drain, over a plate, and he cannot work out a way of not paying it. Wick comes anyway, with a bar, and will not be a keeper and will not hold the branch. The draw-off comes out at nine minutes past four and runs for two hours. The plate is still ticking, unchanged, and he writes *unchanged* twice. At ten past six he sets the log at the pin, puts both palms on the stone, and says **"Glassstep low pump branch,"** and **the water does not change** — no bed going quiet, no third of a sound dropping away, and the channel starts to cut. He offers the branch his own private memory of the lane, standing in it, and **nothing takes it**, because a private memory is not a public record and nobody offered: he is on the wrong side of the rule he worked out on the seventh. He tries the blank face with the chisel and cannot seat it, and writes *06:15. Set chisel, blank face, second attempt. Hand will not seat. Name not cut. Blank face not cut.* Then the compounding error: the tank fills in **3 h 10 min** against a computed 7 h 40. He has the number and does not use it — when the branch opened on the 20th the run's sound dropped by a third, so the branch was taking a third of a run he has never measured, which comes into the yard through an eighteen-inch lead pipe half silted, at a rate nobody has ever had a reason to find. The tank is filling twice as fast. He watches the vessel, because a man has to watch the vessel. **At twenty-five past nine the ticking stops, and at about twenty-five past nine the floor of the lowest room comes up.** Wick gets the boy out. Tarin runs up the lane; Niko is at the branch; three men and a bar take eleven minutes to lift the log, and Tarin's left shoulder goes again. The branch stopped itself at the right level in the right tank, at 10:10, and the lane does not drain. He writes the whole of it up and then writes the thing he is obliged to write: **he cannot say whether he lost it at the witness, or at the record, or at his standing**, keeps the three apart, estimates the unnamed channel's extra third and will not stake anything on it, and will not write down a guess in a book. Niko's closing line: *That is not a mystery, Tarin. That is a job. You watched the tank because the tank is the vessel, and nobody watched the thing under the floor, because you cannot post a man on a floor.*

### Chapter 0018 — What The Ground Did (Yara Sen, Day 31, half past six to eleven at night)
Yara brings a spade, as promised, and puts it in four places: the ground is wet at a hand's depth at all four, and at two at the throat. She does the work in the open, in order, and refuses to interpret for them — a jar set to noon so that chemistry is in charge for one morning, a silver spoon in a pint of water on a plate in the sun, and the spade. The finding: thirty years of a plate over an outfall has turned the lane's ground into a closed sponge with a water table standing at the surface of the street, and the well in the dispensary awning survived only because it goes down into something below the sponge. **The terrace road main runs brackish by eleven o'clock** — the same water at the other end of the same problem, a lead main six feet from where she is standing — and it goes off the moment the pressure drops, so keep it running. Nine children with the marks; one at three points for the second time in nine days with burning palms, in the third stage. The lowest room has a hand's depth in it that is not going to stop, and Ondra takes his frame apart and leans it on the outside wall and his boy sleeps in a chair, and Yara tells him the room is wet and then damp and then a room with a damp corner, and that she has not got a better offer than the top step and a tarpaulin. Then the money. On the 27th a man from the Fenner district was given a pail; on the 28th he was back with four men and two carts, and by the 31st **the blue steps is selling its own cistern** at a penny rising to threepence, to people who do not live there, **six hundred and eighty gallons and eight pounds** — ten shillings on the first eighty, seven pounds ten on the remaining six hundred. Bel Crale stopped it for an hour and moved when a woman with a low barrel asked her to. The clean-water cart stops coming on the 30th — not told to, not threatened, just a man with a name and a row and a wife — and the next two will not come into the lane at all. Yara's requirement is seventy gallons a day; the dispensary had fourteen pounds and sixpence on the 1st and has **fifteen shillings and fourpence** on the 31st, which at eightpence a gallon buys twenty-three gallons, a third of one day. The clean-water price goes 2d, then 9d on the 30th, then 8d on the 31st. She goes to the fourth step and asks for two hundred gallons, and says out loud what it will cost: a household's water going into a clinic is a thing a man with a form can write two lines about. Hanne Roeg's answer: **write it down, put the fourth step against it, and write beside it that the water is not fit to drink and we are giving it away anyway.** Yara also tells her plainly that she cannot be findable and unfindable at once, and Hanne Roeg says she priced it on Saturday. The day's last entry goes in Yara's book under the date: a sale is a fact, a fact with a price on it is a record, and the next physician is going to arrive in about four weeks and want to know why there is money in it.

### Chapter 0019 — The Cut Off The Quarry Side (Niko Venn, Day 33, and two in the morning on the 2nd of the next month)
First Niko chapter. He has been useless for thirteen days, and worse, the contract of his apprenticeship died with the district reference on the 10th. He has bought eight fathoms of rope on credit off a chandler he knows from a pipe requisition, two hooded lamps and oil, and a tin-lid hood for the second one, and Wick helped him carry them and looked at him a long time. **The thump has gone from two a second to three**, and up to that point it has never stopped, and a pump making up for a head it has lost is not a mystery: somebody is drawing the Ashgate head and Tarin drew it for nine hours on the 30th. Niko catches Tarin on the mill wall and lays out the descent: a cut in the rock face two miles past the mill, not in the district and not on any plan, forty feet down to the quarry wall at the back of the filter house, a rope-eye in an iron ring; the only other way in is the works' front door, which carries a warden's seal six years old. He makes Tarin say all three reasons out loud with a time on them — no authority, no money, no crew — and makes him say why he cannot be the man on the record for the rope: a second name under a suspended man's name in the same month is a continuation, not a rescue. And the finding: **there is no man in the district who will put a name on a board about a rope.** Hanne Roeg refuses at half past nine and gives six reasons, and the sixth wins: a district that has been visible for eleven days has a barrow, a bucket, eight pounds, a doctor's book and a discharged tap, and in three weeks a man with a form; add a hole in a hill and a coil of rope and a debt at a chandler's and the district gets taken off the schedule in October — not out of wickedness, because it will be busy. She is right, and the reason she is right is Tarin's own Day 30. He promises not to come up the lane on this business without asking, and she tells him the promise is one he cannot keep and will be held to anyway. They go up the mill road at two in the morning on the second with Wick, who comes because Tarin does not ask him, and whose condition is that he is standing in the dark holding a rope and not putting his name on anything. The cut is at the north end of the face and invisible from thirty feet below. The **spoil at the mouth is a week old**, there is standing water rilling down it, four size-nine prints going in and one set coming out, a lantern lit for about two hours, an open tin of oil, and a coil of good hemp **cut by somebody else's knife**. And then the thump stops, in the middle of Niko's hand on it, and a voice twenty feet down on a staging says *get the ladder, and tell Hallam to bring the other can, we are running her dry*, and the man on the staging looks up at three people on a ledge and says: **"Well. You will be the Water Office, and you will be the wrong week."**

### Chapter 0020 — A Named Route (Sluice Warden Pell, Day 34, morning, the blue steps)
First Pell chapter. A warden's office cannot refuse anything; it can only **return** an application, with reasons, in a form, on a date, signed — and a return is a thing done to a schedule, not to a person. He took a day to write four grounds. **One:** the route exists and the works exist, but the office holds no service record; its only entries are the Day 1 notice addressed to no district with an empty cause box and the Day 20 timed entry, and both are conditions, and a warden enters conditions and does not enter services. **Two:** the source is unverified, and the volume that documents it has been in restricted handling since the seventeenth under rule nine. **Three:** three names and two refusals on a sheet in an unstated hand, and a witness is entered against a route, and there is no route to enter him against. **Four:** the date is accepted, and he writes that it is accepted, because a return with no truth in it can be thrown out in an hour. He also reads out the **warden's note**, which is the worse half: the **Day 22** return with blank boxes, entered under the warden's discretion and carried into the district book on the 23rd, is the first document in the district book in which the words *the blue steps* appear, and it says the water is fit for no use, the plate is weeping, and there is no row, no keeper, no schedule entry. Hanne Roeg's reading of it: *the only name I have ever had in any city document is the name of a defect.* Pell then gives her the thing he will not put in writing and says so: the district's legal existence and one leaf in Vellum are the same object, and if the leaf can be read you have a source, and if you have a source the leaf can be read, and neither can be got first. What he *will* put in writing is a warden's note that records that grounds one and three are declined for want of an office record and an entry, that the volume is restricted under rule nine, that **a restricted volume may be consulted by a named applicant on a scheduled reading day with a second reader's countersignature, and the next one is the eleventh of the next month, a Friday**, that this office has no power to enter a district, amend a schedule, inspect a sealed works, certify a source, or answer a question about Ashgate, that the hearing on charge one was set for the 22nd and adjourned without a date and the midsummer review has been set for three other days since, and that on the 21st a named engineer under two charges said in a public yard that he did not know the source of the water, and the district drank it anyway, and the warden wrote it down. He refuses to say one word about the charges in a return on a district application. **Tarin names the two ways out in the yard**: the volume will tell him the head and not the place, and there is a descent off the quarry side that he cannot authorise and cannot pay and has stopped trying to stop. A hundred and twenty people decline to leave the yard when Hanne asks whether anybody wants to say something. She sends him to talk to the boy and then asks for one day in the lane where nothing happens. Pell goes up the track with a return in his coat and a reading day in his head, and says he will write down what he can see in the district book every week for as long as he holds the post, including what is very bad for this lane.

### Chapter 0021 — The Water Is Going Down (Tarin, Day 35, Friday the 4th, the blue steps and the fourth step)
The batch's first job is a fact nobody in the district can feel: **the run has stopped.** Two days of silence under the flags, and not one of a hundred and twenty-six people noticed, because the only two men in this city who had ever heard the pumping plant at three a second were a suspended engineer and a boy of twenty-two. **The chapter is dated the 4th and only the 4th** — the day the lane finds out and the day before the descent — and its ledger covers two days, not a week. Tarin marked the coping himself on Wednesday morning and the water is a hand's width below that mark by four o'clock, at six foot three in a tank of eight feet of room. Bel Crale's boy brings the sale ledger: 340 on the second, 340 on the third, 340 today, and the barrow man can get sixpence. **The arithmetic is done in public on a ward notice and it is Tarin's second time doing arithmetic in a yard and the first it has not gone against him:** standing at the lip on the night of the 2nd, 4,700 gal; sold in Bel Crale's book 340 and 340; **ninety gallons of fenner pails that Bel Crale is right not to have written down and Tarin counts at the arch, thirty pails of three gallons**; lane drawing 140; evaporation 100; out 1,010; **standing 3,690 gallons on the evening of the 4th, which is six foot three.** And the finding is not a sum: Wednesday out at 460, Thursday out at 550, **the whole of the difference being the ninety fenner gallons** on a rain morning in the fenner district, so take the ninety away and the two days are the same day — and Tarin says out loud that *two days is two days and I am not going to stand in a yard and call two days a week.* So **the water stopped coming off the hill on the night of the second, between sunset and midnight, and it was being put here.** *Yara Sen* puts 70 gal a day back in front of a yard with its derivation (16 + 10 + 44) and says **today is the fourteenth day of the fortnight and tomorrow it is a fortnight and a day past a number she wrote herself and was proud of**, that the count has not moved in a fortnight and that is not good news, and that the fourth child is at three points again, three days sooner than the last time. *Hanne Roeg refuses the descent again and is right again, and says two things.* She says he is not going down there to get water — *it is a thing somebody said* — and then she says she is not going to stop him, does not consent, was not asked, and does not want to know: not on the day, not the morning after, not in a week. And: *write that down, because in nine days one of you is going to want it in a different shape.* **The four shillings come out of the bucket here** — paid that morning for a reading on the **eleventh**, with a refund a quarter of a year out, which is a long way past the morning they need it. Marda Ollan counts the eight pounds out on the step and says the two things that are on the step, which are four shillings buys a reading and two hundred and forty gallons buys three days and ten hours, and which is not a third thing. At the cut at dusk on the fifth: the lantern is out, the oil tin is gone, the cut coil is gone, there is fresh spoil again, three sets in and none out — **and a good new timber ladder leaning on the staging, because a man on a staging said *get the ladder* on the second and had sent for one.**

### Chapter 0022 — Past The Staging (Niko Venn, the evening and night of the 5th)
The threshold beat, and it is the ladder. The switchback in three pitches, which he goes down without touching the wall and is aware of and will not mention for a year. Three nights of deciding what he is going to be, and the answer he arrived at is *an apprentice with a master under two charges*, which has the merit of being the least flattering version available. On the ledge: a ladder eight or nine feet long, scaffold board with the rounds left on one side, pale wood, clean rungs. **Niko: *they sent for it.*** A kindness would have been no ladder and a locked ring. A man who has been told three engineers are coming and has decided to make it easy for us is the thing that goes in the mouth of the hand that is already full of figures. Past the staging — four boards, a rail on two sides, a coil of new hemp, a **tin reflector on a hook with a hood over it against the draught**, a fold of oiled rag, and **a boy of about fifteen asleep on the boards with his boots in a basket.** Niko's whole speech, delivered before they go on: the boy is paid, the boy is asleep at midnight because he has been on it every night since the second, and he is not evidence and he is not a man on a staging. **The filter house:** four filter beds gone to lime and cheese, a settling tank, and a suction that goes *down* through a floor into a cast gland. **The plant is ordinary and enormous in its ordinariness** — a single-cylinder beam pump, walking beam on a frame, pump rod into a bore, and at the near end a horse gin: squared timber, a vertical spindle, a four-bar sweep about eleven feet across, and a pinion. **The sweep is still.** A block of fresh-sawn oak, sap still wet, under the beam's heel. No horse, mangers down, trough scraped, one set of hooves going out and a week old. Nobody broke it: somebody was told, and the telling came from an office, and there is no letter. **The tally board** nailed by the beam: date, cans, *head at wheel* — 5·7 as far back as the lamp goes, and a third of the way up where the 7 becomes a 9 and everything above it reads **5·9**. Niko copies the whole column onto the back of his hand, because he does not trust a number he cannot put down. Then he takes the second lamp — the tin-lid hood — and goes down the adit, having been told by a man on a wall that he has spent his life putting a dangerous place in front of himself and calling it a job. **The chapter's action is Wick's hands.** He is left at the staging holding a ladder, alone with the good rope and the bad one, and the ladder is not a rope, and a ladder slides, and the foot of it comes off a rail he was told he could not knock out, and Wick Venn puts the flat of his left hand on a quarry face and slides it eight inches before it stops. **The end of Chapter 22 is his hand, turned over, and the sentence: *I cannot feel any of it at all.***

### Chapter 0023 — A Man Holding A Rope (Wick Venn, night of the 5th into the 6th and the day)
First Wick chapter, and the chapter is his hands. He establishes in the first three lines that he is not going to be the man who makes a noise about it, and then he says *right*, and *that is me then*, and then nothing for six hours. Niko tests the fingers one at a time and Wick watches his own face, and stops helping and lets the boy do all five. **Tarin Rusk's entry on the flat of the staging boards, read out because Wick asked for it:** grazed eight inches on the quarry face at about ten past one, full thickness off the heel of the hand and the base of the thumb, outer two fingers white and cold, no sensation in the outer two, sensation in the other three only under pressure, cannot close the hand, cannot hold a rope or a bar. *Not treated by me. Nothing in this kit.* Nobody can get up the ladder: Tarin gets eight feet before the rail comes under his hand, Niko six, and the reason is in a sentence — he could not get four fingers round a square and could not get two either, and the two he had been using for forty years were not there. **The ring is out:** a quarter of an inch of movement in lime mortar gone to sand, a puff of it, and *it will come out with the stone round it the day somebody loads it properly.* **The chapter's discovery is what a hand is for:** he cannot grip, he can tie. A becket, a bowline and a half hitch, onto the ladder rail and out into a bight, with his knee on the rope and the tail in his teeth, an hour and a half and Tarin's thumb and Niko's two fingers. *I can do knots to the end of my life with this hand. What I cannot do is carry a bar, hold a pipe, take a cistern lid off a hinge, or go down a ladder. And that is the whole of what I have done for nineteen years.* **He blames himself, out loud, and rules Niko out of it:** I took a lamp off this staging because a boy went off down a hole and I am a man who holds things. Both of those are the ordinary way that ordinary men get into holes, and I have watched it four times and it has not once been stupidity. It is nobody else's. The boy on the staging brings him a box of clean linen and a jar of oil because a Mrs. Drust sends it down on Mondays, and Wick will not have his name taken. **End: *we were not let out of here by anybody. We were not rescued. We walked in.***

### Chapter 0024 — Beneath The Houses (Tarin, the 7th, the chamber at the end of the adit)
**The midpoint.** The adit is four feet high, six wide, pine sets squared to a pattern, four hundred and some yards, down, two turns. Niko is at the first bend with a camp, a swept floor, four notched pebbles and a fifth half-cut, a lamp rationed to half an hour four times a day, and the whole of the competence on display: *I made a camp. I swept it. I went down twice and I did not go over the edge and I came back up both times.* **The air changes on the second turn** — not cold, not warm, not moving. A pressure behind the ear, the way a thumb pressed into a soft place is a pressure and not a sound, and it has the quality of a wall on the other side of which a street is going on with its business. A dressed wall, damp, with a dry conduit groove a hand wide at hand height. **The chamber:** tooled rock, a six-foot dressed ledge on the north-east side with a nine-inch kerb, and water that is clear to the bottom, absolutely still, with no drift, no weed, and a line cut in the rock at the level, an inch deep and a foot long, by a man who expected to want it again. **The measurement, twice, with string and a chalk on a stick because a folded rule against a plumb line in a lamp is a folded rule against a plumb line in a lamp:** 9 ft 4 in above the adit floor; 1 ft 9 in from that floor to outside ground at the filter house door; **11 ft 1 in above the outside ground at the door of the works.** Then he looks up, and it takes him a long time, and the chapter says so. **The roof is not rock. It is the underside of a floor** — flagstones on putty, a hearth and flue, a chimney breast eleven feet over standing water, a cut lead stub of a floor drain, a wooden stair of eleven steps hanging with nothing at the top. And where the light reaches the flagging changes direction and the level of the floor is not the same all the way along: *this is a street, not a house.* The underside of a flagstone is warm. **The works' record stone** is in situ at the head of the ledge, the name cut twice out of register, the second cut the better. He puts his hand on it and the lamp goes *away* — not out. **The pressure becomes a room with a lot of people in it going about their work at the same time and not one of them for him**, no silence anywhere in it and no beat, and the noise stays on the floor. **> The Gauge: This is not beneath the works. It is beneath the houses.** He gives it an hour, because that is what he would do with a stuck valve. Counting: nine, then eleven, then not reproducible. Timing: no gap, and *a room with people in it breathes*. Speech, his own name, and his mother's name once, taken straight back out of the air because a name put into a thing that has not asked for it is a thing he refused to do to a man on a stone in Vellum. Both hands on the stone, asking for a number, a direction, a date, a word with a vowel in it. **Nothing changes. Not one grain.** The work is work: a man saying one number over and over; a woman calling a child's name twice and then not; two men arguing patiently about a boundary, *that is the low field* / *it has never been the low field*; somebody counting stock in tens past a hundred; a price; *bring the ladder*; the water up to the third step; a child asking one question with no pause and no answer and no louder. **And four women giving directions about pumps at once.** He cannot tell which of the four, and he has wanted to tell for twenty-six years, and he writes down **four and not one.** The entry is the chapter's spine: the measurement, the roof, *nine, then eleven, then not countable; no addressee; no response*, and *I have not got a single fact out of it that I could put in a report and I have tried.* He tries to cut the blank face and the stone takes a white bruise a quarter of an inch off and cuts nothing. **Niko counts the stair: two hundred and forty-one steps. And I am going to be the one who does, and I would like it written down that I said that first.**

### Chapter 0025 — The Well That Never Needed A Pump (Tarin, the 8th, the stair and the surface)
It takes a day to get out of the chamber and **the reason it takes a day is a saw.** Niko goes up and down 241 steps eleven times and cuts at an oak bar that will not come out of its sockets because oak dry in a door frame for thirty years has shrunk half an inch at each end and set like iron. Tarin goes up and comes down eleven times, and on the fifth sits on the fourteenth step and works out that if the boy puts his hand on that saw the way he put his hand on a coil of rope on the second, they will be in a hole for three days. Wick goes up once and down once, two hours up and four down, with his right hand and a wall, and says afterwards it was the easiest thing he had done in nine years, and nobody believes him and everybody lets it stand. **The bar comes out at ten past four.** The door opens into a filter-bed store with a cold hearth and a shelf of candle ends, and a girl of about fourteen in the lane with two pails and a windlass, waiting, because she has heard a saw. *You'll be the pit. You're the pit they said had water in it.* **Ashgate: eleven houses, thirty-four persons, thirty years, called Ashgate by the people in it, and on every sheet in the city Ashgate is a structure with a closure date and a warden's seal.** **Nell Drust, 39,** has kept the row for sixteen years and what she keeps is the water, and the water comes up out of the ground under her own kitchen floor and has always done. There is no pump and there has never been a pump. *I have four in this row who cannot be got to the far well and the far well is dry.* **The measurement, checked twice:** six gallons on the well-head slab in three minutes thirty seconds, so 1.71 gal a minute, **103 gallons an hour, 2,466 a day.** Her verdict, and the line the batch is built around: *there is more coming up out of that hole than thirty-four of us can use in a month, and you have been telling me that as though it were a discovery. It is not a discovery. It is a kitchen.* **Coll Preece, about fifty, four years on the pump, not frightened of anybody:** he said *you will be the Water Office, and you will be the wrong week* to see what a man with a lamp would do, and the man went down a hole, and the office shut the plant on the Wednesday with no letter, and the horse went out of the basin on the Thursday. *I have got a ledger of cans and I get paid by the can, and there is a name on the certificate, and I have never met him and I do not believe he knows this hill exists.* **The store, the deal box, two sheets:** a certificate of condition certifying the chamber void at decommissioning, and the same certificate four years later, both in the same pen, both with *maintained* beneath, and thirty yards away a chalk board that says 5·9. **The entry, four lines, because a third charge is forming in his coat:** the chamber is not void, a plant has been pumping out of it for four years against a certificate that says there is nothing in it, that is abstraction out of a stored head, a stored head that is not on a schedule is in no allocation, and *I cannot do one single thing about any of the four lines above, because the only man in this city who can act on it has signed both pieces of paper, and he is not standing in this lane.* Nell Drust makes him a cup of tea and it has no taste in it and it has had none since the seventh of the month, and he drinks all of it and says nothing to anybody then or afterwards. **The chapter's last two paragraphs are its whole argument:** nobody put thirty-four people on top of a chamber, there was never an order, there was a works and then a closure and then empty houses and then people and then a well and then thirty years, and *at no point in any of it had a single person decided to do any of it to anybody.*

### Chapter 0026 — The Word Discharge (Yara Sen, the 9th, the yard and the dispensary)
The cost chapter, and the physician gets in first. She stands in front of the yard at about eight and does it before anybody can ask her a question, *because a physician who answers a question has already lost the ground.* The cistern is **three feet four inches** down and a woman who has been selling out of it for thirty-one years is feeling for the bottom with her arm, because she has stopped trusting her arm. **The word she puts on the slate under the tank is *discharge*** — not a leak, which is a fault and gets mended, but water somebody is moving on purpose from one somewhere to another, and the people at the end of it are not the people who started it. *Every drop that has come into this lane since the first day of the month has come off a works pump two miles up the mill road… it is not ours.* The yard asks its four questions and none of them is the one she would have chosen: a landlord and whether he can read; whether to go on selling; *will they do to this lane what they did in the spring*; and Old Marden, at eighty-one, asking the correct one — **is this water stopped or diverted?** *It is not a matter of luck and I have watched four men this month go silent rather than say that out loud.* Marda Ollan writes it on the second slate without being asked. **The arithmetic, in public, on a slate, and it goes against him:** **1,950 gallons on the evening of the ninth is twenty-seven days at seventy a day — and it is not twenty-seven days of what is in it.** *I have not been waiting for a tank to fix it, because it cannot.* **Two hundred and forty gallons**, and the day is a Saturday, and nobody decides today. Then the dispensary, last, because Bel Crale made it last. Seven of nine holding, which is *stationary*, which is a finding and not a comfort. **Sab Doulton, seven, of the second step** — at three points on the 28th, on the 6th, and again this morning, three times in a fortnight, each for longer; the skin of her palms cracked; sick twice since Tuesday; *has asked her mother twice this week where the front door is.* **The beginning of the fourth stage, and the formula stops:** *a child in the fourth stage does not take four gallons. I do not know what it takes. There is no figure for it in this basin.* And then the sentence she has been not saying for two days, put in a yard in daylight so nobody can say afterwards that a physician kept it back: there are thirty-four people two miles up the hill whose water comes out of their own kitchen floor at about a hundred gallons an hour, and if you asked her this morning whether the nine children in this lane should drink it she would have to say yes, and *I have a right to that answer only if somebody who is not a physician asks me for it and not if I go and take it.* A man near the arch says he would ask. *I know you would, and I have said it where he can hear me, and that is what daylight is for.*

### Chapter 0027 — A Named Applicant (Mara Quill, Friday the 11th, the reading room, Vellum)
The reading day, and it succeeds, and it does not give him what he came for. The four shillings came out of a bucket in the blue steps. **The desk asks name, purpose, and second reader, in that order, to everybody.** Tarin's purpose is eleven words, and Mara Quill refuses to help him write it, because in eleven years somebody will want to know who put the words in a box. *To establish the recorded condition of the Ashgate intake main wheel.* **She cannot be the second reader** — she is the officer whose initials are on the shelf-marker — and Kreft, who is right, says she is correct and should not have said it, because rule nine applied to a contested volume and then staffed by the officer who contested it is a rule that has stopped working without anybody noticing. **Senna Rull, second reader, nineteen years, no connection to the case**, and her one rule before she touches anything: a paper of this age is not improved by anybody being gentle with it; it is a piece of rag somebody made wet and beat and dried, and it wants to be *held*, in both hands, off the table, and turned. The volume: one hundred and eighty-one years, four entries a year, **seven hundred and twenty-four leaves**, rebound twice and once badly. Every entry says four things — date, gate shut and dogged, head at wheel, chamber void — and the head moves within a hand's breadth across a century and three quarters. **Tarin's find in the gate column:** the same chain through the same spokes, the same padlock, the same hand, four times a year for a hundred and eighty-one years, with a rust mark of the same shape on four leaves, one in the first year of the log and one in the last. *Nobody has opened that gate since the flood, and I opened it on the first day of this month with a bar, and every single thing in this book is true about every single leaf in it except the last one.* **The last line:** head 5·89, chamber *see ref*, and beneath it in a second heavier hand **maintained**. Mara Quill read the shadow of that line on the second of the month and said she would swear to five and eight and was right to; **she has never been in a room with the other three words.** Four minutes with a lamp and a press-glass, because a shadow of a number is a shadow of the pressure of a number: **ref. 118.** **The check, made by two people in two buildings out of two public documents:** entry 118 of the closure schedule of the lower terrace road — terrace row, lower fold, rows 7 to 12, outfalls sealed and plated, no key issued, out of service, removed from the distribution schedule by consequential correction, dated twenty-six years ago, **the same entry carrying the removal of the row from the sheet.** *Then it is one act. Not two documents that resemble each other. One entry, in one schedule, in one year, in one office, and it plated a row's drain and it took a hundred and four names off a survey and it took the last line out of a maintenance book.* **And Tarin, in a public room, refusing the easy version:** *That is a date and a number and a hand and a piece of paper. It is not a source… I have got the condition of an office. That is what the volume had in it.* **The certificate, four sentences, read out, countersigned by Rull**, recording the existence, form and date of a passage and a reference and **not transcribing the reference**, comparable against a public document. And the sentence about the hand, which is the one sentence a conservator writes that nobody who can read can falsify, and which therefore puts a registrar onto a man of forty-one who is her mother's brother — filed anyway, because the registry's marginal note of a year out was against a provenance note about two documents and *this is not that.* **Then the register**, and the best beat in the chapter: Tarin misstates his own district as **one hundred and twenty-four houses and one hundred and nineteen persons**, and Mara Quill catches it — *that is not what you have just said to me* — and he understands in a public room that he has gone back to a number a man invents about a place that is not on a sheet, and asks for the correction in the same hand in the same book, and the clerk strikes it out with one stroke, writes *so entered at the applicant's correction, applicant present*, and turns the book round so he can read both entries, which no clerk in nineteen years has ever done for anybody.

### Chapter 0028 — Two Hundred And Forty Gallons (Hanne Roeg, Saturday the 12th, the yard and the fourth step)
First Hanne chapter, and she is not a monument: she is seventy-one, tired, vain about her tracing, frightened of an examiner, and she has a grandson of ten at a school in the fenner district who does not know any of this is going on. She calls the yard for eleven and does not have to call it — a hundred and thirty people are there before she asks — and she works out what that is: *it is not being counted. It is being spoken to at eleven o'clock on a Saturday by a person who cannot be spoken back to.* She makes them wait four minutes, because a thing asked for is a thing given and a thing waited for is a thing owed. **Three things, in the wrong order on purpose.** Water first, because a yard that argues about paper for two hours while a tank goes down has already decided what it is: *seven pounds sixteen, and the last two men who came up this road would not come into the lane at all. They would come to the cattle track and stand there. So the money in the bucket has no seller. That is the thing I want said first, because everybody has been doing that sum as though there were a man at the other end of it, and there is not, and it is not anybody's fault and it is not a market because I said no to a market on the thirty-first and I meant it.* **Marda's corrected ladder** — £8 at 8d is 240 gallons, at 9d is 213, at a shilling is 160 — and then the discovery that stops the yard cold: **a cart holds forty gallons**, so 240 gallons is six cart-loads and two days each way, and the money was never the binding constraint. **Bel Crale, with a piece of paper, against herself:** *I do not want to spend it… and I am going to spend it, because on the twenty-seventh a man is going to come down this track with a form and one of the boxes says water supply in connection, and if the answer to that box is a cistern and a jar and a barrow and eight pounds then this lane is a place where the water comes in and goes straight out again.* Yara Sen refuses the arithmetic of singling out one child: *if you spend the whole of the eight pounds on the Doulton child you have told every other family in this lane what a child is worth in gallons.* **The second thing is the letter, and Hanne has costed it.** To Pell, not to the commissioner's office, because a warden can only return a thing in a form on a date and because in nineteen years nobody has found one word of his made up. It names the chamber, the head, the row above, the eleven houses and thirty-four persons, the certificate — and asks for the examination to be brought forward **and** for a structural examination, in a paragraph that says in its own words that the warden's office has no power to order it, *because a document that asks for what cannot be given is a document that cannot be refused for asking too much.* **And the price, in a yard, in daylight, from her:** three documents in this city now carry the name of this lane, and there were none on the 22nd, and the third column of the examiner's form will be the only thing any of them has ever wanted. And then the sentence nobody expected: *if that box gets filled in this lane stops being a place he can be told he came from and becomes a thing in a report, and there is no road back off that, and I would rather you knew that about me in a yard than found it out.* **The third thing she leaves until four in the afternoon**, because it is the only one she cannot decide alone: asking thirty-four people for their water. *It will mean that the first thing a person in that row ever hears from this city is a knock on their door from a district with no row, asking for the thing they have never had to share… and if the office ever finds out, the row is finished, and eleven houses are finished, and it will not be our fault and it will not matter whose fault it was.* She puts it in the letter as a question in her own hand: *whether a district with no row may ask a settlement with no row for water.* **The third sheet, which is not the letter, and which costs her most:** the fourth step's record, in her own hand, that on the fifth three men went without her knowledge or consent, that she refused them on the first with six reasons and does not withdraw one, that she did not stop them, that it was his decision and not an accident of the morning, and that she keeps the second tracing and will make any man from Vellum sit down on her step for it. A woman says afterwards that it is a shame, and is told twice, patiently, that it is a record and not a thing to be sorry for. **£4 at a shilling for eighty gallons, entered in this book, entered in this book.**

### Chapter 0029 — What A Man Can Still Do (Wick Venn, the 13th and 14th, the cattle track and the dispensary)
The recovery chapter, and its thesis is in the first line of work: **a cart is two wheels, a shaft and no grip, and steering with a rein in a hand that cannot feel is not a matter of grip.** The road crowns at a mile and a half and a cart works and a dead hand does not know it, and he gets the last three hundred yards to the fenner turn by pushing it with his shoulder. ### Chapter 0030 — What A Warden Writes Down (Tarin and Pell, Tuesday the 15th, the yard)
Pell comes down the cattle track on a Tuesday because he writes to the district book on the Tuesday, and a hundred and forty people stand in the yard from eleven until three, which is the largest a yard in the blue steps has ever been and which nobody arranged. **He says three things before he reads anything, and the third is the price:** a warden's office cannot inspect a sealed works, cannot order a structural examination and cannot enter a district; he is going to read it out because a warden's entry nobody has heard read is a scrap of paper in a book; **and it is going up, and there is nowhere above him except the commissioner's office, which is the office that sealed the works six years ago and holds the file on the engineer whose name is in the letter** — and he wants the yard to hear that before it decides how it feels. He reads Hanne's letter out, all eleven minutes of it, without editorialising, and slows on the fourth paragraph, which is about a woman who had already refused a hole in a hill in writing and put the refusal in a city document on purpose. **Then the form — the same form as the 22nd, five printed boxes: row number, keeper, tanks, gallons, last serviced — and he fills in two of them and leaves three empty.** Filled: *row number*, entered as **none held**, and *tanks*. Empty: **keeper, gallons, last serviced.** The remark of about four hundred words is read aloud and is the chapter's spine: the condition, the head, the settlement, and then the sentence the whole chapter turns on — **there is no box headed *time* on the form at all, a warden enters the time a condition begins and this warden was not present and will not enter a time he did not witness, and this form has no box headed time, and it is the only box he has ever wanted in it**, so any reader may take the entry as a statement that nobody in this city knows what hour of what day a district of a hundred and twenty-six persons started to stand on top of a chamber of water. **The other two empties are one reason and not two:** a *gallons* box can hold only one of the cistern, the dispensary and the daily draw, so putting 950 in it would be true and a lie; *last serviced* is a date a keeper writes and this office has no keeper. And a line nobody had put there: **the Day 1 notice with the empty cause box is the reason the condition could be entered at all — and he puts his own six days on the record next to it**, reported to his office on the 9th, entered on the 15th, fourteen days from the notice. **Nobody notices the empty boxes for four minutes and then Tam Roke does, and a voice at the arch says *then put somebody in it*, and Roke's answer is the fastest thing in the chapter: *No. And it is not going to be me, and I would like it entered that I have said that in front of a hundred and forty people and not two.*** Then Tarin's **engineer's statement, six numbered paragraphs, on the low house step**, which is the first document he has signed since the 10th and it is thirty-six days: the head is 5·89, **read once off a pressure shadow on the 1st, and the plant's own board has carried 5·9 for two years, and he has had no way and no authority to put a gauge on that wheel since the 2nd**; the chamber is intact; the pump has been stopped since the second and was run for four years; eleven houses and thirty-four persons on the roof and a well at a hundred gallons an hour out of it that has never been tested and that he cannot promise will be the same water tomorrow; **I cannot make water come to this district**, and the only supply it has had since the first has been a contractor's discharge stopped by a person he has not met; four people asked to put a hand on a route, four refusals, three of them right, and he will not argue with the fourth either. He names what the office has been wrong about and refuses the one thing that is not his — **the hand lost in the small hours of Sunday morning on a mill road was lost on a rope on an engineer's descent, it was not this office and it was not him, and he will not pretend he would have stopped it** — and turns it into the chapter's own argument: *if there had been a name in that box a fortnight ago there would have been a person in this district a man with a form could have come and found, and I do not know what that would have been worth, and I am not going to stand in this yard and tell you it would have saved a hand.* And that he is going to write **keeper, none held** in the empty box, in his own fair hand, in a book that four people in this city have copies of, *so that a man who comes down that track in six weeks with a form is going to find those two words, in a fair hand, in a fair book, and he is going to know that a district of a hundred and twenty-six persons looked at a box and left it empty on purpose and not because nobody noticed.*

### Chapter 0031 — A Form With Six Printed Lines (Tarin and Pell, the 15th evening and the 16th, the yard)
The cistern arithmetic, counted forward and re-derived, and then a form. **950 on the evening of the 14th = one foot seven inches; out 120 a day (70 lane + 50 evaporation) = 830 on the 15th.** Niko Venn finishes the fifth of five notched pebbles and says out loud that he is not going up the hill and that a pebble is not a keeper and that there is no clause in this city for a boy with a stick and a chalk. **The Office's reply comes down the cattle track on the same horse that went up, in one day, because a form is quick and nobody had to think about it.** Six printed lines. *Water supply in connection* is filled: **a contractor's discharge, delivered by gravity, is not a supply of this office and is not a supply of this city and no part of it is charged to any district rating.** *Cause* is filled: **the contractor was instructed on the second to suspend, the plant is therefore not running, and no abstraction is taking place.** Pell says three things first, and the third is that the sheet **asks this district a question, in a printed line, and the answer is yes or no, and he has been on this road nineteen years and has never had to explain to a yard what a yes means.** It does not mention Tarin's six numbered paragraphs. Pell: *I am not calling that a conspiracy. I am calling it a form.* **Yara's answer is the movement's seed.** She puts the box on a slate so a hundred and forty people can point at it, reads the three answers aloud — suspended (none from the 17th, and a tank at that rate is at the bottom of its draw-off on the 22nd), continued (the same bad water, four at three points, one at the fourth stage), without comment — and **declines to tell the yard which one it is.** Then she names what happens when a stopped pump is started again, and she has two thirds of it and says so: the first water out of a main standing **thirteen days** is the worst of it, and it has been standing full and still since the night of the second; and **the salt in the tank is not being used up, it is being left, because seventy gallons a day goes to mouths and fifty gallons a day goes to the sun, and the sun takes the water and not the salt** — so thirteen days of this is a tank getting stronger day on day. **The plant has not run for thirteen days on the 16th and fourteen on the 17th; the days of no delivery are the 3rd to the 19th, seventeen of them.** The third part she does not have: a body drawn from gives up less, and there is no instrument in the basin that could say by how much. **And she is wrong, and a patient tells her so.** A woman at the top end says *Petra is not one of yours* — Petra Olask, nine, cracked hands since the spring before last, lime, in the column at two points on the 28th of the month before and on the 6th, the 9th and the 16th. ### Chapter 0032 — The Answer In Her Own Hand (Tarin and Nell Drust, the 17th, Ashgate, and the 18th in the lane)
Tarin goes up the mill road at half past four in the morning with a sheet Hanne Roeg put in his hand the day before and would not let him read on the step. **Nobody shouts.** He tells a bench of twenty-eight people in Nell Drust's yard what is under them, the measurement twice out loud while the girl with the good hand writes it on a slate, the roof that is a floor and a street, the stair in eleven steps to a door under a store that a girl of about fourteen has been sweeping out on a Monday for a year. Then the part he walked two and a half miles to say: **the pump will be started again, the well and the pump are on the same water, and the well may change and he cannot promise it will be the same water in a month** — a sentence he had put in a signed document two days earlier in front of a hundred and forty people. ### Chapter 0033 — Somebody Who Did Not Ask (Coll Preece and Yara Sen, Saturday the 19th and Sunday the 20th)
**The plant is started at twenty past three on Sunday morning by a man who asked nobody, and the first water into the blue steps is at about twenty to five.** Coll Preece hired a horse out of his own pocket, walked it up himself, and told one person — the girl at the well-head, at about eight on the Saturday evening, because there are thirty-four of them and a horse on a works the city sealed — walked the horse up the mill road on the Saturday evening behind the animal, and sent the boy home on the Friday at eight with **a day and a night** of warning and a week's wages in his coat. **The yard's first hour is joy:** the tank goes up a hand's width an hour, the queue forms, Old Marden is on his step in his shirt at seven in the morning. ### Chapter 0034 — Proximate And Contributory (Yara Sen, Monday the 21st, the yard)
A **return of supervision** on the contractors' own paper, read out in the yard by Pell, who says first that it is not his paper and that he did not fill it in and has not seen it before this morning. *Immediate cause: the pumping plant was resumed at approximately twenty past three of the morning of the twentieth without instruction, and by the contractor, and the condition reported is not attributable to any act of this office.* *Contributory cause: the district holds no drain and no outfall, its sole outfall having been sealed and plated twenty-six years ago; the discharge has been stored in a single open cistern for a period of **twenty days, that is to say for the whole of the ration period**; the cistern has not been emptied, vented, or sampled by any person. **The twenty and the seventeen are two different numbers and Yara says so in a yard: the office has put twenty days of storage and seventeen days of a stopped plant on one page, both are correct, they are not the same claim, and only one of them was ever measured.*** **It names the works, the plant and the hour, and it does not name a firm and it does not name a man and it does name a district.** **And its last line is the most useful sentence anybody in this city has written about that lane in a month:** no sample from that cistern has at any time been submitted to any assay, and none was made by this office or by any other person in this basin. **Yara's fight is in the only language it has, and she does not call the form a lie** — because the plant was resumed without instruction, and a man on a hill told one girl at eight o'clock on Saturday evening and told her in a yard this morning and told the rest of that row nothing at all, and that is true. Her ground is that a form has named a proximate cause of a change in a body of water that **no person in this basin has measured once in the month it changed in.** Then she re-derives the requirement, and the number moves: **four in the third stage at four gallons a head = 16, four in the second at two = 8, a hundred and eighteen at three pints = 354 pints = 44 and a quarter, and 16 + 8 + 44 = 68 a day**, and it was seventy for three weeks and the error was hers. **The stage and the allowance are different numbers and the chapter now says so: a child in the third stage is given four gallons, one in the second is given two, and one at the beginning of the fourth stage has no figure at all.** *A figure that goes down when you check it is a figure you check twice* — and correcting it has made the district look better, which is the most frightening thing she has found in a book in three years. Marda Ollan's ledger has stopped being true: the tank is at the coping and not falling by a measurable amount. **Marda also does the cistern's other half of the sum out loud in a yard and finishes it:** between the 3rd and the 14th that tank lost **3,750 gallons**; the yard's own 120 a day accounts for **1,440** of it; **the other 2,310 went out of the gate in other people's pails**, and Bel Crale's sale book holds the first week of that and nothing after it, because a tank at its coping in a district with no keeper has never had a keeper's book. **Yara's sworn claim is narrowed on the page to what she can account for, and the tank's two histories are now the chapter's argument instead of an error under a number.** Tarin gives the cistern's own dated figures in a yard (**950 on the 14th, 830, 710, 590, 470 on the 18th = nine and a half inches on the coping, and then the 20th came up to the coping in about nine hours, from about twenty to five, and has not been measured since**), and **under five hundred gallons an hour** through a hole eighteen inches across that half of it is silted, with no keeper, no setting and no way of stopping it. Yara files two pages in six numbered lines, a heading, a date, a signature, and one request: *that any person about to record a cause for a change in a body of water in this district be required to state in the same hand and the same line whether a measurement was made, by whom, on what day, and by what method. A cause without a measurement is an account of somebody's day.*

### Chapter 0038 — The Number A Man Invents (Mara Quill, Friday the 25th, the discipline room on the terrace road)
Mara Quill comes up a cattle track with a printed summons, and Averell examines her for nineteen minutes without once asking her about a man, and she lets him. She establishes the volume, the leaf, the second hand, *maintained*, the shadow, the three digits, the schedule entry, and the plating of seven rows' outfalls and the removal of that row in the same year in the same office. **She refuses to be made into an engineer:** *I am a woman who can read a shadow, and I have never once in my life made an engineering judgment about a head, a gate or a grade.* She refuses to say whether 5·89 was true and says the shadow is a shadow of the pressure of a number. She says the sentence about the hand and that it will make a registrar examine a man of forty-one who is her mother's brother, and **does not say the name in the room.** Then Averell asks the question. **Is his judgment sound.** She answers in three parts and asks for them to be minuted separately. **First, she cannot answer it and will not be made to.** **Second, his record-keeping, and she uses the whole of her time on it:** in eleven years no person from that office has ever sat at her bench and handed her a figure she did not have to check, and he put a corrected figure into a public register in the same hand in the same book with a note that it was so entered at the applicant's correction, applicant present, and he asked for that, and *a register is read by comparing its lines, and a line that has been struck with nothing beside it is a line nobody can check.* **Second, and in the same part, his own record: he filed his own certificate, refused to have her transcribe the reference, put three names on the desk list and not one on the document, and signed six numbered paragraphs on the fifteenth **in a yard in front of a hundred and forty people — the first document he has signed since the tenth, when he was stood down** — and four of the six are against his own interest. (The first draft gave *forty-five days* here and Chapter 30 gave *thirty-six days*; neither was derivable from the tenth and the fifteenth and both are gone, replaced by the fact they were reaching for.) **Third, the thing against him, and it costs her:** consultation 34 — **the eleventh day of this month, ten o'clock until twenty past one, against folio eleven of the Ashgate intake maintenance series**, which is how the register of Chapter 27 records it and which the first draft of this chapter misquoted as nine o'clock with the series dropped — where he said twenty-four houses and one hundred and nineteen persons, and the count is one hundred and twenty-six, and the 119 is not a clerical error — *it is the number a man says about a place that is not on a sheet,* and it is in four copies of a register and in a copy in this city, and **the correction is in the same hand. So is the error.** He answers it himself: *I reached for a number about a district of no row before I reached for a number I had counted myself, and I did not know I had done it until a woman read it back to me in a public room.* **Condition four is said out loud on the record, in the road, on the Thursday afternoon at four o'clock with a cart going past.** In the corridor she tells him three things for nothing: a Guild surveyor asked at the public desk in Vellum what his engineer's statement was, a document or a private one; the examiner will not come early because he asked; and the one thing in the basin that could get an examiner here faster is somebody asking in a way that costs that office something, **and there is not one of those things in this lane.** And she files it under the second reason, not the gift: *I told you first, and that is the whole of what I did.*

### Chapter 0039 — The Door He Did Not Knock On (Tarin, Hanne Roeg and Ondra, the night of the 25th into the 26th)
**Nobody is asked.** At half past three in the morning he goes up the lane with no rope and no bar and stops at the bottom of the four steps and does not knock, and Hanne Roeg is on the top one with a lamp. *"I have not come to ask you a question." / "Then you have come for nothing." / "Yes."* A minute and a half of nothing. Then: **there is a channel at the arch with a head on it and no keeper, no witness and no record, and there is one clean account of the night of the breach within two miles of that arch, and it is hers, and he has come up four steps at half past three in the morning to tell her he is not going to ask her for it.** She notices that a man who has come to say he will not take a thing is still a man who has come, and that it took him the same two miles. She answers anyway, because he has not asked: **yes, she remembers; she was forty-five and the oldest of the four signers and read a list of names aloud in a doorway in the wet while a man copied them and a man struck them; and if he had asked she would have said yes** — and she tells him that so that he does not spend the week telling himself he did the right thing, *because you did the careful thing, and the careful thing and the right thing are not the same, and I have watched men in this lane confuse them for thirty years, and the ones who confused them were always the ones who had a bar over their shoulder while they did it.* She has given it to nobody — not a conservator, not a warden, not a boy with pebbles, not her grandson. **A hundred and seventeen people in that lane were not born and nine were, and nine of them were in that doorway, and a boy in a chair at the low house is eleven and cannot walk and when he is thirty he will say *what happened that night, ask the old woman*, and she will be here.** Her reason for not giving it is not a sulk; her reason for not refusing it is that she is seventy-one and does not make promises on steps in the dark. And the last thing: **you did not knock, and that is on you, and I am not going to give you anything for it either, because in about nine years somebody who has not been born yet is going to ask me what you did that night and what I am going to say is *he stood at the bottom of my steps at four in the morning and he did not knock*, and that will be the whole of what there is.** He says the rule out loud — *a private memory is not a public record; a thing nobody agreed to is a thing nobody is answerable for* — and knows he has said it before, and goes down the steps. **Then he does his own work and it is not a route:** he stands in the branch with his forearm to the elbow while the bed cuts, and at 03:40 he ties a string across, marks ten feet, drops a chip of limestone and times it on his own pulse — **ten feet in seven seconds, one and three sevenths of a foot a second, a twelfth of a square foot, a little under an eighth of a cubic foot a second, about fifty-three gallons a minute, about three thousand two hundred gallons an hour**, against the 610 the gauged oak would put through the same channel. *The log is in my pack and it is not in the gate and I have not put it in. So I can tell you what is in the branch. I cannot tell you what is not in it.* Niko Venn comes down with an unlit lamp and the two of them stand in it for forty minutes, and at ten past six the tank stops rising, because the ground has come up to meet it. **Tam Roke shuts the low pump for two days and will not open it**, in front of forty people, and his reason is not the hand: *I cannot see the bottom of the channel that is filling my tank, and there is a boy of mine at the top end drinking out of it, and I am not going to run a pump off a bed I cannot see.* **Ondra feeds the tools** — the tin pot filled at the butt, a new length of string, the chalk in a tin, the folding rule oiled and set on the wall — nobody asked, and nobody will ever mention it — and says I did not hear you knock, and then says the two things: that he has had four men through that door this month and three of them wanted them, **and that the man who went up at half past three came down at ten past four in a lane where he has not slept since the thirtieth of the month before** — the night his own floor came up and the boy went into the chair, and not the sixth of the month before, which gave his sleeplessness no cause and collided with Wick's hand on the sixth of *this* month; and that **he is telling him in this road that he was right, and he knows it because of the other three men and the door frame: a man who takes it is a man who has it, and then it is gone, and then he has not got it either, and by the time he finds that out there is nobody in the room to ask.** The marker stone stays under the plinth, one face cut, one face blank. The only thing that happened to anybody on the night of the 25th is that a man went up four steps at half past three in the morning and did not knock.

### Chapter 0040 — The Yard On A Sunday (Marda Ollan, Hanne Roeg and Tarin, Saturday the 26th and Sunday the 27th)
No examiner on the cattle track, and not until the twelfth of next month, which is the earliest day the office has offered anybody this quarter. **The midsummer ration review is set for the fifteenth of next month**, and the sheet ends with the only empty thing on the page: *Matters wished examined. [ ]* **The sheet is dated the TWENTY-SIXTH, not the twenty-seventh** — it came up the cattle track on the Saturday afternoon and Marda Ollan read the last line out in the yard at about seven that evening, and the district's own request was signed and read out on the Sunday the 27th. **The two dates are two different documents and are not merged.** Marda: *a letter goes in a bag on a cart, and the box on the warden's form that would have made this district countable is empty on purpose and is going to read **keeper, none held** in a fair hand, and if we put something in this one then a form with a thing in it is a file, and a file is a schedule, and Bel Crale has just told this yard herself what a schedule does — so we do not fill the box.* **Bel Crale argues for the water and costs herself the sentence:** *I am fifty-nine and I have kept this room open on forty-one pounds and I have not been able to keep it open this month, and I have got three pounds and sixteen in a bucket that is the district's, and at a shilling the gallon that is seventy-six gallons, and a cart holds forty, and Errol Slee came up this track at four in the morning on the thirteenth and would not look at anybody and has not come back. So there are two things in that box and I can only put one of them in.* **Yara Sen refuses to write into the box at all**, because she is the only person in the lane with a title and filling it would fill the box she has spent a month saying nobody should fill. **Her inventory of what she has is exact and the stage history is now exact too: a girl of seven at the fourth point for seven days, not a fortnight** — four on the morning of the 20th, four and holding on the 27th, three on the 28th of the month before, three on the 6th, three again on the 9th. **Hanne Roeg answers her own question, in her own hand, and reverses herself on the record: *I was right on the seventeenth day. I am wrong on the twenty-seventh day,* and gives the reason — on the 17th there were eighty gallons of clean water in a churn and the tank had 590 and was going down at 120 a day; on the 27th there is nothing clean in the lane, the tank is at its coping and has not gone down by a measurable amount, a man has shut his own pump for two days, a girl of seven has been at the fourth point since the 20th, a boy of nine is drinking out of a tank we cannot test, and a settlement of thirty-four people has put its refusal on a slate with its own name on it.** What she asks for is narrow: the condition of the works at Ashgate, the condition of the ground beneath this district, and whether the two may be treated as one matter and heard together in one room by one examiner. **What she does not ask for is the chapter's real decision:** not a schedule, not a route, not water, not a keeper, **and not the setting out of a single charge standing against the man who has carried her water up and down the lane since the fourth day of this month with a bar on his shoulder.** And she pays for it: the examiner is coming on the 12th, the sheet is a file, the file is in four buildings, **and there is a boy of ten at a school in the fenner district who does not know any of this is going on, and that is the fifth thing she has signed in this lane's life and every one of them has cost somebody something and this is the first she chose on a Saturday by herself with nobody asking her.** Marda copies it out and writes the two charges beneath it, because a request that costs a man something ought to carry what it costs, and heads the sheet: *not written, not signed, and not asked for by the engineer named in the charges standing against him.* Tarin carries it to the cattle track and Niko Venn takes it off him and goes up the hill, because a boy who has said out loud that he is not going to chase the Water Office is not going to start now, and because somebody has to tell a girl of about fourteen with a pail in each hand that a document is coming with her name on it.

# Volume 01, Batch 0005 — Chapters 41–50 (Days 59–79)

### Chapter 0041 — The Name On Somebody Else's Paper (Tarin, Tenna Sagg and Nell Drust, Monday the 28th and Tuesday the 29th, at the top of the mill road)
Tarin and the girl of about fourteen go up at ten past five on the Monday, and he gives her the copy on the track because a document which names her ought to be in her own hand for two and a half miles before it gets to a row. **The row is told a day before the paper is in an office.** Twenty-six people on a bench in daylight. He reads the whole of the district's request including the part about a boy of ten at a school in the fenner district, and then says that the district was not careful, it was fast, and that in a day and a half you cannot write down a chamber without writing down the houses it is under. **Nobody shouts and everybody is angry and every word of it is accurate.** Nell Drust's speech is the chapter: a slate refusal in her own hand on the seventeenth is not the same thing as a paragraph in a district's paper, and she is not able to take herself out of it, and the only thing she has got to hold on to is that she is in a piece of paper instead of a piece of paper *about* her — and that the four lines the district wrote about what it is *not* asking for are the only part of that paper about anybody and nobody in four buildings is going to read them. **Tenna Sagg's question: *Is my name on it?* — and the answer is that it is not her name and it is going to be her.** The row's answer is a slate of six numbered lines and a signature: not consent, not refusal, the girl taken out of the paper, a woman of thirty-nine to read it in the room, the answer of the seventeenth repeated in a different hand, and *we will come to that room.* Tarin looks at the lid of the cistern at the bottom end and finds a salt ring four inches above the frame and says he will not open a thing in a row that drinks from under the row, and Nell Drust says come back on the twelfth with the man with the form, and he says he is not the man with the form.

### Chapter 0042 — What The Return Does Not Say (Pell and the district, Wednesday the 30th and Thursday the 31st)
The Guild's report arrives as a **return**, which is filed by the district that contains the works, and there is no district at Ashgate, so it goes into the warden's book and is entered *received, Guild return, works at Ashgate, no district*. The form kept it.** The plate explanation is in the return for the board, and the return says the board is a day-book and not an instrument, and says nothing at all about the certificates, and Tarin writes that down and does not resolve it.

### Chapter 0043 — Three Rooms And A Word (Marda Ollan, Niko Venn, Tam Roke, Bel Crale and Yara Sen, Friday the 1st to Sunday the 3rd of next month)
The month crosses and every date in the week after it changes meaning. **A district with no row cannot lose its ration and cannot be given one**: a review cuts a list, the district is not on the list, and the box on the form is called *district*. Niko Venn's speech is the engine of the whole batch and it is not a chase of the Water Office: **the six who said yes on the twenty-fifth of the month before said yes to a window, a date and a warden — not to a route — and nobody in this lane has ever been asked about a route, and the only thing in this basin that gets an examiner here faster is somebody who asks in a way that costs that office something.** He pays a penny at a chandler's and is owed on the first. Roke has the pump open again and says he will give a review the number, because the number is the only true thing he has got. **What the district decides, in the yard, in daylight, is four things and a refusal:** no row, the examination of the twelfth and the request of the twenty-seventh, no clean water, and a girl of seven at the fourth point — and it will not say there is a chamber, will not say there is a route, will not say anybody has done anything wrong, and will not say the name of a single man, *because a district that has spent a month refusing to put a name to anybody is not going to begin on the fourteenth of next month by putting a name in a return.* Yara Sen's number is for two people and it is not an argument: *it is a hand on a handle, and the handle is in this yard and the water is not.*

### Chapter 0044 — Yes, And The Wrong Man In The Chair (Pell, Hanne Roeg, Marda Ollan, Mara Quill and Tarin, Monday the 4th and Tuesday the 5th)
Amos Trevail, district examiner, asks for the store to be opened, the deal box produced, and the acting commissioner notified. **The Office agrees, and puts the man who signed the two sheets in the chair to produce them, and the district cannot object without the objection reading like a request to keep the box shut.** Hanne Roeg fills the *matters wished examined* box on the 4th with one line — that the two certificates be read aloud, produced and minuted as produced and as read — and Marda Ollan says out loud that she argued for an empty box on the Sunday and is part of the reason it is not empty. **Mara Quill publishes a provenance note in Vellum that names the district by referring to the lower terrace road closure schedule**, which is the exact move Tarin refused in Marrow's report, and Tarin enters the disagreement in the district book in Marda's hand under the fifth condition, in both halves, and says he was right about it and is not going to be able to un-know that he read about a girl of about fourteen on a slate. Neither softens. Niko reads the whole of it twice to Ondra, who cannot read.

### Chapter 0045 — A List With Names On It (Marda Ollan, Coll Preece, Oriel Fenn, Pell and Wick Venn, Wednesday the 6th to Friday the 8th)
Standing order 114 requires a hearing about a route to be held at the route and to name its speakers **in print, in advance**, and the second clause of it has never been worked in two hundred and nine years. **Marda Ollan asks six of seven people and writes a seventh name she does not ask**, because the plant is the reason the water is where it is — and Tarin's rule, which the district has spent a month learning from him, is broken by the district's own instrument. **Coll Preece refuses before anybody has asked him anything**, in his own hand, on a contractor's tally counterfoil, and says she is right to write him down; the plant has to be stopped to count the water, he has not been paid since the second, there is a boy of fifteen on a staging unpaid since the second, he sent that boy down once already with his own money, and he is not doing it a second time for the city — *and I am not refusing the water.* Oriel Fenn reads the third line back in a room and is asked by standing order whether the declination may be used against him, and Coll Preece asks that somebody read the words *he is not refusing the water*. He is not on the list and cannot read and asks for a sheet in three places. Wick Venn is engaged as a day-labourer at four shillings a day and says he is not going to be thanked for it. The warden's return: *row number* — none held; *tanks* — three tanks, thirty-eight pails, the yard cistern, the dispensary churn; *gallons* and *last serviced* empty; *keeper* — **keeper, none held**.

### Chapter 0046 — A Confession With A Price On It (Pell's yard, Bel Crale, Marda Ollan, Tarin and a clerk, Saturday the 9th to Monday the 11th)
Roe releases the two certifications **himself, in public, in advance of being asked**, in five numbered lines, with Marrow's plate-and-bore explanation in his own hand as a footnote — and the counter is ready before the circular is, because it is in a return, on a slate, in a warden's book, in a circular and in a transcript, and the yard says it three times. *No district is served from this works, and there is no district to be served from it.* The confession offer has **two slots**: admission by the engineer, and **acknowledgment by the district** — *we are not a document, and a district with no row cannot be the injured party in a ration review, so the only clean story for the fifteenth has got a man who falsified a figure and a place that took water without right.* **Tarin refuses in nine sentences and says the cost plainly: a ration of the same water is not a ration, and refusing puts a drop in nobody's bucket and he has not saved anybody anything, he has only not signed.** The district refuses the second slot itself, ninety to eleven, and the eleven are named and one of them is a woman with a barrow with two children in the second row. ### Chapter 0047 — The Deal Box (Trevail, Oriel Fenn, Halden Roe, Nell Drust, Yara Sen and Tarin, Tuesday the 12th)
The store is opened with a key cut on the Monday the Office wrote that it had taken measures, and the Tuesday, for eleven shillings. **The sitting runs from ten in the morning: the certificates read at the door, Roe's statement, Trevail's four questions, Drust's slate at half past two, Sen at half past three, the descent at about four, the well timed at twenty past five, and the finding signed at four minutes past six — after the last piece of evidence, which is the only order in which those three times can all be true.** **The Office's own trap becomes the mechanism: the only lawful way to open the box was to put the officer responsible for the records in the chair, and he duly produced them, in his own hand, and nobody has to accuse him of anything.** Roe states the basis before he is asked — the plate, the day-book, a decommissioning certificate of forty years before, and the practice that a chamber certified void has no allocation. **The letter goes at last, on the Friday cart of the nineteenth, with the fourth copy of the count card for a city sixty-one miles east where a boy of twenty-two has been keeping a count by himself for nineteen days and has not been written to.** Jory Vant carries both to the bar himself; the driver asks what the hole in the card is for and is told a padlock, and says that a card with a hole in it is the only honest thing he has carried this year, and Jory does not tell anybody at the bar.
**Margit Saye writes four sides and seals it herself and shows it to nobody. It says that nothing was taken out of the specification book, that a stranger stood beside it and asked for nothing, that the weep is four and a half inches with a rind on it, and that a fitter of twenty-four years standing in a company was asked by a man with no office to put her name to a return, and that she is writing that down while she can still remember it accurately, which is a fitter's reason and not a grievance's. She refuses to show it, refuses his apology, and says: *you have not done a thing yet. Ask me in a month, if I am still the only person who can read a hand.***
**What a fitter takes with her is written down in a list.** The specification book in its oilcloth case, which is not hers; the forms; a rule, a lamp, a bottle, a tin, string, and an eleven-inch canvas book of her own with every plate she has set in it; **and twenty-four years of the ability to read a hand.**
**On Saturday afternoon she puts three specimen slips on a table nobody asked for.** Three clerks at an index desk since about 1880, the same hand on all three, the company keeps no withdrawn specimen, and a man is never to be taken to a desk on a specimen. **She refuses to carry the specimens out of the counting-house, because the day a fitter carries a hand out of an office in her own bag is the day an office stops writing anything down at all.** What she does instead is write the hand out from memory, in her own hand, on her own paper, badly, and give it to the Reach: worth nothing as evidence and everything as a hand.
**On Sunday she says the thing she has failed to say twice: there is a line at the bottom of that form, it is a hundred and thirty years old, it is not in a box, he has read it four times and never mentioned it, and she has read it twice and has not told him what it says, and that is the worst thing she has done this month and she has broken a Guild seal on a Monday. *Ask him. Before anybody signs anything.*** And the boy of fifteen is paid the second time out of the wages column and Perdie Ammid writes *as before* beside the entry, because a thing written twice in a column is a rule and a thing written once is an accident.

### Chapter 0073 — Four Documents On A Table (the room over the shop at Brant, Monday the 22nd and Tuesday the 23rd)

**A table is carried into the room and the chest is opened on it in front of everybody.** Four field books; the Reach's own copy of the schedule of 1902 with the flyleaf; the card off the nail in the shed door, carried down four hundred yards in Sef Ordry's good coat because a card is not to be carried in a barrow; **and the Guild's Sallow Reach maintenance volume, which has been in that chest for eight years, came down in a cart with a return, and Perdie Ammid has never opened it, and she says why out loud: a Guild volume in a village stops being a Guild volume, and she has been using twenty-six years of rightness about a box as a reason for not reading the only one of the four anybody outside this valley has ever held.**
**The split is dated on the page in public, out of a volume about nine pages in, in two hands.** The 1903 return for the works: *the long drain from the wet common at Nether Sallow, 4 m. 2 ch., by two men and a chain, the sluice at its head found shut and sound, the channel below the second mile not walked* — and, in the other hand, *the Sallow Dyke, from the head of the low fields to the Brasse Fen, 2 m. 4 ch. Land drain. No supply. No plate. Not maintained. Cleared in the winter of — and no entry after that winter.* Two men, both dead, and the volume does not say which half either wrote.
**Tarin Rusk puts the 1902 slip off the pin and does the thing the whole month is for.** Four miles two chains and two miles four chains are not two versions of one figure; **they are the same two figures with the places swapped**, and a man who finds a length wrong writes a new length, and a man carrying a line down from the line above him copies the figures. **Three dates on the page in daylight: 1902, printed in this village; 1903, two men, half a page each; the sixth year, a clerk's index hand, on both copies at once.** And: the method is the same both times, and it is a name, an address and a record written down twice, by two people, in two places, in two centuries, in two hands, **for two good reasons** — and he will not say what the method is called, and says a man who explains a thing in a room like this is the man who gets thanked for it.
**Aldis Kerr, seventy-one, is asked a question about a word and is not asked for the box.** Waterweed grows where the water was and not where it is, so a man in June reads a bend off the weed and is wrong. The dyke carries. Her mother's people called it **the Sallow** and it went west and everything in the low field was built to throw water away from it, **and that is the fourth thing the word means and it is the oldest of the four and the only one nobody in the room has ever said out loud, and she has been waiting sixty years for somebody to ask her about a word instead of about a box and she has not been thanked once and is not going to be now.**
**And on Tuesday morning Perdie Ammid puts the bind on the table in four sentences.** The oath says the takers are named so that a man who is thirsty can come to a door; the form says it in print; a door is where a man is; a party is a person and not a village; this Reach is a reach and not a district; district 14 is a district in a city sixty-one miles east with no acting commissioner; and the man it would have to be is him, and he cannot be named. **He has a road with nobody on it, and he has told her once in a doorway that he cannot leave the sheet in his coat, and he has not left the village and has not sent it and has not torn it up, and she has run out of ways of saying it that he would enjoy.**

### Chapter 0074 — What It Is Agreeing To (the Kerrow lane and the room over the shop at Brant, Wednesday the 24th)

**The asking goes up the lane in four lines with a date on it, and no person is asked for.** The boy of fifteen carries it four miles in an hour and a half and does not stop; at Wyle a clerk of about sixty writes a whole side, dated the twenty-fourth, and signs it *Wyle*, and nothing else.
**Wyle agrees to be a party to a paper and not to the truth of it.** The paper is quoted from the card at the culvert and *a card can be got wrong, and if the card is wrong then Wyle is wrong with it and no more wrong than any other village that signs.* It is not asked for water, money, the use of a drain, a tank, a throat or a field, **or a man.** It will not sign the paper itself, because it said on the twelfth that it would sign on the second of the month after this one, **and a village that signs a paper on a Tuesday because somebody came on a Wednesday is a village that a man in an office can move, and a village that cannot be moved is worth a great deal more to a village than the paper is.**
**And it puts the sentence in the book that Tarin Rusk does not hear, because he is listening for something else: *a party is a party and not a person. A village can be asked what it agreed to and can answer and can refuse and can be cross-examined and can be sued and can be shut, all without one single person inside it being named. A person is a different thing. There is no form in this basin that lets anybody have the one without the other, and anybody who tells you there is, in a hurry, on a Thursday, with a widow of seventy-one who will not be asked, is telling you about a form and not about a village.***
Perdie Ammid puts it in the second book with the whole of it, and says the clerk of about sixty wrote it on the Wednesday morning, which means he has had it since the twelfth, **and that it is the only thing anybody has sent anybody in three weeks and it is four miles away and nobody in this room did it.**
**And then she says what a paper road costs, before a shilling is spent, and she is right, and it is the four books.** A name in a line on a form is a debt for the rest of a life with a rule attached instead of a box. Three of the four men on the roster can write their own names; the fourth cannot and has said so in a wages column. **I built that chest out of four books that nobody was to turn into a record for twenty-six years and I said that sentence out loud in this room on the twenty-seventh day of the month before and it is the best sentence I have ever said, and you have built a vehicle out of it, and the day that return is subscribed the four books are the Reach's record and the record goes to Vellum — not a copy, the record — and I am not going to refuse it, and a woman of fifty-four who has spent twenty-six years on a sentence does not get to have it both ways in a fortnight.** About four people put their hands over their mouths and none of them was laughing.

### Chapter 0075 — The Reason, Said Twice (the flume, the field and the shed at the foot of the embankment, Thursday the 25th and Friday the 26th)

**The ground goes like a bench on the twenty-fifth and about a dozen people stop setting apple, and it is not a decision anybody had to make.** The window is the first ten days from the fifteenth and after the tenth the seed rots in it. Nobody wrote anything down about it. Tarin Rusk and the boy time the stone throat twice out of habit and write nothing down, and on Friday he refuses Perdie Ammid the figures on the ground that he will not have a fitter's figure off a Thursday in a drought with a boy holding the watch.
**Margit Saye goes home on the Friday at ten, on a cart, with the specification book in its case and nothing else.** She says goodbye in ninety words, all of it to Bess Ilk, and the last part of it is the best thing anybody says about ownership in this book: *twenty-four years of plates and every one of them somebody else's number, and a fitter's whole value is that she is right about a hole, and nobody has ever once sent for me about a hole. They send for me about a hand. And there is no such thing as a fitter who is sent for.* She refuses his apology in four words: **you have not done a thing yet. Ask me in a month whether you would still be sorry.**
**Sef Ordry sends him out of the shed for three hours, and at about one o'clock tells Jory Vant to copy the four lines of the return in his own hand, properly, and tells him the fifth is not his to copy and not the boy's, because a line that names people is not a line a boy of nineteen should be the hand on.** Jory does it in nine minutes, and signs the copy: *the four lines above are copied in the hand of J. Vant, apprentice, and the fifth line is not copied by me and I was not asked to copy it.* **Margit Saye would have said the rest of it. She was on the cart.**
**At about four o'clock Sef Ordry says the crew's one reason a second time, to one man, in a yard, and it is not the same sentence.** The one for the yard was *he counted a water in a lane at the top of his own hill when everybody in his city was telling him there was none.* **The one for a man with a sheet of paper in his coat is: a man who is going to do a thing on a Saturday tells the men who have to be there on the Saturday. Not asks. Tells. And he tells them before he does it, while they can still say no, and a man who has told a man and been told no has to do a different thing, and a man who has done a thing and then told a man has done it alone and dressed it afterwards.** He says the second one is a rule and the first one is the reason and the reason is worth more. Tarin asks him to say it on Saturday. **No.** The second time it is a rule, and a rule is a thing a man says when the reason has gone out of the room, and the reason has not gone out of the room, and the reason is a habit, and a habit cannot be handed back in a different voice. **And I have been standing in a yard waiting for you to ask me one question since about eleven o'clock and you have not asked me one question all day, and that is what I have been doing with my Friday, and I have wasted four hours and it is not your fault and it is not mine.** Then he goes and scrapes a gate bed: the stop on the Cold Shoulder line, shut, plated, with no water within nine feet of it. At about eight the boy of fifteen says the only thing he says all day, and it is that he has noticed the sheet in the coat and is not to be asked and is telling him anyway, *because a thing I have noticed and a thing I have not noticed are two different things and I would rather you had the first one.*

### Chapter 0076 — The Fifth Line (the room over the shop at Brant, the flume field and the barrow, Saturday the 27th)

**The count is read on the last Saturday in the month and the twenty-seventh is the last Saturday, and the four of them went to the shop at eight in the morning instead of to the three rods.** Nobody said the word *count* in that room for about three and a half hours.
**At a quarter past eleven the fifth line is filled.** Three names, read out in the order they were written, in the ordinary voice, because his hand is not what it was and there is a boy in the room who has to know. *Sef Ordry, sluicewright, of Bexhill, of no district. Jory Vant, apprentice, of Bexhill. Wick Venn, of this Reach, of no district.* **Jory Vant: *that is three.*** And the fourth is a boy of fifteen who is not named and is not to be asked, so the line cannot be completed, so a return with an unfilled line in it is not a return, **and Tarin Rusk says out loud that he was going to tell the four of them at eight o'clock and it is a quarter past eleven and he knew at eight that he was not going to come at eight.**
**Sef Ordry asks four questions, in order, and says in advance that he has decided not to get angry in a room with fourteen people in it, because if he does then in eleven years there is a better story than the return.** The fourth is *why did you not ask us.* And the answer is the right answer and is not a good enough one, and he takes the end off it himself: he knew the answer at half past nine on the Thursday morning and the answer was no, and if they had said no he should have sent up a form with a line in it that somebody could not be named and had been entered by him without being asked, **which is exactly the thing the small type warns about, and worse than nothing, because it may be read. And if I had been asked on Friday afternoon what I was going to do I should have said ask them on Monday morning over a table and let them write their own names or not write them at all, and I should have believed it, and I did not come at eight, and the reason I did not is that a man who has got a thing in his pocket and a week in his hand is a different man from the one who made it on Thursday, and I have known that about myself for fourteen years and have never once written it down.**
**And Jory Vant says the sentence that is the knife: *you said that in a shed on the eleventh* — *I did* — *you said the takers and you said the reasons, in that order, and you have just written them on a form. Then you did not find them on Thursday. You found them in this room on the twelfth, and you wrote them down that night, and you have been carrying them about for nine days.***
**Sef Ordry: I gave him a reason at four o'clock yesterday afternoon, he asked me to say it again at eight this morning, I said no, and I was right to say no, and it is the only thing in this business I have been right about, and I am not resigning off a rope.** He will be at a gate bed on Tuesday and the man will hold the other end of it, and if either decides the other stops, and there is no argument in a shed about it.
**The sheet is torn up at about four o'clock in the front room in front of Perdie Ammid and Bess Ilk and Jory Vant, and the four lines Jory Vant copied stay in the second book under the returns, and Perdie Ammid says that is the part that cannot be got out again, and that she would not take them out if she could, because they are true.** *Four true lines about four different reaches, in a book in this village, under a boy's signature saying he copied four of the five and was not asked to copy the fifth.*
**And at about two o'clock, a mile and a half down the lane, in a barrow, at the flume, the boy of fifteen tells Wick Venn, and Wick Venn puts the barrow down and looks at eleven feet of dry launder and says: *then the count was not taken today. It was the last Saturday. — Yes it was.*** He does not go up to Brant. About nine hundred acres of apple are three days past the day the ground went like a bench, and nobody had to be told because there was no notice.

### Chapter 0077 — It Lasted A Day (the shed at the foot of the embankment, the shop at Brant, Sunday the 28th)

**Sef Ordry comes down the bank on foot at ten with a bar and there is no cart until Tuesday, and he says at once that he has not come to cut anything, and that on Monday he is going to be a man working and this is a man talking.**
**The bill is stated as a professional bill and not a grievance.** On the fifteenth he stood in that shed at a quarter to eight with a disc of red wax in his open hand and did not touch anything, for about two hours and a half and a quarter, so that in four years there would be one man in the basin who could say he did not break a Guild seal. **And on Saturday morning at a quarter past eleven a man of thirty-three put *Sef Ordry, sluicewright, of Bexhill, of no district* into a printed line, without asking him, as a man who has the water, so that a thirsty man can come to his door. Both of those are in a row now and nothing in this basin will ever separate them, and there is no form in this basin in which a man may object to a sentence in a margin, and he has known that since he was twenty-three and has used it about other men twice and been ashamed of it once.** He refuses to be thanked for it: *in about four years a man being grateful about a professional cost is a man who has not understood it.*
**And then the reason.** *Give it back to me.* **No.** You are going to put it on the record in place of my name, and it will not go in, because a name goes in a book and a reason does not. A name is a mark and a mark can be looked at; a sentence in a book about a route is an argument, and an argument can be carried from room to room for a hundred and thirty years and a route cannot, and I have never in nine years seen a man carry a route in a sentence. And he agrees with two thirds of what a woman of fifty-four said in a room on the twenty-seventh of the month before, and disagrees with the third, because people do carry things in sentences, and what they carry is not water. **He would not be relieved of it by having it entered.** And the only comfort he will take: you asked me to say it again this morning. You did not ask me what was on the sheet, or whether I had read it, or whether I had seen your coat. **You are not a fool. That is what is wrong with you. I have met fools and I have met careful men and I have never met one that was both.**
**The boy of fifteen comes up the road on the Sunday on his own, because the roster said the last Saturday and it had gone by with four men in a room.** He is paid the Saturday at five shillings and a tenth of a shilling for the Sunday, and neither is entered as a day of the count because the count was not taken. He asks for one thing in the book, and it is that he has not been asked what is on the form and did not offer, and that he told a man on Friday night that he had a sheet in his coat and **it did not help and he is not going to be the man who says it helped.**
**And the chapter ends on the second and larger bill: Margit Saye is four miles down a lane with the only reading of a hand in this country, and in about a year somebody will take a page out of a drawer in a counting-house at Vellum and put it beside a flyleaf in a chest, and it will be a very ordinary afternoon, and nobody has asked her. That is the bill, and it is not payable in about four years. It is payable whenever anybody next wants to know.**

### Chapter 0078 — A Hole You Dig To Get Rid Of Water (the room at Brant, and the lowest field at the second bridge, Monday the 29th)

**Nesta Sarg asks the question, standing up, in about forty words, and then sits down and lets twenty people try to talk over it, which she has not done in thirty-two years.** She has been asked three times this month what she takes out of a drain and never once what is in one. Eight hundred and fifty at the throat, a hundred and ninety at the plank, six hundred and sixty into the ground under a lane three hundred and forty yards above her pail, **the lane is on a clay, the clay is under the lower field, the lower field is nine hundred acres drained by moles, the moles empty into the Sallow Dyke, and nobody in this Reach has ever measured it.**
**The measurement, four times, on the page.** The cut at the second bridge is one foot wide at the bottom and a foot deep: **a square foot of section.** Twenty feet in **twelve seconds** = a foot and two-thirds a second = a foot and two-thirds of a cubic foot a second = twelve and a half gallons a minute = **750 gallons an hour.** Twenty feet in **twelve and a half seconds** = one and three-fifths of a foot a second = **12 gallons a minute = 720 gallons an hour.** Both pairs are the same pair. **The figure is 735**, and the reason is given by Tarin Rusk and not by a fitter: **a fitter would take 750, because 720 is the figure that is true and 750 is the one that is generous, and a card has to be generous for thirty years after everybody in the room is dead; and he is taking the middle one because there is nobody in this Reach left to check him, and he is thirty-three and has never had to say a sentence like it and says it once.**
**And the pre-echo pays.** Jory Vant said *seven hundred and thirty-five* in a room on the eighteenth with no idea where it came from, and Tarin Rusk told nine people there was no such measurement, and now: *there was not, there is now, and I was wrong for eleven days and I did not know it, and I want that written down, because in about eleven years somebody is going to find a card with a number on it and no history, and the history is that a boy of nineteen said it eleven days early in a room and was wrong.*
**The sum, done twice.** 735 × 24 = **17,640 gallons a day**, which is **fifteen times** the whole of the Reach's drinking at the fourth book's 1,160 a day, and which is the Reach's own season of 5,481,000 gallons in **three hundred and ten days.** And the second sum: 735 at the dyke plus 190 at the plank is **925** against the throat's 850, **so seventy-five gallons an hour out of nothing**, and he does it in a field in front of eleven people and refuses to fix it, gives the one bound he will go to (the tank has not moved an eighth of an inch in six years, so the works pass about what the throat passes and not a millimetre more, so the two figures must be the same water counted twice), and then says he cannot prove it and will not stand in a lane with a ladle and call it the same.
**Aldis Kerr says it cannot be lifted, in one sentence, and she is right and it is the whole of the physical case: *your flume drops four feet onto a stone and the water is gone; the bottom of this cut is below the place where your water leaves the world.*** And the second half of it is worse and is his: **it is the Reach's own drainage.** The cut is the lower field's own water table, the moles put it there, the assize's house in the lower field is a house standing in nine hundred acres that is not a marsh because that cut has been taking it away since before the assize, and **the assize is a hundred and thirty years old and it is not a plan for this year, it is a plan for ever.** And the weep at the fourth mile needs that gate, and the gate cannot be fed, and both of those are the same gate and neither can be moved this month. And on the twenty-fourth the ground went like a bench.
**Nesta Sarg says the sentence that takes the paper road apart, in about twenty words, to him and not to a room.** *A mole is a hole you dig to get rid of water. That is all a mole is. That is what the word is. And you have spent a fortnight of a planting window building a road about a hole you dig to get rid of water, and you have written on it a figure of eight hundred and fifty gallons an hour and a tank of six thousand gallons of room and a mark in a wall and two miles four chains of a channel, and every one of those is true, and the true thing about all of them is that they are about a reach this Reach has never had. And the Reach has got one. The Reach has got a hole, and the hole is full. It cannot be shut because there is nothing on it to shut, and it cannot be used because using it is drowning the field it is in.*
**Perdie Ammid writes four lines, because a statement can be improved on in an afternoon and a list has to be got right, and the fourth is: the water was not lost. It was made blind, by one name, written down twice, in two places, in two centuries, in two hands, for two good reasons.** And when the boy of fifteen asks whether 735 is going on a card, Jory Vant says no, there is not one, and the boy says: **that is exactly what a padlock is.**

### Chapter 0079 — Two Books And Two Hands (the room at Brant and the shed at the foot of the embankment, Tuesday the 30th)

**The first, in the second book, in Perdie Ammid's hand, read out twice and initialled on a page of its own.** That the four field books, with the third book and the Guild volume annexed and the first fifty pages of the second excepted, **be entered as the record of the Sallow Reach; and that there be no copy of them made in this village.** That the Reach be entered as supplying itself and no other district, and not supply district 14, North Sallow, and not any district, and no return be received in respect of the works until an assembly of the villages has said otherwise in writing on a day it has named. Witnessed by nine.
She will not let the *no copy* clause go, because a record that is copied is a thing with a copy of it in it and in eleven years the copy is the one anybody checks. **And it goes to Vellum, and she says that sentence for the last time and asks to be watched while she says it, because she has noticed that each time she has said it she has got about nine minutes further into the room, and that is not a system.** She is not saying yes because a man of thirty-three made a road out of her sentence: a woman of seventy-one went west out of a field the day before and said it cannot go back, a fitter went home on a cart on the Friday with the only reading of a hand in the basin in her head, and a boy of nineteen said in a room *then you did not find them on Thursday, you found them in this room on the twelfth.* **And under the entry, in the same hand: I did not come round. I was out of reasons and I had a week. — P.A.**
**The second, in the shed, on the top form of the same forty, headed REQUISITION FOR A PLATE OR OTHER INSTRUMENT.** To be made out by the person having the care of a work, or by a person of the district supplied, and not by an officer. A particular that is not known is not to be given. **And, printed: an instrument is not fitted until a keeper is named. A keeper is named by the assembly of the district supplied. A district supplied that has no keeper has no supply, and an instrument in a slot with no keeper in it is a piece of iron in a hole.**
**Jory Vant: then we have a form that says no to itself and we are about to write it out and pay a post for it. Sef Ordry: and we are going to write it out and pay a post for it, because a request that is not sent is not a request, it is a thing somebody thought of, and the day the plate is fitted is the day a keeper is named, and that is a rule and it is on the form and it has been there since before the assize, and it is the only rule in this basin nobody has ever managed to get round.**
**The particulars are written in the hand of Tarin Rusk, because Sef Ordry will not write them, and the reason is that a particulars column filled in by the man who wants the iron is a column with a man in it.** The throat, a slot two inches wide and an inch and a half deep; **the trials of 865 and 833, off twenty feet in thirteen seconds on the twenty-ninth of the month before and twenty feet in thirteen and a half seconds on the first of this month, and the agreed 850**; three-eighths asked for and not the half-inch in the ring; **that no plate has ever been in that slot, that a plate is either in water or it is not, and that a lath and a stop-watch is not the same quantity as the reading of a plate, and that nobody in this basin has ever put the two side by side; and that the fitter of twenty-four years went home on the twenty-sixth and is four miles down a lane and cannot be asked.** Sef Ordry makes him take one line out — the fitter — because it is true and is not a particular of a work and it will open a woman's door in four years for nothing. He leaves it in. It is permitted knowingly in a shed by a man who is not thanked for permitting it.
**The requisition goes up on the Tuesday evening cart with Jory Vant. The counterfoil stays on a nail beside the card, and by the company's own rule a counterfoil is nothing, and it is the only copy there is. And on Wednesday the thirty-first Perdie Ammid writes one sentence in a book on a page of its own, and it is the only sentence in the whole month that was not read out in a room to anybody at all: his name is in the second book in her hand on a request, and his hand is on a particulars column in a shed, and there are two books and two hands and two places, a village and a shed, and he is in both of them, and neither of them is a record of a route, and both of them are his.**

### Chapter 0080 — The Last Saturday In The Month (the room over the shop at Brant and the shed at the foot of the embankment, Wednesday the 31st)

**The card is read on the last Saturday in the month. The thirty-first is a Wednesday. The last Saturday in this month was the twenty-seventh, and it is gone.** Perdie Ammid works it out on the back of a broadsheet in about nine minutes and does not enjoy it, and Bess Ilk does not check it and never has in thirty-one years and that is what she complains about. Four men were in that room at a quarter past eleven on the twenty-seventh and there was no count taken at the tank, at the plank or at the flume sluice. **She will not take the padlock off the card, because the padlock is the reason and a Saturday is what happened in a room, and a card is a card and not an account of a week.**
**The count is therefore on Saturday the third of next month**, because the first of next month is a Thursday, the second a Friday, the third a Saturday, and she has checked it twice and would like somebody to check it. **Bess Ilk: that is the first day in about six weeks this Reach has been able to name that is not in this month.** The sowing shut on the twenty-fourth, so on the day of the next count the ground will have been like a bench for ten days, and the next sowing is not a thing anybody in this basin has a date for, and she has looked, and there is no date.
**And the card has to be printed, and the only press is at Culm.** The next Friday is the ninth of next month and a card that goes up on the ninth comes back on the twenty-first. **So the count is on the third and the card is printed on the twenty-first, and between those two dates there is a hole on a post at a culvert that anybody may look at.** And the printer will want to know what goes in the empty space, and nobody has asked him, and Perdie Ammid says in a room that she is not going to tell him. **The volume's closing beat is set up here and is not performed here.**
**The seventh line of the card in the shed door is written at about eleven by Sef Ordry, and it is the first entry in eleven years and it is not a clearing.** *The seventh line of this card is not a clearing. There has been no clearing at this work in eleven years and there is not going to be one… A plate was requisitioned for the stone throat on the thirtieth and it will not be fitted until a keeper is named, and a keeper is named by an assembly of a district, and there is no district. The count is read on a Saturday and the last Saturday in this month was the twenty-seventh and it was not taken, and the reason is a room in a village, and the reason for that is on a page of a book in a village and is not mine.* Jory Vant says it is a long line for a small card. **It is eleven years long. You would write it shorter if you had done six of the entries and one of these.**
**And then Wick Venn prices it, in a yard, out loud, in about four minutes, in hours.** The count of the fourteenth was four and a half hours. The twenty-seventh was five and a half hours of his barrow and a lath and a boy of fifteen. **Ten hours is a day and an hour, and a day is what I said in a yard on the fourteenth day of this month in front of about four hundred people and nobody thanked me and I did not want to be. I said: a count is a day and a window is a morning, and every village in this yard can give a morning and not one of them can give a day. And on Saturday a room in this village gave a morning and took a day, and it did it with paper.** He will not be a moral about it and he will not be a man who has been let down by paper, **because he has been let down by a window on the fifteenth by a woman standing in a drain and he was not let down by her either, and the only thing he has been let down by in six weeks is a ground that went like a bench on Wednesday, and no form in this basin has anything to do with that.** What he wants is a column: *the two sets of hours in a book, headed, with the date on it, a column and not a sentence, because a sentence is a thing a man says once and a column is a thing you can add a line to in November.* Perdie Ammid rules it and heads it *Days lost, and by whom, and to what. To be added to and not taken back*, and writes two lines, and the third line is not ruled and there is nothing at the bottom of it yet.
