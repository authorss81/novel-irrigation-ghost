## Volume 01 — The Empty Gauge

### Chapter 0001 — The Gauge at Ashgate (Tarin, Day 1, dawn, Ashgate works)
First measurable rain in fourteen months. Tarin climbs the sealed works three hours early to recalibrate a decommissioned float gauge before the monthly warden check. The float pins at the top stop; the stilling tube is live from below. Head above the gate seat: **5·89 m**. He forces the seized inspection gate with a bar and his shoulder and the residual head runs **uphill** down the gallery outlet channel, a channel laid at a foot in the hundred, dry and furred with six years of dust. He cannot shut it: the main wheel is loaded and a foot of head in the intake channel. He writes *by bar, at 06:11, before the warden's check* in his own hand. The kit's brass float comes out of the water with a grey gritty bloom that is not rust. He walks the covered terrace run downhill and finds it emerging at a low arch on the lower fold where the warden's sheet says *level four, no settlement beyond* — and hears a door being worked on behind it. He turns back up the hill to get the row sheet.

### Chapter 0002 — The Warden's Road (Tarin, Day 1, midday, warden's station)
Pell will not act on a story, only on a condition. Tarin is made to give the head, the gate state, and the direction, and the giving of it puts his own opening in a document. Pell cannot find a receiving district on any sheet he owns. The plate arrives as a work fact on a schedule: outfall **14**, *terrace overflow, row 7–12, lower fold — SEALED, PLATED, NO KEY ISSUED, OUT OF SERVICE*, closure entry 118, dated **twenty-six years**, the same entry that removed the row from the distribution sheet. No keeper, no key, no service window. Tarin takes the notice of uncontrolled channel condition, reads the empty **cause** box, and does not write in it — a decision, not a slip. He asks Pell for an hour and walks the line instead. Niko Venn, his apprentice, tells him the fold has people in it whom nobody has ever asked about.

### Chapter 0003 — A District of Blue Doors (Tarin, Day 1, late afternoon, Glassstep)
The water has nowhere to go. The row is occupied, washing, a woman beating a mat, a cistern filling to a third and stopping. Tarin names the cause in his working voice: the yard's outfall is a plate of iron three-eighths thick over four fist-sized bolts, so a courtyard cistern is the only vessel the district has. He gives Hanne Roeg his own name and his father's, and tells her what happened to the branch in his own hand, and asks for help. He is a stranger to the row he was born in. A Water Office runner catches him with requisition eighteen: the Memory Office has exhibited the Ashgate maintenance volume, which says the gate was shut before the Ninefold Flood and dry for a hundred and eighty-one years, and the commissioner has put Tarin's name against it.

### Chapter 0004 — The Weight of a Leaf (Mara, Day 2, morning, Municipal Memory Office, Vellum)
Routine re-binding of folio eleven. One leaf is a different weight, a different sizing, and has no watermark. Raked light shows a **pressure shadow** on the reverse: an intake head at the main wheel, gate state shut, *5·8_*, dated before the flood, and — in a second, heavier hand — **maintained**. The reading is real and the page has been removed and replaced. Mara refuses to certify, enters the discrepancy in the requisition book in her own hand, and keeps working after hours; the supervisor notes her hours. She reads the missing figure as **5·89** under raking light and a press-glass mirror. The requisition goes to the Water Office with the figure attached, and Cresset will pick the engineer who was nearest.

### Chapter 0005 — Two Professionals, One File (Tarin and Mara, Day 5, morning, provenance room, Vellum)
Batch midpoint. Three days of delay on purpose, and a day and a night of mountain road each way. She suspects he altered a record; he suspects she will seal a working route under cover of historical accuracy. He shows her his notebook — **5·89 m**, measured at 06:11 on the first — knowing it hangs him. She tells him the mechanism: not a forged number but a *true* one lifted off the original leaf so a clean leaf could say *chamber void*, with the second-hand word *maintained* under it. He confesses the notice with the empty cause box; she calls it a complaint with a route in it. In the lower room she shows him the cracked Ashgate record stone, carted down six years ago and left on a *pending disposal* slip. He puts his hand on it. **First Gauge statement:** *One route remembers. It asks for a witness.* Incomplete (does not say which route) and slightly wrong (the stone has two cut lines and he counts one; the request is given to the water rather than to the person). A second pressure sits underneath it, unnamed, with a mundane explanation available. He perceives and can do nothing. The hook: the only witnesses to Ashgate live in a district that does not legally exist.

### Chapter 0006 — The Low Pump on Glassstep (Tarin, Day 7, dawn, Glassstep low pump)
**First reach of Name.** He writes the requisition answer on the coach overnight and puts 5·89 in a second document, knowingly. He hears the low pump knocking under the flags at night — a pumping plant that is on no sheet he has ever signed. With Wick he digs out the blind branch and its stop-gate. **Niko Venn** knows which stones on the terrace are markers and which are only stones, brings the chisel, and beats Tarin at his own district; he refuses, out loud, to be Tarin's reason. Half a mile down the lane Tarin considers and **refuses** to use his own private memory of the row as an anchor, because a private memory is not a public record and nobody offered. **Tam Roke**, who keeps the low pump, is asked to witness and is told the full terms, including what Tarin does not know. Residents argue the name; Hanne Roeg supplies *Glassstep*. Tarin re-cuts the weathered face of the pump's marker stone with the borrowed chisel; Roke cuts a crooked mark. The water stops fighting and runs. Roke's mother drinks and says: *That is not our water.*

### Chapter 0007 — What the Water Brought With It (Tarin with Yara Sen, Day 7, daytime)
**Salt fever is physical, fast, and caused by badly addressed water.** Yara Sen finds early salt fever in three children and says to his face that the crosscurrent is his opening's consequence. She shows the three diagnostic points on the pad of the fingers. He confesses the cause before she proves it — the route was opened with no legitimacy condition, and the people it runs past were not in the decision. He closes the branch at 11:20 in front of the row, and tells Tam Roke it was his doing and not the water's, on Hanne Roeg's instruction. **First name debt, minor:** the two-finger press he has used since he was thirteen — the tap, on spindles, floats, and page edges — is gone, and his hand will not make the notation he has made ten thousand times. His bitter tea tastes of nothing.

### Chapter 0008 — The Replaced Leaf Has a Cousin (Mara, Day 9, after hours, restricted stack, Vellum)
Same day the low pump goes dry on the count Tarin gave Yara. Court-supply ink dates by nib cut, gum, and the **issue stamped on the tin lid**. The tin is the same batch for the Ashgate replacement leaf and for the Glassstep survey redactions — a batch issued **twenty-six years** back, from a tin the Office has never had cause to empty. Same hand, same ink, one hand in service across the whole period. The Glassstep redactions strike out one hundred and four household names; the Ashgate leaf removed true pressure figures and set a clean leaf in their place. Mara draws a one-to-one key of both in her own ink, and **does not enter it in the hand-drawing register** — choosing to be the only person in the basin who can read the evidence on condition that nobody can check her. The hand belongs to the Commissioner's registry and to her mother's brother, who was nineteen at her mother's table when she learned to read it. Vellum's district return shows the terrace cisterns at twelve days and the low pump not running.

### Chapter 0009 — The Commissioner's Kindness (Tarin, Day 10, morning, Water Office)
Halden Roe is gracious, exact, and better at this than Tarin is. He offers North Sallow pumping district — a real post, nine hundred and forty properties, a finished cistern programme — before he says anything else. He reads Tarin's own log back to him. The case: the certified discrepancy shows a figure identical to one a hundred and eighty-one years old, therefore Tarin's log must be a copy, therefore the copying is the tampering. The blank-cause notice of the first is in the file. Tarin refuses the transfer and **accuses Roe of falsification in front of Deryn Fenn and Osla Vint** — that the decommissioning certificates and the gauge certificate are Roe's own work over six years, and that a pumping plant in the old filter house has been running at night for the better part of a year, and that a dead reservoir has no lawful allocation. **Charge one, filed and read on Day 10: tampering with city records.** Stand-down, seal, keys, district reference and desk taken; he keeps the blue book, the bar, the rule, the chalk, the tin pot.

### Chapter 0010 — One Name, One Window (Tarin, Day 20, 15:40–03:00, Glassstep courtyard)
Batch climax. Ten days of suspension, a letter refused by the Memory Office, nineteen of twenty-three people asked to stand on a stone saying no, and Niko naming a descent into the old filter house that no survey records. Tam Roke leaves a gauged length of oak at the wall. Pell arrives at 15:40 with a clerk and leaves the right-hand column of his entry book blank, because a warden records the time a condition **begins**. Yara Sen states the charge aloud before he reaches the stone. **He cannot cut his own name on the blank face** — the lost two-finger press from Chapter 7 — so the naming has to be spoken. **Second Gauge statement:** *The stone is blank. Two hands are on it.* He works out the rule himself, aloud, where the yard can argue: a witness who cannot walk away is not a witness, and the second hand has to belong to somebody free to refuse. Roke refuses, out loud, and is right to. He lays out the terms: no second hand, the charge, salt fever, the plate, the empty cause box, the arithmetic (**6,700 gallons of vessel, 610 gallons an hour, eleven hours**), and that it stops by itself at the balance point. A man walks out. Then Tarin says the name. **16:00** — the water settles and runs. Pell's entry is timed and dated. **18:00** — the warden's men carry him out; Pell tells him there will be a **second charge, distinct and unjoined**. He tells the yard plainly: *I do not know where it comes from.* At **03:00** a nine-year-old walks up to the second gatehouse to say it stopped, and that it was not his. The run is dry, the cisterns are full, and the plate under the yard is ticking.

### Chapter 0011 — A Yard Full Of Water (Tarin, Day 21, dawn to late morning, the blue steps and the warden's station)
The stand-down order is nailed to the second gatehouse wall, two numbered charges on one sheet of paper, the hearing set for the 22nd and adjourned without a date. Down in the lane the row has put the water out — 38 pails along the low house wall, the cistern at its coping, washing on the line. Tarin walks the vessels with a stick and gets 6,700 gallons, the same figure as the 20th, and understands that the window bought a cistern with no headroom in it. **Yara Sen's finding:** the cistern is the worst, the fourth pail the best, evaporation is the only good news; it is not fit to drink and not fit to wash in, and the row has been living on two ounces a head a day since the 21st. Old Marden asks how long the cistern has left with nobody drinking it, and Tarin does it in front of the yard: six thousand seven hundred gallons at fifty a day, which is a hundred and thirty-four days, a hundred and thirty. Yara says the row is not short of water, it is short of water it is allowed to give anybody. A district-return runner comes down with a form with four boxes — row number, keeper, tanks, gallons — and cannot fill any of them, and takes it back blank, having written on it: *if there is no row, then no row can ever be wrong.* **Pell** comes down on foot and states the whole of his power: he can enter a condition, not a district, and offers a return with blank boxes and a filled remark, knowing it does nothing. Tarin writes his list — water, head, trace, record, keeper, standing — and sees that five of the six lines are ticked. *Witness* is deliberately absent from the page: he does not yet know what he is looking for. Hanne Roeg, from the fourth step: *This row is not a row.* And then: **"Then we will be a row."** Asked who signs: *"Then one of us signs it."*

### Chapter 0012 — A Named Representative (Tarin and the lane, Day 22, warden's station and the dispensary step)
Pell reads out the six numbered boxes of an application for entry of a district row: name, representative and household, houses and persons, water works maintained, source of supply, date of continuous maintenance. Four shillings, not refundable, paid by Bel Crale. **The count is taken by the district itself** (Marda Ollan on a slate, a day and a half, door by door) because a man under a stand-down may not take a census: **twenty-four houses, one hundred and twenty-six persons**, with two people entered at the margin as declining enumeration, one of whom holds a ration card in the Fenner district. Tarin objects twice and is refused twice. His first objection is the correct one: a name on that form is a name in the commissioner's registry by the end of the week. Hanne Roeg's answer: *Then in eleven months we will be a thing that can be refused, instead of a thing that cannot be.* She refuses to be made a brave old woman: four signers, she is the oldest and the only one with a legible hand. **Twenty-two sign, nineteen asked and said no**, and both numbers are read out twice into the card. Box two is rewritten at Hanne's insistence to add *answerable for the truth of it, and not having been put forward by any office in this city.* Box five states the source truthfully — a branch off the Ashgate intake at 5·89 m through a channel cut by Rusk, Tarin, named on a stone, at a gate the city ledger records as shut — which puts the engineer and his charge into a public document. She refuses to let him add *source not known to the district*. Pell warns that the application will get a file number, that the file number is a handle, and that the answer will come from the commissioner's office and not from him. Marda Ollan signs and says she has made herself the easiest person in the row to find.

### Chapter 0013 — The Shelf-Marker (Mara, Day 23, morning, lower room and reading supervisor's office)
Folio eleven is gone from the ninth slot. The **shelf-marker** says *ASHGATE INTAKE, MAINTENANCE SERIES, FOLIO 11. WITHDRAWN. R. QUE.* with the date **17/4** under it — six days ago, while she was in the low court at the Fair Copy. **Hollis Kreft** invokes **rule nine** of the reading rules: an unverified record may not decide a public route. He is right, and he is also the mechanism, and he has done the rule *publicly*: the monthly finding aid now reads, in the public reading room, *contains one substituted leaf, uncertified* — Mara's finding converted into an Office posture, in the Office's hand, without her name. She gets no thanks and no argument. Kreft then tells her to go and get a second instance independently, somewhere it is not this building. She writes out what one would have to be: the same substitution of a whole leaf, the same institutional correction wording from a different archive, the same ink from a dated tin. The only survey originals of the blue steps in the basin are in the blue steps — the field book went to the house the surveyor lodged in, and her mother told her the name of that house when she was eleven: *the Roegs have the book, and mind it.* She takes the **legitimate route** — a quarterly **collation** warrant, five boxes, eleven words of purpose — and Kreft signs it and records that she proposed it herself, which is the fourth note in her file this quarter. The district application comes down with the morning's tray and the second box stops her dead. She copies box two and box five into the **public** requisition book, with a footnote that the applicant's consent to the copy is not held.

### Chapter 0014 — Nineteen Said No (Tarin, Day 25, all day, up and down the lane)
Tarin builds a witness list properly: name, exactly what each person would be answerable for, date, and a ruled line for a mark. Every refusal is sound. **Wick** took the door frame down on the first and the row has held it against him since, and he works the Reach six days a week. **Bel Crale refuses and explains why the dispensary has been unfindable for a year** — *illegible* is a wall, thirty years thick, and it is why forty-one pounds has lasted. Tarin gives her his word, twice, that the dispensary will not be on the list. **Old Marden asks for his name to be cut in two**: a witness to where the water comes from, not to what it does. Tarin discovers that a partial witness is a category. **Marda Ollan signs** with four conditions, including *you write the actual date under my name*. **The Tall boy signs** as the one who got lost on the seventh. And the **boy of nine** asks to sign, and Tarin refuses, and says why on the page: a witness must be able to answer for ten years, and a route resting on a nine-year-old is a route resting on a family. The turn: three names and two refusals, with the condition stated five times on the way up the lane by people who have not met, and it is the same condition — *I will not be the second hand for a route I cannot stop.* That is Roke's reason from the twentieth, and it has walked round the lane by itself. Tarin understands that the missing second hand is not a neighbour but **the other end of the pipe**, which is up a sealed hill behind a chained wheel, and every man between pays a wage out of the office that charged him twice. Niko makes him say the money out loud: no authority, no money, no crew.

### Chapter 0015 — Six Thousand Seven Hundred (Yara Sen, Day 27, six in the morning to five in the afternoon, dispensary and yard)
Yara's own book: forty-one names, nine with the marks, four at all three points, five at two, and a column of her own invention headed *at risk*. The dispensary's whole clean supply is eleven gallons of rain in a stone jar. She argues the six thousand seven hundred gallons out in the open, in four pieces. **The crust:** a jar boiled down to a finger of white ring; boiling takes water out and leaves the salt, so it makes it worse per gallon. **The skin:** touch the wet stone inside the lid, then look at the pads of your fingers — a day early, the same three points, and there is no washing in it and no drinking it. **Cooking:** boiled, kept out of cups, the best the row has, and the arithmetic has changed. **The arithmetic:** four children in the third stage at four gallons a day, five in the second stage at two gallons, a hundred and seventeen people to keep alive on a floor of three pints a head — sixteen, ten, and forty-four, which is **seventy gallons a day, and nine hundred and eighty over a fortnight** — and there is no figure in this basin for it: a cart twice a week off the Sallow road at twopence, and Bel has only ever been able to afford seventy gallons a *week*, and that is the whole of the reason those four children are where they are. And the six thousand seven hundred is nearly seven times the fortnight they need and they cannot touch a drop. A woman at the back names it: *it is not a cistern, it is a cistern with a hose on it.* Yara agrees and explains that there is no route to move it and no row to move it on. **She files the case** — nine cases, dated findings, in a district with no schedule entry — and says out loud, to Bel Crale, that filing will bring a district examiner and that the examiner's form has a box saying *water supply in connection*, and Bel Crale says do it now, with the water on the table, and *do it deliberately and do not do it by accident.* A man from the Fenner district walks four miles with a barrow, is given a pail, and Yara tells him plainly in front of a yard full of people that it is not drinking water and not wash water and cannot be mixed because he has no water to mix it with. He asks what it is for. Nobody answers.

### Chapter 0016 — A Hundred And Four Names (Mara, Day 28, half past eight to six, Glassstep, with Tarin from five)
Mara arrives on a **collation warrant** with three rules written on the back of her hand, the third being *do not leave this lane with anything in your hand that only you can read.* Hanne Roeg tells her at the door that the field book is not hers — it is the lane's, and there is a difference. Forty people stand round a trestle table and look at two pieces of paper about themselves, and being allowed to look is the only thing Mara has that nobody has given her. She reads the fair copy against the field book aloud — 230 properties, 109 returned for closure, 31 for exclusion, 90 retained, and a folio promised to carry the list of a hundred and forty. Then the trouble: twenty-six years under a floor has foxed the folio and taken the ink *into* the fibres, so **a stroke-for-stroke comparison is impossible** and she will not pretend otherwise. She can give batch, margin, schedule reference and the fact that it is one hand — the pattern, not the hand. Marda Ollan points out that an uncheckable claim about a hundred and four houses is what Mara has spent four days refusing to be. Old Marden solves it: make it checkable with one. **Hanne Roeg offers the fourth step** — one name, read aloud, in a yard, by the fourth step herself, on a date. The folio carries a hundred and four strokes where the field book promises a hundred and forty, and that gap is now on the page as the first thing in twenty-six years anybody in this lane has been in a position to notice. Mara reads the hundred and four. At the eleventh an old man says *that's mine* — the second house, his mother's — and at the fourteenth Hanne says *mine* and Mara stops both times and writes down that she stopped. Then nine thousand separate little agreements of the retained properties, all in the open, twice corrected by people in the crowd. **The two tracings:** Mara makes one, then hands the second sheet and the pen to Hanne Roeg and does not dictate it, and the old woman writes a hundred and four house numbers in her own hand over four hours. Mara certifies both as made from one original in one room in one sitting by two named hands. *That is a second attestation. It is not proof.* Hanne Roeg is now a custodian of a record. The **Tarin–Mara agreement**, on the dispensary step in front of nine people: neither uses the other's method in public; **nothing goes on paper with one name on it** (she catches him spotting her own Chapter 8 decision in four seconds and neither pretends otherwise); neither takes a private memory without an answer that is not silence; neither produces the other's evidence without telling them first; they disagree in public and the disagreement is recorded. Neither gets what they want. She will not write down the sentence Rusk wants — *the district's legal existence and the restricted folio are the same object* — because a sentence in a conservator's hand is not a record. The coda: the certificate's hand-characteristic is the only sentence she writes that is not falsifiable by anybody who can read, and reading it means the commissioner's registry will examine a man who is her mother's brother and who was nineteen at her mother's table. She could file the weaker certificate before Friday. She files the real one on the eleventh of the next month, and writes to her mother.

### Chapter 0017 — What There Was Room For (Tarin, Day 30, four in the morning to half past two, the blue steps)
The arithmetic is on the back of a ward notice: 6,700 gallons of vessel, 610 gallons an hour at the pin found on the 19th, eleven hours. And under it, in his own hand, on the 30th: **there is no room, so make the hole.** He wakes Wick and gives him the whole of it at four in the morning, including the price: 4,700 gallons is going to come out of the bottom of the cistern into a lane with no drain, over a plate, and he cannot work out a way of not paying it. Wick comes anyway, with a bar, and will not be a keeper and will not hold the branch. The draw-off comes out at nine minutes past four and runs for two hours. The plate is still ticking, unchanged, and he writes *unchanged* twice. At ten past six he sets the log at the pin, puts both palms on the stone, and says **"Glassstep low pump branch,"** and **the water does not change** — no bed going quiet, no third of a sound dropping away, and the channel starts to cut. He offers the branch his own private memory of the lane, standing in it, and **nothing takes it**, because a private memory is not a public record and nobody offered: he is on the wrong side of the rule he worked out on the seventh. He tries the blank face with the chisel and cannot seat it, and writes *06:15. Set chisel, blank face, second attempt. Hand will not seat. Name not cut. Blank face not cut.* Then the compounding error: the tank fills in **3 h 10 min** against a computed 7 h 40. He has the number and does not use it — when the branch opened on the 20th the run's sound dropped by a third, so the branch was taking a third of a run he has never measured, which comes into the yard through an eighteen-inch lead pipe half silted, at a rate nobody has ever had a reason to find. The tank is filling twice as fast. He watches the vessel, because a man has to watch the vessel. **At twenty-five past nine the ticking stops, and at about twenty-five past nine the floor of the lowest room comes up.** Wick gets the boy out. Tarin runs up the lane; Niko is at the branch; three men and a bar take eleven minutes to lift the log, and Tarin's left shoulder goes again. The branch stopped itself at the right level in the right tank, at 10:10, and the lane does not drain. He writes the whole of it up and then writes the thing he is obliged to write: **he cannot say whether he lost it at the witness, or at the record, or at his standing**, keeps the three apart, estimates the unnamed channel's extra third and will not stake anything on it, and will not write down a guess in a book. Niko's closing line: *That is not a mystery, Tarin. That is a job. You watched the tank because the tank is the vessel, and nobody watched the thing under the floor, because you cannot post a man on a floor.*

### Chapter 0018 — What The Ground Did (Yara Sen, Day 31, half past six to eleven at night)
Yara brings a spade, as promised, and puts it in four places: the ground is wet at a hand's depth at all four, and at two at the throat. She does the work in the open, in order, and refuses to interpret for them — a jar set to noon so that chemistry is in charge for one morning, a silver spoon in a pint of water on a plate in the sun, and the spade. The finding: thirty years of a plate over an outfall has turned the lane's ground into a closed sponge with a water table standing at the surface of the street, and the well in the dispensary awning survived only because it goes down into something below the sponge. **The terrace road main runs brackish by eleven o'clock** — the same water at the other end of the same problem, a lead main six feet from where she is standing — and it goes off the moment the pressure drops, so keep it running. Nine children with the marks; one at three points for the second time in nine days with burning palms, in the third stage. The lowest room has a hand's depth in it that is not going to stop, and Ondra takes his frame apart and leans it on the outside wall and his boy sleeps in a chair, and Yara tells him the room is wet and then damp and then a room with a damp corner, and that she has not got a better offer than the top step and a tarpaulin. Then the money. On the 27th a man from the Fenner district was given a pail; on the 28th he was back with four men and two carts, and by the 31st **the blue steps is selling its own cistern** at a penny rising to threepence, to people who do not live there, **six hundred and eighty gallons and eight pounds** — ten shillings on the first eighty, seven pounds ten on the remaining six hundred. Bel Crale stopped it for an hour and moved when a woman with a low barrel asked her to. The clean-water cart stops coming on the 30th — not told to, not threatened, just a man with a name and a row and a wife — and the next two will not come into the lane at all. Yara's requirement is seventy gallons a day; the dispensary had fourteen pounds and sixpence on the 1st and has **fifteen shillings and fourpence** on the 31st, which at eightpence a gallon buys twenty-three gallons, a third of one day. The clean-water price goes 2d, then 9d on the 30th, then 8d on the 31st. She goes to the fourth step and asks for two hundred gallons, and says out loud what it will cost: a household's water going into a clinic is a thing a man with a form can write two lines about. Hanne Roeg's answer: **write it down, put the fourth step against it, and write beside it that the water is not fit to drink and we are giving it away anyway.** Yara also tells her plainly that she cannot be findable and unfindable at once, and Hanne Roeg says she priced it on Saturday. The day's last entry goes in Yara's book under the date: a sale is a fact, a fact with a price on it is a record, and the next physician is going to arrive in about four weeks and want to know why there is money in it.

### Chapter 0019 — The Cut Off The Quarry Side (Niko Venn, Day 33, and two in the morning on the 2nd of the next month)
First Niko chapter. He has been useless for thirteen days, and worse, the contract of his apprenticeship died with the district reference on the 10th. He has bought eight fathoms of rope on credit off a chandler he knows from a pipe requisition, two hooded lamps and oil, and a tin-lid hood for the second one, and Wick helped him carry them and looked at him a long time. **The thump has gone from two a second to three**, and up to that point it has never stopped, and a pump making up for a head it has lost is not a mystery: somebody is drawing the Ashgate head and Tarin drew it for nine hours on the 30th. Niko catches Tarin on the mill wall and lays out the descent: a cut in the rock face two miles past the mill, not in the district and not on any plan, forty feet down to the quarry wall at the back of the filter house, a rope-eye in an iron ring; the only other way in is the works' front door, which carries a warden's seal six years old. He makes Tarin say all three reasons out loud with a time on them — no authority, no money, no crew — and makes him say why he cannot be the man on the record for the rope: a second name under a suspended man's name in the same month is a continuation, not a rescue. And the finding: **there is no man in the district who will put a name on a board about a rope.** Hanne Roeg refuses at half past nine and gives six reasons, and the sixth wins: a district that has been visible for eleven days has a barrow, a bucket, eight pounds, a doctor's book and a discharged tap, and in three weeks a man with a form; add a hole in a hill and a coil of rope and a debt at a chandler's and the district gets taken off the schedule in October — not out of wickedness, because it will be busy. She is right, and the reason she is right is Tarin's own Day 30. He promises not to come up the lane on this business without asking, and she tells him the promise is one he cannot keep and will be held to anyway. They go up the mill road at two in the morning on the second with Wick, who comes because Tarin does not ask him, and whose condition is that he is standing in the dark holding a rope and not putting his name on anything. The cut is at the north end of the face and invisible from thirty feet below. The **spoil at the mouth is a week old**, there is standing water rilling down it, four size-nine prints going in and one set coming out, a lantern lit for about two hours, an open tin of oil, and a coil of good hemp **cut by somebody else's knife**. And then the thump stops, in the middle of Niko's hand on it, and a voice twenty feet down on a staging says *get the ladder, and tell Hallam to bring the other can, we are running her dry*, and the man on the staging looks up at three people on a ledge and says: **"Well. You will be the Water Office, and you will be the wrong week."**

### Chapter 0020 — A Named Route (Sluice Warden Pell, Day 34, morning, the blue steps)
First Pell chapter. A warden's office cannot refuse anything; it can only **return** an application, with reasons, in a form, on a date, signed — and a return is a thing done to a schedule, not to a person. He took a day to write four grounds. **One:** the route exists and the works exist, but the office holds no service record; its only entries are the Day 1 notice addressed to no district with an empty cause box and the Day 20 timed entry, and both are conditions, and a warden enters conditions and does not enter services. **Two:** the source is unverified, and the volume that documents it has been in restricted handling since the seventeenth under rule nine. **Three:** three names and two refusals on a sheet in an unstated hand, and a witness is entered against a route, and there is no route to enter him against. **Four:** the date is accepted, and he writes that it is accepted, because a return with no truth in it can be thrown out in an hour. He also reads out the **warden's note**, which is the worse half: the **Day 22** return with blank boxes, entered under the warden's discretion and carried into the district book on the 23rd, is the first document in the district book in which the words *the blue steps* appear, and it says the water is fit for no use, the plate is weeping, and there is no row, no keeper, no schedule entry. Hanne Roeg's reading of it: *the only name I have ever had in any city document is the name of a defect.* Pell then gives her the thing he will not put in writing and says so: the district's legal existence and one leaf in Vellum are the same object, and if the leaf can be read you have a source, and if you have a source the leaf can be read, and neither can be got first. What he *will* put in writing is a warden's note that records that grounds one and three are declined for want of an office record and an entry, that the volume is restricted under rule nine, that **a restricted volume may be consulted by a named applicant on a scheduled reading day with a second reader's countersignature, and the next one is the eleventh of the next month, a Friday**, that this office has no power to enter a district, amend a schedule, inspect a sealed works, certify a source, or answer a question about Ashgate, that the hearing on charge one was set for the 22nd and adjourned without a date and the midsummer review has been set for three other days since, and that on the 21st a named engineer under two charges said in a public yard that he did not know the source of the water, and the district drank it anyway, and the warden wrote it down. He refuses to say one word about the charges in a return on a district application. **Tarin names the two ways out in the yard**: the volume will tell him the head and not the place, and there is a descent off the quarry side that he cannot authorise and cannot pay and has stopped trying to stop. A hundred and twenty people decline to leave the yard when Hanne asks whether anybody wants to say something. She sends him to talk to the boy and then asks for one day in the lane where nothing happens. Pell goes up the track with a return in his coat and a reading day in his head, and says he will write down what he can see in the district book every week for as long as he holds the post, including what is very bad for this lane.

### Chapter 0021 — The Water Is Going Down (Tarin, Day 35, Friday the 4th, the blue steps and the fourth step)
The batch's first job is a fact nobody in the district can feel: **the run has stopped.** Two days of silence under the flags, and not one of a hundred and twenty-six people noticed, because the only two men in this city who had ever heard the pumping plant at three a second were a suspended engineer and a boy of twenty-two. **The chapter is dated the 4th and only the 4th** — the day the lane finds out and the day before the descent — and its ledger covers two days, not a week. Tarin marked the coping himself on Wednesday morning and the water is a hand's width below that mark by four o'clock, at six foot three in a tank of eight feet of room. Bel Crale's boy brings the sale ledger: 340 on the second, 340 on the third, 340 today, and the barrow man can get sixpence. **The arithmetic is done in public on a ward notice and it is Tarin's second time doing arithmetic in a yard and the first it has not gone against him:** standing at the lip on the night of the 2nd, 4,700 gal; sold in Bel Crale's book 340 and 340; **ninety gallons of fenner pails that Bel Crale is right not to have written down and Tarin counts at the arch, thirty pails of three gallons**; lane drawing 140; evaporation 100; out 1,010; **standing 3,690 gallons on the evening of the 4th, which is six foot three.** And the finding is not a sum: Wednesday out at 460, Thursday out at 550, **the whole of the difference being the ninety fenner gallons** on a rain morning in the fenner district, so take the ninety away and the two days are the same day — and Tarin says out loud that *two days is two days and I am not going to stand in a yard and call two days a week.* So **the water stopped coming off the hill on the night of the second, between sunset and midnight, and it was being put here.** *Yara Sen* puts 70 gal a day back in front of a yard with its derivation (16 + 10 + 44) and says **today is the fourteenth day of the fortnight and tomorrow it is a fortnight and a day past a number she wrote herself and was proud of**, that the count has not moved in a fortnight and that is not good news, and that the fourth child is at three points again, three days sooner than the last time. *Hanne Roeg refuses the descent again and is right again, and says two things.* She says he is not going down there to get water — *it is a thing somebody said* — and then she says she is not going to stop him, does not consent, was not asked, and does not want to know: not on the day, not the morning after, not in a week. And: *write that down, because in nine days one of you is going to want it in a different shape.* **The four shillings come out of the bucket here** — paid that morning for a reading on the **eleventh**, with a refund a quarter of a year out, which is a long way past the morning they need it. Marda Ollan counts the eight pounds out on the step and says the two things that are on the step, which are four shillings buys a reading and two hundred and forty gallons buys three days and ten hours, and which is not a third thing. At the cut at dusk on the fifth: the lantern is out, the oil tin is gone, the cut coil is gone, there is fresh spoil again, three sets in and none out — **and a good new timber ladder leaning on the staging, because a man on a staging said *get the ladder* on the second and had sent for one.**

### Chapter 0022 — Past The Staging (Niko Venn, the evening and night of the 5th)
The threshold beat, and it is the ladder. The switchback in three pitches, which he goes down without touching the wall and is aware of and will not mention for a year. Three nights of deciding what he is going to be, and the answer he arrived at is *an apprentice with a master under two charges*, which has the merit of being the least flattering version available. On the ledge: a ladder eight or nine feet long, scaffold board with the rounds left on one side, pale wood, clean rungs. **Niko: *they sent for it.*** A kindness would have been no ladder and a locked ring. A man who has been told three engineers are coming and has decided to make it easy for us is the thing that goes in the mouth of the hand that is already full of figures. Past the staging — four boards, a rail on two sides, a coil of new hemp, a **tin reflector on a hook with a hood over it against the draught**, a fold of oiled rag, and **a boy of about fifteen asleep on the boards with his boots in a basket.** Niko's whole speech, delivered before they go on: the boy is paid, the boy is asleep at midnight because he has been on it every night since the second, and he is not evidence and he is not a man on a staging. **The filter house:** four filter beds gone to lime and cheese, a settling tank, and a suction that goes *down* through a floor into a cast gland. **The plant is ordinary and enormous in its ordinariness** — a single-cylinder beam pump, walking beam on a frame, pump rod into a bore, and at the near end a horse gin: squared timber, a vertical spindle, a four-bar sweep about eleven feet across, and a pinion. **The sweep is still.** A block of fresh-sawn oak, sap still wet, under the beam's heel. No horse, mangers down, trough scraped, one set of hooves going out and a week old. Nobody broke it: somebody was told, and the telling came from an office, and there is no letter. **The tally board** nailed by the beam: date, cans, *head at wheel* — 5·7 as far back as the lamp goes, and a third of the way up where the 7 becomes a 9 and everything above it reads **5·9**. Niko copies the whole column onto the back of his hand, because he does not trust a number he cannot put down. Then he takes the second lamp — the tin-lid hood — and goes down the adit, having been told by a man on a wall that he has spent his life putting a dangerous place in front of himself and calling it a job. **The chapter's action is Wick's hands.** He is left at the staging holding a ladder, alone with the good rope and the bad one, and the ladder is not a rope, and a ladder slides, and the foot of it comes off a rail he was told he could not knock out, and Wick Venn puts the flat of his left hand on a quarry face and slides it eight inches before it stops. **The end of Chapter 22 is his hand, turned over, and the sentence: *I cannot feel any of it at all.***

### Chapter 0023 — A Man Holding A Rope (Wick Venn, night of the 5th into the 6th and the day)
First Wick chapter, and the chapter is his hands. He establishes in the first three lines that he is not going to be the man who makes a noise about it, and then he says *right*, and *that is me then*, and then nothing for six hours. Niko tests the fingers one at a time and Wick watches his own face, and stops helping and lets the boy do all five. **Tarin Rusk's entry on the flat of the staging boards, read out because Wick asked for it:** grazed eight inches on the quarry face at about ten past one, full thickness off the heel of the hand and the base of the thumb, outer two fingers white and cold, no sensation in the outer two, sensation in the other three only under pressure, cannot close the hand, cannot hold a rope or a bar. *Not treated by me. Nothing in this kit.* Nobody can get up the ladder: Tarin gets eight feet before the rail comes under his hand, Niko six, and the reason is in a sentence — he could not get four fingers round a square and could not get two either, and the two he had been using for forty years were not there. **The ring is out:** a quarter of an inch of movement in lime mortar gone to sand, a puff of it, and *it will come out with the stone round it the day somebody loads it properly.* **The chapter's discovery is what a hand is for:** he cannot grip, he can tie. A becket, a bowline and a half hitch, onto the ladder rail and out into a bight, with his knee on the rope and the tail in his teeth, an hour and a half and Tarin's thumb and Niko's two fingers. *I can do knots to the end of my life with this hand. What I cannot do is carry a bar, hold a pipe, take a cistern lid off a hinge, or go down a ladder. And that is the whole of what I have done for nineteen years.* **He blames himself, out loud, and rules Niko out of it:** I took a lamp off this staging because a boy went off down a hole and I am a man who holds things. Both of those are the ordinary way that ordinary men get into holes, and I have watched it four times and it has not once been stupidity. It is nobody else's. The boy on the staging brings him a box of clean linen and a jar of oil because a Mrs. Drust sends it down on Mondays, and Wick will not have his name taken. **End: *we were not let out of here by anybody. We were not rescued. We walked in.***

### Chapter 0024 — Beneath The Houses (Tarin, the 7th, the chamber at the end of the adit)
**The midpoint.** The adit is four feet high, six wide, pine sets squared to a pattern, four hundred and some yards, down, two turns. Niko is at the first bend with a camp, a swept floor, four notched pebbles and a fifth half-cut, a lamp rationed to half an hour four times a day, and the whole of the competence on display: *I made a camp. I swept it. I went down twice and I did not go over the edge and I came back up both times.* **The air changes on the second turn** — not cold, not warm, not moving. A pressure behind the ear, the way a thumb pressed into a soft place is a pressure and not a sound, and it has the quality of a wall on the other side of which a street is going on with its business. A dressed wall, damp, with a dry conduit groove a hand wide at hand height. **The chamber:** tooled rock, a six-foot dressed ledge on the north-east side with a nine-inch kerb, and water that is clear to the bottom, absolutely still, with no drift, no weed, and a line cut in the rock at the level, an inch deep and a foot long, by a man who expected to want it again. **The measurement, twice, with string and a chalk on a stick because a folded rule against a plumb line in a lamp is a folded rule against a plumb line in a lamp:** 9 ft 4 in above the adit floor; 1 ft 9 in from that floor to outside ground at the filter house door; **11 ft 1 in above the outside ground at the door of the works.** Then he looks up, and it takes him a long time, and the chapter says so. **The roof is not rock. It is the underside of a floor** — flagstones on putty, a hearth and flue, a chimney breast eleven feet over standing water, a cut lead stub of a floor drain, a wooden stair of eleven steps hanging with nothing at the top. And where the light reaches the flagging changes direction and the level of the floor is not the same all the way along: *this is a street, not a house.* The underside of a flagstone is warm. **The works' record stone** is in situ at the head of the ledge, the name cut twice out of register, the second cut the better. He puts his hand on it and the lamp goes *away* — not out. **The pressure becomes a room with a lot of people in it going about their work at the same time and not one of them for him**, no silence anywhere in it and no beat, and the noise stays on the floor. **> The Gauge: This is not beneath the works. It is beneath the houses.** He gives it an hour, because that is what he would do with a stuck valve. Counting: nine, then eleven, then not reproducible. Timing: no gap, and *a room with people in it breathes*. Speech, his own name, and his mother's name once, taken straight back out of the air because a name put into a thing that has not asked for it is a thing he refused to do to a man on a stone in Vellum. Both hands on the stone, asking for a number, a direction, a date, a word with a vowel in it. **Nothing changes. Not one grain.** The work is work: a man saying one number over and over; a woman calling a child's name twice and then not; two men arguing patiently about a boundary, *that is the low field* / *it has never been the low field*; somebody counting stock in tens past a hundred; a price; *bring the ladder*; the water up to the third step; a child asking one question with no pause and no answer and no louder. **And four women giving directions about pumps at once.** He cannot tell which of the four, and he has wanted to tell for twenty-six years, and he writes down **four and not one.** The entry is the chapter's spine: the measurement, the roof, *nine, then eleven, then not countable; no addressee; no response*, and *I have not got a single fact out of it that I could put in a report and I have tried.* He tries to cut the blank face and the stone takes a white bruise a quarter of an inch off and cuts nothing. **Niko counts the stair: two hundred and forty-one steps. And I am going to be the one who does, and I would like it written down that I said that first.**

### Chapter 0025 — The Well That Never Needed A Pump (Tarin, the 8th, the stair and the surface)
It takes a day to get out of the chamber and **the reason it takes a day is a saw.** Niko goes up and down 241 steps eleven times and cuts at an oak bar that will not come out of its sockets because oak dry in a door frame for thirty years has shrunk half an inch at each end and set like iron. Tarin goes up and comes down eleven times, and on the fifth sits on the fourteenth step and works out that if the boy puts his hand on that saw the way he put his hand on a coil of rope on the second, they will be in a hole for three days. Wick goes up once and down once, two hours up and four down, with his right hand and a wall, and says afterwards it was the easiest thing he had done in nine years, and nobody believes him and everybody lets it stand. **The bar comes out at ten past four.** The door opens into a filter-bed store with a cold hearth and a shelf of candle ends, and a girl of about fourteen in the lane with two pails and a windlass, waiting, because she has heard a saw. *You'll be the pit. You're the pit they said had water in it.* **Ashgate: eleven houses, thirty-four persons, thirty years, called Ashgate by the people in it, and on every sheet in the city Ashgate is a structure with a closure date and a warden's seal.** **Nell Drust, 39,** has kept the row for sixteen years and what she keeps is the water, and the water comes up out of the ground under her own kitchen floor and has always done. There is no pump and there has never been a pump. *I have four in this row who cannot be got to the far well and the far well is dry.* **The measurement, checked twice:** six gallons on the well-head slab in three minutes thirty seconds, so 1.71 gal a minute, **103 gallons an hour, 2,466 a day.** Her verdict, and the line the batch is built around: *there is more coming up out of that hole than thirty-four of us can use in a month, and you have been telling me that as though it were a discovery. It is not a discovery. It is a kitchen.* **Coll Preece, about fifty, four years on the pump, not frightened of anybody:** he said *you will be the Water Office, and you will be the wrong week* to see what a man with a lamp would do, and the man went down a hole, and the office shut the plant on the Wednesday with no letter, and the horse went out of the basin on the Thursday. *I have got a ledger of cans and I get paid by the can, and there is a name on the certificate, and I have never met him and I do not believe he knows this hill exists.* **The store, the deal box, two sheets:** a certificate of condition certifying the chamber void at decommissioning, and the same certificate four years later, both in the same pen, both with *maintained* beneath, and thirty yards away a chalk board that says 5·9. **The entry, four lines, because a third charge is forming in his coat:** the chamber is not void, a plant has been pumping out of it for four years against a certificate that says there is nothing in it, that is abstraction out of a stored head, a stored head that is not on a schedule is in no allocation, and *I cannot do one single thing about any of the four lines above, because the only man in this city who can act on it has signed both pieces of paper, and he is not standing in this lane.* Nell Drust makes him a cup of tea and it has no taste in it and it has had none since the seventh of the month, and he drinks all of it and says nothing to anybody then or afterwards. **The chapter's last two paragraphs are its whole argument:** nobody put thirty-four people on top of a chamber, there was never an order, there was a works and then a closure and then empty houses and then people and then a well and then thirty years, and *at no point in any of it had a single person decided to do any of it to anybody.*

### Chapter 0026 — The Word Discharge (Yara Sen, the 9th, the yard and the dispensary)
The cost chapter, and the physician gets in first. She stands in front of the yard at about eight and does it before anybody can ask her a question, *because a physician who answers a question has already lost the ground.* The cistern is **three feet four inches** down and a woman who has been selling out of it for thirty-one years is feeling for the bottom with her arm, because she has stopped trusting her arm. **The word she puts on the slate under the tank is *discharge*** — not a leak, which is a fault and gets mended, but water somebody is moving on purpose from one somewhere to another, and the people at the end of it are not the people who started it. *Every drop that has come into this lane since the first day of the month has come off a works pump two miles up the mill road… it is not ours.* The yard asks its four questions and none of them is the one she would have chosen: a landlord and whether he can read; whether to go on selling; *will they do to this lane what they did in the spring*; and Old Marden, at eighty-one, asking the correct one — **is this water stopped or diverted?** *It is not a matter of luck and I have watched four men this month go silent rather than say that out loud.* Marda Ollan writes it on the second slate without being asked. **The arithmetic, in public, on a slate, and it goes against him:** **1,950 gallons on the evening of the ninth is twenty-seven days at seventy a day — and it is not twenty-seven days of what is in it.** *I have not been waiting for a tank to fix it, because it cannot.* **Two hundred and forty gallons**, and the day is a Saturday, and nobody decides today. Then the dispensary, last, because Bel Crale made it last. Seven of nine holding, which is *stationary*, which is a finding and not a comfort. **Sab Doulton, seven, of the second step** — at three points on the 28th, on the 6th, and again this morning, three times in a fortnight, each for longer; the skin of her palms cracked; sick twice since Tuesday; *has asked her mother twice this week where the front door is.* **The beginning of the fourth stage, and the formula stops:** *a child in the fourth stage does not take four gallons. I do not know what it takes. There is no figure for it in this basin.* And then the sentence she has been not saying for two days, put in a yard in daylight so nobody can say afterwards that a physician kept it back: there are thirty-four people two miles up the hill whose water comes out of their own kitchen floor at about a hundred gallons an hour, and if you asked her this morning whether the nine children in this lane should drink it she would have to say yes, and *I have a right to that answer only if somebody who is not a physician asks me for it and not if I go and take it.* A man near the arch says he would ask. *I know you would, and I have said it where he can hear me, and that is what daylight is for.*

### Chapter 0027 — A Named Applicant (Mara Quill, Friday the 11th, the reading room, Vellum)
The reading day, and it succeeds, and it does not give him what he came for. The four shillings came out of a bucket in the blue steps. **The desk asks name, purpose, and second reader, in that order, to everybody.** Tarin's purpose is eleven words, and Mara Quill refuses to help him write it, because in eleven years somebody will want to know who put the words in a box. *To establish the recorded condition of the Ashgate intake main wheel.* **She cannot be the second reader** — she is the officer whose initials are on the shelf-marker — and Kreft, who is right, says she is correct and should not have said it, because rule nine applied to a contested volume and then staffed by the officer who contested it is a rule that has stopped working without anybody noticing. **Senna Rull, second reader, nineteen years, no connection to the case**, and her one rule before she touches anything: a paper of this age is not improved by anybody being gentle with it; it is a piece of rag somebody made wet and beat and dried, and it wants to be *held*, in both hands, off the table, and turned. The volume: one hundred and eighty-one years, four entries a year, **seven hundred and twenty-four leaves**, rebound twice and once badly. Every entry says four things — date, gate shut and dogged, head at wheel, chamber void — and the head moves within a hand's breadth across a century and three quarters. **Tarin's find in the gate column:** the same chain through the same spokes, the same padlock, the same hand, four times a year for a hundred and eighty-one years, with a rust mark of the same shape on four leaves, one in the first year of the log and one in the last. *Nobody has opened that gate since the flood, and I opened it on the first day of this month with a bar, and every single thing in this book is true about every single leaf in it except the last one.* **The last line:** head 5·89, chamber *see ref*, and beneath it in a second heavier hand **maintained**. Mara Quill read the shadow of that line on the second of the month and said she would swear to five and eight and was right to; **she has never been in a room with the other three words.** Four minutes with a lamp and a press-glass, because a shadow of a number is a shadow of the pressure of a number: **ref. 118.** **The check, made by two people in two buildings out of two public documents:** entry 118 of the closure schedule of the lower terrace road — terrace row, lower fold, rows 7 to 12, outfalls sealed and plated, no key issued, out of service, removed from the distribution schedule by consequential correction, dated twenty-six years ago, **the same entry carrying the removal of the row from the sheet.** *Then it is one act. Not two documents that resemble each other. One entry, in one schedule, in one year, in one office, and it plated a row's drain and it took a hundred and four names off a survey and it took the last line out of a maintenance book.* **And Tarin, in a public room, refusing the easy version:** *That is a date and a number and a hand and a piece of paper. It is not a source… I have got the condition of an office. That is what the volume had in it.* **The certificate, four sentences, read out, countersigned by Rull**, recording the existence, form and date of a passage and a reference and **not transcribing the reference**, comparable against a public document. And the sentence about the hand, which is the one sentence a conservator writes that nobody who can read can falsify, and which therefore puts a registrar onto a man of forty-one who is her mother's brother — filed anyway, because the registry's marginal note of a year out was against a provenance note about two documents and *this is not that.* **Then the register**, and the best beat in the chapter: Tarin misstates his own district as **one hundred and twenty-four houses and one hundred and nineteen persons**, and Mara Quill catches it — *that is not what you have just said to me* — and he understands in a public room that he has gone back to a number a man invents about a place that is not on a sheet, and asks for the correction in the same hand in the same book, and the clerk strikes it out with one stroke, writes *so entered at the applicant's correction, applicant present*, and turns the book round so he can read both entries, which no clerk in nineteen years has ever done for anybody.

### Chapter 0028 — Two Hundred And Forty Gallons (Hanne Roeg, Saturday the 12th, the yard and the fourth step)
First Hanne chapter, and she is not a monument: she is seventy-one, tired, vain about her tracing, frightened of an examiner, and she has a grandson of ten at a school in the fenner district who does not know any of this is going on. She calls the yard for eleven and does not have to call it — a hundred and thirty people are there before she asks — and she works out what that is: *it is not being counted. It is being spoken to at eleven o'clock on a Saturday by a person who cannot be spoken back to.* She makes them wait four minutes, because a thing asked for is a thing given and a thing waited for is a thing owed. **Three things, in the wrong order on purpose.** Water first, because a yard that argues about paper for two hours while a tank goes down has already decided what it is: *seven pounds sixteen, and the last two men who came up this road would not come into the lane at all. They would come to the cattle track and stand there. So the money in the bucket has no seller. That is the thing I want said first, because everybody has been doing that sum as though there were a man at the other end of it, and there is not, and it is not anybody's fault and it is not a market because I said no to a market on the thirty-first and I meant it.* **Marda's corrected ladder** — £8 at 8d is 240 gallons, at 9d is 213, at a shilling is 160 — and then the discovery that stops the yard cold: **a cart holds forty gallons**, so 240 gallons is six cart-loads and two days each way, and the money was never the binding constraint. **Bel Crale, with a piece of paper, against herself:** *I do not want to spend it… and I am going to spend it, because on the twenty-seventh a man is going to come down this track with a form and one of the boxes says water supply in connection, and if the answer to that box is a cistern and a jar and a barrow and eight pounds then this lane is a place where the water comes in and goes straight out again.* Yara Sen refuses the arithmetic of singling out one child: *if you spend the whole of the eight pounds on the Doulton child you have told every other family in this lane what a child is worth in gallons.* **The second thing is the letter, and Hanne has costed it.** To Pell, not to the commissioner's office, because a warden can only return a thing in a form on a date and because in nineteen years nobody has found one word of his made up. It names the chamber, the head, the row above, the eleven houses and thirty-four persons, the certificate — and asks for the examination to be brought forward **and** for a structural examination, in a paragraph that says in its own words that the warden's office has no power to order it, *because a document that asks for what cannot be given is a document that cannot be refused for asking too much.* **And the price, in a yard, in daylight, from her:** three documents in this city now carry the name of this lane, and there were none on the 22nd, and the third column of the examiner's form will be the only thing any of them has ever wanted. And then the sentence nobody expected: *if that box gets filled in this lane stops being a place he can be told he came from and becomes a thing in a report, and there is no road back off that, and I would rather you knew that about me in a yard than found it out.* **The third thing she leaves until four in the afternoon**, because it is the only one she cannot decide alone: asking thirty-four people for their water. *It will mean that the first thing a person in that row ever hears from this city is a knock on their door from a district with no row, asking for the thing they have never had to share… and if the office ever finds out, the row is finished, and eleven houses are finished, and it will not be our fault and it will not matter whose fault it was.* She puts it in the letter as a question in her own hand: *whether a district with no row may ask a settlement with no row for water.* **The third sheet, which is not the letter, and which costs her most:** the fourth step's record, in her own hand, that on the fifth three men went without her knowledge or consent, that she refused them on the first with six reasons and does not withdraw one, that she did not stop them, that it was his decision and not an accident of the morning, and that she keeps the second tracing and will make any man from Vellum sit down on her step for it. A woman says afterwards that it is a shame, and is told twice, patiently, that it is a record and not a thing to be sorry for. **£4 at a shilling for eighty gallons, entered in this book, entered in this book.**

### Chapter 0029 — What A Man Can Still Do (Wick Venn, the 13th and 14th, the cattle track and the dispensary)
The recovery chapter, and its thesis is in the first line of work: **a cart is two wheels, a shaft and no grip, and steering with a rein in a hand that cannot feel is not a matter of grip.** The road crowns at a mile and a half and a cart works and a dead hand does not know it, and he gets the last three hundred yards to the fenner turn by pushing it with his shoulder. He stands at the lodge of the Reach waterworks in the dark and works out the whole of his nine years: Osgar Pike's journeyman's paper, offered in a doorway nine years ago and never withdrawn, and the door frame, and a carter, and a sheet. Then he knocks on another door, because there is a more urgent thing. **Errol Slee**, the water cart, who stopped on the thirtieth because a man's speech in the road told him the price was going to three: *I did not send you away on the thirtieth.* And what he says about having a row of his own and no basin in his yard this month, and Wick Venn's answer, which is nothing: *I will not argue with you in your own doorway at six in the morning, and if it is wrong you can have it for what it is worth and I will not say a word about it afterwards either.* **The eightieth gallon is in the dispensary by nine on the evening of the 13th**, entered in four lines and then in the column Bel Crale made for it, with *entered in this book* and the note that it was not drawn from the district's tank and no worse for it. Yara: *it buys four days.* **Niko comes into the dispensary at half past eight and stands in the doorway and does not come in**, counts the points on the child with his own fingers, and says the thing the whole batch has been walking towards: his aunt kept a low pump for nine years and when she died there was nothing to hand on, because there was no *name* anywhere, so the pump stopped being a pump and became a hole in a wall with a handle on it. *So that is what I am going to be. Not an apprentice in the Water Office.* And then, against his own instinct, he does not want it written down: *that is the whole difference between me and you and it is why you are the man on the record and I am not… I will say it to you, and you will remember it, and if it turns out to be a load of nonsense then I will be twenty-three and able to be embarrassed about it myself.* Wick offers a slate and a chalk in the yard and is refused, because it is a rule in this lane and he is not breaking it with a thing nobody asked for. **Yara enters it anyway, in her own book, in the small writing** — because this book is the only thing in the district that will not be lost, and a thing remembered by one man is a thing that dies with him. Closing state: the tank at **950 gallons, one foot seven inches**, two slates of figures on the low house step, the word *discharge* in the lane, the struck correction in a register in Vellum, and Hanne Roeg's sheet in a bag on a cart going up the cattle track.

### Chapter 0030 — What A Warden Writes Down (Tarin and Pell, Tuesday the 15th, the yard)
Pell comes down the cattle track on a Tuesday because he writes to the district book on the Tuesday, and a hundred and forty people stand in the yard from eleven until three, which is the largest a yard in the blue steps has ever been and which nobody arranged. **He says three things before he reads anything, and the third is the price:** a warden's office cannot inspect a sealed works, cannot order a structural examination and cannot enter a district; he is going to read it out because a warden's entry nobody has heard read is a scrap of paper in a book; **and it is going up, and there is nowhere above him except the commissioner's office, which is the office that sealed the works six years ago and holds the file on the engineer whose name is in the letter** — and he wants the yard to hear that before it decides how it feels. He reads Hanne's letter out, all eleven minutes of it, without editorialising, and slows on the fourth paragraph, which is about a woman who had already refused a hole in a hill in writing and put the refusal in a city document on purpose. **Then the form — the same form as the 22nd, five printed boxes: row number, keeper, tanks, gallons, last serviced — and he fills in two of them and leaves three empty.** Filled: *row number*, entered as **none held**, and *tanks*. Empty: **keeper, gallons, last serviced.** The remark of about four hundred words is read aloud and is the chapter's spine: the condition, the head, the settlement, and then the sentence the whole chapter turns on — **there is no box headed *time* on the form at all, a warden enters the time a condition begins and this warden was not present and will not enter a time he did not witness, and this form has no box headed time, and it is the only box he has ever wanted in it**, so any reader may take the entry as a statement that nobody in this city knows what hour of what day a district of a hundred and twenty-six persons started to stand on top of a chamber of water. **The other two empties are one reason and not two:** a *gallons* box can hold only one of the cistern, the dispensary and the daily draw, so putting 950 in it would be true and a lie; *last serviced* is a date a keeper writes and this office has no keeper. And a line nobody had put there: **the Day 1 notice with the empty cause box is the reason the condition could be entered at all — and he puts his own six days on the record next to it**, reported to his office on the 9th, entered on the 15th, fourteen days from the notice. **Nobody notices the empty boxes for four minutes and then Tam Roke does, and a voice at the arch says *then put somebody in it*, and Roke's answer is the fastest thing in the chapter: *No. And it is not going to be me, and I would like it entered that I have said that in front of a hundred and forty people and not two.*** Then Tarin's **engineer's statement, six numbered paragraphs, on the low house step**, which is the first document he has signed since the 10th and it is thirty-six days: the head is 5·89, **read once off a pressure shadow on the 1st, and the plant's own board has carried 5·9 for two years, and he has had no way and no authority to put a gauge on that wheel since the 2nd**; the chamber is intact; the pump has been stopped since the second and was run for four years; eleven houses and thirty-four persons on the roof and a well at a hundred gallons an hour out of it that has never been tested and that he cannot promise will be the same water tomorrow; **I cannot make water come to this district**, and the only supply it has had since the first has been a contractor's discharge stopped by a person he has not met; four people asked to put a hand on a route, four refusals, three of them right, and he will not argue with the fourth either. Pell takes it and does not ask for it back and does not offer to certify it. **Where it goes:** up with the condition, into the district book and the commissioner's registry, and in about nine days a third copy in a building in Vellum, and in six weeks a fourth somewhere he cannot reach. *Which is the office that holds two certificates in a deal box thirty yards from a chalk board, and which will receive this, and which is the reason I have written in the remark that I have no power to inspect and no power to enter a district, because a man is not obliged to act on a condition and I would like that on the record with my name at the bottom of it.* **And the closing beat, which is the volume's whole method coming back round:** he explains that a keeper is not a hero but a name in a box that a man with a form can come and find, and that nobody has to answer him now. He names what the office has been wrong about and refuses the one thing that is not his — **the hand lost in the small hours of Sunday morning on a mill road was lost on a rope on an engineer's descent, it was not this office and it was not him, and he will not pretend he would have stopped it** — and turns it into the chapter's own argument: *if there had been a name in that box a fortnight ago there would have been a person in this district a man with a form could have come and found, and I do not know what that would have been worth, and I am not going to stand in this yard and tell you it would have saved a hand.* And that he is going to write **keeper, none held** in the empty box, in his own fair hand, in a book that four people in this city have copies of, *so that a man who comes down that track in six weeks with a form is going to find those two words, in a fair hand, in a fair book, and he is going to know that a district of a hundred and twenty-six persons looked at a box and left it empty on purpose and not because nobody noticed.*
