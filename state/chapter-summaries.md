## Volume 01 — The Empty Gauge

### Chapter 0001 — The Gauge at Ashgate (Tarin, Day 1, dawn, Ashgate works)
First measurable rain in fourteen months. Tarin climbs the sealed works three hours early to recalibrate a decommissioned float gauge before the monthly warden check. The float pins at the top stop; the stilling tube is live from below. Head above the gate seat: **5·89 m**. He forces the seized inspection gate with a bar and his shoulder and the residual head runs **uphill** down the gallery outlet channel, a channel laid at a foot in the hundred, dry and furred with six years of dust. He cannot shut it: the main wheel is loaded and a foot of head in the intake channel. He writes *by bar, at 06:11, before the warden's check* in his own hand. The kit's brass float comes out of the water with a grey gritty bloom that is not rust. He walks the covered terrace run downhill and finds it emerging at a low arch on the lower fold where the warden's sheet says *level four, no settlement beyond* — and hears a door being worked on behind it. He turns back up the hill to get the row sheet.

### Chapter 0002 — The Warden's Road (Tarin, Day 1, midday, warden's station)
Pell will not act on a story, only on a condition. Tarin is made to give the head, the gate state, and the direction, and the giving of it puts his own opening in a document. Pell cannot find a receiving district on any sheet he owns. The plate arrives as a work fact on a schedule: outfall **14**, *terrace overflow, row 7–12, lower fold — SEALED, PLATED, NO KEY ISSUED, OUT OF SERVICE*, closure entry 118, dated **twenty-six years**, the same entry that removed the row from the distribution sheet. No keeper, no key, no service window. Tarin takes the notice of uncontrolled channel condition, reads the empty **cause** box, and does not write in it — a decision, not a slip. He asks Pell for an hour and walks the line instead. Niko Venn, his apprentice, tells him the fold has people in it whom nobody has ever asked about.

### Chapter 0003 — A District of Blue Doors (Tarin, Day 1, late afternoon, Glassstep)
The water has nowhere to go. The row is occupied, washing, a woman beating a mat, a cistern filling to a third and stopping. Tarin names the cause in his working voice: the yard's outfall is a plate of iron three-eighths thick over four fist-sized bolts, so a courtyard cistern is the only vessel the district has. He gives Hanne Roeg his own name and his father's, and tells her what happened to the branch in his own hand, and asks for help. He is a stranger to the row he was born in. A Water Office runner catches him with requisition eighteen: the Memory Office has exhibited the Ashgate maintenance volume, which says the gate was shut before the Ninefold Flood and dry for a hundred and eighty-one years, and the commissioner has put Tarin's name against it.

### Chapter 0004 — The Weight of a Leaf (Mara, Day 2, morning, Municipal Memory Office, Vellum)
Routine re-binding of folio eleven. One leaf is a different weight, a different sizing, and has no watermark. Raked light shows a **pressure shadow** on the reverse: an intake head at the main wheel, gate state shut, *5·8_*, dated before the flood, and — in a second, heavier hand — **maintained**. The reading is real and the page has been removed and replaced. Mara refuses to certify, enters the discrepancy in the requisition book in her own hand, and keeps working after hours; the supervisor notes her hours. She reads the missing figure as **5·89** under raking light and a press-glass mirror. The requisition goes to the Water Office with the figure attached, and Cresset will pick the engineer who was nearest.

### Chapter 0005 — Two Professionals, One File (Tarin and Mara, Day 5, morning, provenance room, Vellum)
Batch midpoint. Three days of delay on purpose, and a day and a night of mountain road each way. She suspects he altered a record; he suspects she will seal a working route under cover of historical accuracy. He shows her his notebook — **5·89 m**, measured at 06:11 on the first — knowing it hangs him. She tells him the mechanism: not a forged number but a *true* one lifted off the original leaf so a clean leaf could say *chamber void*, with the second-hand word *maintained* under it. He confesses the notice with the empty cause box; she calls it a complaint with a route in it. In the lower room she shows him the cracked Ashgate record stone, carted down six years ago and left on a *pending disposal* slip. He puts his hand on it. **First Gauge statement:** *One route remembers. It asks for a witness.* Incomplete (does not say which route) and slightly wrong (the stone has two cut lines and he counts one; the request is given to the water rather than to the person). A second pressure sits underneath it, unnamed, with a mundane explanation available. He perceives and can do nothing. The hook: the only witnesses to Ashgate live in a district that does not legally exist.

### Chapter 0006 — The Low Pump on Glassstep (Tarin, Day 7, dawn, Glassstep low pump)
**First reach of Name.** He writes the requisition answer on the coach overnight and puts 5·89 in a second document, knowingly. He hears the low pump knocking under the flags at night — a pumping plant that is on no sheet he has ever signed. With Wick he digs out the blind branch and its stop-gate. **Niko Venn** knows which stones on the terrace are markers and which are only stones, brings the chisel, and beats Tarin at his own district; he refuses, out loud, to be Tarin's reason. Half a mile down the lane Tarin considers and **refuses** to use his own private memory of the row as an anchor, because a private memory is not a public record and nobody offered. **Tam Roke**, who keeps the low pump, is asked to witness and is told the full terms, including what Tarin does not know. Residents argue the name; Hanne Roeg supplies *Glassstep*. Tarin re-cuts the weathered face of the pump's marker stone with the borrowed chisel; Roke cuts a crooked mark. The water stops fighting and runs. Roke's mother drinks and says: *That is not our water.*

### Chapter 0007 — What the Water Brought With It (Tarin with Yara Sen, Day 7, daytime)
**Salt fever is physical, fast, and caused by badly addressed water.** Yara Sen finds early salt fever in three children and says to his face that the crosscurrent is his opening's consequence. She shows the three diagnostic points on the pad of the fingers. He confesses the cause before she proves it — the route was opened with no legitimacy condition, and the people it runs past were not in the decision. He closes the branch at 11:20 in front of the row, and tells Tam Roke it was his doing and not the water's, on Hanne Roeg's instruction. **First name debt, minor:** the two-finger press he has used since he was thirteen — the tap, on spindles, floats, and page edges — is gone, and his hand will not make the notation he has made ten thousand times. His bitter tea tastes of nothing.

### Chapter 0008 — The Replaced Leaf Has a Cousin (Mara, Day 9, after hours, restricted stack, Vellum)
Same day the low pump goes dry on the count Tarin gave Yara. Court-supply ink dates by nib cut, gum, and the **issue stamped on the tin lid**. The tin is the same batch for the Ashgate replacement leaf and for the Glassstep survey redactions — a batch issued **twenty-six years** back, from a tin the Office has never had cause to empty. Same hand, same ink, one hand in service across the whole period. The Glassstep redactions strike out one hundred and four household names; the Ashgate leaf removed true pressure figures and set a clean leaf in their place. Mara draws a one-to-one key of both in her own ink, and **does not enter it in the hand-drawing register** — choosing to be the only person in the basin who can read the evidence on condition that nobody can check her. The hand belongs to the Commissioner's registry and to her mother's brother, who was nineteen at her mother's table when she learned to read it. Vellum's district return shows the terrace cisterns at twelve days and the low pump not running.

### Chapter 0009 — The Commissioner's Kindness (Tarin, Day 10, morning, Water Office)
Halden Roe is gracious, exact, and better at this than Tarin is. He offers North Sallow pumping district — a real post, nine hundred and forty properties, a finished cistern programme — before he says anything else. He reads Tarin's own log back to him. The case: the certified discrepancy shows a figure identical to one a hundred and eighty-one years old, therefore Tarin's log must be a copy, therefore the copying is the tampering. The blank-cause notice of the first is in the file. Tarin refuses the transfer and **accuses Roe of falsification in front of Deryn Fenn and Osla Vint** — that the decommissioning certificates and the gauge certificate are Roe's own work over six years, and that a pumping plant in the old filter house has been running at night for the better part of a year, and that a dead reservoir has no lawful allocation. **Charge one, filed and read on Day 10: tampering with city records.** Stand-down, seal, keys, district reference and desk taken; he keeps the blue book, the bar, the rule, the chalk, the tin pot.

### Chapter 0010 — One Name, One Window (Tarin, Day 20, 15:40–03:00, Glassstep courtyard)
Batch climax. Ten days of suspension, a letter refused by the Memory Office, nineteen of twenty-three people asked to stand on a stone saying no, and Niko naming a descent into the old filter house that no survey records. Tam Roke leaves a gauged length of oak at the wall. Pell arrives at 15:40 with a clerk and leaves the right-hand column of his entry book blank, because a warden records the time a condition **begins**. Yara Sen states the charge aloud before he reaches the stone. **He cannot cut his own name on the blank face** — the lost two-finger press from Chapter 7 — so the naming has to be spoken. **Second Gauge statement:** *The stone is blank. Two hands are on it.* He works out the rule himself, aloud, where the yard can argue: a witness who cannot walk away is not a witness, and the second hand has to belong to somebody free to refuse. Roke refuses, out loud, and is right to. He lays out the terms: no second hand, the charge, salt fever, the plate, the empty cause box, the arithmetic (**6,700 gallons of vessel, 610 gallons an hour, eleven hours**), and that it stops by itself at the balance point. A man walks out. Then Tarin says the name. **16:00** — the water settles and runs. Pell's entry is timed and dated. **18:00** — the warden's men carry him out; Pell tells him there will be a **second charge, distinct and unjoined**. He tells the yard plainly: *I do not know where it comes from.* At **03:00** a nine-year-old walks up to the second gatehouse to say it stopped, and that it was not his. The run is dry, the cisterns are full, and the plate under the yard is ticking.

### Chapter 0011 — A Yard Full Of Water (Tarin, Day 21, dawn to late morning, the blue steps and the warden's station)
The stand-down order is nailed to the second gatehouse wall, two numbered charges on one sheet of paper, the hearing set for the 22nd and adjourned without a date. Down in the lane the row has put the water out — 38 pails along the low house wall, the cistern at its coping, washing on the line. Tarin walks the vessels with a stick and gets 6,700 gallons, the same figure as the 20th, and understands that the window bought a cistern with no headroom in it. **Yara Sen's finding:** the cistern is the worst, the fourth pail the best, evaporation is the only good news; it is not fit to drink and not fit to wash in, and the row has been living on two ounces a head a day since the 21st. Old Marden asks how long the cistern has left with nobody drinking it: a hundred and forty days. Yara says the row is not short of water, it is short of water it is allowed to give anybody. A district-return runner comes down with a form with four boxes — row number, keeper, tanks, gallons — and cannot fill any of them, and takes it back blank, having written on it: *if there is no row, then no row can ever be wrong.* **Pell** comes down on foot and states the whole of his power: he can enter a condition, not a district, and offers a return with blank boxes and a filled remark, knowing it does nothing. Tarin writes his list — water, head, trace, record, keeper, witness, standing — and sees that six of the seven lines are ticked. Hanne Roeg, from the fourth step: *This row is not a row.* And then: **"Then we will be a row."** Asked who signs: *"Then one of us signs it."*

### Chapter 0012 — A Named Representative (Tarin and the lane, Day 22, warden's station and the dispensary step)
Pell reads out the six numbered boxes of an application for entry of a district row: name, representative and household, houses and persons, water works maintained, source of supply, date of continuous maintenance. Four shillings, not refundable, paid by Bel Crale. **The count is taken by the district itself** (Marda Ollan on a slate, a day and a half, door by door) because a man under a stand-down may not take a census: **twenty-four houses, one hundred and twenty-six persons**, with two people entered at the margin as declining enumeration, one of whom holds a ration card in the Fenner district. Tarin objects twice and is refused twice. His first objection is the correct one: a name on that form is a name in the commissioner's registry by the end of the week. Hanne Roeg's answer: *Then in eleven months we will be a thing that can be refused, instead of a thing that cannot be.* She refuses to be made a brave old woman: four signers, she is the oldest and the only one with a legible hand. **Twenty-two sign, nineteen asked and said no**, and both numbers are read out twice into the card. Box two is rewritten at Hanne's insistence to add *answerable for the truth of it, and not having been put forward by any office in this city.* Box five states the source truthfully — a branch off the Ashgate intake at 5·89 m through a channel cut by Rusk, Tarin, named on a stone, at a gate the city ledger records as shut — which puts the engineer and his charge into a public document. She refuses to let him add *source not known to the district*. Pell warns that the application will get a file number, that the file number is a handle, and that the answer will come from the commissioner's office and not from him. Marda Ollan signs and says she has made herself the easiest person in the row to find.

### Chapter 0013 — The Shelf-Marker (Mara, Day 23, morning, lower room and reading supervisor's office)
Folio eleven is gone from the ninth slot. The **shelf-marker** says *ASHGATE INTAKE, MAINTENANCE SERIES, FOLIO 11. WITHDRAWN. R. QUE.* with the date **17/4** under it — six days ago, while she was in the low court at the Fair Copy. **Hollis Kreft** invokes **rule nine** of the reading rules: an unverified record may not decide a public route. He is right, and he is also the mechanism, and he has done the rule *publicly*: the monthly finding aid now reads, in the public reading room, *contains one substituted leaf, uncertified* — Mara's finding converted into an Office posture, in the Office's hand, without her name. She gets no thanks and no argument. Kreft then tells her to go and get a second instance independently, somewhere it is not this building. She writes out what one would have to be: the same substitution of a whole leaf, the same institutional correction wording from a different archive, the same ink from a dated tin. The only survey originals of the blue steps in the basin are in the blue steps — the field book went to the house the surveyor lodged in, and her mother told her the name of that house when she was eleven: *the Roegs have the book, and mind it.* She takes the **legitimate route** — a quarterly **collation** warrant, five boxes, eleven words of purpose — and Kreft signs it and records that she proposed it herself, which is the fourth note in her file this quarter. The district application comes down with the morning's tray and the second box stops her dead. She copies box two and box five into the **public** requisition book, with a footnote that the applicant's consent to the copy is not held.

### Chapter 0014 — Nineteen Said No (Tarin, Day 25, all day, up and down the lane)
Tarin builds a witness list properly: name, exactly what each person would be answerable for, date, and a ruled line for a mark. Every refusal is sound. **Wick** took the door frame down on the first and the row has held it against him since, and he works the Reach six days a week. **Bel Crale refuses and explains why the dispensary has been unfindable for a year** — *illegible* is a wall, thirty years thick, and it is why forty-one pounds has lasted. Tarin gives her his word, twice, that the dispensary will not be on the list. **Old Marden asks for his name to be cut in two**: a witness to where the water comes from, not to what it does. Tarin discovers that a partial witness is a category. **Marda Ollan signs** with four conditions, including *you write the actual date under my name*. **The Tall boy signs** as the one who got lost on the seventh. And the **boy of nine** asks to sign, and Tarin refuses, and says why on the page: a witness must be able to answer for ten years, and a route resting on a nine-year-old is a route resting on a family. The turn: all four conditions, stated five ways by four people who have not met, are the same condition — *I will not be the second hand for a route I cannot stop.* That is Roke's reason from the twentieth, and it has walked round the lane by itself. Tarin understands that the missing second hand is not a neighbour but **the other end of the pipe**, which is up a sealed hill behind a chained wheel, and every man between pays a wage out of the office that charged him twice. Niko makes him say the money out loud: no authority, no money, no crew.

### Chapter 0015 — Six Thousand Seven Hundred (Yara Sen, Day 27, six in the morning to five in the afternoon, dispensary and yard)
Yara's own book: forty-one names, nine with the marks, four at all three points, eleven at two, and a column of her own invention headed *at risk*. The dispensary's whole clean supply is eleven gallons of rain in a stone jar. She argues the six thousand seven hundred gallons out in the open, in four pieces. **The crust:** a jar boiled down to a finger of white ring; boiling takes water out and leaves the salt, so it makes it worse per gallon. **The skin:** touch the wet stone inside the lid, then look at the pads of your fingers — a day early, the same three points, and there is no washing in it and no drinking it. **Cooking:** boiled, kept out of cups, the best the row has, and the arithmetic has changed. **The arithmetic:** four children in the second stage at four gallons a day, eleven at a gallon, a hundred and fourteen people to keep alive — about **seventy gallons a day for a fortnight**, and there is no figure in this basin for it: a cart twice a week off the Sallow road at twopence, and Bel has only ever been able to afford seventy gallons a *week*, and that is the whole of the reason those four children are where they are. And the sixth thousand seven hundred is eleven times what they need and they cannot touch a drop. A woman at the back names it: *it is not a cistern, it is a cistern with a hose on it.* Yara agrees and explains that there is no route to move it and no row to move it on. **She files the case** — nine cases, dated findings, in a district with no schedule entry — and says out loud, to Bel Crale, that filing will bring a district examiner and that the examiner's form has a box saying *water supply in connection*, and Bel Crale says do it now, with the water on the table, and *do it deliberately and do not do it by accident.* A man from the Fenner district walks four miles with a barrow, is given a pail, and Yara tells him plainly in front of nine people that it is not drinking water and not wash water and cannot be mixed because he has no water to mix it with. He asks what it is for. Nobody answers.

### Chapter 0016 — A Hundred And Four Names (Mara, Day 28, half past eight to six, Glassstep, with Tarin from five)
Mara arrives on a **collation warrant** with three rules written on the back of her hand, the third being *do not leave this lane with anything in your hand that only you can read.* Hanne Roeg tells her at the door that the field book is not hers — it is the lane's, and there is a difference. Forty people stand round a trestle table and look at two pieces of paper about themselves, and being allowed to look is the only thing Mara has that nobody has given her. She reads the fair copy against the field book aloud. Then the trouble: twenty-six years under a floor has foxed the folio and taken the ink *into* the fibres, so **a stroke-for-stroke comparison is impossible** and she will not pretend otherwise. She can give batch, margin, schedule reference and the fact that it is one hand — the pattern, not the hand. Marda Ollan points out that an uncheckable claim about a hundred and four houses is what Mara has spent four days refusing to be. Old Marden solves it: make it checkable with one. **Hanne Roeg offers the fourth step** — one name, read aloud, in a yard, by the fourth step herself, on a date. Mara reads the hundred and four. At the eleventh an old man says *that's mine* — the second house, his mother's — and at the fourteenth Hanne says *mine* and Mara stops both times and writes down that she stopped. Then nine thousand separate little agreements of the retained properties, all in the open, twice corrected by people in the crowd. **The two tracings:** Mara makes one, then hands the second sheet and the pen to Hanne Roeg and does not dictate it, and the old woman writes a hundred and four house numbers in her own hand over four hours. Mara certifies both as made from one original in one room in one sitting by two named hands. *That is a second attestation. It is not proof.* Hanne Roeg is now a custodian of a record. The **Tarin–Mara agreement**, on the dispensary step in front of nine people: neither uses the other's method in public; **nothing goes on paper with one name on it** (she catches him spotting her own Chapter 8 decision in four seconds and neither pretends otherwise); neither takes a private memory without an answer that is not silence; neither produces the other's evidence without telling them first; they disagree in public and the disagreement is recorded. Neither gets what they want. She will not write down the sentence Rusk wants — *the district's legal existence and the restricted folio are the same object* — because a sentence in a conservator's hand is not a record. The coda: the certificate's hand-characteristic is the only sentence she writes that is not falsifiable by anybody who can read, and reading it means the commissioner's registry will examine a man who is her mother's brother and who was nineteen at her mother's table. She could file the weaker certificate before Friday. She files the real one on the fourth, and writes to her mother.

### Chapter 0017 — What There Was Room For (Tarin, Day 30, four in the morning to half past two, the blue steps)
The arithmetic is on the back of a ward notice: 6,700 gallons of vessel, 610 gallons an hour at the pin found on the 19th, eleven hours. And under it, in his own hand, on the 30th: **there is no room, so make the hole.** He wakes Wick and gives him the whole of it at four in the morning, including the price: 4,700 gallons is going to come out of the bottom of the cistern into a lane with no drain, over a plate, and he cannot work out a way of not paying it. Wick comes anyway, with a bar, and will not be a keeper and will not hold the branch. The draw-off comes out at nine minutes past four and runs for two hours. The plate is still ticking, unchanged, and he writes *unchanged* twice. At ten past six he sets the log at the pin, puts both palms on the stone, and says **"Glassstep low pump branch,"** and **the water does not change** — no bed going quiet, no third of a sound dropping away, and the channel starts to cut. He offers the branch his own private memory of the lane, standing in it, and **nothing takes it**, because a private memory is not a public record and nobody offered: he is on the wrong side of the rule he worked out on the seventh. He tries the blank face with the chisel and cannot seat it, and writes *06:15. Set chisel, blank face, second attempt. Hand will not seat. Name not cut. Blank face not cut.* Then the compounding error: the tank fills in **3 h 10 min** against a computed 7 h 40. He has the number and does not use it — when the branch opened on the 20th the run's sound dropped by a third, so the branch was taking a third of a run he has never measured, which comes into the yard through an eighteen-inch lead pipe half silted, at a rate nobody has ever had a reason to find. The tank is filling twice as fast. He watches the vessel, because a man has to watch the vessel. **At twenty-five past nine the ticking stops, and at about twenty-five past nine the floor of the lowest room comes up.** Wick gets the boy out. Tarin runs up the lane; Niko is at the branch; three men and a bar take eleven minutes to lift the log, and Tarin's left shoulder goes again. The branch stopped itself at the right level in the right tank, at 10:10, and the lane does not drain. He writes the whole of it up and then writes the thing he is obliged to write: **he cannot say whether he lost it at the witness, or at the record, or at his standing**, keeps the three apart, estimates the unnamed channel's extra third and will not stake anything on it, and will not write down a guess in a book. Niko's closing line: *That is not a mystery, Tarin. That is a job. You watched the tank because the tank is the vessel, and nobody watched the thing under the floor, because you cannot post a man on a floor.*

### Chapter 0018 — What The Ground Did (Yara Sen, Day 31, half past six to eleven at night)
Yara brings a spade, as promised, and puts it in four places: the ground is wet at a hand's depth at all four, and at two at the throat. She does the work in the open, in order, and refuses to interpret for them — a jar set to noon so that chemistry is in charge for one morning, a silver spoon in a pint of water on a plate in the sun, and the spade. The finding: thirty years of a plate over an outfall has turned the lane's ground into a closed sponge with a water table standing at the surface of the street, and the well in the dispensary awning survived only because it goes down into something below the sponge. **The terrace road main runs brackish by eleven o'clock** — the same water at the other end of the same problem, a lead main six feet from where she is standing — and it goes off the moment the pressure drops, so keep it running. Nine children with the marks; one at three points for the second time in nine days with burning palms, in the third stage. The lowest room has a hand's depth in it that is not going to stop, and Ondra takes his frame apart and leans it on the outside wall and his boy sleeps in a chair, and Yara tells him the room is wet and then damp and then a room with a damp corner, and that she has not got a better offer than the top step and a tarpaulin. Then the money. On the 27th a man from the Fenner district was given a pail; on the 28th he was back with four men and two carts, and by the 31st **the blue steps is selling its own cistern** at a penny rising to threepence, to people who do not live there, **six hundred and eighty gallons and eighty-six pounds**. Bel Crale stopped it for an hour and moved when a woman with a low barrel asked her to. The clean-water cart stops coming on the 30th — not told to, not threatened, just a man with a name and a row and a wife — and the next two will not come into the lane at all. Yara's requirement is seventy gallons a day; the dispensary had fourteen pounds and sixpence on the 1st and has **seven pounds and sixteen shillings** on the 31st, which at the new price buys twenty-two gallons, a third of one day. She goes to the fourth step and asks for two hundred gallons, and says out loud what it will cost: a household's water going into a clinic is a thing a man with a form can write two lines about. Hanne Roeg's answer: **write it down, put the fourth step against it, and write beside it that the water is not fit to drink and we are giving it away anyway.** Yara also tells her plainly that she cannot be findable and unfindable at once, and Hanne Roeg says she priced it on Saturday. The day's last entry goes in Yara's book under the date: a sale is a fact, a fact with a price on it is a record, and the next physician is going to arrive in about four weeks and want to know why there is money in it.

### Chapter 0019 — The Cut Off The Quarry Side (Niko Venn, Day 33, and two in the morning on the 2nd of the next month)
First Niko chapter. He has been useless for thirteen days, and worse, the contract of his apprenticeship died with the district reference on the 10th. He has bought eight fathoms of rope on credit off a chandler he knows from a pipe requisition, two hooded lamps and oil, and a tin-lid hood for the second one, and Wick helped him carry them and looked at him a long time. **The thump has gone from two a second to three**, and never stops, and a pump making up for a head it has lost is not a mystery: somebody is drawing the Ashgate head and Tarin drew it for nine hours on the 30th. Niko catches Tarin on the mill wall and lays out the descent: a cut in the rock face two miles past the mill, not in the district and not on any plan, forty feet down to the quarry wall at the back of the filter house, a rope-eye in an iron ring; the only other way in is the works' front door, which carries a warden's seal six years old. He makes Tarin say all three reasons out loud with a time on them — no authority, no money, no crew — and makes him say why he cannot be the man on the record for the rope: a second name under a suspended man's name in the same month is a continuation, not a rescue. And the finding: **there is no man in the district who will put a name on a board about a rope.** Hanne Roeg refuses at half past nine and gives six reasons, and the sixth wins: a district that has been visible for eleven days has a barrow, a bucket, eighty-six pounds, a doctor's book and a discharged tap, and in three weeks a man with a form; add a hole in a hill and a coil of rope and a debt at a chandler's and the district gets taken off the schedule in October — not out of wickedness, because it will be busy. She is right, and the reason she is right is Tarin's own Day 30. He promises not to come up the lane on this business without asking, and she tells him the promise is one he cannot keep and will be held to anyway. They go up the mill road at two in the morning on the second with Wick, who comes because Tarin does not ask him, and whose condition is that he is standing in the dark holding a rope and not putting his name on anything. The cut is at the north end of the face and invisible from thirty feet below. The **spoil at the mouth is a week old**, there is standing water rilling down it, four size-nine prints going in and one set coming out, a lantern lit for about two hours, an open tin of oil, and a coil of good hemp **cut by somebody else's knife**. And then the thump stops, in the middle of Niko's hand on it, and a voice twenty feet down on a staging says *get the ladder, and tell Hallam to bring the other can, we are running her dry*, and the man on the staging looks up at three people on a ledge and says: **"Well. You will be the Water Office, and you will be the wrong week."**

### Chapter 0020 — A Named Route (Sluice Warden Pell, Day 34, morning, the blue steps)
First Pell chapter. A warden's office cannot refuse anything; it can only **return** an application, with reasons, in a form, on a date, signed — and a return is a thing done to a schedule, not to a person. He took a day to write four grounds. **One:** the route exists and the works exist, but the office holds no service record; its only entries are the Day 1 notice addressed to no district with an empty cause box and the Day 20 timed entry, and both are conditions, and a warden enters conditions and does not enter services. **Two:** the source is unverified, and the volume that documents it has been in restricted handling since the seventeenth under rule nine. **Three:** four names on a sheet in an unstated hand, and a witness is entered against a route, and there is no route to enter him against. **Four:** the date is accepted, and he writes that it is accepted, because a return with no truth in it can be thrown out in an hour. He also reads out the **warden's note**, which is the worse half: the Day 17 return with blank boxes, entered under the warden's discretion, is the first document in the district book in which the words *the blue steps* appear, and it says the water is fit for no use, the plate is weeping, and there is no row, no keeper, no schedule entry. Hanne Roeg's reading of it: *the only name I have ever had in any city document is the name of a defect.* Pell then gives her the thing he will not put in writing and says so: the district's legal existence and one leaf in Vellum are the same object, and if the leaf can be read you have a source, and if you have a source the leaf can be read, and neither can be got first. What he *will* put in writing is a warden's note that records that grounds one and three are declined for want of an office record and an entry, that the volume is restricted under rule nine, that **a restricted volume may be consulted by a named applicant on a scheduled reading day with a second reader's countersignature, and the next one is the fourth of the next month**, that this office has no power to enter a district, amend a schedule, inspect a sealed works, certify a source, or answer a question about Ashgate, that the hearing on charge one was set for the 22nd and adjourned without a date and the midsummer review has been set for three other days since, and that on the 17th a named engineer under two charges said in a public yard that he did not know the source of the water, and the district drank it anyway, and the warden wrote it down. He refuses to say one word about the charges in a return on a district application. **Tarin names the two ways out in the yard**: the volume will tell him the head and not the place, and there is a descent off the quarry side that he cannot authorise and cannot pay and has stopped trying to stop. Two hundred people decline to leave the yard when Hanne asks whether anybody wants to say something. She sends him to talk to the boy and then asks for one day in the lane where nothing happens. Pell goes up the track with a return in his coat and a reading day in his head, and says he will write down what he can see in the district book every week for as long as he holds the post, including what is very bad for this lane.
